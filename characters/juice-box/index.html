    Juice Box¶

    Quick facts

    • Role: Changeling prostitute/bartender NPC, Hump Town (Floor 5, Book 4)
    • Species: Changeling — one of a refugee population of shapeshifters displaced from their original home, "the Hunting Grounds" (Floor 6)
    • True form: Not physically shown — changelings shift into a generic race-form (human by default here) with cosmetic adjustment, gaining minor real abilities of the mimicked race
    • Type: Dungeon-born NPC (never a crawler); warlord as of Book 6
    • Class: None stated — changelings in the text have forms and a library, not a class
    • Subclass: None stated
    • Level: Unreliable by design — displays 17 (Book 4), 35 in human form and 120 in her blank form (Book 7). Mordecai says there is no way to read a high-level changeling's real level, even in blank form. She claims to have lived "at least 400 different lives"
    • Notable spells/abilities: Changeling shapeshifting into anything she or her line has physically touched, including forms nobody else has — the Unwashed / The Beautiful Place (the personification of the Nothing), a hunter wraith (which can possess and puppet a body, and carries the Inevitable Ruin truth-curse), and a Fleshmancer capable of grafting transplanted organs
    • Notable items: None named
    • Status: Alive as far as the text shows. Sent through the Gate of the Feral Gods to Floor 9's Larracos at the end of Book 4; co-warlord of the Home Team in Book 6, Faction Wars' tenth, NPC-populated team, alongside Sir Ferdinand; the team arrives on Floor 9 in Book 7 renamed Team Retribution. Leaves the ninth floor at the very end of Book 7 by jumping into the Nothing, bound for the 12th floor "the long way" — what she becomes is never shown
    • Relationship: Engaged to Louis by the end of Book 4; marries him in Book 7 in a changeling small wedding and divorces him on the same floor

    Introduction¶

    Introduced as a ditzy, cheerfully transactional bartender at Hump Town's saferoom tavern, handing out juice boxes to the town's children and running a cosmetic-persona side hustle for paying crawlers. Carl initially treats her as comic relief — until she reveals a much sharper political and personal awareness than she lets on.

    What She's Hiding¶

    Juice Box is the sister of Henrik, the leader of Hump Town's hidden changeling "principals" — a faction secretly using the town's dromedarian collateral arrangement to try to obtain a resurrection spell for the necropolis's ghost queen, Quetzalcoatlus, in hopes of finding a cure for compression sickness, a birth defect increasingly common among changeling children born in this world (one in four births, by her account). She initially disapproves of her brother's methods — particularly his torture of the gnome hostage Wynne — but ultimately helps Carl's plans once she comes to trust him.

    Awakening¶

    Like Fire Brandy and Tizquick before her (Floor 4), Juice Box begins remembering fragments of a past life once exposed to Earth media (movies playing in Carl's personal space) and to enough honest conversation from Louis and Carl about the nature of her world. She recalls a dream/memory of being a schoolteacher forced to help kill infected students during some past outbreak, and recognizes the significance of "the Hunting Grounds" (Floor 6) as her people's true, lost home.

    Role in the Story — Book 4¶

    • Helps shelter dozens of Hump Town's children (both dromedarian and changeling) in Carl's personal space during the Wasteland bombardment, in exchange for a promise from Carl to try to bring her people down through the floors with him wherever possible
    • Reveals the existence and mechanics of the Gate of the Feral Gods' three-piece structure and the changelings' true goal, once Carl proves himself by saving the town
    • Discovers her brother Henrik is dead (killed by the ghost Quetzalcoatlus while trying a desperate backup plan inside the flooded necropolis) and processes the loss while continuing to help Carl
    • Sacrifices her own safety to walk into Carl's confrontation with Chris Andrews (possessed by Maggie My), correctly identifying the threat as "worse" than a simple changeling
    • Adopts the orphaned gnome girl Bonnie into Hump Town's changeling/dromedarian community at the book's end, promising Carl's group she'll always have a home there
    • At Carl's request, becomes the sole carrier sent through the Gate of the Feral Gods to Floor 6's city of Larracos in the book's climax — tasked with quietly telling other changeling survivors the truth about what they are, ahead of Carl and Donut's own eventual arrival

    Relationships¶

    • Henrik — her brother, leader of the changeling principals; dies in Book 4
    • Louis — reveals at the very end of the book that the two are engaged to be married, to Carl's open astonishment
    • Carl — a trust built slowly over the book; extracts and receives a genuine promise that he'll try to bring changeling survivors down through the floors with him

    Book 6: The Home Team¶

    Juice Box survived the trip to Floor 9 at the end of Book 4, and Book 6 reveals what she has been doing down there. She has been running an insurrection.

    Wearing the Enemy's Face¶

    She crashes the Faction Wars preproduction meeting by beheading the viceroy of the Madness mid-session and taking his seat — wearing the face of Epitome Noflex, mother of the Dream's representative Epitome Tagg, and the bald elf whose photograph caused all of Louis's problems.

    "You. My men told me they found you and killed you."

    "Obviously not."

    Tagg had ordered his own mother killed. The executive producer Cascadia rules that nobody can touch her: "The rules haven't changed. She's an NPC. The AI won't let us touch this." Tagg's conclusion, and the book's: "They're becoming self-aware. All of them. It's going to be impossible for anybody to win."

    She also reminds Crown Prince Stalwart of the arrow she once put through his eye, shifting into his form with the arrow still in it — "Do you remember this one? I bet it hurt. Next time, I'll get your other eye." Stalwart does take a second arrow through the eye and die at that table, though Carl notes it wasn't her: "It hadn't been Juice Box unless she'd learned how to teleport."

    The Demand¶

    "If you want us to stop knocking down all of your pretty structures and killing your army folks, or to let you finish draining Larracos, there's only one way to do it. I want you guys to make a new rule… I want you to add my people as their own team, afforded all the safeties as the rest of you. I want regeneration for all of us."

    And, when Carl formally proposes it: "And with full voting rights." Her self-assessment in the same scene is the whole point of her arc: "I am my own team, thank you very much… I know how this works now. I know what I am."

    Winning¶

    The proposal sits unvoted for most of the book because the other warlords have stopped participating entirely. In the last minutes of Floor 8, Carl burns the first of his three Emergency Action Items to hand all outstanding votes to the audience — and the audience decides it:

    Pending Action Item, originally proposed by the Princess Posse: A tenth team populated with NPCs, afforded the same protections as the rest.

    This item has been passed by a 52% audience vote.

    This team will be designated as Home Team with the city of Larracos as their base… This team's assigned co-warlords are the NPCs Juice Box and Sir Ferdinand.

    Her co-warlord is Sir Ferdinand — Donut's pre-apocalypse cat love, now titled. Donut gasps at the mention of "Gravy Boat."

    Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin¶

    Book 6 won her people a team. Book 7 reveals what she wanted it for, and it was never the team.

    Team Retribution¶

    The tenth team the audience voted into existence at the end of Book 6 arrives on Floor 9 under a name of the NPCs' own choosing: Team Retribution, the defending team, headquartered in Larracos, with Juice Box and Sir Ferdinand as co-warlords. Carl's summary early in the book: "there are ten teams total. Nine attacking teams and one defending team, the NPCs, who have decided to call themselves Team Retribution."

    The partnership is lopsided in a way that infuriates her. Ferdinand — "Warlord on High, Sir Ferdinand. Cat. Level 100" — holds the warlord menus, the mayorship of Shanty Town and control of the city defense system, because the system classes him as crawler stock rather than dungeon-born. Juice Box has no menus at all, only the mental messaging and the floating words.

    "This cat has done nothing but cause problems, and now he has been given control of my people… If he dies, we lose control, so he must be kept safe."

    That, she admits, is the only reason he is still breathing.

    She has changed since Floor 5. When she takes her human form for Carl in Chapter 9 she has scars she didn't have before, and her left ear doesn't form with the rest of her — it appears ten seconds late, "almost like an afterthought." Her displayed level is useless as a measure: 17 on Floor 5, 35 in human form here, 120 in her blank form at the wedding. Mordecai says there is no way to read the real number off a high-level changeling.

    Married, Then Not¶

    She marries Louis in Chapter 10, in the entrance bar of the Desperado Club, in the private "small wedding" that is the legally binding half of a changeling marriage. She wants the houses bonded; Louis agrees mostly because the alliance needs it. What she actually feels she tells Carl much later, watching over Louis's unconscious body:

    "There is so much chaos and death in this world, and I needed an anchor… When you see someone like that, and it is so free of the rot of cynicism, so free of corruption, it's like the most alluring, beautiful thing in the world… He is to me like Donut is to you."

    Then she saves his life by taking the form of a hunter wraith and possessing him — and the form carries a curse called the Inevitable Ruin, which the book also takes its title from. It shows the possessor the truth of what they possessed.

    "But for me, when I had Louis possessed in order to save him, the curse showed me the truth. And the truth is that he's terrified of me. The truth is he's not in love with me. He thinks he has a special ability that causes NPCs to fall in love with him… It doesn't matter that it's not true. He doesn't trust what I feel, and he never will."

    She files the divorce papers inside the month. The same conversation carries the first hint of what she is really carrying:

    "I have something inside me, Carl. Something very, very powerful, and I've been afraid of it for a long time. But the thought of using it, really using it has been unthinkable because there are people like him out there."

    The Prophecy¶

    At the warlord council (end of Chapter 43), Odette presses her for a statement in front of the galaxy. Juice Box gives one — "Fuck all of you… You had no right to do what you did to my people" — and then changes into something nobody at the table has ever seen in the flesh: a midnight-black, thin alien with no arms and electrified tentacles waving from its shoulders. Cascadia shrieks and drops her drink. Ferdinand bolts for Carl's shoulder. Architect Houston stands up shaking with excitement.

    Her voice changes with the form — "Gone was that trailer park girl drawl, replaced with the voice of a demon."

    "It's coming. And not just for these here with me today, all of whom are already marked and damned. Those of you at home, safe with the knowledge everything you've ever known is real, true. Those of you watching this, riveted to your screens, waiting to see what happens next. It is coming. It is coming for you all."

    Odette tentatively floated closer. "What's coming?" she asked.

    She doesn't answer. She walks out through the wall on hundreds of root-like legs.

    Chapter 44 is the table trying to work out what she was. Houston calls it "the personification of the Nothing," and says it has a thousand names — "The Hag. The Unwashed. The Stalker. The Last Light. The Thirst. The Fell. The Inexorable" — and that the Viceroys call it The Beautiful Place. Cascadia: "We're banned from using the Unwashed as a mob." Carl's chill is the obvious follow-up question: a changeling can only take a form it has physically touched, so how does she have this one?

    What She Actually Wants¶

    The Justice Light interlude is where the floor's real plot gets stated out loud. Justice, building the trap system beneath the castle, works out that one of his sluggalo assistants has been swapped for a changeling spy named Jessica, and uses her to open a channel to Juice Box. Along the way she casually revises her own age: not a hundred lives, but "at least 400 different lives up until this point."

    He lays out what her alliance with the crawlers is actually subordinate to:

    "The gods. It's all about the gods. It has been about them the whole time. You're risking it all so you and your kind can physically touch as many of them as possible."

    She does not deny it. Her accounting of the Club Vanquisher incident is a status report on the programme:

    "We lost three. We did not cause the gods to appear, but we were in position just in case. One of us managed to touch Bast—the panther god—but she died before she could escape. At this point, only one of us has touched a deity, and that one is... wrong."

    This is her brother Henrik's project from Floor 5, scaled up to the divine: changelings can only become what they have touched, and every god touched is a god added to the library. Justice Light's offer — a way to touch a god and survive — is what buys the crawlers her full cooperation for the rest of the war. He also notes she was never as boxed in as she let on: "You could've killed the Sultana and her entire court quite easily. Hell, I believe you could end all this right now."

    Her spies are everywhere, in every base, including Carl's.

    The Rest of the War¶

    She keeps her side of it. Her changelings hold the Dream's throne room; she pulls them out on request to keep Tagg from killing Louis, and puts them back when it's time. She takes the hunter wraith form to extract Louis from the Reaver castle. She is the one who identifies Tagg inside his chest. And in the surgery that frees him she shifts into a Fleshmancer — a tall, dark-skinned male figure — and casts the spell that makes the transplanted naiad organs take: "Casting now," Juice Box said, her male voice calm.

    Into the Nothing¶

    With Emberus burning the floor, the crawlers take the stairwell and the NPCs transfer to the 12th. Juice Box doesn't take the transfer.

    Juice Box: I will be leaving approximately 10 seconds after everyone else.

    Carl: Wait, what the fuck? Where are you? Where are you going? You're not transferring to the 12th?

    Juice Box: Oh, I'm going to the 12th floor all right. But Justice Light and I are taking the long way. It's okay, Carl. Trust me. You'll like this one.

    The long way is the Nothing, through a permanent portal made from the broken winding box of the Gate of the Feral Gods. Her departure and Ferdinand's are logged as abandoning their posts — Team Retribution is defeated, and the Princess Posse wins Faction Wars.

    Justice Light told her he was coming with her. He lied; he stayed behind to spring his trap, which breaks the Nothing open and drains it into the Halls of the Ascendency, Sheol and Scolopendra's lair. His last thought about her is the last thing the book says on the subject:

    Juice Box would be fine, if her short time in the Nothing didn't drive her mad. She had a journey ahead of her, but like he told her, if she managed to survive this next part, she would be reborn as the ultimate weapon to end this madness. This war.

    Unresolved

    What Juice Box becomes is never shown. The line above is Justice Light's belief, relayed in his own point of view in the book's final chapter, and it is conditional on her surviving and on her sanity holding. She does not appear again before the end of the book, nothing confirms she reached the 12th floor, and the text gives no description of the "ultimate weapon" she is supposed to be reborn as.

    Sourced from Book 4: The Gate of the Feral Gods, Book 6: The Eye of the Bedlam Bride, and Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin.
