    Quasar¶

    Quick facts

    • Role: Carl's attorney, retained on Floor 6 (Book 5). By Book 7 he is a "volunteered" lawyer for the season, assigned to negotiate end-of-floor exit deals
    • Type: NPC (non-combatant, off-screen legal representative)
    • Race: Nullian ("Null") — a gray-skinned, bulbous-headed, big-eyed alien (a classic "Area-51 gray" look), about four and a half feet tall, appearing as a holo in a tan suit with a tie featuring an animated Nullian hula girl. Confirmed outright in Book 7, whose opening framing device is narrated by his niece Tempest, "the young nullian"
    • Family (Book 7): A brother whose daughter is Tempest; an ex-wife, Nova; his people live "far out in the middle of nowhere," well away from the inner system
    • Class/Subclass: Not applicable — a lawyer, not a combatant
    • Notable spells/abilities: None — operates entirely through legal maneuvering, contract loopholes, and negotiation
    • Notable items: None described

    Overview¶

    Quasar is assigned to Carl as legal counsel once Carl becomes a "taxpaying stockholder" of his own new corporation, following the events of Book 4. He speaks entirely in crude, cheerfully vulgar one-liners — professional advice delivered in the cadence of a drunk buddy giving relationship tips — and communicates almost exclusively via chat, with an automated "out of office" style reply implying he spends much of his downtime drinking and womanizing.

    Role in the Story — Book 5¶

    Quasar negotiates Carl's opening deal with Syndicate liaison Orren: in exchange for temporarily surrendering the Gate of the Feral Gods (usable again once Carl reaches Floor 9), Carl receives four mercenary "cretin" bodyguards — Bomo, the Sledge, Clay-ton, and Very Sullen, good for floors six through eight — plus two bonus spells for two of them: Bomo gets a level-15 Teleport to Stairwell (with a roughly 25-day cooldown), and the Sledge gets Zerzura, which was Quasar's idea. Quasar explains Zerzura in detail beforehand — a town-teleportation spell that sends an entire town, NPCs and buildings included, one floor down per cast, with one exception: on "Scolopendra levels" it skips straight to the next Scolopendra level instead. He cites a past crawler, Remex the Grand, who got the spell on the fourth floor and moved his town down normally, floor by floor, until a cast made from the sixth floor (itself a Scolopendra level) skipped straight over floors seven and eight and dropped his unprepared people onto floor nine — precisely what later happens when the Sledge casts Zerzura from Floor 6 during the Butcher's Masquerade. The caster can't steer where within that floor it lands, though, and it almost always drops the town into the middle of a monster nest or boss room, killing everyone caught in the impact — which is the real danger, not a casting failure: everyone killed this way is instantly resurrected as a powerful "Children of Inpewt" revenant, and every surviving townsperson plus the caster is marked and actively hunted by those revenants until all of the marked are dead. Separately, the spell can only be attempted once — if the casting itself simply fails ("miscasts"), there's no second try. He also:

    • Secures an emergency extension of Vengeance of the Daughter's broadcast license by exploiting an obscure Borant "emergency use" statute (for shows running at lower-than-expected profits), tying Carl and Donut's safety to the show's continued ratings
    • Successfully sues to end the practice of hunters receiving live outside intelligence on crawler locations — a major turning point that lets Carl's Floor 6 counter-offensive succeed
    • Warns Carl, without directly saying so, that keeping Vengeance of the Daughter's ninth-floor extension alive will require Carl to keep the show exciting without outshining its actual star, or the producers may simply let the story kill him off
    • Flags a mysterious "other problem" that will cost about 150 million credits — more than Carl's entire net worth, and a possible bribery/legal risk even if he could raise it — and says he's "working on" an idea, leaving the matter unresolved at the end of the book

    Role in the Story — Book 6¶

    Quasar gets substantial screen time in Book 6, most of it spent working around a hostile liaison standing in the room.

    Faction Wars Intel¶

    His single most useful scene is a rushed sitrep delivered in a gap during the Shadow Boxer taping, with the caprid liaison Harbinger watching:

    "There's only so much I can say, legally that is, with that scary fucker standing over there. I can't answer too many questions, so keep your honey hole closed while I get this out. There's been a lot of movement on the Faction Wars front. Your team's castle landed right in the middle of the Lemig Sortion's battlefield, and it splattered half their army."

    The rest: the Zerzura ghouls are still loose but most of Carl's army made it to his own battlefield; the Lemigs are squatting in the wrecked elf castle and "consensus is they're fucked," with their commander still stranded in the Desperado Club; Signet's tattoo golems survived and "People really like the shark"; Tina is still an allosaurus; the changelings have started building Carl's stronghold; and Ferdinand "lost his hat and moved straight to the city."

    The legal win buried in that is his: the Lemig Sortion filed a grievance arguing the castle drop was an attack, and Quasar successfully argued it was an AI action. He fights the liaison for the right to say any of it — "Oh, keep your weird goat dick in the bag. There ain't no explicit rules against this. He's a sponsor." — and signs off with: "I hope plenty of you guys survive this floor because you're gonna need as big of an army as you can get."

    Adjutant Advice, in Mime¶

    At the adjutant selection he is not permitted to speak at all, so he conducts the entire negotiation by gesture from offstage: nodding along with Odette, shaking his head vigorously No when Carl challenges the candidate list, an enthusiastic thumbs up for the orc attorney Baroness Victory, and — bafflingly to Carl — "a tepid thumbs down" for Ripper Wonton, the obvious pick. Carl takes the steer: "Again, I looked to Quasar. He was bouncing up and down, pointing at her, giving me a thumbs up."

    The Residual's Bin¶

    His actual negotiation of the book is the seizure of the Residual's plastic bin, which he opens on precedent — "This is already settled caselaw. Grixist Swarm V. Syndicate Council. He's already picked up the loot. You can't un-lick a butthole that's already been licked." — and then, when Carl blurts out that he'll just trade for it, salvages by stealing the idea: "Shut your titty hole… A great idea just came to me. What if we trade for it?"

    Net result: one wand recharge scroll, three Cracker Jack scrolls, and Orren agreeing that the guild keeps the four cretins free of charge for the ninth floor. Carl does not get the Gate of the Feral Gods back. Quasar is "unceremoniously kicked away in the middle of a sentence once the deal was done," and takes his 15%.

    Not Quasar's deal

    Carl's 990,000-gold interest-free church loan and the Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus are arranged inside Club Vanquisher by the high cleric Pater Coal and a satyr jeweller. Quasar is not present and has no part in it.

    Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin¶

    Quasar bookends the whole novel. He opens it, in a domestic scene that has nothing to do with the dungeon, and he closes it, in a freezing empty room with Carl covered in ash. In between he appears twice, briefly, and both times he is delivering bad legal news from a long way off.

    Tempest’s Floor 8 Recap¶

    The book’s prologue material is a school assignment. Quasar’s niece Tempest — “the young nullian,” which is the clearest confirmation in the series of what Quasar himself is — needs a recap of Floor 8 for a report on “perceptions of the crawl,” and she has been assigned Carl and Donut specifically because she is related to their attorney. He makes her fix his sink for it.

    “Most people love the sound of their own biscuit holes flapping. I am very busy, and my time is valuable. You want information, you should learn to trade for it. It’s good for you.”

    “But why have me fix your sink? Why do you even have this thing? I don’t know shit about this old plumbing stuff. Can’t you do it yourself?”

    “I could, but I’m teaching you a valuable life lesson. Never give shit up for free because if you do, they’ll just keep coming back to you and will expect more and more.”

    His first attempt at the recap is four sentences long:

    “Okay,” Quasar said. “Carl fucked shit up. He fucked up a little in the end and now has a spider stuck in his chest. The end.”

    Blackmailed into a real answer, he delivers the entire Floor 8 recap in his own register — the card game (“these fights were like a Saccathian orgy”), Quan stealing Carl’s key, Tsarina Tserendolgor’s death and the flamethrower and meatball Carl inherited from her, Sister Ines, Amayon, Paz killing the goddess Ysalte — and then pivots into the setup for Floor 9 that the reader actually needs: that neither Donut nor Katia can leave until all the Naga are dead, that only one of them can leave at all, and that Katia’s exit depends on the Orchid and on Huanxin Jinx. He also teases, without knowing the name, the thing Book 7 will spend its length on: “something else happened after that god died that only us viewers saw, something that’s going to be a big deal later. It has to do with one of the ladies in the party, but I can’t remember her name.”

    The scene ends with the recorder switched off:

    “Are you in danger? For representing him, I mean? Like, real danger?”

    Quasar let out a long stream of smoke. He wanted to lie, but he decided to tell her the truth. The kid deserved it.

    “I think we all are, kiddo. It’s the first time in my life I’m glad we live so far out in the middle of nowhere.”

    His interjections about Tempest’s skin, her teacher, her mother and her grandmother are all present and all unprintable. Her assessment: “The only lesson I’ve learned today is that my uncle is a colossal prick.”

    The Third Season Problem¶

    Carl’s first message on arriving at Larracos is Quasar’s status report, and it is the only piece of legal groundwork Quasar lays in the book. The Vengeance of the Daughter third-season guarantee he negotiated back in Book 5 has been bought:

    “But remember that contract with Sensation Entertainment, Incorporated? The one for the Vengeance of the Daughter storyline? Yeah. So, even though the main character is dead and gone, that guarantee of a third season we negotiated is about to haunt the fuck out of us. Sensation Entertainment just got purchased by a subsidiary of the Dream, and things are moving. They’re demanding their third season.”

    His advice: stay away from every elite Carl meets, expect the worst, and accept that the courts are not the final authority here — “the AI gives zero shits about what the courts say anyway. If it thinks it’s going to be funny, it’s going to let it happen. Just keep your head down and listen to what Judge Victory tells you. She’s a smart one.”

    He is also, as ever, outclassed on paper. Asked by Donut whether Quasar went to the same law school as the orc judge Baroness Victory and the Syndicate’s Panford: “Victory let out a surprisingly pig-like snort. ‘I think not.’” And when Carl tries to invoke him against the liaison Orren mid-crisis, Orren shuts it down: “We are beyond lawyers at this point, Carl, and I think you know that.”

    The Exit Interview¶

    The epilogue is Carl and Quasar alone in a doorless zero-zone cubicle, minutes after the ninth floor burns. Quasar is wearing a tie with Mongo on it. He gives Carl a moment before starting, which Carl notes as uncharacteristic.

    “Before we start, buddy boy, on behalf of the Syndicate Intergalactic Bar Association, I’d like to personally thank you. There being just about 20,000 crawlers making it to the 10th floor is quite the accomplishment. Lots of my colleagues getting work today, and some of them are pretty easy on the receptors, if you know what I’m saying.”

    The record number of survivors is also why the offers are worthless — “That, unfortunately, is a side effect of having so many survivors. You’d be better off working as a scrub boy at a bathhouse in a dromedarian leprosy colony.” The list runs to fifty seasons undead, the Desperado Club bomber’s guild, and one genuinely interesting option: Chaco’s job, offered jointly to Carl and Donut, still open at the end of Floor 10.

    The one real moment between them comes when Carl pushes:

    “Do you? Do you know what I’m going through?”

    He was silent for a long moment, which was unusual for the attorney.

    “Of course not,” Quasar finally said.

    He also sells Carl the next floor’s premise, at a cost of one extra season on any future deal: Floor 10 is called “Don’t Come in Last.” Crawlers pick a conveyance, biological or mechanical, make a fixed number of point-to-point journeys with upgrades along the way, and “if you come in last place, the survival containment around your vehicle is removed.”

    Carl declines every offer. Quasar writes it down: “I am shocked.”

    The book’s last line of dialogue is his. Donut was assigned a different lawyer for her own meeting, which did not go well:

    “Her name is Princess Chandra, Esquire. Apparently her and Donut already had their meeting, and now that you’ve officially declined your offers I can tell you she declined as well. I am hearing there were some serious fireworks during their meeting. And not the good kind. We need to have a serious talk about her. Donut’s lawyer, I mean.”

    I grunted. “Why? Is she another catgirl?”

    “No. Worse, buddy. She’s your new wife.”

    Book 8: A Parade of Horribles¶

    Quasar gets his first full character-POV interlude — opening the transition from Floor 10 to Floor 11 — and it turns his usual comic-relief register into something much more personal.

    Watching the Ascendency Begin¶

    The interlude opens with Quasar at an attorney bar with his old law-school friend Kiki, watching the Floor 10-to-11 transition screens: 203 crawlers reached Floor 11, 172 have already taken exit deals, and rogue, unsponsored gods are teleporting briefly into real space near tunnel nodes, triggering galaxy-wide panic — the same crisis Carl's Plenty of Plenty broadcast helped set off (see Carl).

    The Attack on His Family¶

    Mid-conversation, Quasar gets a video call from his niece Tempest (already established from Book 7 as his equal in casual abuse): Taurin mercenaries have attacked his brother's home. Tempest fends them off herself, killing two with an illegal blaster Quasar had given her.

    Chandra's Retaliation¶

    Quasar identifies the client behind the hit immediately — Princess Chandra, Esq., Donut's own attorney, retaliating for the "hit" Donut placed on her after their confrontation. He hires Kiki on the spot for both legal and physical protection:

    "Both," he said to Kiki. "Fucking both."

    He also confirms, in the same interlude, that his own legal fight against Chandra is ongoing but losing ground: a hearing over the legality of Carl's marriage certificate is scheduled, and he expects to lose it, though the filing itself has at least paused several of Chandra's other actions.

    Sets up Book 9

    Book 8 does not resolve Quasar's vow of revenge against Chandra on the page — it's raised here and then left as a personal subplot, alongside the still-unresolved marriage and conservatorship fights.
