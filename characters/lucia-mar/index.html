    Lucia Mar¶

    Quick facts

    • Age: About 13
    • Likely origin: South America (broadcast context, unconfirmed)
    • Signature gear: A mace and a full set of glowing silver armor, worn under her original yellow-and-blue soccer jersey as a tabard
    • Pets: Two rottweilers, Cici and Gustavo 3 — both dead by the end of Book 7 (Cici decapitated by Donut's Hole in Book 5; Gustavo 3 killed by Carl's pet Sir Rendlegore in Book 7)
    • Notable stat: Very high Dexterity (wall-running, flips, acrobatic combat); over 200 player-killer skulls plus 20 dead hunters by Book 7
    • Status (Book 7): Alive. Captured and conscripted by Carl on Floor 9, then released when the floor ends; she descends to Floor 10 partied with Florin. She is also a dead-man's switch for roughly 120,000 children held on the Earth surface, and by her own account an ally of "the Eulogist"
    • Type/Class/Subclass: Not shown in Book 1 → Lajabless race / Black Inquisitor General class (cleric/mage/warrior triple class), revealed Book 2
    • Level: 29 — 1st on the Floor 3 leaderboard, the highest-ranked crawler shown
    • Notable spells/abilities: No named spells shown; a personal magical shield noticed late in Book 1; her Lajabless race halves her strength but doubles spell power in human form, and the reverse in her skeletal "goat-leg" form
    • Notable items: A mace; a full set of glowing silver armor; a personal magical shield

    Overview¶

    Lucia Mar is never met directly by Carl — she exists entirely as a recurring figure on the in-universe broadcast, one of the season's breakout stars and a running benchmark Carl measures his own progress against. She's first shown in an early recap tearing through a cow-sized spider with her two rottweilers before graduating to a mace and full magical armor, still worn over her original soccer jersey.

    Cici & Gustavo 3¶

    Her two rottweilers are dungeon "familiars" rather than standard pet-class mobs, which is why she can have two of them at once (Mordecai later explains this exception applies to certain classes, races, or unusually high-Charisma crawlers). Cici wears a chainmail sweater; Gustavo 3 develops the ability to shoot lightning when he barks. Per Carl's naming-convention guess, "3" indicates he's the third dog named Gustavo (without a surname) to enter the dungeon.

    Recurring Broadcast Presence¶

    Lucia Mar reappears across multiple recap episodes throughout Book 1:

    • Shown obliterating a nest of monsters before descending a stairwell, then whisked away for a guest appearance on Dungeon Crawler After Hours with Odette immediately before Carl and Donut's own appearance — one of her dogs attacks a producer backstage.
    • By roughly the book's midpoint, she has four player-killer skulls by her name, with the circumstances never explained on-air.
    • After Carl's "Pork Boy" incident goes viral, his appearance fee is reported to have doubled to the point of equaling hers — used as an explicit measure of how famous he's become.
    • Later shown smashing a Brindled Vespa with a personal magical shield Carl hadn't previously noticed her have.

    Role in the Story¶

    Lucia Mar never interacts with Carl directly, but functions as his constant point of comparison for how a "successful" crawler is supposed to look — decked out in flashy magical gear, high Dexterity, camera-ready — which Carl explicitly contrasts with his own scrappy, unarmed, improvised approach.

    Book 2: Race/Class Reveal¶

    On Floor 3, the recap show reveals Lucia Mar's choices: race Lajabless, one of the most extreme options shown — she spends half the day as a beautiful adult woman (double spell power, half strength) and the other half with a skull face and a transformed goat leg (double strength, half spell power, and a permanent limp that ends her wall-running). Her class, a triple cleric/mage/warrior hybrid called Black Inquisitor General, is described by Mordecai as making her likely "the most powerful crawler in this game." By the time Floor 3 collapses, she's ranked 1st on the leaderboard at Level 29, ahead of every other named crawler including Hekla and Carl. Donut, jealous, calls her "awful" and "ugly" on-air.

    Book 2: Desperado Club Incident & the Feud¶

    Lucia Mar's dogs go berserk inside the Desperado Club's pet playroom, killing two attendants and another pet before breaking loose onto the dance floor; while the room is frozen by one of her spells, she steals liquor and kills an elite gnoll with her mace, getting her club membership permanently revoked. The incident (and Donut's own drunken, overheard insult of the dogs — "bitch-ass rottweilers") is spun by the broadcast into a manufactured rivalry, "Princess Donut Versus Cici and Gustavo 3." Confronted about it on the arena show Knuckle Cracking, Lucia Mar — in her skull-faced Lajabless form — threatens to kill Donut and "rip Carl to pieces" for his gear; Mordecai confirms the two parties will eventually be forced together for a televised confrontation, but not before Floor 6 at the earliest.

    Book 5: The Fight with Donut, and the Voices in Her Head¶

    Lucia Mar finally collides with Carl and Donut directly on Floor 6, seizing an entire village (already named "Alucarda" — much to Donut's annoyance that she didn't get to name it herself) and setting a network of saferoom teleport traps as an ambush. While Carl is pulled away to a mandatory intergalactic fan convention, Donut faces her — and her rottweilers, Cici and Gustavo 3 — alone. Caught by a snare trap in Lucia's own treehouse pub, she survives Cici's lunge behind a stacked Entourage illusion (with an invisibility potion layered onto her own "copy"), then casts a single Hole beneath her own feet to drop straight through the floor to the ground below and break the snare. When Cici sticks her head down through that same hole hunting for her, Donut simply snaps the spell off from below — decapitating the dog with the closing hole rather than a second, separate cast. The recap footage of the fight becomes one of the season's biggest broadcast moments.

    The fight reveals something new and unsettling: Lucia's personality visibly flips mid-conversation between a genuinely scared, homesick child (in one aside, speaking with a Dutch accent and naming herself "Jill," a name Florin later confirms belongs to a real, ten-year-old crawler) and her usual violent, "Lajabless kid warlord" persona — implying multiple people, possibly children, are somehow sharing or fighting over control of her body via her dogs. Florin, confronting her at the Butcher's Masquerade, concludes the dog Gustavo is the one truly "in charge" and that the real Lucia/Jill can only surface in short bursts. She escapes the masquerade's final battle using a pre-set teleport trap before the boss fight even begins, avoiding the "Cage Fight Extreme" that kills several other crawlers.

    Book 6: The Battle of Beijing, and a Threat¶

    Lucia Mar spends Book 6 entirely offstage and is one of its most consequential figures anyway. She sits at #4 on the leaderboard and, per Florin, buys up every teleport trap and stairwell-chamber item that appears on the market: "Teleport traps and alcohol are the only things she purchases." Florin's read on her, before the phases split: "She has good days and bad days, and it's been more bad than good lately."

    The Battle of Beijing¶

    It happens off-page. The only direct glimpse is an announcer's aside — "We are interrupting that one-sided Lucia Mar slaughter in Beijing to bring you whatever this is" — and Li Jun's account of the aftermath:

    "It was Lucia Mar." He swallowed. "They're calling it the Battle of Beijing. Lucia, she never really collected any cards. She refused to play it. She got the six totems they automatically gave her, but they were all low-level monsters. And whenever a battle started, she discarded the first one, and she played the rest until her hand was empty which allowed her to use her spells and magic and inventory. She and her dog went in and killed everything."

    "She killed so many people. She set up a teleport trap, and Fang's team got caught up in it. He had a key. She killed him and his wife and took it. After that, we thought she'd already gone down the stairs, but when we got there, she was waiting for us. She can hide her presence now."

    The Doggie Doors¶

    Before going down the stairs she drops five Tier-3 Doggie Door personal-space upgrades on the ground and leaves them for Carl's guild. Li Jun: "She said Florin had messaged her before he'd gone down the stairs and told her to help us."

    Nobody can work out where she got them. Katia: "She shouldn't have had one, let alone five of them. It's a tier-3. Mordecai says these shouldn't be available until the ninth floor. He thinks Lucia stole them when she went on a show or something." Imani prices a guild-wide rollout at twenty-five million gold; they install one in Imani's room and hold the other four. Elle, on what it means: "It would've changed everything… We've been chasing a fast travel system since the whole guild system opened."

    The Threat¶

    Li Jun holds one detail back for Carl alone:

    Li Jun: This is just for you, Carl. There's more. Lucia dropped the upgrades, and she said she was helping us because Florin had asked, but before she went down the stairs, she went from really friendly to mean again, and she said she was saving something special for herself because she had a score to settle with Donut and that she would be seeing her on the next floor. She said she was going to kill both Donut and her pet dinosaur. That's why my sister wanted to fight her.

    Reported, not quoted

    The threat reaches Carl — and the reader — only through Li Jun's paraphrase. Lucia herself never appears on the page in Book 6.

    Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin¶

    Book 7 finally pays off the threat Li Jun carried down from Floor 8. Lucia comes for Donut and Mongo, she gets close, and the encounter ends with her losing her last dog, losing her autonomy, and handing Carl the worst piece of information in the book.

    She spends the ceasefire drinking. Li Jun’s early sighting: “She’s here in Shanty Town right now with her dog, drinking at the Possum Envy right on the edge of the border with the robot team. The Reavers. One of the new guys tried to recruit her, and she almost ripped his head off.” She refuses every recruiter, hides her position from the Find Crawler skill, and joins no army. Zev will not even try to warn her: “Lucia doesn’t respond to messages anymore. She was never my client, thank goodness.”

    The Nest Tower¶

    Chapter 70. Donut is alone at the top of the Nest, the Princess Posse’s tallest synergy tower in Shanty Town, sniping the Larracos defence towers while two gods fight below. Lucia walks in through the front door. The garrison dies in the time it takes to send three messages:

    Toyotomi: No. It is a crawler. A woman with a dog. She’s coming in.

    This message is from a deceased mercenary.

    Sergeant Bret S: We need backup! Backup!

    Bomo: Will stop her now.

    This message is from a deceased mercenary.

    Bomo — one of the four cretin mercenaries Quasar negotiated out of the Syndicate back in Book 5 — and the Sai sergeant Toyotomi are both killed, along with a squad of mages. Carl finds them on his way up: “I spared a quick, sad glance for Toyotomi, Bomo, and the defending mages, all of whom were cleaved right in half with a spell.” Toyotomi is dead for good; Bomo would regenerate on the 10th floor, but the guild no longer holds his contract.

    Lucia also drops a net over the tower so Donut cannot teleport out, then starts climbing. What she wants is stated plainly: “Give me your dinosaur, and I will leave. It is justice I seek. Nothing more.” Donut answers by activating Donkey Kong — a last-ditch defence that turns the tower’s stairs into a ramp and rolls barrels down it — and holds her off for several minutes while Carl levitates, bombs a theatre, accidentally triggers the entire Larracos city defence system, and fights his way in behind her.

    Conscripted¶

    The fight ends by accident. Donut throws Gustavo 3 down the ramp, Lucia goes with him, and the two of them bowl into Carl halfway up, along with Raul — the crab from Donut’s Floor 8 card deck, now mythic-rarity and level 110, who pins Lucia in the entrance hall while announcing his own magnificence.

    Carl uses the potion Li Na had just duplicated for him: a Hell-Kissed Potion of Conscription, level 18, looted from a dead war mage and brewed in the Nothing by Krakaren Prime. The item text notes it “will work against pretty much any non-affiliated entities, regardless of their protections, including most deities.”

    I smashed the screaming, Hell-kissed Conscription potion against Lucia’s head.

    The potion worked. A pink glow appeared around Lucia’s dot.

    Under Orders appears over her head, her terrified act drops instantly, and she spends the next several minutes trying and failing to attack him. He orders her protections down — a list the warlord menu shows as astonishingly long — and sits with her on the floor. His observation, examining her: “Through the stench of burned hair, I could smell sweat and alcohol. But there was something else, too. A faint perfume. A sweet one. The kind a child would pick.”

    Diah¶

    Carl is about to mark her for death when the thing Florin has been warning about for two books surfaces and speaks:

    “Sir,” she said. Her eyes had changed. Her voice had taken on a raspy quality. “My name is Diah. Lucia is with Alexandro. And Alexandro is only here when the dogs are close. My name is Diah. We are in the dark. You need to wait until the dog is close, and Lucia will be back. Wait until she is back. Please, sir. Please.”

    Under a direct order to tell the truth, she confirms it, and describes where “we” are:

    “I... I. Yes. Sir. Please. We are in the dark, and it’s so cold. Sometimes we can hear them talk. We can hear them whispering. It’s like there’s a river running through our room, and if we touch it, it hurts. They say such sad things. When the dog wanders off, Lucia fades, and one of us takes over, and we can see again. We take turns. Sometimes it’s day, and we can see the light.”

    Carl cancels the death mark. He never gets to ask a follow-up question.

    Rend Kills the Last Dog¶

    Gustavo 3 gets to Mongo. Carl, pinned by a gale-strike tower with a shattered arm, watching the dog and the dinosaur between him and safety, thinks: She’s never going to forgive me. This is what will break her.

    Then the meatball arrives.

    A terrible, deep giggling filled the night.

    The dark, round silhouette appeared above the ridge. Rend.

    Carl casts Hi-Yo Silver to yank his pet toward him, and Rend uses the only skill he has on the way past:

    The back half of Gustavo 3, the second of Lucia Mar’s two rottweilers, terror of the dungeon, exploded in a red mist as Rend rushed right up to me, stopping inches from my face.

    “Good boy,” I gasped at Rend.

    Mongo’s throat is torn open and his health is at a sliver. Lucia — still conscripted, and for a moment genuinely herself — drops to her knees and pours a pet healing potion into him, sobbing “You did it. You did it.” Donut’s thanks, later: “Thank you, Rend. I still think you’re disgusting, but you saved my boy’s life, and I will never forget that.” Rend belches in her face.

    “Now You Can’t Kill Me”¶

    Seconds after saving Mongo, her posture changes, and she delivers the book’s parting shot:

    “You should’ve killed us both when there was still a connection,” the girl said to me. “Now it’s too late. Now the Eulogist has another ally in the dungeon, and now you can’t kill me. Not unless you want all the children of Earth to die.” She shook her head and walked off.

    Zev fills in the rest, badly and reluctantly. Lucia’s presence in the crawl was originally a glitch; Borant chose to leave her in once she started earning, and the AI now says it is “unable to extract her.” What nobody understood until Gustavo 3 died is what is tied to her:

    Zev: It’s complicated, Carl. It’s not all the children. Just the ones that tried to get into the dungeon. And it’s only half of those. All the ones who were in a specific wing. They’re on the surface in the kinder facility.

    Carl: So what happens if she dies?

    Zev: As of about five minutes ago, we thought the glitch would self-correct and release everyone. That’s what’s supposed to happen. Now? We don’t know for certain. The Eulogist is another name for the AI in the center system. We don’t know how it’s all connected for certain. But now we think... we think if she goes, they all go.

    Carl: How many is it?

    Zev: I shouldn’t be telling you any of this.

    Carl: How many fucking children is it?

    Zev: About 120,000. Give or take a few.

    Carl’s response: “I almost killed her, Zev. You almost let me kill her.” And then: “She’s a goddamn dead man’s switch. A psychotic one. And she just lost her bodyguard. Don’t let her leave. Keep her safe.” Zev’s answer: “It’s out of my control, Carl.”

    Unresolved

    “The Eulogist” is the name for the AI of the centre system, and it runs through the whole book attached to the residual Agatha, the Nebular cult and the Open Intellect — see Game Mechanics. What Lucia’s alliance with it consists of, who “Diah” and “Alexandro” are, how children came to be inside her head, and why the dogs govern which of them is driving are all raised and none are answered.

    Afterwards¶

    The conscription only runs to the end of the floor. Lucia spends the rest of the book in an interview room with the liaisons — Florin, who has appointed himself her keeper, hopes “they talk her into taking a deal at the beginning of the 10th. If not, I will volunteer to keep her safe.” Jurgen the barbarian, whose pregnant wife Heidi is in the same kinder facility, attaches himself to the same job: “if Lucia’s fate is tied to that kinder facility where they’re keeping all the kids and the pregnant women, he’ll die protecting the girl.”

    The last sight of her on the ninth floor is in the rubble of Larracos:

    Lucia Mar was here, sitting on the ground, sobbing. Florin, Jurgen, and Imani surrounded her, talking.

    They were trying to convince her to take a deal. I hoped it would work.

    It does not, quite:

    Florin had talked Lucia Mar into partying with him. He said she’d agreed to take a deal, but she’d been sketchy.

    None of us believed she actually would. And as a result, he would not take one, either.
