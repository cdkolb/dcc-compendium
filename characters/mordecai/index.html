    Mordecai¶

    Quick facts

    • Role: Tutorial guide (Floors 1-3), then personal Manager (Book 2 onward)
    • Species: Skyfowl (true form) — currently a Changeling
    • Race/form (Floor 1): Rat Hooligan, level 50
    • Race/form (Floor 2): Bugaboo, level 50
    • Race/form (Floor 3): Incubus, level 50 ("Gigachad of the Over City")
    • Race/form (Floor 4): Grulke Infantry, level 50 — a militaristic toad-warrior race
    • Race/form (Floor 5): Still Grulke Infantry — coincidentally, a Bautista-supplied distraction beanbag toy Carl uses on Floor 5 summons an unrelated level-15 grulke toad mob that Carl notes looks just like Mordecai's own Floor-4 form
    • Race/form (Floor 6): Pocket Kuma, level 50 — a magic-infused, half-fairy creature (small bear crossbred with a capybara spirit and a sugar glider) originally bred as a pet for the High Elf royal court; knee-height, fuzzy, huge-eyed, buck-toothed, and "disgustingly cute," but can barely walk or fight
    • Race/form (Floor 8): Hills Cyclops, level 50 — the weakest and dumbest of the cyclops types
    • Race/form (Floor 9): Human, level 50 (Book 7) — specifically "something called a Canadian," per the system's examine text, snowsuit and raccoon hat included. His level does not change; the book never shows or explains the transition, and he is never described as reclassing
    • Status: Non-Combatant NPC; indentureship requirement fulfilled at the end of Book 2; served a Book 3 seven-day system ban after attacking a game-show host. Alive at the end of Book 7, still on Floor 9 with Carl and Donut
    • Type/Class/Subclass: Not applicable — Non-Combatant NPCs don't carry a crawler class
    • Notable spells/abilities: None described — as a Non-Combatant NPC, he cannot be attacked and isn't shown fighting. In Book 7 his upgraded alchemy table lets him reverse-engineer any potion he is handed and mass-produce the result
    • Notable items: None described beyond personal mementos (a photo of his brother, his mother's ashes) kept on a shelf in his guildhall. In Book 7 some of those ashes are built into a crossbow bolt (see below)

    What He Is¶

    Mordecai is a Changeling — a race that reshapes into a new form (always a type of mob native to the current floor) every time his guildhall relocates to a new level. His true species is the skyfowl, an eagle-like race; he keeps a framed photo of his brother (also a skyfowl) as one of the only possessions he was allowed to bring across dungeon moves. On Floor 1 he appears as a Rat Hooligan (a "smartest, fastest, ugliest" Rat-Kin variant); on Floor 2, after the guildhall relocates, he shapeshifts into a hulking, owl-eyed Bugaboo.

    Mordecai was a crawler himself, centuries ago, on his own now-mined homeworld. He made it to Floor 11 before deciding to stop, later telling Carl the deepest anyone has ever gotten in the game's history is Floor 13 — a human crawler from a different Earth-like world, who died within thirty minutes of arriving.

    Role in the Story¶

    As tutorial guide, Mordecai:

    • Grants Carl and Donut access to their inventory and menu systems
    • Explains core mechanics: loot box tiers (Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Legendary → Celestial), the Views/Followers/Favorites/Patrons system, safe zones, and boss types (Neighborhood → Borough → City → Province → Country → Floor)
    • Gives tactical advice after boss fights, generally in the form of "look for the clue" (e.g., pointing out that both the Bad Llama and the Juicer boss had an exploitable throat/vein weak point)
    • Is restricted from revealing certain information directly (spoilers about the current floor, deeper floors, or living admins) but often finds loophole ways to hint at it — telling Carl to "ask directly" or communicating through pointed non-answers

    The Odette Connection¶

    Mordecai was trained by Odette during his own crawl, generations ago. He reacts with immediate, visceral anger when Carl relays her request that he "seek her out" once his indentureship ends, saying he'd rather commit unspeakable acts than be in the same solar system as her. The full history between them is left unresolved in Book 1 — Odette only says she "pushed him too hard, and he paid dearly for it."

    What He Reveals (Under Pressure)¶

    Mordecai is more forthcoming than most NPCs seem to be allowed to be, especially once cameras aren't recording him talking (viewers can see/hear crawlers, but not NPCs, unless the NPC has press credentials):

    • His indentureship doesn't count toward completion unless one of his crawlers reaches Floor 4 — a fact Odette reveals, not him
    • Borant is being pressured by the kua-tin government (the Bloom, aka "the Party") to end the season as fast as possible due to a looming bankruptcy/debt-collection crisis
    • The Skull Empire, Orcish Supremacy, and Blood Sultanate are separate, rival political/factional entities that matter more from Floor 9 onward
    • Floors 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18 are linked "settings" that exist and progress independently of the crawlers

    Personality¶

    Grumpy, self-interested, but ultimately protective of his charges within the limits the system allows him. He's visibly weary from centuries of running tutorials, drinks, and keeps a shrine-like shelf of personal mementos (his mother's ashes, his brother's photo) that Donut repeatedly disturbs, to his great irritation.

    Book 2: The Manager¶

    Carl and Donut deliberately trick Mordecai into activating the hidden Manager benefit tied to Donut's Former Child Actor class (see Carl for how) — he doesn't learn it was intentional in Book 2. As Manager, he can physically accompany the party (including outside guildhalls, on urban floors) but can never engage in direct combat; in exchange he loses his own room, guild access, the information codex, and television access, relying only on memory to help them. His Incubus form comes with a built-in weakness to alcohol, leading to a memorable early-Book-2 bender that leaves Carl and Donut without guidance during a dangerous first night on Floor 3.

    He also secretly steals a physical newsletter (meant for NPC shopkeepers without feed access) to warn Carl and Donut about a coming "major battle event" — explicitly telling them that as Manager, "we can't engage in direct battle with mobs. Everything else is fair game."

    His indentureship requirement — reaching Floor 4 with a crawler — is fulfilled at the very end of Book 2. He is now technically free to leave crawler service, though the book ends with him still accompanying the party.

    Book 3: The Chaco Incident and a Seven-Day Absence¶

    Early on Floor 4, Mordecai attacks talk-show host Chaco — tongue-whipping a chair at him during a live segment — and is hit with a system-enforced seven-day ban/timeout, vanishing from the story for nearly the entire floor at the worst possible time. Chaco's on-air needling implies Mordecai did something under orders from "his manager" that hurt Mordecai personally and caused the pair to "lose Odette" — a specific accusation Mordecai refuses to discuss when he returns, only promising "it won't happen again." The full backstory behind it, and its connection to the still-unexplained rift between Mordecai and Odette, remains unresolved.

    He reappears in his new, Floor-4-mandated Grulke Infantry form — a squat, toad-headed warrior race — just as the floor's crisis is winding down, and is immediately confronted with a room soaked in blood, two new player-killer skulls, and a floor's worth of catching up to do.

    Book 3: The Ring Lecture¶

    On learning Carl has looted and worn the Enchanted Night Wyrm's Ring of Divine Suffering, Mordecai reacts more violently than at any prior point in the series — physically smacking Carl and threatening to bar Donut from associating with him past Floor 6 if he doesn't get rid of it. He explains that Divine Suffering rings are seeded every season specifically to bait crawlers into becoming farmable power sources for the Floor 9 faction wars, that every winning faction army historically includes a ring-bearer, and that simply possessing one paints an enormous target on its owner. Carl agrees to sell it before Floor 6.

    Book 3: The Iron Tangle as a Map of the Galaxy¶

    Using Katia's obsessively detailed hand-drawn map of the floor's subway system, Mordecai identifies the Iron Tangle's track layout as a disguised recreation of the Syndicate's own corporate logo — a schematic of real interstellar wormhole routes converging on a galactic core (the floor's central "abyss"). He connects the floor's Krakaren-vs-Pooka ghoul/drug plotline to real, ugly interstellar politics: a xenophobic caricature of an actual alien collective mind (the "Krakaren," more accurately translated as "the Apothecary") and the Plenty, the real species behind the dungeon's faster-than-light communication network, whom Borant-aligned factions (including the kua-tin Bloom) publicly demonize as a existential threat. He also lays out the dungeon's full deity-summoning rules for Carl (celestial boon, indentured summoning, and involuntary summoning; duration, size, and re-summoning mechanics) — directly enabling Carl's Grull gambit against the floor's mimic boss (see Carl).

    Book 4 Notes¶

    Mordecai plays a quieter, more support-focused role on Floor 5, spending much of the book at his alchemy and sapper's tables. Notable contributions:

    • Brews the Dolores Doesn't Splat potion (a rock-buffalo-mating-derived, five-application fall-cushioning potion with a signature side effect Carl and Katia never forget) and the Phase Lava Rock potion, the latter essential to freeing Chris Andrews from the Scree parasite possessing him
    • Correctly identifies the Infiltrator/Scree parasite controlling Chris on sight, and lays out the full shapeshifter taxonomy relevant to the floor: Doppelgangers (Katia's race) vs. Changelings (his own) vs. Mimics vs. Illusionists vs. Gondii/Valtay brain-worms vs. Infiltrators
    • Explains the dungeon's full deity-worship and celestial-grenade mechanics just in time for Carl to accidentally join Emberus's faith
    • Reacts to Carl's assassination of Admin Loita with open alarm ("You don't know the forces you're dealing with"), repeatedly urging him to sell off dangerous, high-profile loot (the Ring of Divine Suffering, the Gate itself) rather than keep provoking the season's producers
    • Eventually determines a use for a mysterious sponsor-gifted root vegetable (the "yam") with help from the hired NPC Samantha, producing the Phase Lava Rock potions in time

    Book 5 Notes¶

    Mordecai's guildhall relocation this floor turns him into a Pocket Kuma — a fuzzy, knee-high, buck-toothed, monkey-tailed creature so absurdly adorable that both Carl and Katia burst out laughing at him on sight, to his fury.

    He finally solves a Charm Animal potion recipe he's been chasing for 50 cycles — keyed to the mongoliensis species via a full liter of Mongo's own blood as the special ingredient (drained over three cut-and-heal cycles, to Donut's horror) — after a sponsor mails Carl the missing recipe step alongside a drawing (not a photo, despite Donut calling it one) of Mongo. Donut uses the finished potion to tame the velociraptor pack leader Kiwi. Mordecai also brews Uncle Morty's Insta Lawn-Kill and Undead Repellant, Extra-Strength Edition (an anti-plant-monster weedkiller laced with anti-undead boosts, used against the floor's Odious Creeper world-quest monsters) and a pair of Holy Gooper potion balls (blessed shrine water plus a healing potion and a coagulant, effective against vampires) with almost no notice. He talks Carl into taking the "Swole" strength milestone upgrade over "Prison Bitch" or "Stepson," and reacts to Carl's account of the Ring of Divine Suffering being marked onto Miriam Dom, and of Prepotente swallowing his own copy of the ring whole, with equal parts horror and professional curiosity.

    Mordecai also mentions, in an aside to Carl, that he once testified against Odette in an official trial — but is careful to clarify this happened when she was his manager, not back when she was a fellow crawler ("It was when she was my manager"), adding one more piece to the still-unresolved rift between them. He continues managing the party's rapidly growing collection of hired NPCs and rescued children from his post inside the guildhall.

    Book 6: The Prologue — How Uzzi Died¶

    Book 6 opens with the thing Mordecai has never talked about. The prologue is his own crawl, on the tenth floor, with a level collapse imminent.

    His party: Mordecai, his brother Uzzi (a skyfowl Storm Commander), the wolf-headed bard Chaco, and a skyfowl named Hold Steady. Their manager, non-corporeal and watching from outside, is Odette. The exit puzzle needs nine assembled pieces and will only open if it physically touches a storm god or is hit with god-tier storm magic. If Mordecai reaches the eleventh floor, Odette gets a bonus big enough to buy her way out of her own indentureship — nine seasons knocked off, and a residency berth in the inner system.

    The scheme was Odette's friend Armita's idea: use Armita, then working as a demigod attendant, as a cutout to reach Huanxin Jinx, the grixist heiress who sponsored the storm goddess Dodola every season, and pay her to drive Dodola into hitting the puzzle. The text is blunt about what this is: "Once money became involved, it clearly became cheating… It was cheating, and it was fraud."

    Huanxin escalates — ten percent, then a third, then half, then a threat to zero out the whole party. Odette breaks the deal: "Armita, tell her we don't have a deal."

    Mordecai offers to kill himself with his Final Blow skill instead. Odette has a better idea. She orders Chaco — a crawler in her own party, not a subordinate, and a worshipper of Dodola — to put the Dart of Ophiotaurus into Uzzi. Chaco refuses: "You want me to kill Uzzi? It's not going to happen." She coerces him with a promise of protection and a lie about the Outreach Guild.

    He throws the dart. Crawler Uzzi has fallen. Dodola is force-summoned into the shell of Uzzi's body inside a Circle of Containment; her own lightning ricochets and kills her in the immunity gap. A deity has fallen. The heavens tremble with rage. Chaco jumps ten levels and takes the godkill credit. The stairwell opens.

    Mordecai tackles Chaco into it, and then sends Odette a message:

    Mordecai: I am going to kill you, Odette. I am going to get free of this place, I am going to find where you are, and I am going to kill you.

    This retroactively explains the Book 3 incident in which Mordecai threw a chair at Chaco's head on live television and served a seven-day system ban for it.

    Book 6: Floor 8¶

    Form¶

    Hills Cyclops, level 50 — per the system, the weakest and dumbest of the cyclops types, shepherds by trade, and unable to see their own reflections. Mordecai's running complaint: "I hate it when I'm a cyclops. No depth perception plus I can't see my own reflection."

    The Children¶

    Carl and Donut catch him wearing a face full of children's makeup — "dragged face first through the makeup aisle at the dollar store" — and find a custom split hand-controller on the floor next to a pillow, built for a child with no arms. He has been quietly fostering six changeling orphans, using his own attendant slot plus all five of Carl's:

    "It's all the system would let me."

    "Combined, it's a little more than 1,000 gold a day. I've been paying myself with the money you've been giving me for supplies."

    Donut calls it embezzlement. They are specifically the children with compression sickness, a birth defect caused by their parents' malnutrition on a new world, which means their shapeshifted forms are always missing parts — including a girl named Ruby, who in human form has no arms and a sunken-in head. He took them because he judged they wouldn't survive the end of Floor 7.

    He can be criminally charged for the theft. Zev's response to the story is to try to monetise it — the AI refuses to let viewers watch it, so Borant licenses the story to a documentarian, Titan, who is preparing a vid about it with virtual characters. Donut isn't in it ("They didn't want to pay the extra licensing fee"). Mongo is. Mordecai is "especially grumpy" about the whole business.

    The Advice He Gives Once¶

    In Chapter 30, privately, he says the thing nobody else will say about Katia's succession problem — that the safe answer is to let her go.

    Mordecai: I'm helping Katia with this because she's become my friend. No, more than that. She's part of my family… You, Carl, you're helping her for the same reason. That, plus you have this sense of honor that's going to get you killed. I know you love Katia… But I also know you love Donut more.

    Carl: No. We're not doing this. Not now.

    Mordecai: Son. I can't have this conversation with Donut, but this needs to be said. The right thing to do is to help Katia. Yes. But the smart thing, and the safe thing is to step back and let her self-destruct… It's either her or Donut… It's not your fault. It's not Donut's fault. And honestly, it's not Katia's fault. Some breaks aren't clean.

    Carl: No. We're not giving up on her, and I don't want you to bring it up again.

    Carl's narration closes it: "Mordecai didn't answer. I knew he thought I was being stubborn. Stupid. Suicidal, even. He was right. I didn't care."

    He never raises it again.

    Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin¶

    Canadian¶

    The Floor 9 guildhall relocation turns him human. Carl examines him in chapter 2:

    Mordecai. Human. Level 50.

    Manager of Princess Donut.

    This is a non-combatant NPC.

    This is a human. This one is something called a Canadian. Part French. Part maple syrup. He’s weirdly obsessed with ice hockey and snowmobiles and semi-erotic lumberjack fan fiction. Has a well-worn Tim Hortons loyalty card in his Velcro wallet. He says “aboot” instead of “about” and gets really, really upset when you point it out, claiming you’re hearing things and that it’s a harmful stereotype. It’s not a stereotype, and that’s exactly how it sounds. He has a relative who was trampled to death by a moose. You get the idea.

    He arrives in a snowsuit, a raccoon hat and glasses, and takes the reaction badly:

    “You make fun of me when I’m something stupid, and when I’m the most normal thing possible, at least to you, you also laugh? Fuck both of you.”

    The "aboot" is not a one-off gag — it survives into his serious dialogue for the rest of the book.

    No reclass is described

    The book never narrates the change, never names a mechanic, and never uses the word "reclass." He simply walks out of the crafting room human in chapter 2. His level is unchanged at 50, and he is still flagged a Non-Combatant NPC, so nothing about his class or role moves — only the racial form, which is what the Changeling relocation mechanic has always done to him. Floor 9's playing field is populated largely by the crawlers themselves, which fits, but the text does not say so.

    The alchemy table¶

    The single biggest change to what he can do. His table came out of Floor 8 upgraded past anything he has ever had:

    He was in the crafting room, excitedly working on something on his newly-upgraded alchemy table. His table was now leveled up higher than he’d ever gotten it, and he was excitedly sending me messages about this potion and that. He now, apparently, had the ability to examine potions and see how they were made. This was the first time he’d had access to this ability, and his excitement was palpable.

    Reverse-engineering does not consume the sample — "with my newest table upgrade, I can figure out the recipe without ruining it" — which means any rare potion the party loots becomes a recipe he can then mass-produce. Over the course of the floor that yields the hour-long aura potion used in the Club Vanquisher raid (the ones Prepotente had before "only lasted for two minutes"), gallons of a privacy-bubble potion, mass-manufactured Black Nimbus potions, and Mordecai's Special Brew, the potion that holds Louis's chest open during surgery. Donut's read on his mood is that he is excitable because he is Canadian now; Carl's is closer:

    “He’s spent literally hundreds and hundreds of years wondering about some of these potions, and now he finally has the chance to learn all their ingredients and secrets. It’s gotta be exciting for him.”

    Surgeon¶

    In chapter 84 Mordecai performs the heart-and-lung transplant that saves Louis Santiago, whose organs have been colonised by the Reaver CEO Epitome Tagg. Edgar the tortoise directs; Mordecai cuts. The donor is the flesh golem Princess Lunette, whose naiad heart and pulmonary system are extracted by Rosetta, Imani and Elle while Carl and Tipid hold Louis's ribcage open against the constant healing trying to close it. They have a thirty-second window.

    The non-combatant rule holds even here — "Mordecai couldn’t be the one to remove the organs, lest it constitute an attack," so Bucket Boy does the pulling. Mordecai does the graft, slits the bronchial for a rebreather, and plumbs the new organs into a set of gills grown with a Water Breathing scroll.

    Donut: DID YOU KNOW MORDECAI KNEW HOW TO DO THIS STUFF?

    Carl: There’s still so much about all of them we don’t know.

    Uzzi's bolt¶

    The Book 6 prologue debt comes due in chapter 86. Donut has Huanxin Jinx — the grixist heiress who extorted Odette's party on the tenth floor, and whose demand is the reason Uzzi was killed — pinned inside a circle of power words, stripped out of her goddess's soul armor, unable to teleport. Katia's crossbow is mounted on Donut's back, unloaded. And then Donut says they have a mutual friend outside.

    “You’re a non-combatant NPC,” Huanxin hissed at Mordecai as she pulled herself up. She held her arm, which dripped blood. “You can’t touch me. You can’t fucking touch me.”

    “No, I can’t. Not directly,” Mordecai said. He held something up. It was a simple crossbow bolt. There was nothing magical about it. “You see this?” He flicked the tip. “My brother died because of you and because of Odette and because of Chaco. But mostly because of you. Some of my mother’s ashes are built into the tip of this bolt. It’s a funeral rite in my culture. To make a weapon with the ashes of those you lost. My mother died before the crawl came to my world, and I’m grateful for the opportunity to allow her to avenge her son.”

    Mordecai turned his attention to Katia. “Goodbye, Katia. Be safe, kid.”

    He loaded the bolt into the crossbow on Donut’s back, turned, and he walked from the tent.

    He does not stay to watch. Donut fires it between Huanxin's eyes a page later. The ashes are the same mementos he has kept on a shelf in his guildhall since Book 1, and the funeral rite is the first time the text explains what they were for.

    Ruby, and losing the children¶

    The six changeling orphans with compression sickness that he took in on Floor 8 come down to Floor 9 with him, along with the gnome girl Bonnie and the flesh-golem children. They are a constant low presence through the book — he plays them war movies (Hamburger Hill, Platoon, Saving Private Ryan) hoping to talk them out of wanting to fight, which backfires completely and leaves the kids louder than ever about being allowed to join up.

    The quiet scene is chapter 70. Carl bursts into the saferoom mid-crisis and finds him reading Anne of Green Gables to Ruby, the armless girl with the sunken-in head:

    I almost said something, but I held back, seeing how young Ruby clutched onto Mordecai, how she had her misshapen head on his shoulder. I had a sudden memory of Katia and Annie from the dream, and I realized this was the same thing. He thinks it’s over, and this is how he’s choosing to spend his last few hours. If Donut died, he’d be done. I wasn’t sure exactly what would happen to him with the quarantine, but I knew he’d be out of the dungeon, and he would never see Ruby or any of the other kids again.

    At the end of the book, Zerzura — the spell that lifts the entire city of Larracos and everyone in it to the 12th floor — takes them. Mordecai does not go; the text states it flatly and does not say why.

    I gave one last cursory glance around me. Mordecai was here, too. He would not transfer with the Zerzura, and he held little Ruby in his arms, who would.

    “It’s for the best, little one,” he said.

    That is the whole goodbye. The book gives it two lines and moves on.

    Zerzura, as far as the text supports it

    Zerzura is a spell, cast at the end of Book 7, which transfers the city of Larracos — thousands of mercenaries, the NPC army, the children, Kiwi and the babies, and Edgar — to the 12th floor. Epitome Tagg names it: "the Zerzura spell, which will take the city of Larracos to the 12th floor." A handful of NPCs choose to stay behind with Carl instead. The mechanics beyond that are a Locations/Mechanics topic, not settled here.

    One more glimpse¶

    During the shared-dream accident in the Several Days Earlier interlude, Carl's mind floods over everyone in the room. What he catches of Mordecai is one clause:

    Of Mordecai and that deep, bottomless well of sadness within.

    Book 8: A Glitching New Form¶

    Floor 10's guildhall relocation gives Mordecai his strangest form yet — and the first one the book frames as an actual malfunction rather than just an unflattering species.

    Form¶

    Carl finds him floating in the safe room "like a grim-reaper-style monster, but with really long arms," desiccated undead-like hands, and a black hood that hides everything but the occasional glint underneath:

    Mordecai. Level 50. Moon Reaper.

    From a dark, terrifying corner of Sheol, Moon Reapers are one of several different types of janitor mobs that occupy the 15th floor... Their gaze attack can come in many forms. That includes inflicting the dreaded Jaunt sickness.

    He isn't actually a Moon Reaper, and he corrects the misconception himself once he stops malfunctioning: "I'm actually not a Moon Reaper, but a—" is interrupted by his own hood falling back to reveal a flaming eyeball, then a brief, unwilled prophetic trance in which he delivers cryptic doom pronouncements to Carl and Donut in a voice not his own ("Death awaits you at every turn... You should remember that, Oak Fell Champion of Nekhebit"). He downplays it immediately afterward as harmless theater rather than real prediction: "It's always vague bullshit that can be twisted to fit whatever happens... This stuff is designed to be scary, not true." He then glitches through several more shapes in quick succession — the Canadian form from Book 7, a black-and-white cat, an otter, a massive slug — before settling back into the reaper shape.

    His own explanation for the instability: "I'm a shadow mimic. Mimics only take on the shape of the creature. Changelings can get some of the powers, though. That's why it's glitching, I think." He lays out why the combination is unstable at all — "shadow mimics and changelings are mortal enemies... shadow mimics think changelings are delicious" — which becomes load-bearing information later in the floor, when a real Adolescent Shadow Mimic turns out to be posing as a Desperado Club croupier (see Carl).

    Kept Away From the Others¶

    Rosetta, watching him glitch every time an unfamiliar race gets near him for the first time — he nearly loses himself entirely when the sluggalette Bigs first wanders in — recommends isolating him for the floor: "We should probably keep you sequestered from the others for this floor." Later, when the party heads out for the finale, "only Mordecai remained inside the safe room" while everyone else, including non-combatant NPCs like Bucket Boy, went along.

    No stated floor rule

    Book 8 never states an official rule confining Mordecai to the safe room this floor. What the text actually gives is a practical, self-inflicted limitation — his glitching around unfamiliar races — and Rosetta's resulting recommendation to keep him isolated. Whether there is a system-level restriction behind it as well is not confirmed either way.
