    The T'Ghee Card System¶

    Quick facts

    • Floor: 8 ("The Ghosts of Earth") — see Timeline
    • What it is: A collectible-card battle system layered over ordinary combat, in which captured monsters become summonable "totem" cards held by a squad leader
    • Who it binds: Every crawler on the floor, whether or not they ever build a deck — non-leaders are reclassified as minions during a card battle
    • Carried forward: Exactly one card per surviving squad, kept past the floor's collapse

    Floor 8's defining mechanic, and the thing every other Floor 8 system eventually routes through. Crawlers are sorted into squads, handed flags, and told to go capture monsters; the monsters become playing cards; the cards become a duel format that overrides normal combat for everyone in range. Carl's first-pass explanation to Donut is the right mental model — "Do you remember when I used to play that Pokémon game on the Nintendo? I think it might be a little like that."

    Squads and Flags¶

    Announced in the floor's opening broadcast, before any card is ever shown.

    Every crawler is placed in a squad of no more than five. Parties of six or more are split into equal squads of fewer than five, and the split is permanent in a way parties are not: "These squads are not able to be broken. If you leave your party, you will not leave the squad."

    A squad has six more spots than it has crawlers — a solo crawler gets seven slots total, a five-crawler squad gets eleven. Filling those spots is phase one's entire job, and it is mandatory: a squad that hasn't filled out by the phase-one timer has the remainder filled randomly, which the announcement goes out of its way to warn against.

    The squad leader receives twenty flags bearing the squad's auto-generated logo:

    "If you plant the flag within the body of a living entity while they are at less than 5% health, they will be transformed into something called a T'Ghee totem, which will be added to the squad leader's inventory."

    The 5% threshold is the whole design: capture requires beating something almost to death without killing it. Noncorporeal monsters can be flagged too. Flagged monsters automatically join the squad and become minions of whoever summons them, following orders "to a certain extent, depending on the level difference and nature of the monster" — which is the announcement's way of warning squads against building a roster that outclasses them.

    Other flag rules:

    • Flags are not replaced if lost or wasted. Twenty is all anyone gets.
    • A squad may hold more totems than it has squad spaces.
    • Totems may be traded with other crawlers — and stolen from them.
    • Dismissing a squad member means ripping its totem card in two.
    • Cards may only be held and activated by the squad leader.
    • On the start of phase two, every totem not placed in an active deck dissipates.

    Phase Structure¶

    The floor's three phases each change what the deck is for.

    Phase Dates What the deck does
    One Dec 10 – Dec 24 (14 days) Build it. Capture totems, fill the squad, practice in the mock arena. No exit key is obtainable.
    Two Dec 24 – Dec 26 (48 hours) Use it. A new region populated by intelligent deckmasters who carry their own decks; beat them for cards and for the region's exit key.
    Three Dec 26 onward Defend it. Keyholders teleport back to their original region; keyless squads are deliberately dropped onto keyholders' doorways.

    Phase two hard-locks the roster: "Once phase two starts, you may never have more or less than six totem cards in your possession." Taking a killed deckmaster's totem therefore means discarding one of your own. Phase three's design is openly adversarial — "you'll be blocking their path. If you can kill them before they exit, you can take the key to save yourself" — and the announcement is frank that this is the point: "the potential of crawler on crawler violence is quite high. Doesn't that sound just great?"

    Combat Rules¶

    The full rulebook is delivered mid-book by Cascadia and mirrored in each crawler's deck tab.

    Activating a deck. "Each squad must have six totems active in their deck. You may activate your deck with as little as six cards total." Six totems is the floor, not the ceiling; observed decks in the back half of the book run fifteen to twenty-two cards.

    Starting and ending a fight. "Combat is engaged when a nearby monster's aggro is activated, and they are actively hunting you. Combat disengages when the mobs are dead, rendered neutral, or one of the parties has fled." Crucially, "Overlapping combat with multiple mobs will count as a single session" — you do not get a fresh hand by picking a second fight mid-fight, and most totems cannot be reused once knocked out within a session.

    The hand. Four random cards are drawn the moment combat initiates. Spent cards are replaced "at the rate of one card every ten seconds." A single discard is available at the start of combat, and "This ability resets once every thirty seconds." (Ten seconds is the crawler default, not a universal constant — at least one enemy deckmaster is observed drawing on an eight-second cycle.)

    The squad leader's handicap. This is the rule that makes the system dangerous:

    "Once deck combat is initiated, the Squad Leader loses access to their regular magic abilities and their inventory system. They may still fight using melee attacks. They may use potions, scrolls, wands, etc. as long as the items are physically available to them."

    Access returns only when the deck is empty: "Once all cards in your deck are played or discarded, the Squad Leader regains their ability to use their inventory and spells." A spellcaster squad leader in an active card battle is, functionally, an unarmed person holding cards.

    Everyone else is a minion. Non-leaders present at a card battle are reclassified, and the system enjoys telling them so:

    "By the way, have you realized yet that you're considered a minion during card battles? That's something you should probably pay attention to."

    Enemy totems generally cannot strike an opposing squad leader while that leader has a totem of their own active — but minions are fair game, and the system awards an achievement (Smeat!: "Expendable. Cannon fodder.") for the privilege. In a deckmaster duel the win condition is to "Strike a single blow against the deckmaster," and the lose condition is to "Have an enemy totem or deckmaster strike you."

    If the leader dies. "If the squad leader dies during deck combat, the deck will transfer to the next in line."

    Card Types¶

    "There are five types of cards: Totems, which are the summonable monsters. Utility cards, which can only be played on one or more of your summoned totems. Snare cards, which can only be played on an enemy's totem. Mystic cards, which are similar to regular magic spells, and special cards which are usually one-time use."

    • Totem — a captured monster, summonable for a timed duration. Reusable across battles even if the summoned creature dies.
    • Utility — buffs, playable only on your own actively summoned totem. Most persist for the duration of combat "even over multiple summonings." A totem's capacity for utility buffs is limited by its rarity.
    • Snare — debuffs, playable only when an opponent has an active totem. Also persistent across resummonings of the same totem. A totem has one snare slot.
    • Mystic — the disruption layer: "usually have a direct effect on squads, squad leaders, or the squad leader's deck." Some are persistent, occupying a hand slot for as long as they stay active.
    • Special — one-off effects, usually consumable and usually aimed at the caster.

    A taxonomy the book breaks itself

    The Combo card's own printed face reads "Utility Card," even though the narration and every subsequent use treat it as a Special. Separately, the Worm Fulcrum is explicitly "a combination of a mystic Necro card and a Walking Stick totem" — simultaneously a totem with hit points and a persistent mystic — which Donut correctly protests is not supposed to be possible.

    Rarity¶

    Seven tiers, weakest to strongest: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique. Mythic cards carry a black border and are "the second highest quality, only behind the orange unique cards."

    Rarity governs how many utility buffs a totem can carry. The only figure the system actually states is Donut's: "Carl, it says uncommon totems can only hold two utility cards!" Carl infers from that a clean 1-through-7 scale — Common holds one, Unique holds seven — but that scaling is his guess, not system text, and the book never confirms it.

    The Mock Battle Arena¶

    A practice facility where a squad can run its deck against generated opposition. Three difficulty tiers at 1,000 / 2,000 / 5,000 gold respectively for an easy opponent, an opponent matched to your deck, and a "challenge." The generated mobs can't hurt you, but the arena environment still can.

    Consumables are genuinely spent: a one-time card used in practice is gone, and cards on a long cooldown don't reset just because the fight was simulated. Beating the challenge tier unlocks further options, including a free Secret Challenge Mode — a true deck-versus-deck duel against an AI opponent with its own deck, usable three times.

    Prices rise the night before phase two ("Starting tonight, the prices in the mock battle arena are going up"); the middle tier is confirmed to move from 2,000 to 2,500 gold, and the other tiers' new prices aren't given.

    The Champion Pack¶

    Awarded for defeating a phase-two keymaster:

    "Champion Pack. Offered only to those who have defeated the keymaster, the champion pack contains the following items: Backup slot: Allows space for two extra totems to be kept on reserve. With this pack you may now carry a total of eight totems, but you still may only keep six in your active deck. A champion trophy to display in your personal space. A cooler filled with yellow Gatorade… Two Flee cards. Warning: Flee card mechanics will change during the final phase."

    The backup slot is the only reserve-totem mechanic in the book; outside this pack, six is six.

    Carrying a Card Forward¶

    "All surviving squads may choose a single T'Ghee card to keep upon the collapse of this floor." In the event, Carl and Donut weigh keeping Lazarus or the Golden Combo and take neither — they spend their choice on ripping Paz Lo's card instead, at Paz's own earlier request.

    Named Cards¶

    Utility¶

    Card Effect
    Time Extend Doubles a summoned totem's duration
    Stout +25% health to a mob for the duration of combat
    Blue Stuff Increases a totem's mana pool
    Greased Lightning +25% speed
    Precision Improves a totem's chance to land an attack; stacks, and ten of them still occupy only a single buff slot
    Heal Totem Appears in deck lists only; the book never states its mechanic
    Temporary Shield Protects a totem from exactly one snare
    Timeless Consumable; keeps a totem summoned permanently

    Snare¶

    Card Effect
    Hobble (printed once as Hobbled) Cuts a totem's summoning time by 90%, persisting through resummons
    Hole in the Bag Drains a totem's mana; not consumable
    Damnation Forces an enemy totem's card to be discarded when it times out, dead or not
    Lamp Paralyzes a totem for ten seconds and makes it glow; consumable
    Cripple Slows a totem and greatly reduces its strength; consumable
    Snatched! Steals an enemy's active totem outright, flipping it to your side
    GET BACK IN LINE! Forcibly un-summons an enemy's just-played totem back into their deck

    Mystic¶

    Card Effect
    The Thief Steals a card from someone else; not consumable
    Force Discard Discards a random card from the opponent's hand
    Steal Totem Consumable, described only as "ridiculously powerful"
    Golden Combo Unique-rarity, non-consumable version of the Combo effect — reusable indefinitely
    Peekaboo! Reveals the opponent's entire hand for the rest of the battle
    Summon Lock Locks a target's cards against summoning for thirty seconds
    To Hell With Ya! Legendary; force-discards every copy of one named card from the target's hand and deck
    Force Play (announced as "I pick that one!") Pick a card in an opponent's hand and it plays automatically; the caster sees their hand
    Toss-Up! Redistributes control of every totem currently on the field between the two combatants
    Raid Stash (announced as "Yoink!") Unique; steals one card from each of several people at once
    Idolized! Persistent; turns non-totem NPC mobs hostile — and because they aren't totems, they can strike a squad leader directly
    Ralphie Persistent; dispels all active magic and mutes every minion and totem on the target's team from casting

    Special¶

    Card Effect
    Combo Consumable; fuses two monster cards into one creature for the shorter of their two durations, after which both are tagged out for the rest of combat
    Glow-Up Consumable; permanently rewrites a card to reflect its current buffed state — playable on friend or enemy
    Flee Consumable; teleports the whole squad out of combat
    Midnight Express Consumable, persists until it times out; hands the deck-holding squad-leader role to a different squad member, freeing the real leader's spells and inventory
    Temporary Immortality! Consumable; cast on an active totem

    Notable Totems¶

    A partial roster of totems that matter to the plot: the monk seal sentries Yago and Geraldo (Carl and Donut's first captures); Skylar Spinach, a ghommid splitter; Asojano, Uzi Jesus, and Raul the crab; Der Kornwolf and Die Kartoffelwölfin, a phase-two deckmaster's wolves; Jola, Lazarus A-Bang-Bang, Basquiat the Mongolian Death Worm, and the Demon Queen; the parody pair Alpha Male Carl and King Croissant, built from a captured "Carl" mob; the hybrid Worm Fulcrum; and Shi Maria, the Unique totem the book is named after.

    Crawlers as Cards¶

    The system permits flagging a crawler. Sister Ines does it to her own squadmate Paz Lo rather than let him die to Ogun's smite, over his explicit objection — "Let me go" — and his card afterward reads "Paz Lo. The God Predator. Level 101. Former Crawler." A second card, "Frank," raises the worse possibility Carl names outright: that the dungeon can bring back dead crawlers as cards.

    Unique totems can also arrive with obligations attached. Capturing Shi Maria triggers an escort quest, The Bedlam Bride — "Get her crazy ass to the city of Larracos. That's it. That's the quest." The quest survives the card being ripped.

    In-World: Stolen Sacred Objects¶

    T'Ghee is not a game's brand name. It is somebody's religion. Allister, author of the cookbook's 13th edition, knew T'Ghee as a faith that "had nothing to do with capturing and summoning monsters," and Carl is blunt about what the dungeon has done with it: "Even the cards themselves — T'Ghee cards — were considered sacred to the aliens they stole the idea from. They were never meant to be used like this."

    Sister Ines makes the broader version of the argument about the whole floor, whose monsters are drawn from real Earth folklore:

    "It is all a mockery of our faiths and legends. They ignore the meanings behind the myths and stories, and they just see the surface, steal it, and use it to make something shiny and pretty and completely devoid of its meaning… These cultures and creatures are holy to people, and they are making jokes of it all."
