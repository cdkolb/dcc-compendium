    The Toraline Ink Tattoo System (Skill and Spell Transfer)¶

    Quick facts

    • What it is: A method of copying a spell or skill from one person to another by tattooing them with ink made from toraline root and the donor's blood
    • Built from: The leftover ink Shi Maria brewed in Book 6 with the Enchanted Prison Tattoo Kit, plus the remaining toraline root Carl's sponsors (the Open Intellect Pacifist Network) gave him
    • Developed by: Rosetta, Edgar the shell-mage tortoise, Mordecai, and later the crawler Milk
    • The core rule: Blood freely given lends a spell temporarily. Blood taken by violence steals it permanently
    • Status at book's end: The toraline is used up; Milk supersedes it with a new ink and gives Carl Stayfast Ink and an Adept's Fountain Pen

    This page covers the mechanic. The physical kit that applies it has its own page at Enchanted Prison Tattoo Kit, and the three tattoos actually inked in Book 7 are detailed below.

    Origin: Rosetta Asks for the Ink Within Minutes¶

    Book 6 ended with Shi Maria's parting note that Carl "will likely be doing more of this soon with the remaining ink." The payoff lands almost immediately. In chapter 11, minutes into the party's first real negotiation with Rosetta on Floor 9, she examines Carl, reaches down his shirt to touch the Bedlam Bride tattoo, and asks a question with no preamble:

    "How much do you have left?"

    "Of what?"

    She pulled her hand free. "The toraline. Give it to me, and give me the ink the spider used to make that tattoo."

    What Carl has left is thin: "a finger-sized slice" of the vegetable and "only a few thimblefuls" of Shi Maria's ink, still hot to the touch in its glass container. Mordecai has a little more toraline, "but not much." Rosetta's asking price for her part is saferoom access, access to the bomber's studio, and four million gold.

    Rosetta already knows what this ink is for before anyone else does. It takes the party the better part of forty chapters to catch up.

    The Mechanic¶

    Consent determines everything¶

    The single rule the whole system turns on is intention. Katia lays it out in chapter 40, after Li Na donates blood for Carl's first transfer tattoo:

    "She says it didn't hurt at all. It's not permanent, unlike what happened with her brother. She lost five levels temporarily, but she can still use it. Intention matters. You pulled out his eye with violence when you were under control of that thing. If the blood is freely given, the potion allows you to choose the spell or skill. The tattoo itself, unfortunately, is permanent."

    Rosetta's version, chapter 47, is blunter:

    Spells and skills taken against the owner's will were much more powerful and resulted in the owner losing them permanently. My Walk on Air skill, stolen from Li Jun, was proof of that.

    And Edgar's, chapter 74, is the cleanest one-line statement of the system:

    "The ink Carl had from the toraline allows one to borrow spells and skills temporarily from willing subjects. It allows one to take them permanently from unwilling subjects."

    The Walk on Air revelation¶

    This is the mechanic's retroactive gut-punch, and it lands in chapter 4, long before anyone explains the system. Reading the description of his new Enchanted Toe Ring of the Well-Balanced, Carl hits a line that does not make sense:

    Physical Balance, Level 5. At level five, this skill imbues both the Extra Steady Balance skill and the Walk on Air Skill (already obtained.)

    "Already obtained?" I asked out loud. That didn't make sense. I moved into my menu. I knew what Walk on Air did, but I didn't have that skill. It was what Li Jun…

    I swallowed, suddenly feeling very, very dirty.

    It was what Li Jun used when he fought.

    The memory that follows is the taste of Li Jun's eye — which Shi Maria forced Carl to gouge out and eat with this very tattoo kit's stick in Book 6, telling him at the time that "you may gain some of your friend's powers, too, with the blood on the needle." Carl has had Walk on Air since that night and never noticed. Li Jun lost it permanently; his brother "got Walk on Air back, but they had to buy a book." The eye itself is unhealable.

    So the system's first documented transfer was a mutilation, and the party learns the rules by working backward from it.

    Decay, re-energizing, and levels¶

    Property Rule
    Donor cost The donor temporarily loses levels in the donated ability. Li Na lost 5; Carl lost 6 on Tripper
    Donor recovery The ability "heals" back over roughly four days
    Recipient decay The borrowed copy "slowly loses potency" and eventually disappears entirely
    Re-energizing A faded copy can be restored: the donor drinks the yam potion again, selects the spell, and the existing tattoo is re-charged. No new tattoo needed
    The tattoo Permanent, regardless of whether the ability inside it has faded
    Size Scales with the level of the ability. Li Na's level-13 skill "has to be pretty big" and consumed nearly a full vial; Tripper needed only a small one
    Theming "The tattoo doesn't have to actually be a specific image, but if it's 'properly themed,' it'll last longer"
    Artistry "And if it's drawn well, it could possibly be even stronger than the original version"

    Katia's full statement of the sizing and quality rules, chapter 40:

    "So, it depends on the level of the spell or skill. Since this is a level-13 skill, it has to be pretty big. I have to use almost all the ink in the vial, otherwise you won't get it at full strength. It took up a third of the potion already just to make the ink. We'll have just enough to do Imani's small tat next. Rosetta says the tattoo doesn't have to actually be a specific image, but if it's 'properly themed,' it'll last longer. And if it's drawn well, it could possibly be even stronger than the original version."

    The system grades the artwork. Katia's chain dragon comes back rated expert quality — "Edgar said that's the second highest one can get" — which is a notable result given she had never tattooed anyone before and was only doing it because Edgar was away scouting naga territory.

    The achievement that confirms it¶

    Chapter 57, after Carl kills with a borrowed ability:

    New Achievement! Hey Larry, can I borrow your chainsaw?

    You have utilized a borrowed skill or spell in battle. You have used this borrowed ability to kill an opponent. Oh, man. This opens up a whole new possibility of fighting styles, don't you think? Just keep those tattoos above the knee.

    Reward: You've received a Platinum Have You Goddamned Figured Out How To Use This In Conjunction With the Fucking Voodoo Book Yet? Jesus Christ I Can Only Help You So Much box.

    The AI is, characteristically, yelling the answer at Carl a full twenty chapters before he works it out. See The Voodoo Book Connection below.

    How it differs from the Necklace of Indelible Woe¶

    The dungeon already has a device for taking someone's spell: the Necklace of Indelible Woe, which swaps one of the wearer's spells for one of a target's and works best if you kill them. Tipid's comparison in chapter 32 is the cleanest statement of what the tattoos are for:

    "Rosetta's tattoos will help you share skills and spells without actually killing anyone. It just needs to brew for a little longer."

    The Three Tattoos¶

    1. The Chain Dragon — Carl's right forearm (chapter 40)¶

    • Artist: Katia, her first tattoo ever
    • Donor: Li Na, blood freely given
    • Ability: Profane Iron, level 13
    • Design: "It has to be a chain. A chain shaped like a dragon." Rated expert quality

    Donut, following remotely by Voodoo book, demands design approval in all caps — "KATIA MAKE SURE YOU DRAW IT IN THE VOODOO BOOK FIRST SO I CAN APPROVE AND SUGGEST CHANGES TO THE DESIGN" — and when she finally sees it: "You're starting to look like one of those white rapper doodle notebook guys, Carl. And it's a little crooked!"

    See Profane Iron for the ability itself.

    2. The Barbed Wire Ankle Band — Imani (chapter 47)¶

    • Artist: Katia
    • Donor: Carl, blood freely given (he loses 6 levels of the spell)
    • Spell: Tripper
    • Design: "a small, barbed wire symbol that circled her ankle"

    This one exists because of an equipment restriction, not a tactical preference. Carl's Tripper is level 15, but it is item-based — it comes from Drakea's Enchanted Kerchief of Disorder. The plan was simply to lend Imani or Elle the bandana, "but it turned out that neither of them could wear it. Apparently fairy-class, and other magical races, were extremely limited on the sort of magical gear they could wear." The tattoo is the workaround.

    A useful side-discovery: taking the bandana off revealed that Carl's own underlying Tripper had trained itself to level 7 independently of the item, which set off "its own, mind-numbing conversation with Mordecai" because the spell version has a 10-minute cooldown and the item version a five-hour one, and the two cannot be mixed.

    Donut's objection is sustained and entirely about aesthetics: "I'M SORRY, BUT A PAMELA ANDERSON TATTOO IS MOST CERTAINLY A DISFIGUREMENT. I KNOW THIS IS IMPORTANT, BUT SOMETIMES I WORRY ABOUT THE COST." And: "THIS ISN'T THE 1990's. I SWEAR TO GOD IF IMANI STARTS USING THE PHRASE 'HOME SKILLET,' I'M GOING TO LOSE MY MIND."

    Carl's own reaction is guilt, and he traces it all the way back: "All of this stemmed from her putting that goddamned tiara on her head way back on the very first floor."

    3. The Cartoon Dog — Donut's right flank (chapters 74–75)¶

    • Artist: Edgar, working at speed
    • Donor: A fallen cleric of the god Wepwawet who died defending the FUPA — the ability is extracted from a corpse, using Edgar and Milk's new ink rather than toraline
    • Spell: Bijanbi, originally called Heel
    • Design: "a simple, rather adorable line art tattoo of a dog," which then "lifted its leg and started to lick itself"
    • Overwritten by: Milk, with Words of Power calligraphy

    This is the one with real stakes attached. The endgame plan requires Donut to have a mass-drag spell at level 8 or higher to combine into War Crime from the Nest tower, and she does not have one. Rosetta explains the substitution:

    "We were going to go with Hamzo, but even with the Synergy tower's effect tied in with the bonus from the War Crime spell, it won't be a wide enough area. So we went with a Dinner Bell variant from a cleric who fell defending the FUPA. It's a spell that came from him worshiping his deity, but it appears it's going to work. Edgar extracted it properly, and with some ink tips from Milk, we don't need the toraline anymore to do what we need." She eyed Donut nervously. "It used to be called Heel, but apparently the translation makes it sound like another spell, so this season it's called Bijanbi."

    Edgar picks the placement — "Best would be on her right flank, I think. Rosetta, quickly now. Shave her. I need a spot about as big as my foot" — and Donut's protest is immediate and sustained: "You will not be shaving my ass!"

    The transfer works, but lands at level two, far short of the required eight. Milk fixes it by inking calligraphy over the finished tattoo:

    "Not quite yet, child. We must get it temporarily above level eight. This may sting a little," Milk said. She came forward and produced a pen, dripping with black ink… She wrote some words atop the tattoo with a flourish as Donut hissed with pain. I didn't recognize the script, but when I examined the characters, the system simply said, Words of Power.

    "It's a magic style that I haven't seen used often in the dungeon. The calligraphy guild teaches it."

    The result overshoots the requirement badly: "The spell went up to level 15!" Milk's caveat is that it is temporary — "As the ink fades, the power will decrease, but if you practice using the spell at that high of a level, it will assist greatly with training." Edgar confirms the moving artwork is not alive: "I do not believe the art is sentient, as it hasn't been imbued with anything other than the spell itself."

    Donut also picks up two achievements she does not appreciate — one calling her a traitor, one awarding "something called a stolen catalytic converter" — and the option to worship Wepwawet, who turns out to be in the Court of Nekhebit, which also unlocks worship of Odette.

    Donut's condition¶

    The negotiation that gets her into the chair is one of the book's better quiet scenes. Her objection is not vanity; it is that this one will be visible:

    "Carl," Donut said, looking up at me. "It's okay... You're right. It'll work. It's just... I hoped none of them would be on the outside."

    "What do you mean?"

    "The scars."

    I closed my eyes. "Donut, if you decide to do this, it won't be a scar. It will be a badge of honor. … This is no scar, Donut. Not even close because it was your choice. And, you know what? You can still say no, and we'll find another way."

    She looked at me, eyes shining. "I'll only do it if you also get a matching one. A matching ass tattoo."

    I smiled. "Absolutely. But it'll have to wait a day or two."

    She holds the line on placement, though — "So help me God, Carl. I will not have a tramp stamp on me" — and after the ink sets: "Now, cover it up. I don't even want to look at it. We'll get it lasered off on the next floor."

    Unpaid setup

    Carl's promised matching tattoo never happens within Book 7. There are no further references to it after chapter 75. The promise carries forward unresolved.

    Running Out, and the Replacement¶

    The toraline supply is a hard constraint from the moment Rosetta takes it. Katia's chapter-40 accounting — "We'll have just enough to do Imani's small tat next" — turns out to be exactly right, and by chapter 47 Carl notes "we were just limited by the fact we had barely any of the vegetable left."

    The bottleneck is broken in chapter 74 not by finding more toraline but by replacing it. Edgar, working with Milk, develops a different ink that does something the toraline ink could not:

    "This is a different kind of tattoo," he said… "The ink Carl had from the toraline allows one to borrow spells and skills temporarily from willing subjects. It allows one to take them permanently from unwilling subjects. With help from Milk, I have learned a few new tricks. This ink is similar to that made with the toraline, but not identical, and I don't know how it'll form. It captures the essence of the fallen crawler in a unique way, and it allows you to learn one of their spells or skills."

    That is the third category: the dead. Toraline ink needed a living donor, willing or not. Edgar's new ink extracts from corpses.

    Edgar also notes one hard limit, chapter 78: "He's a rock monster. I can't tattoo a rock, let alone extract anything from him. Not with my tools."

    The Voodoo Book Connection¶

    The AI had been screaming about this since chapter 57. Edgar works it out at the end of chapter 74:

    "Rosetta was just telling Milk and myself about that book of Voodoo you have, and I believe you may be able to share skills and spells that way as well, using the same ink. It will be more temporary."

    Milk confirms the distribution mechanic in chapter 75:

    "He also has another book. One that all of you have. The Book of Voodoo, I believe it is called. Anything he writes in it will be given to everybody who already has the book. Best of luck to you."

    Which is precisely why her parting gift is 825 ml of Stayfast Ink and an Adept's Fountain Pen — see Stayfast Ink and Adept's Fountain Pen and The Filthy Little Crawler's Book of Voodoo. The book's "nefarious purpose which will become clear later," flagged as obvious setup since Book 6, is this.

    Scaling¶

    The system has no level of its own. Four independent variables govern output:

    1. The donor's level in the ability — the copy cannot exceed the original except through exceptional artwork
    2. Consent — violent acquisition produces a more powerful and permanent copy, at the cost of permanently stripping the donor
    3. Ink volume, which scales with the ability's level and is the binding constraint all book
    4. Artistry, graded by the system, which affects both duration and raw strength — and can be layered over afterward with Words of Power calligraphy for a temporary, decaying boost
