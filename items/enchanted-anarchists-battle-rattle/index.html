    Enchanted Anarchist's Battle Rattle¶

    Quick facts

    • Owner: Carl
    • Slot: Torso (sleeveless jacket, replaces his original leather jacket)
    • Obtained: Platinum Spicy Box, awarded after the Wasteland assault

    Effect¶

    A sleeveless, canvas jacket that glows faintly purple. Its core value isn't its base stats but its upgrade patch slots — it can host an unlimited number of compatible patches, making it one of the most upgradeable protection items Carl has found.

    Base (unpatched) effects:

    • +1 to all base stats
    • +50% range and accuracy for all thrown explosives
    • Retains previously-granted access to the Desperado Club and the Naughty Boys Employment Agency

    Scaling¶

    The jacket's base stat bonus increases by +1 for every compatible patch attached, doubled if the patch is specifically a "Back Patch." It ships with one starter patch already in the box (see below), and Mordecai immediately flags it as an item the party needs to actively hunt more patches for.

    Starter Patch¶

    The same box includes an Upgrade Patch (Small) depicting Earth, granting:

    • +5% Strength
    • Immunity from cloud-based attacks

    Patches are explicitly described as fragile: removing one destroys it, so once attached, Carl can't easily swap patches around later without losing them.

    Role in the Story¶

    Carl replaces his badly worn Book 1 leather jacket — burned, missing an arm, zipper broken for two floors — with the Battle Rattle at the end of the Floor 5 air-quadrant fight. Mordecai calls it a sign that "the dungeon is starting to solidify your look for you," and the jacket becomes Carl's new default torso slot for the rest of Book 4.

    Book 5: Three New Patches¶

    Carl attaches three new patches to the jacket over the course of Floor 6. Katia carefully sews on the first two herself, to Donut's approval; the third goes on while Katia is away with Eva Sigrid's group, forcing Carl to sew it on himself — badly enough that Donut complains his "wide stitches" have ruined Katia's neat work on the patch beside it:

    • Small — "Twelve Flaming Arrows." Depicts the arrows the Skyfowl hero Radiant Star shot into his grandfather to end a frozen time. Grants +5% Dexterity and +5% damage to all ranged projectiles. From a Gold Ranged Weapon Box.
    • Medium — "The Bomb." Made from Spider Reaper Minion thread silk. Grants +11% Constitution, +6% damage to all explosives, +2 to Dangerous Explosives Handling, and a 15% discount from traveling Spider Reaper Minion merchants. From a Gold Spicy Box.
    • Medium — "The Midnight Epicure." A folk-tale ward symbol depicting a satanic goat, matching the tattoo already worn by Prepotente. Grants Protection against Vampirism, Protection against Lycanthropy, and +25% magical damage against all sapient undead. From a Gold Benefactor Box (the Plenty) — delivered the same day Donut independently receives an identical set of bonuses via a matching bracelet (see Enchanted Silver Bracelet of the Ab-solar), which both Carl and Mordecai read as a deliberate warning.

    Each patch grants Carl's usual +1-per-patch flat stat bonus in addition to its own listed effects.

    Book 6: The Back Patch and Two More¶

    Floor 8 is where the jacket's back-patch slot finally gets filled, and it more than doubles the garment's value. Two new small/medium patches and one back patch are sewn on — by Carl himself, badly, over Donut's loud running commentary on his stitching technique:

    • Small — the peace symbol. +10% Dexterity, neutral mobs less likely to fall into aggro, and +10% skill in playing the bongos. From a Gold Pacifist's Box
    • Medium — the burning book. +15% Intelligence and the Book Burner benefit — once a day, make an opponent forget a spell for 30 hours
    • Back patch — The Scavenger's Daughter. A divine, unique, fleeting patch from a Celestial Predator Box. It doubles every other patch's stat-point benefit, adds +25 Dexterity and +25 Strength on top, alerts Carl whenever a deity enters the area, and grants Climb, Soul Reaper, and the Mysterious Bone Key

    Carl deliberately sews the small peace patch on first so that the back patch's doubling effect is certain to catch every regular patch.

    The jacket is now expendable in exactly one way

    Using the Mysterious Bone Key destroys the Scavenger's Daughter and this jacket with every patch on it. Carl designates it a last-resort item and never spends it in Book 6, but the entire Battle Rattle now sits as collateral against a single one-time escape option.

    Book 7: Never Named, But Still Working¶

    Flag closed

    This page previously asked what the jacket's patch count and contents looked like going into Floor 9. Two answers, and one of them is a non-answer:

    1. The jacket is never named in Book 7. A case-insensitive search of This Inevitable Ruin returns zero hits for "battle rattle" (and zero for "bone key"). It is referred to only as "your jacket" or "my jacket," and the patches only as "the patch."
    2. No total patch count is ever given. The nearest thing is Kevin the announcer counting Carl's Celestial items (the Scavenger's Daughter, the Gloom Wraith Phase potion, and the Hairbrush of the Beefmaster make three), which is a different tally entirely.

    What Book 7 does give is two new patches — one installed, one refused — and a serious new liability on the back patch. Those are below.

    The two oblique references¶

    Chapter 20, mid-battle, with the Scavenger's Daughter's soul charge held deliberately full:

    "Carl," Donut said. "Your jacket is glowing again! Goodness, that patch is disgusting."

    "I know," I said. "I need to keep it turned on for just a little longer. We need the power."

    "Mordecai said not to keep it turned on for long."

    "I'm fine," I said, ignoring the pang of pain that jolted across my midsection.

    Chapter 57, Li Jun — who has noticed that Carl has stopped posting his loot to the group chat:

    "Donut says you received over thirty loot boxes, but you've only really told us about your new brush and the potions. Donut said you got a patch, but you haven't put it on your jacket yet. You usually post all the items in the group chat."

    Carl's reaction is one line: "I shrugged, suddenly uncomfortable." He is hiding the clover patch (below), and Li Jun has caught him at it.

    New — Small: the Reaper Spider Minion merchant patch (chapter 4)¶

    From a Platinum "Hell in a Handbasket Box." A black-and-silver die-cut patch shaped like Shi Maria — shorter neck, shorter hair, and multiple wicker baskets lashed over her bulbous body. Donut recognises her instantly: "Hey! It's Shi Maria!"

    Upgrade Patch. Small.

    This upgrade patch depicts a traveling Reaper Spider Minion merchant. These guys are known for their sense of humor, and I highly recommend the first thing you do the next time you meet one is to insult them. They'll love it.

    Affixing this patch to an eligible garment will imbue the following benefits:

    Access to the Midnight Market. Safety or Sanity not guaranteed.

    You will receive a notification if any traveling merchants are nearby.

    Plus 5% to Dexterity.

    Warning: Upgrade patches are fleeting items. You may remove them, but they will be destroyed in the process.

    Carl installs it right away — "Like all patches, installing them increased my stats all across the board thanks to my Scavenger's Daughter patch, and I'd need to install this right away." The Midnight Market access is the part that matters; the traveling-merchant notification and the Dexterity bump are incidental.

    Refused — Small: the clover / bare-foot "Lucky Shot" patch (chapter 57)¶

    From a Legendary Lucky Bitch Box, awarded for the "Liquified" achievement (blowing himself up while immortal). The artwork is a bare human foot crushing a patch of four-leaf clovers, and the description is written from the foot's point of view.

    Upgrade Patch. Small.

    This upgrade patch depicts a perfectly-perfect, mouthwatering human foot treading upon a patch of four-leaf clovers, creasing them as it presses down with gentle but oh-so-firm force. Despite their non-sentient nature, the clovers have a brief moment of awareness as they suffer, and it is fucking delicious.

    Delicious.

    Affixing this patch to an eligible garment will imbue the following benefits:

    Lucky Shot. A fatal blow will be deflected into the body of a party member instead. This benefit may only be used once per floor.

    Carl will not wear it, and — the more interesting decision — he will not tell anyone why:

    Even with Donut's Cockroach skill, there was no way I would risk wearing this. I didn't care how valuable it was. I wouldn't wear it, and I didn't want the others to know what it did, lest they attempt to talk me into installing it.

    The patch stays in his inventory for the rest of the book. He is tempted exactly once, and not by the survival value: facing a threatened smite from Emberus for killing Hellik, he wonders whether to take the smite instead, and notes that "was the 'out' that the patch gave me. I still wasn't even tempted to use it."

    Exact-verification note

    The description says the blow is deflected into "a party member." It does not say random. Nothing in the text specifies how the recipient is chosen.

    The back patch turns dangerous¶

    The Scavenger's Daughter develops a new failure mode on Floor 9: letting its soul charge sit undischarged now inflicts Soul Poisoning, an escalating condition that culminates in Carl being knocked unconscious for 25 seconds after every use of the Daughter's Kiss. The jacket also cannot safely be unequipped while the charge is full. See that patch's page for the full mechanic.

    Sourced from Book 4: The Gate of the Feral Gods, Book 5: The Butcher's Masquerade, Book 6: The Eye of the Bedlam Bride, and Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin.
