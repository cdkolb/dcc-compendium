# Chicken and Waffles and Upgrades (C&W&U)

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Floor:** [10](../floors/index.md) ("Don't Come in Last," Book 8),
      [Hungry Eyes Village](hungry-eyes-village.md)'s vendor strip
    - **Cover story:** An ordinary 1950s-style roadside diner with a novelty kids' menu
    - **True nature:** A hidden upgrade shop concealing the war mage
      [Akuma](../characters/akuma.md)'s personal hideout
    - **Staff:** **Darla-Dean**, an imp hostess; **Hickster**, the shop's actual upgrade dealer —
      already dead by the time Carl finds the place, killed by Akuma

## First Sighting

The storefront is noticed well before it opens, as an unmarked space with no signage at all:

> A blank storefront with no sign. The moment my eyes passed over it, a notification popped up.
> **C&W&U Location. Illegal Modifications and More. Grand Opening Soon.**

It opens on schedule, exactly as its own promotional invoice promises — "Shop opens after the third
heat" — and Carl's group visits soon after on Akuma's own written invitation (see
[Akuma](../characters/akuma.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) for the note and the interrogation that
follows; this page covers only the location itself).

## The Diner

Walking in reads as an ordinary meal stop, deliberately clashing with the rest of town's aesthetic:

> Entering Chicken and Waffles and Upgrades. The place was a typical diner. Even though the whole
> town had this aesthetic from the 1980s film *Blade Runner*, the restaurant gave the 1950s, at
> least at first glance. There was an overabundance of neon within. The jukebox was playing that
> "Fame" song from the movie.

Staff are flightless imps rather than the gremlins running the rest of town. The hostess,
**Darla-Dean**, plays her cover completely straight — gaudy rockabilly wig, gum-chewing patter,
menus that flip open to a "kiddie menu" secretly stocked with adult buff-food items rather than
children's fare.

## The Hidden Door

The shop's real entrance is the jukebox itself. Playing the right track — Peter Gabriel's
"Sledgehammer" — slides it aside to reveal a passage behind it:

> Darla-Dean nodded. She pointed to the jukebox. I could already see that there was a hidden door
> and hallway past it. "Just push the music machine. Don't push too hard, though. We just opened,
> and we don't need it scratched."

The door reveals "a short hallway with a blinking neon sign that read 'Upgrades' with an arrow,"
sealing itself shut behind visitors and cutting off the diner's noise entirely.

## The Back Room

Beyond the hallway is a legitimate-looking upgrade counter — "a dusty counter lit with flickering
yellow light," vehicle posters on every wall, and a heavy binder of laminated upgrade options — that
turns out to be a crime scene. The shop's actual upgrade dealer, an imp named **Hickster**, is
already dead on the counter, killed by Akuma, with a note left in his place. A second, genuinely
hidden door sits behind a poster of yak armor, itself booby-trapped:

> Attached to the door itself was a hidden disintegration trap.

Carl disarms the trap before opening the final door onto a small storeroom — Akuma's actual hideout,
where the war mage is waiting. See [Akuma's own page](../characters/akuma.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles)
for what happens once the party is inside.
