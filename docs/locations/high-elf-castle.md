# High Elf Castle

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Floor:** 6 ("The Hunting Grounds"), extreme southeast corner of the map, against a rocky bluff
    - **Nickname:** "The Sugar Cube" (per Elle McGibbons)
    - **Ruler:** [Queen Imogen](../characters/queen-imogen.md) of the [High Elves](../species/high-elves.md)
    - **Notable event:** Site of the [Butcher's Masquerade](../mechanics/index.md#the-butchers-masquerade-book-5)
      and the book's climactic battle

## Overview

The High Elf castle is a stark, roughly cubic structure — about 150 meters to a side, seven
visible stories plus several sub-basement levels — built from a single, living white wood carved
all over with a fine acorn pattern, giving it the look of white marble or stone from a distance.
Donut calls it ugly on sight, expecting elven architecture to favor waterfalls and flowing lines;
instead it reads as a "Bauhaus art installation blown up to ridiculous proportions." Four thin
towers stand at each corner, each housing a small garrison of magic-wielding guards, reachable
only from the third floor (or the very top). An empty moat, drained by royal order because it
"smelled funny," surrounds the base. Caves in the surrounding rock bluff house a population of
trolls in a running, unresolved conflict with the castle's guards.

## Layout

Per a map supplied by Edgar the tortoise, the castle is divided into three functional sections:

- **The public/guest wing**, including the visitor stairwell (which only reaches the fifth floor)
  and the two mirrored "goodwill ballrooms" used for the Butcher's Masquerade
- **The royal wing**, all of it restricted, culminating in Queen Imogen's private seventh-floor
  ballroom and living quarters, and a sealed dungeon-level stairwell to Floor 7 that only unlocks
  once Imogen (a country boss) is dead
- **A hidden underground level**, accessible via a secret entrance in the troll caves, used by
  Signet's forces to infiltrate during the climax

The domed ceiling above the main ballroom bears a massive, slowly moving mosaic depicting
Scolopendra, described in-game only as "dreaming... it is said when she finally awakens, she will
destroy everything and free us all" — with a living white tree, roots in the mosaic, growing
downward from the center of the dome.

## The Goodwill Ballroom System

For the Butcher's Masquerade, the castle's magic splits attendees into three functionally separate
but visually merged spaces: **Ballroom A** (crawlers, fourth floor), **Ballroom B** (hunters,
second floor), and the **Queen's ballroom** (seventh floor, unprotected, hers alone). Everyone
appears to share one room, and can speak, dance, and lightly touch across the barrier, but cannot
meaningfully attack anyone in a different physical ballroom — the illusion is powered by five soul
crystals in a control room on the castle's third floor, four for the individual ballrooms and one
larger crystal maintaining the overlay connecting them. See
[The Butcher's Masquerade](../mechanics/index.md#the-butchers-masquerade-book-5) for the full
mechanic and how it's ultimately broken.

## Destruction

During the Masquerade's climactic battle, the castle — badly damaged by simultaneous mantis,
changeling, and elf fighting throughout its towers and halls — is teleported away in its entirety
partway through the final boss fight, relocating (along with everyone still physically inside it,
Ferdinand included) to Floor 9, while everyone outside its walls is left behind to finish the
fight in the open crater it leaves behind.
