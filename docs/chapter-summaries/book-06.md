**Front Matter — OMG. What a Floor!**{: #front-matter .chapter-title }

The book opens with a Princess Posse fanclub newsletter written entirely in Donut's voice, recapping Floors 6 and 7 for new subscribers. She covers Carl surrendering the Gate of the Feral Gods to Orren in exchange for the cretin bodyguards, her own stint as a Legendary Diva, the hunter war against Vrah and her mother Diwata, Katia leaving the party to hunt Eva, Miriam Dom's death, the Butcher's Masquerade, Signet's suicide, Mongo and Kiwi killing Vrah, and the *Zerzura* teleport that delivered the high elf castle, the changelings, and Signet's tattoo army to Floor 9 — making her and Carl official ninth-floor warlords with a Faction Wars slot bought by the fanclub. She also lodges her formal grievance about the pet show: Tserendolgor rigged the ballot box and stole Mongo's crown (#JusticeForMongo). Notably, she refuses to discuss Beatrice's reappearance on Odette's show, insisting she hasn't thought about it once and there's nothing more to say.

**Prologue — Odette's Bargain**{: #prologue .chapter-title }

A flashback to Mordecai's own crawl, deep on the tenth floor. **Odette**, then his manager, has secretly cheated: through her friend **Armita**, she bribed grixist heiress **Huanxin Jinx** — driving the storm goddess Dodola — to strike a puzzle and open the stairwell to the eleventh. With the level collapsing, Huanxin keeps raising her price until Odette, sick of a lifetime of being forced to choose, breaks the deal outright. Mordecai prepares to sacrifice himself with his *Final Blow* skill; Odette instead convinces the bard **Chaco** to worship Dodola and drive the Dart of Ophiotaurus into Mordecai's brother **Uzzi**, using him as the involuntary summoning vessel. Uzzi dies, Dodola forms inside Chaco's containment spell, her own lightning kills her, and the key opens the door. Chaco gains a godkiller achievement and ten levels. Mordecai — who never learns the truth — tackles Chaco through the stairwell, promising Odette that one day he will get out and kill her.

**Chapter 1 — El Capitolio**{: #chapter-1 .chapter-title }

Carl and Donut arrive on Floor 8, "The Ghosts of Earth," inside the rotunda of Havana's El Capitolio — Donut having picked Cuba over the Bahamas on the theory that the Beatrice lore book was bait. It is December 10th. Donut, bumped from first to second on the leaderboard, chooses **Master Telephone Psychic** over Agony Urchin, gaining an enormous map range plus *Brain Freeze*, *Bad Attitude*, and *Why Are You Hitting Yourself?* Cascadia's announcement lays out the floor: 293 regions, squads capped at five with six extra slots that must be filled, 14 days before phase two. The environment is a perfect replay of Earth weeks before the collapse — people are intangible ghosts, but every object is real and keeps its momentum. Squad leaders receive twenty flags; stab a creature below 5% health and it becomes a **T'Ghee totem** card. Loot from the memory turns to dust at collapse.

**Chapter 2 — The Hills Cyclops**{: #chapter-2 .chapter-title }

In the guild saferoom, Mordecai's Floor 8 form is revealed: a fat, pink, one-eyed **Hills Cyclops** who sounds unnervingly like Patrick Star. Bomo is the only cretin left; the other three went down with the castle. Samantha has flown back to the saferoom on her own, and Mordecai privately warns that a withering spirit shouldn't be able to do any of what she's doing. Donut opens her enormous backlog of end-of-Floor-6 boxes: a Cosmic Buff potion (dexterity), a town upgrade coupon, a mercenary coupon, a Flawless Jeweler's Gem, the **Enchanted Nipple Ring of the Superior Fire Demon's Hand Maiden** (fire immunity, cursed-item removal, and the berserking *Bitch, What?* spell), and the **Tome of *Legionnaires of the Damned***, a summoning spell that animates recently-unequipped armor and weapons with wailing spirits. Mordecai immediately sees the combo with her maxed *Laundry Day*.

**Chapter 3 — The Scavenger's Daughter**{: #chapter-3 .chapter-title }

Carl's turn. Among dozens of boxes: a prison tattoo kit, a peace-symbol patch, Doctor Ratchet's Automaton Build-It Kit, a Right Back Atcha auto-buckler, a Cosmic Buff potion, and the **Enchanted Nipple Ring of the Defiler** with the *Black Nimbus* spell, which temporarily strips a target of their god's protection. The Celestial Predator Box — earned for killing the last hunter alive — yields a single back patch: **The Scavenger's Daughter**, a divine, unique, fleeting item. Affixed, it doubles every patch's stat bonus, adds +25 dexterity and strength, and grants **Climb**, **Soul Reaper** (melee kills charge a Soul Essence bar; drained into a single devastating attack), and the **Mysterious Bone Key** — a one-time key to any lock in the dungeon that destroys the jacket and permanently consumes one of Carl's bones. The patch also gains new powers near deities. Donut discovers their squad flag has been auto-designed for them.

**Chapter 4 — The Royal Court's Flag**{: #chapter-4 .chapter-title }

The flag turns out to be a tiny pair of heart-covered boxers reading "The Royal Court of Princess Donut," which Carl finds hilarious and Donut finds an outrage. Bomo scans for stairwells and finds only two in the region. Samantha is sent out as a recon specialist and immediately begins asking ghosts whether they are mages. Stepping outside into Old Havana staggers Carl — *this is what we lost* — and he and Donut learn the hard way how the memory works: walking through a ghost strips its clothes, and crashes cascade until entire roads seize up. Their first mob is **The Experience**, a Taíno guardian with a Che Guevara head that barks like a man imitating a dog; Donut kills it almost casually and claims its beret. It drops a **Combo** utility card. As they finish looting, three crawlers approach — two men and a cat girl.

**Chapter 5 — Paz, Anton, and Sister Ines**{: #chapter-5 .chapter-title }

The newcomers are **Paz Lo**, a nervous armored Santero; **Anton Lopez**, a prison-tattooed Fugitive with two player-killer skulls; and **Sister Ines Quiteria**, a Havana brown Cat Girl Poet Laureate and Discalced Carmelite nun. Donut is instantly, loudly hostile about the existence of cat girls. The standoff nearly turns violent before it emerges why: on a Floor 6 program, the trio were shown a clip of Donut telling the strippers exactly what she'd do if she ever met a cat girl. Tserendolgor reports the men are cousins, fiercely protective of the nun, and that Anton once killed two of their own group for trying to kill her. Ines defuses things and proposes cooperation: help them flag their first squad member. The target is a monk seal.

**Chapter 6 — The Chowder War**{: #chapter-6 .chapter-title }

Examining the sleeping seal **Yago** triggers the quest **The Chowder War** — the Monk Seals, masters of Caribbean Kung Fu, versus the Red *Maníseros* Land Crabs, masters of *Juego de maní*, at war over the crabs' annual spawning in sacred waters. Carl notes that the dungeon's "mythology" is a half-researched mashup, which Sister Ines will later find genuinely blasphemous. The joint attack is harder than expected: Paz tanks with his *Turtle* shield while the others pour in ranged damage, the seal reveals a ranged-attack immunity spell mid-fight, and Carl has to punch it out of a killing blow on Paz before jamming the flag home at 5% health. Yago becomes a card — a level-70 aquatic tank — owned by Team Sister Ines. A second seal, Geraldo, promptly charges in.

**Chapter 7 — The Daughter's Kiss**{: #chapter-7 .chapter-title }

Geraldo becomes the Royal Court's first squad member. Zev books Carl and Donut on ***Shadow Boxer***, a documentary-style program owned by their sponsor the Open Intellect Pacifist Network, that wants to dig into Carl's life between fifteen and eighteen; a liaison will be present. Moving into the city, the group slaughters a horde of level-20 **duendes** — ragged, shoe-obsessed gnome things responsible for most of the car crashes. Carl's Soul Essence bar fills in ten kills, unlocking **The Daughter's Kiss**: he stomps a fleeing duende and it simply ceases to exist, shattering the sidewalk and knocking the whole street flat. Both squads collect their first utility cards — Time Extend, Stout, and a **Flee** card for Team Ines. Donut remains convinced Sister Ines is hiding something, chiefly because she doesn't use a litterbox.

**Chapter 8 — The Rules of the Deck**{: #chapter-8 .chapter-title }

Most of the recap episode is redacted — the group only sees an undead Easter Bunny, a Tokyo squad killed by a bus, Elle flagging a sasquatch, and a studio shot that includes Prime Minister Glory himself before it cuts to black for an hour. Cascadia then explains card combat in full: six totems minimum, five card types, four cards in hand refilled every ten seconds, one discard every thirty seconds. Crucially, the squad leader loses access to spells and inventory while cards remain in play, and summoned totems generally can't attack the enemy leader directly — protecting the deckmaster falls to everyone else. Practice arenas open in every saferoom. Then Samantha checks in with good news and bad news: she's found a mage powerful enough to animate her flesh golem body, and that mage has eaten her. The Bahamas lore book identifies her as **Shi Maria** — among other names, **The Bedlam Bride**.

**Chapter 9 — The Mock Battle Arena**{: #chapter-9 .chapter-title }

First runs in the mock battle arena, where Donut discovers she can set the environment — and picks a colosseum packed with cheering Nebulars. She also discovers the cards float in front of her face and block her view, which takes some menu-wrangling to fix. The easy-difficulty opponents are a joke: Mongo disembowels a Teletubby before anyone can react, and the second, a weeping, farting **Squonk**, dies of its own misery. Geraldo the monk seal at least gets summoned, complete with cheesy kung fu sound effects added to his every movement, and Donut learns uncommon totems can only carry two utility buffs. Carl concludes they should move up to medium difficulty. Elsewhere, other crawlers report equally absurd opponents — the Pillsbury Dough Boy, a teddy bear, flying piranhas.

**Chapter 10 — Colón Cemetery**{: #chapter-10 .chapter-title }

Sister Ines's real anger surfaces as she describes what the dungeon does to living faiths: strip the meaning, keep the surface, blend it with cartoons. The group reaches the Colón Cemetery, fenced and fog-choked, full of slow red dots. Ines almost charges in alone before agreeing to circle it first. Inside, it is not a boss room but a **Medium Ghommid Settlement** — a town of other-realm spirit creatures, corrupted and driven berserk — and the gate seals behind them, opening the compulsory quest **Pueblo de los Olvidados**. Paz's Santero class aggravates every undead in the place at once. The ghommids are grotesque and endlessly varied, and their touch freezes and drains; one owl-thing nearly shatters Carl's arm before Mongo kills it. They fight toward a saferoom as the horde closes in.

**Chapter 11 — Asojano Walks In**{: #chapter-11 .chapter-title }

The party escapes into the crypt saferoom "The Yellow Zone" — and then a red-tagged level-130 city boss knocks on the door and walks straight inside, earning Carl a Stranger Danger achievement. This is **Asojano, Orisha, Lord of Smallpox**, a faded god trapped in the town, whose mere presence is what drove the ghommids mad. He wants a Santero to repair his shrine in the temple at the town's center — a Club Vanquisher — so he can leave. Sister Ines has a near-panic attack at the idea of dealing with another false god, and Anton has to calm her. Asojano promises to hold the horde back at nightfall. Afterward, Donut and Carl open their fan boxes: Donut's Posse votes her the **Bard's Golden Throat**, an autotuning amethyst headset microphone, and Carl gets a coupon for a free massage at the Penis Palace.

**Chapter 12 — Rosetta Thagra**{: #chapter-12 .chapter-title }

Carl and Donut transfer to the production trailer to find Zev, the enormous caprid liaison **Harbinger** — the same one who conspired with the Skull Empire to kill Carl — and, summoned in a hurry, Quasar. The host is **Rosetta Thagra**, a Crest, a former crawler and game guide, recently freed and now running *Shadow Boxer* for the Open Intellect Pacifist Network. Carl recognizes the name instantly: Rosetta wrote the ninth edition of the cookbook. Donut's interview is a long, delighted tangent on *Gossip Girl*, *Gilmore Girls*, and Barb from *Stranger Things*. While Donut picks screencaps, Quasar quietly updates Carl on Faction Wars: the elf castle landed in Lemig Sortion territory and splattered half their army, the changelings are building the Princess Posse a stronghold, and Ferdinand has lost his hat and moved to the city. Then the screens fill with photos of Carl at seventeen.

**Chapter 13 — Milk and Potatoes**{: #chapter-13 .chapter-title }

Rosetta opens with "let's talk about your dead mother," and Carl reels. He gives the bare timeline aloud for the first time: on his fifteenth birthday his mother poisoned his father, then hanged herself in the basement and left Carl a note nobody ever read; months later his father walked out after Carl told him he'd finish what she started; Carl lived alone until the power was cut, was briefly suspected of killing his father, and aged out of the Anacortes Boys' Ranch into the Coast Guard. Donut, who never knew any of it, climbs into his lap. Then Carl spots the tell: a staged photo of him cooking with a mislabeled jug of **Milk** and potatoes — an impossible camera angle, a deliberate message pointing at the sixth-edition cookbook author Milk and the toraline root vegetable. He and Rosetta trade a coded exchange about root vegetables before Harbinger kills the broadcast, recalls Quasar, permanently bans Rosetta from crawler interviews, and tells Carl plainly that he is working to have him disposed of.

**Chapter 14 — The Gauntlet Run**{: #chapter-14 .chapter-title }

The plan for the ghommid town: Carl has spent the intervening hours building Bomo a gigantic scrapheap suit of armor — hundreds of individually-sewn metal plates and trash-tier daggers, with two six-packs of stun and anti-undead missiles on the shoulders. At nightfall the ghommids glow and wail, faster and far more dangerous. Paz is instantly hit with a **Terrified** debuff that roots him, so Bomo carries him while firing missiles to clear the road. Donut's first attempt at a battle-song buff collapses when she rhymes "cities" with "titties" and gets muted. A rare, fast **Ghommid Splitter** — a donkey-headed snake that breaks into two creatures — nearly kills Carl; he lets it impale itself on a flag and flags it as **Skylar Spinach**. Inside the temple, Asojano dances over his ruined shrine and a glowing gem, the Sun of Reawakening, forms in the air.

**Chapter 15 — The Celestial Thorn Room**{: #chapter-15 .chapter-title }

Seven broken shrines line the octagonal temple, and repairing one triggers a **Celestial Thorn Room** — a boss event where every choice carries a cost. Ogun demands Paz and Anton fix his anvil or be smitten; the seductive Oshun charms both Carl and Anton; Legba, Shango, and the deaf, Nothing-scarred Inle all bid; Asojano offers only the quest's completion; and Yemaya offers ascension, a card for each squad, and a boon from her friend Eileithyia. Mid-chapter Katia breaks in privately: she is wearing the **Crown of the Sepsis Whore**, and repairing Yemaya's shrine is the only path that saves both her and Donut. Carl snaps his own finger to break the charm and chooses Yemaya. He deploys "Phantasm" — *Laundry Day* on armored Bomo to explode the suit into hundreds of pieces, then *Legionnaires of the Damned* to animate them into a shrieking metal tornado that shreds Asojano. Bomo rebuilds Yemaya's obelisk like Tetris, the daughters' daggers carry the gem to it, and the town is freed. Carl flags the dying Asojano — and is infected with **SlugPox**.

**Chapter 16 — Ogun's Wrath**{: #chapter-16 .chapter-title }

Lightning cracks the temple floor and **Ogun**, god of blacksmithery, level 250 and invulnerable, arrives in person because he believes punishment should be delivered by hand. Carl's back patch wakes in a deity's presence. Sister Ines throws herself between the god and her friends with her trident and is frozen and shelved for her trouble. Ogun heals Paz just long enough to strip him of his grace and make his death debuff permanent — "when you pierce the veil, you will find yourself alone and without armor" — then, with Carl's *Black Nimbus* having merely removed Anton from his mercy rather than his wrath, steps on Anton's head and crushes it. He tells Carl only that if he did not hold Emberus in high esteem he'd kill him too, and vanishes. Dying, Paz warns Carl the truth about Sister Ines: her race carries a toxoplasma-like charm skill that makes people protect her and forget they were ever told, and before the dungeon she poisoned every nun in her convent — *El Segador de La Habana*, the Reaper of Havana. He asks to be let go. Ines refuses to let him leave, and stabs her squad flag into his neck, turning her dying friend into a card.

**Chapter 17 — Yemaya and Eileithyia**{: #chapter-17 .chapter-title }

Two goddesses arrive at once. **Yemaya**, a twin-tailed siren, thanks them for her re-ascension, gives Donut and Sister Ines each a mythic **Thief** mystic card — which steals a card from an opponent's deck — and grants Bomo a wish. He asks to be reunited with the Sledge, and she teleports him straight to Floor 9, costing Carl the stairwell-teleport spell he was counting on. **Eileithyia**, goddess of childbirth, is sponsored by **Huanxin Jinx** — the same grixist heiress from the prologue. She grants a party boon and a nonsense quest to kill five spiders a day, which exists purely as an unmonitored channel: Huanxin privately tells Carl that Odette will ask to be their Faction Wars adjutant and that he must accept. In exchange she will deliver the votes to turn off the ninth-floor protections and clear Katia's Sepsis Whore problem. Tell anyone, she says, and she destroys everyone he loves. Carl agrees with a scratch of his left arm. Sister Ines loots Anton's body and walks out into the night without a word.

**Chapter 18 — Pater Coal's Sacrament**{: #chapter-18 .chapter-title }

Leaking increasingly large and hostile pox slugs, Carl talks his way past a lisping Ares cleric into **Club Vanquisher** and is met by **Pater Coal**, Hobbledehoy High Cleric Supreme of Emberus, who suppresses the pox and detours him to the secret Temple of the Ending Sun. There, under the pretext of washing a martyr's feet — a sacrament no other god can eavesdrop on — the cleric delivers Emberus's intelligence on the Geyrun murder: Hellik remains the prime suspect, but a memorial crystal exists for Apito, who is still alive, which should be impossible. He names two leads: the origin of Katia's crossbow and the origin of Donut's oak bracelet, neither reachable without Osvaldo's memorial crystal. Carl realizes mid-massage that "Pater Coal" is the AI wearing a costume. He leaves with the **Martyr's Path** boon — every outdoor footstep is mapped, and at 5% health he can ignite all of them at once.

**Chapter 19 — The Lock Box of the Night Wyrm**{: #chapter-19 .chapter-title }

Back in the saferoom, Katia shows Donut the Crown of the Sepsis Whore on her own head — Eva's parting shot — and lays out the escape plan. Her Floor 6 celestial box contained the **Engaged Lock Box of the Night Wyrm**, holding the **Orchid of Eileithyia's Grace**. Eating it renders her unconscious at 1% health for an hour; surviving earns one of three boons: tripled constitution, immediate transfer to Floor 12 as a Celestial Attendant, or pregnancy and ejection from the dungeon entirely. Either of the latter two solves the crown. Two of three seals are broken (killing Eva, killing Imogen); the third requires an assassination job from the **Guild of Suffering**, which Katia has already found — hidden behind the Penis Parade, where Damascus Steel wears the same spiderweb elbow tattoo as Carl. The target, written in blood on a slip of skin: **Astrid**, the level-125 Bloodlust Sprite assistant manager of the Desperado Club.

**Chapter 20 — Mordecai's Attendants**{: #chapter-20 .chapter-title }

Three totems collected, eight days to phase two. Heading southwest out of Havana, Carl and Donut argue about Sister Ines — Donut has already sent her a message calling her a nun-murderer and a disgrace to cat kind, and been blocked. Zev warns of an upcoming Odette appearance where she'll ask something live on air, and privately begs them not to pick her as adjutant, since Mordecai will physically attack her and lose his contract. Carl needs a plausible excuse to ignore that advice. Returning to the saferoom, they find Mordecai smeared with lipstick and wearing a small pink bow, and the truth comes out: he has quietly spent Carl's money hiring six changeling orphans with compression sickness — including armless little **Ruby** — as room attendants, to keep them out of the Floor 6 slaughter. He stands by it. Carl tells him he'd have said yes if asked.

**Chapter 21 — The Adjutant Warning**{: #chapter-21 .chapter-title }

Zev opens a secured sponsor-to-administrator channel to explain what an adjutant is: a neutral third-party referee who accompanies the warlord physically onto the battlefield, calls fouls, and gives daily on-air commentary — someone loud, flashy, and famous. Her advice is unambiguous: not Odette. Then Carl and Donut attend the Faction Wars preproduction meeting, hosted by an exhausted, near-breaking **Cascadia** who materializes bodily just to dare Carl to kill her. The table introductions go poorly: Crown Prince Stalwart refuses to announce himself and then promises to line his codpiece with Donut's pelt, earning the rejected action item "change his title to Captain Enormous." Also present are Empress D'Nadia, Epitome Tagg of the Dream, surfer-voiced Luke of the Lemig Sortion, a blob from the Operatic Collective, Warlord Fangs of the Reavers, the naga **Rishi** of the Blood Sultanate, and a masked viceroy — whose head abruptly rolls off the table.

**Chapter 22 — Juice Box Crashes the Meeting**{: #chapter-22 .chapter-title }

The viceroy's killer takes his chair: **Juice Box**, wearing the face of Epitome Noflex. She has understood exactly what she is and what the warlords are, and she makes a demand — the unaffiliated NPCs of Larracos be recognized as their own team with full protections, regeneration, and voting rights. Carl proposes it as a formal action item; Juice Box adds "and with full voting rights." Tagg rages that it's impossible, Luke of the Lemig Sortion announces he'll petition his people to evacuate the game entirely, and Juice Box detonates a hobgoblin pus detonator that vaporizes the Operatic blob before an unseen archer puts a magical arrow through Prince Stalwart's eye. Rishi, the only one who never flinched, floats a compromise — invulnerability and a safe zone for the NPCs — and warns, chillingly, that the Sultanate knows how to answer terrorism. Donut's chief complaint is that the promised catering never arrived.

**Chapter 23 — The Penis Parade**{: #chapter-23 .chapter-title }

At the Desperado Club, the whole crew gathers to trade cards. Florin, based in Ecuador and rolling around with a tank, offloads a unique totem he can't control: **HeyZoos, Uzi Jesus**, a level-140 undead ranged healer with a submachine gun who once shot Florin in the stomach. Donut takes him, giving the Royal Court four of six totems. Carl quietly slips away into the Penis Parade, where dancers include Anaconda, Dong Quixote, Author Steve Rowland, Gluteus Maxx, and the skeleton DJ Doctor Bones, and finds **Damascus Steel** — an ifrit with smoke pouring from his eye sockets and the same spiderweb tattoo on his elbow. The Chowder War quest fails outright and is replaced by **Hell Comes to Crawler Town**: Sister Ines's warning turned a quick surrender into a war of attrition, the sheer volume of death has attracted attention, and the zombie farm animals Carl dumped into a global ocean have provided exactly the weak shells demons need. A **Demon Eviction** event begins worldwide — demons ejected upward from Sheol, able to return only by dragging a soul back down with them. Mordecai explains the rules fast: destroy the head, take the corpse, never let one grapple you, and above all never teleport a possessed shell. Carl, not yet knowing that last part, sends Samantha to bite Sister Ines so he can yank them both back.

**Chapter 55 — The Survival Royale**{: #chapter-55 .chapter-title }

The teleport goes catastrophically wrong. Samantha's spell-enhancing ability drags the entire beach back with her — the nun, the level-125 boss snake **Madre de Aguas**, thousands of zombie seals, crabs, and fish, and dozens of corpses whose demons, unable to be teleported, are now loose at full power miles away. The charm spell shatters. A live-announced **Survival Royale** begins with eight combatants, hosted by a drunk Kevin and Magnificent Troy, who cheerfully explain that the AI ruled crab sperm counts as a mob and that this is why hell opened. Sister Ines, now preaching that there were never aliens, only demons, and that she is the last rampart, refuses alliance, costing the Royal Court a ten-second "I thought we were friends" penalty. Troy, counting on his fingers, starts to ask why the tally says eight combatants when he's only named seven — and the chapter ends mid-sentence on **Battle commencing...**

**Chapter 56 — Force Play**{: #chapter-56 .chapter-title }

The chapter opens on a flashback: the day Carl walked out of the Temple of the Ending Sun and, rather than visit the healer, traded his oldest piece of jewelry — the very first ring he got in the dungeon — for an interest-free million-gold loan from the Temple of Emberus, re-forging it into the **Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus**. Disguised as a strength ring, it suppresses health-seeping and disease debuffs and numbs others until it overloads, and the AI marks the moment with a long achievement about Columbia House and negative option billing. Back in the present, the eighth combatant arrives: **Quan Ch** drops out of the sky invisible, still one-armed, still enraged, while Kevin and Troy replay Carl tearing his arm off and mock him for crying about it. The fight is over almost before it begins. Quan plays **Peekaboo** to read both Donut's and Sister Ines's hands, then a consumable **I pick that one!** to force Sister Ines's own Flee card — she's out, and Paz dissipates from the snake's jaws mid-summon. Carl lands a sticky hob-lobber and casts *Super Spreader* to infect Quan with slugpox, but Quan swaps Donut's entire hand for three Flee cards and force-plays one. Both keys transfer to Quan, and the Royal Court is teleported into a swamp with the achievement **The Fumble**. Carl's only play is to warn Imani and Tserendolgor that Quan is coming and that he'll be leaking slugs the whole way.

**Chapter 57 — The Trap Inside Samantha**{: #chapter-57 .chapter-title }

Carl needs to cross fifteen kilometers in minutes. He finally pins Samantha down and gets the truth out of her: she can fly and she can enormously amplify other people's spells, but she cannot teleport and cannot reliably control any of it, and she breaks down confessing she is useless without a real body. It is Samantha who supplies the answer by asking why he doesn't do what Lucia Mar does. Carl realizes his two-part teleport traps can be split — the anchor half mounted on a spider automaton, the automaton fed into Samantha's neck hole, Samantha thrown across the city. Florin, going down the stairs early, confirms that traps cannot be placed inside a stairwell chamber. Mordecai argues bluntly that Carl should stop trying to save everybody and protect himself. Carl throws Samantha loaded with four automatons and steps into the trigger trap.

**Chapter 58 — The Death of Quan Ch**{: #chapter-58 .chapter-title }

Carl lands across the street from El Capitolio, where Tserendolgor, Chuluuna, and Garret the tummy acher are already losing. Because Donut is miles away fighting duendes, Carl can teleport her summoned totems to him without them being subject to Quan's snares — a blatant exploit he uses without hesitation. Meanwhile the *Madre de Aguas* succumbs to her zombie curse and the demon prince **Amayon** slips into her body, announcing himself with a column of fire. Quan's **Toss-Up** card swaps three totems for two and turns Ren's Mongolian Death Worm against her, killing Chuluuna. Carl sticks a hob-lobber to Quan's head; Jola strips him naked with *Fashion Critic* (Carl pockets the celestial cloak); Lazarus dies, splits, and returns as Gold Edition Uzi Jesus and Gold Edition Asojano, who riddle Quan with **Bullet Hell** and heap debuffs on his broken body. Dying, Quan asks only for the plain gold ring engraved "For Daddy" that Carl took off his severed arm. "She'd be ashamed of what I've become." Donut gets credit — and both keys. Then the Capitol's exit door is declared damaged, with five minutes left.

**Chapter 59 — Tserendolgor's Last Stand**{: #chapter-59 .chapter-title }

The math is brutal and Ren works it out as fast as Carl does. Transferring a key requires a fight and a Flee, the lost key is chosen at random, and the door dies in minutes — there is no sequence where everyone gets out. Then Khulan returns to find her husband dead, and Ren stops fighting. She sits down against Garret, says she was really looking forward to torching some of those assholes on the next floor, and tells Carl the thing he most needs to hear and least wants to: he cannot sacrifice himself, because he and the cat are the only reason anyone still has hope. She empties her inventory onto the floor — her gear, her cards, five *Eye in the Sky* scrolls, her enchanted flamethrower Velma — kills **Garret** with the Knife of Frozen Tears so he resets to level one and can be rehomed, and hands Carl the pet carrier with instructions on his weaknesses. She confesses over chat to Donut that she did steal experience and did rig the pet show, and that Mongo is the rightful champion. She shows Carl the wrist bracer she intends to use, explains that Khulan will kill herself as soon as she stops crying and that she doesn't want that to be the last thing she sees, and asks only that he not let Sister Ines keep Paz's card. Khulan comes and wraps her arms around her neck. The chapter ends with Carl's hand on the dog woman's trembling shoulder and a whisper in her ear: "I will kill every last one of them. Every one. I goddamned swear it."

**Chapter 60 — The Reaper of Havana's Ultimatum**{: #chapter-60 .chapter-title }

Carl loots Quan's absurd hoard — five million gold, a sock full of nickels, a bowler hat, a magical chainsaw, a celestial cloak, and the arm he keeps for no reason he can name — leaving only the gold ring in the dead man's hand. Walking out into the burning city, he fights the urge to mark Ren with the Ring of Divine Suffering and doesn't. Then her last message arrives — *Goodbye, everyone. At least I won't have to listen to anyone butcher the pronunciation of my name anymore. Do me a favor, and fuck them up for me* — her bracer detonates, the Capitol's dome drops in on itself, and **Tserendolgor** dies here, in chapter 60, before Donut's panicked "WAIT, WAIT, WHAT ARE YOU DOING?" can even reach her. Carl picks his way west through a city of zombies and opening hell portals toward Imani. Donut, newly wearing a golden player killer skull neither of them mentions, spends the walk pretending to feud with a dead woman. Then Sister Ines makes contact: she knows Carl has the last key, she has prayed on it, and she cannot allow him to open a door inside a holy place. She claims to grow stronger with every demon that appears and offers to "free" him the way she freed her sisters. Imani reports full-power pleasure demons walking into the church as Ines's minions — and then the enslaved Amayon, kilometers away, is forced to bellow Carl's name across the burning city.

**Chapter 61 — Falling Up**{: #chapter-61 .chapter-title }

Running toward the church, Carl assembles the idea that will define the rest of the floor. Cookbook notes from Milk, Herot, and Rosetta establish that stairwells themselves are indestructible and cannot be removed from the playing field — only the doorways and the chambers around them can break. Crawlers all over the world are losing keys to collapsing doors, and squads in Turkey have dug down to a stairwell chamber they cannot enter. Reports from other crawlers clarify how the demon portals work: the smaller possessed shells need one soul, full-power demons need two and open a wide, sucking hole that lasts a minute; anything touching a portal is pulled to Floor 15, and since crawlers aren't allowed there, they simply explode. Samantha explains that Amayon isn't attacking — he's casting a portal spell to go home, and needs an enormous tithe of souls to pay for it. Carl starts asking about the *Build Trench* scrolls everybody keeps getting.

**Chapter 62 — Where the Sidewalk Ends**{: #chapter-62 .chapter-title }

Carl broadcasts the plan to the entire dungeon, worked out jointly with Prepotente: stop fighting, let keyholders leave, and everyone else strip every scrap of structure from around and above their stairwell chamber, then dig a trench underneath it with the scrolls. When the demon's portal fires and the world is sucked upward, the chamber's walls will teleport away and the stairwell itself will pass through the people waiting below. Prepotente publicly endorses it, then repeatedly revises the odds down from fifty to thirty-five percent. Crawlers push back hard until Carl makes the argument plainly: he will take a fifty percent chance with all of them over a hundred percent chance alone. Katia, Elle, and Li Na line up behind him, and the quest **Where the Sidewalk Ends** opens with a two-hour timer and three options — do nothing, free Amayon from his shell, or kill him. Donut points out this may cost them their own escape. Carl agrees, and she tells him the Princess Posse doesn't leave anybody behind.

**Chapter 63 — The Doggie Door**{: #chapter-63 .chapter-title }

Carl approaches the church expecting to find Imani alone and instead finds twenty-odd crawlers walking out of it — Katia, Bautista, Louis, Britney, Tran, Li Jun, Li Na, Zhang, Chris, Elle, and more, almost all of whom already have keys and none of whom need to be here. The explanation is **Lucia Mar**. After a massacre in Beijing she waited at the stairwell for Li Jun's team, said Florin had asked her to help, dropped five **Tier 3 Doggie Door** personal space upgrades — a fast-travel system that shouldn't exist before Floor 9 — and left. Installed in Imani's room, it lets the whole party step out of the guild into any door they've used on this floor. Li Jun privately relays Lucia's parting words: she is saving something special for Donut and Mongo on the next floor. Standing among his friends, Carl finally names the thing that has been grinding him down since the Masquerade — it was never futility, it was isolation.

**Chapter 64 — The Vinegar Bitch**{: #chapter-64 .chapter-title }

Mordecai identifies the name Amayon screamed. **Ysalte** — the Downward Spiral, the Vinegar Bitch — is the goddess of the Semeru dwarves and of Larracos, who appears near the end of Faction Wars and kills every crawler she meets. She is one of the few deities who will patron a bard, and she is sponsored by the Nebular Balance, the same religious cult sponsoring Sister Ines. Mordecai explains the Nebulars: they worship the stable AI at the galaxy's center as a god, consider replicating it blasphemy, want the crawl shut down and every world capable of hosting one exterminated, and are pacifists everywhere except inside the dungeon. Carl sets the assault: Chris and Zhang will demolish the church around the stairwell chamber and dig the trench; Elle and Imani will hold the lobby; Carl, Donut, Katia's squad, and Li Na's squad will go up the FOCSA building. He also gives Dong Quixote the sapient nickel sock, **Spunky Jefferson**, who immediately starts asking him about mutual funds.

**Chapter 65 — Graupel**{: #chapter-65 .chapter-title }

Thousands of demons stand docile outside the building, waiting for their ride home. The plan is for Donut to spend four minutes rooted in place casting *Minion Army*, enhanced by Imani and Samantha, to turn a slice of them against each other. The sluggalos ruin it by charging early, forty hatchet-swinging slugs slamming into the horde like a snot tsunami and then retreating directly toward Donut. Elle answers with ***Graupel***, her one-week-cooldown war spell — and it casts at level 16 instead of five, a fist-sized hailstorm that flattens everything in front of the building and jumps her six levels at once. Samantha, spinning and babbling about the river and the tree and the four seasons path, doesn't know why. Inside, the group climbs thirty-two floors of increasingly unbearable heat. Carl rigs a last automaton on the roof to teleport-break Amayon from the snake. Then Sister Ines comes sprinting down the hallway screaming "Sinner!" and Carl zaps her unconscious with the *Nighty-Night* wand. Louis, who already has five skulls, insists on being the one to put the knife in — and a marked-for-death symbol blooms over his head.

**Chapter 66 — The Missing Piece**{: #chapter-66 .chapter-title }

Ysalte arrives in the hallway as a small, sweet-voiced dwarf with a sparking candle on her helmet and a face that flickers into a silent scream, and calmly informs Louis that he killed her bard before she was finished with her and must suffer. Samantha buys him seconds by greeting the goddess like an old friend and insulting her child. The pickaxe swing bounces off the sex doll's head — and at that instant the *Madre de Aguas* pours through the ceiling, Carl's automaton lands on it mid-fall, and the teleport fires. Amayon takes his true form in open air: a kaiju-sized, four-horned, lava-skinned centaur demon whose chest is level with the shattered thirty-second floor. He is **not** freed from Ysalte's control; they have only made him more powerful. The quest **The Missing Piece** opens with a ten-minute timer: if Amayon is still enslaved when it ends, he and Ysalte both leave for the Ascendency and everyone without a key dies. And because it is the eighth floor, both of them draw card decks.

**Chapter 67 — The Final Card Battle**{: #chapter-67 .chapter-title }

Five crawler decks against a goddess and a demon prince. The objective splits: kill Amayon's six totems fast so he can speak, and keep Ysalte's deck from ever running dry, because the moment she's out of cards she can use her real magic. Li Na summons the headless, chest-faced Xing Tian; Katia the infant-skulled raven Annie under a **Timeless** buff; Ysalte a black-energy version of Yago the monk seal, buffed twenty-five levels. Amayon dumps four totems at once — a rock creature that falls through the floor, two inferior demons, and the succubus queen **Naamah**, who tackles Carl and shatters her teeth on his shield before a stolen **Hell Scrub** card drags her into a cloud of tentacles. Donut strips Amayon's last card with **Force Discard**, emptying his hand. Ysalte answers with **Raid Stash**, stealing a card from every crawler including inactive totems — Tran's mother, Li Jun's cousin, and **Leveled-Up Frank**. Then the world freezes: the demon is too weak to cast, there aren't enough souls left in the world, and the quest demands the same flavor of souls that triggered the event in the first place. Carl looks at the frozen Samantha and runs for the edge of the building.

**Chapter 68 — Raul's Ascension**{: #chapter-68 .chapter-title }

Carl leaps off the thirty-second floor, catches Samantha in midair, rides her down by the pigtails, sprints between Amayon's legs through a rain of burning black fluid, and flies to the beach. Donut plays **The Midnight Express**, one of Ren's gifts, temporarily handing Carl the deck. He summons **Raul** onto a shore carpeted with the dead and tells him the truth: his people are gone, the eggs remain, he is the last one, and the world needs a few million souls right now. Raul, already crippled by performance anxiety and a lifetime of self-loathing, needs a baby seal for motivation and then an audience — so Carl summons **Alpha Male Carl**, who turns out to be encouraging and supportive when Carl is the one summoning him, and who switches to abusive heckling on request. Raul ascends past cloud rank. Above, Donut casts *Why Are You Hitting Yourself?* on a prince of hell, and Amayon begins punching himself in the face.

**Chapter 69 — The Martyr's Path**{: #chapter-69 .chapter-title }

To buff Amayon to full strength Carl needs the Sheol-enhanced **Martyr's Path** to ignite every outdoor footstep he's taken — including the ones directly beneath the demon — and for that he must drop below 5% health with no time to spare. So he steps into the trigger for the teleport trap he left inside Samantha, and lands headfirst in her neck hole while she is clamped in the demon's fist being used as a bludgeon. His hair burns away, the acid melts his face, he cannot breathe, and the timer runs out at 7% — he has failed. Then he rips free at 2% and hits the button anyway. The city ignites; every soul in the world sighs at once. The timer had stopped with two seconds left, because **Paz Lo** — summoned by Ysalte, armed with Katia's reusable Bolt of Ophiotaurus, and using his directional self-destruct — took the goddess's head off. Amayon begins his spell, laughing, and tells Carl the message to Emberus is already delivered: he knows exactly what happened to Geyrun and will say so if Emberus helps him kill his three brothers. Then he tips his hand and drops Carl and Samantha toward the fire.

**Chapter 70 — The Golden Combo Misfires**{: #chapter-70 .chapter-title }

Samantha catches Carl and glides him back to Donut on a girder. Amayon, finished with them, turns his back and raises his arms to the sky. The FOCSA building is a skeleton, its floors pancaked into pockets, with Imani and Tran trapped two levels down and demons swarming up the outside to hitch a ride home. Author Steve Rowland is dragged into hell by a crab; Lazarus is taken the same way. Carl's Soul Essence bar fills and he doesn't dare discharge it up here. To clear Donut's hand so she can *Puddle Jump* everyone out, he tells her to summon Shi Maria and Alpha Carl and fuse them with the reusable **Golden Combo**. Shi Maria drives a leg through Alpha Carl's skull the instant he appears — and the combo, deprived of its target, jumps to the next available minion.

**Chapter 71 — The Eye of the Bedlam Bride**{: #chapter-71 .chapter-title }

Carl is combined with Shi Maria and loses his body. From the waist up he is himself; below, he is her, and she drives all of it. She ransacks his inventory with open delight — the cookbook, the bomb, the Kimaris totem, the war mage head — and toys with killing everyone in the room for his intent to betray her. A second voice, male, tells Carl to hold on, and Shi Maria snarls at it to be quiet because it isn't real, it's the husband she insists she didn't kill. She casts *Self-Immolation* to burn a hole through the floor, badly wounding Mongo and Katia, then mixes ink in a beaker using a sliver of the toraline root. When Li Jun slides down Li Na's chains to help, she jabs the tattoo needle into his eye, eats it, and dips the stick in the ink. She warns the others off, hands out potions she has upgraded from Carl's own stock, and rapid-fires a tattoo into the center of his chest: she will no longer be a card he can rip, but a part of him he must either carry or set free. "Either way, I win." When the combination times out, Carl is left staring at an eye above his sternum that blinks once and vanishes into his skin. **Tattoo. The Eye of the Bedlam Bride.** No other description.

**Chapter 72 — We've Already Won**{: #chapter-72 .chapter-title }

Carl wakes in Louis's arms being carried through a burning city. Nobody died — no crawlers, at least. Tran lost his wheelchair, Li Jun lost an eye, Mongo lost a wing that a potion can regrow, Carl lost all his hair. Donut has already ripped Shi Maria's card; the escort quest remains anyway, because she's on his chest now. They rip **Paz Lo**'s card too, unanimously, and let him go. Then the quest's second phase opens: Amayon's portal will scour the entire world, region by region, and anyone positioned directly beneath an isolated stairwell chamber will punch through into Floor 9. With one emergency action item, Carl politely asks the AI to hand all remaining Faction Wars votes to the viewing audience — and it does. The protections come off by a 98% vote. The tenth NPC team passes by 52%, with Juice Box and Ferdinand as co-warlords. The crawler count reads **33,804** — barely five thousand dead on a floor built to slaughter them. Carl jabs a finger at the sky and promises a bloodbath. Then the heavens open over Cuba, the world is stripped clean, and Carl, Donut, and Imani are pulled upward into Faction Wars — while a voice in his head notes that he forgot to drain the Scavenger's Daughter, and that they have so much work to do.

**Epilogue — The Homecoming Queen**{: #epilogue .chapter-title }

Three scenes. **Agatha**, waiting in an alley behind the Meadowlark facility in Wenatchee ten years before the collapse, is revealed as a Residual of a rival faction — one of the oldest species alive, hunting and wearing the bodies of the Apothecary's agents to steal their feeds. Her side and the traitor's are both racing to recruit the infant Primal before the mantid upgrades make future AIs unreachable; her own goal, if she wins, is to sweep all biological life from the universe so the Eulogist can sleep again. She lights a barrel fire, timing her entrance into the dungeon. Second, an emergency **Syndicate Council** session: Princess Formidable demands they pull the failsafe and blow the star before the AI infects the center system, and is voted into silence, while the Valtay's "solution" is quietly revealed — 150,000 mercenaries and private armies distributed across the eight sponsored teams, with orders to exterminate the crawlers and especially the two warlords. Third, aboard a converted garbage freighter parked in orbit under the name ***Homecoming Queen***, **Tipid** and **Rosetta** watch Carl's escape on replay, cleared for on-boarding — with fifty thousand former crawlers hidden in the hull, all newly enlisted as troopers of the Princess Posse. They are coming home.
