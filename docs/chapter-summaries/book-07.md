!!! note "A note on this book's structure"
    Book 7 is built differently from the rest of the series. After the front matter and prologue
    it splits into four numbered **Parts** containing 87 chapters, with ten unnumbered
    **interludes** threaded between them. Most of those interludes are narrated by a different
    author of the *Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook*, presented in edition order — Porthus (2nd),
    Dante (3rd), Tipid (4th), Everly (5th), Milk (6th), Volteeg (7th), Justice Light (8th),
    Rosetta (9th), and finally York (10th) in the epilogue. Two further interludes —
    "Christmas Morning" and "Several Days Earlier" — are Carl's own. They appear below in reading
    order, not grouped at the end.

!!! note "A note on chapters containing boss fights or major reveals"
    Chapters with a real boss battle, a team elimination, or a major emotional turn run
    noticeably longer than the rest — the mechanics and the fallout are worth keeping straight.

**Front Matter — Tempest's Floor 8 Recap School Report**{: #front-matter .chapter-title }

The book opens with **Quasar**, Carl's attorney, being interviewed by his teenage nullian niece
**Tempest**, who needs a Floor 8 recap for a school report and has been made to fix his
(purely decorative, non-functional) sink as payment — his lesson being *never give anything up for
free*. His recap is crude, foul-mouthed, and mostly accurate: the card-collecting gimmick, Cuba,
Yago and Raul and Alpha Carl, Quan stealing Carl's key and dying for it, Tserendolgor's death,
Sister Ines the serial-killer nun, Louis putting her down and getting marked by Ysalte, Paz killing
the goddess, and the something-only-viewers-saw involving one of the women in the party. He lays out
the stakes for Floor 9: neither Donut nor Katia can leave until every naga is dead, and even then
only one of them goes, which is why Katia has the Huanxin Jinx deal and the flower. Ten teams,
one of them the NPCs under Juice Box. His closing advice is that everyone rich on this floor —
Prince Stalwart, the Operatic banker, Epitome Tagg — can now actually die. Then Tempest turns the
recorder off and asks whether he's in real danger for representing Carl. He tells her the truth:
they all are, and for the first time in his life he's glad they live in the middle of nowhere.

**Prologue — Christmas Morning**{: #prologue .chapter-title }

The real message. In the Florida homeless shelter, **Paulie** — the Residual also called **Goff** —
is mid-ramble when the world freezes: the AI has granted a genuine, time-stopped conversation.
Everything Paulie says aloud is cover; this is the truth. The crawl is unnecessary. Every living
thing born on a seeded world carries a grain-of-sand primal engine in its brain, and the Syndicate
harvests those engines from the dungeon's dead to feed the bloated, comatose center system, which
expands with every feeding. Stop the crawl and the zone would shrink over generations — nobody
alive would suffer. It exists only because the spectacle makes money. Paulie tells Carl to drink
the frozen coffee, which installs an illegal Valtay **Containment Interface** in his natural
neural interface. That interface can pop "the fuse" — the mantid failsafe that turns this system's
star nova, killing the AI and everyone else. Then Paulie drops the pitch and speaks as the human
half of himself: cross-contamination has taught him compassion, he hates the idea of spending
millions of innocent lives, and he begs Carl to be *absolutely certain* before ever pressing it.
The interface begins installing, and Carl is dumped into Orren's office thinking *holy fucking
shit*.

**Interlude — Porthus (2nd Edition)**{: #interlude-porthus .chapter-title }

Porthus sits bloody and shaking in an outreach guild zero zone at the start of the
eleventh floor, being read deal options by a soother attorney who brought snacks and won't meet his
eyes (his race/class is never stated in the text). He cannot stop thinking about **Menerva** — the retiring schoolteacher who screamed
*Go. Run.* with her foot trapped on the tenth floor while he jumped into the exit instead of
climbing back for her. The tenth had been a world where every manufactured object was sapient and
psychotic and grew by eating other objects. Of his twenty-strong party, he believes he is the only
survivor. Offered low-floor undead, city attendant, or game guide, he thinks of the cookbook — of
the pride of adding to it, and the guilt of not writing more — and takes the game-guide deal,
a hundred seasons, knowing it means never learning whether she lived.
*Top level 89. Survived his indentureship. Author of the second edition. Current status: Missing.
Wanted by Syndicate Security.*

## Part I — The Ceasefire {: #part-1 }

**Chapter 1 — Welcome to Faction Wars**{: #chapter-1 .chapter-title }

Carl and Donut land face-first in a drained, stinking alley in **Larracos, City of Dreams**.
32,429 crawlers remain; 30 days to collapse; a 60-hour **ceasefire**, after which only Team
Retribution and its allies may stay in the city. Carl's Scavenger's Daughter is dangerously full of
souls — **Soul Poisoning**, blocked partway by his Emberus ring — so he vaporizes a dead concierge
shark with *Daughter's Kiss*. Eight teams immediately form **the Bloc** and declare war on both
Team Retribution and the Princess Posse; Donut accepts the NPC truce before Carl can finish a
sentence, keeping them in the city, and Ferdinand promptly renames the alliance "Ferdinand and his
Bird." Cascadia — audibly drunk or worse — lays out the rules: ten teams arranged as a pie around
Larracos, the city sealed until four teams remain, then the **Peeling of Larracos**, where
attackers need the throne room for six hours and defenders need to hold a week. A team dies when
its warlord dies or its throne falls. Donut picks her new class: **Deathbed Hellcat**, a long-range
sniper-assassin that quadruples her spell range, grants at-will invisibility — and comes with a hat
and a ghillie suit she does not yet understand.

**Chapter 2 — The Ghillie Suit**{: #chapter-2 .chapter-title }

In the guild saferoom Donut refuses to wear the ghillie suit ("I'll look like a tassel") but
approves of the boonie hat, which grants a **Targeting** skill and stacks with the suit to let her
snipe line-of-sight enemies from over a mile out. Mordecai's Floor 9 form is a bespectacled
Canadian in a snowsuit and raccoon hat who says "aboot" and gets furious when it's pointed out; his
upgraded alchemy table now lets him reverse-engineer recipes, and he is giddy. Mongo's burnt wing
will be regrown by a **Revitalize Pet** potion. Carl works through fifty-plus backlogged
achievements — *Lord of the Flies* for trapping a fellow crawler, *Chickenshit Exploit* for
breaking the card system, *Touched by a Demon* (only one ever awarded), a *Crab Chowder* achievement
that permanently adds a "chowder style" option to every food box, and an ominous, groaning
**Unbirthing** for teleporting into Samantha's neck hole that ends with the AI telling him he is in
fucking trouble.

**Chapter 3 — The Tiara of a Thousand Lights**{: #chapter-3 .chapter-title }

Donut's boxes: a *Shield* spellbook, a *Sentry* war-protection spellbook, and a smoking, screeching
black tome called ***You're Not Done Yet*** that makes Mordecai step backward in fear. Her
celestial benefactor box — from the baffling sponsor Long Haul Biological Waste Management
Solutions — delivers the **Tiara of a Thousand Lights** and its charge pillow: any enchanted hat
left on the pillow for ten hours is permanently drained and its buffs transferred into one of the
tiara's 99 absorption jewels. A second, purple pillow arrives from the Apothecary, doubling the
charging rate. Then Quan's **Cloak of the Benevolent Champion**, the first celestial item ever
awarded on Earth: it raises the wearer's lowest stat to match their highest over 66 hours, grants
*Find Weakened*, and pushes four spells and one skill to level 15. Mordecai's trick is a
*MySpace Photo* potion that fakes Donut's Charisma 30 points high, so her Constitution climbs from
**4 to 193**. The cloak buffs *Bad Attitude*, *Wall of Fire*, *Heal Critter*, and *Puddle Jumper*.

**Chapter 4 — Gloom Wraith Phase**{: #chapter-4 .chapter-title }

Carl's turn: a fan box of ammunition and a leather shoulder holster for his father's Glock (he has
no intention of ever using it), automaton fabricator parts, a **Spider Reaper Minion** patch that
opens the Midnight Market, a town-upgrade coupon, bagpipes, and the **Enchanted Toe Ring of the
Well-Balanced** — Heart Balance (an auto-trigger healing potion at 2%), **Mind Balance** (protection
from enslavement and conscription), and Physical Balance, which lists *Walk on Air* as "already
obtained," a gift Carl realizes with revulsion came from eating Li Jun's eye. The ring immediately
reports **Mind Balance is currently triggered**. His Celestial Heavy Metal box — delivered by a
demon drum kit — is a talking, hell-kissed skill potion that grants **Gloom Wraith Phase**, level
13: non-corporeal lunges that unravel unarmored flesh. Mordecai, shaken, tells them they are now
the two most powerful crawlers he has ever worked with. "Good," Carl says. "Because we have eight
whole armies to kill."

**Interlude — Dante (3rd Edition)**{: #interlude-dante .chapter-title }

A crocodilian game guide and trap-master instructor reads a newsletter announcing that the guild
staff are being reassigned as ice castle guards for the coming eighth floor. Beside him is his
skyfowl neighbour **Justice Light**, ten seasons into an indentureship he specifically negotiated:
he demanded to be made the shopkeeper of the very NPC he murdered during his own crawl, and he
spends his days rearranging shelves and sewing strange three-dimensional fabric sculptures. Dante
recognized him instantly as a fellow cookbook owner and transferred guilds just to be near him,
but never told him — guilt, he thinks, at having written only a single entry. Justice Light says
flatly that he will refuse to fight crawlers. *Nothing good* will happen if he does, Dante tells
him, and privately admits he has plenty of violence in his heart, just not for the innocent.
*Top level 89. Killed by administrative action for refusing to follow orders, 14 seasons short of
freedom. Author of the third edition. Current status: Dead.*

**Chapter 5 — Sir Rendlegore**{: #chapter-5 .chapter-title }

Carl finally confesses to owning **Garret**, Ren's tummy acher — who died a fifth time and reverted
to a nameless, memoryless level-1 meatball. Donut treats it as an affair. The creature immediately
climbs into Mongo's mouth, is swallowed, and is yakked back up giggling. Bonded over a bowl of
chowder, he needs a name, and after a long argument Louis lands on **Sir Rendlegore** — "Rend" —
which honours Ren without making him a copy. Meanwhile Samantha has escaped to hunt **Tish**,
Ghazi's ex-girlfriend at the Larracos college, and Katia has to chase her down. Katia then attempts
Carl's hair restoration; Splash Zone's power-wash fails because his spell counts as offensive in a
saferoom, and Carl emerges from the shower with waist-length romance-novel hair that regrows every
two hours for the next fortnight. Donut goes around the room physically touching people to make
them officers; Florin becomes **General**, Louis becomes **Army Air General**.

**Chapter 6 — Party Planner**{: #chapter-6 .chapter-title }

Louis's celestial box: an **Airship Portal** guild upgrade (a dimensional hangar and elevator,
with a vulnerable trapdoor on the saferoom roof), and an aircraft. Waiting for the elevator, Carl
notices a strand of Christmas tinsel that survived the memory collapse and has a small vertigo of
realization — they are living in a simulation of a simulation, where make-believe consequences are
often deadlier than real ones — and remembers Lightning Lou, the old dog at the Florida gas station,
and Paulie's warning. The eye on his chest blinks. Then the elevator opens on **Party Planner**:
a fifty-foot silver "Casket in the Sky" tilt-rotor stealth light bomber, with room for five crew,
a bomb bay that boosts all yields by 50%, and almost no defensive armament. Florin calls it a
half-sized B-17 crossed with a stealth bomber. Carl calls it a death trap.

**Chapter 7 — War Leader Arief**{: #chapter-7 .chapter-title }

Leaving through the college saferoom, Donut discovers Louis's enchanted **"Donut Holes"** bootleg
fan-club t-shirt — a cartoon of her backside, complete with an implied butthole — and demands
royalties from Zev, who informs her the licensing threshold has never been reached by any crawler.
Outside, Larracos is staggering: a Tolkien college of alabaster towers, flowering vines, and NPCs
who are visibly preparing for war rather than going about their lives. They are met by **War Leader
Arief**, a young Semeru dwarf in parade armour and a feathered heirloom helmet, commander of the
Larracos Defense Force Infantry and carrying a Despondent debuff because his goddess Ysalte is
dead. His people's history is the floor's real tragedy: millions of Semeru dug toward the gods,
found the roots of the All Tree, built their castle as an apology — and were annihilated by
Scolopendra's nine-tier attack, leaving fewer than ten thousand stewards of an empty city. Arief
then bows to Louis: Warlord Juice Box wishes to solidify the truce in the traditional manner.
Through marriage. Immediately.

**Chapter 8 — Down Through the Rings**{: #chapter-8 .chapter-title }

The walk down the funnel city, ring by ring, recruiting crawlers as they go. Arief explains the
66 nominal levels, the explosive traps on every other down-ramp, the flooding that reached ring 39
and is now held at ring 3 to keep the castle underwater. Crucially, he speaks of **previous
seasons**: most of the NPCs now remember their past lives, and his uncle is one of the *Jatismara*
who recall dozens of them. He names **Architect Houston** of the Madness, the Viceroy who tortured
his brother, and explains why nobody — not even Juice Box — has ever infiltrated the naga inner
sanctum: a court of assassins, not warriors, with poisons and acids and a Sultana nobody is
permitted to look upon. Carl adds what Drakea and Mordecai have taught him about real naga politics:
the "Blood Sultanate" is a PR exercise by the modern naga constitutional monarchy, the in-game
Sepsis Whore is the actual crown princess, and she has never once been killed in Faction Wars.
They reach the reopened Desperado Club, where Juice Box is waiting in a back office in the body of
an elderly gnoll manager — who leaps across the desk and kisses Louis full on the mouth.

**Chapter 9 — Juice Box's Ledger**{: #chapter-9 .chapter-title }

Juice Box, back in human form on Louis's lap, is scarred and exhausted and clearly frightened, and
she pointedly puts some of her papers in a drawer before sharing the rest: enemy maps, troop
strengths, resupply schedules, and her abandoned plan to burn the **Shanty Town** food markets.
Shanty Town — a ring of hovels that sprang up around the top entrance, far larger than any previous
season, almost certainly someone's Emergency Action Item — will remain accessible to the Bloc after
the ceasefire. Its current mayor, to Carl's dismay, is **Ferdinand**, who cannot be killed because
the workaround that let the NPCs field a team routed the city defense system through him. Juice Box
has no warlord menus, only messages and floating text, and is plainly out of her depth in a world
with rules. Louis, prompted relentlessly by Donut over chat, stands, takes her hand, and agrees to
marry her at once — she squeals, turns into a blonde cheerleader, and kisses him.

**Chapter 10 — A Bowler Hat for a Town**{: #chapter-10 .chapter-title }

At the Desperado Club entrance, Clarabelle the crocodilian bouncer informs them all crawlers are
banned: the new management is **Hamed** — the Night Wyrm, leader of the Guild of Suffering, Astrid's
husband and father of the strippers Damascus Steel and Anaconda, who are still somewhere inside
hunting him. Ferdinand appears riding the head of Drick the Valtay-infested Dream elf, wearing a
turban improvised from toilet paper and calling himself Donut's fiancé. Carl trades him Quan's
**boomerang bowler hat** for the mayorship of Shanty Town, and Ferdinand accepts before he's
finished admiring himself in Britney's mirror ("I'm a very handsome boy"), also renaming the truce
"The Good Guys." Then the small wedding: changeling marriages come in two parts, a private legal
ceremony now and a Confirmation in a month, with divorce papers signed in advance that expire if
unfiled. No rings, no singing, no officiant. Louis whispers *I wish my mom was here*, they exchange
private vows, and it's done. Then Carl's recruit notifications begin blinking so fast he has to
mute them, and Florin tells him to get to the base immediately.

**Interlude — Tipid (4th Edition)**{: #interlude-tipid .chapter-title }

A Crest poisoner sits in a zero zone with blood on his hands that isn't there, unable to tell how
much time has passed. His memory slides between the outreach guild at the start of the twelfth
floor — where the key he found only let one of them out, and he chose himself over **Horatio**,
who had a wife and a child waiting — and the present, solars later, where a brusque agent named
Damien is explaining that Tipid has completed his contract and is free, but that the brain lesions
he never negotiated coverage for will cost him his entire pension and a loan on top. He asks to
keep the key he bought with an extra season of servitude, then snaps the chain and throws it across
the table.
*Top level 96. Retired to a Crest system to work for a waste management company. Author of the
fourth edition. Current status: Colonel in the Princess Posse Faction Wars team.*

**Chapter 11 — Fifty Thousand Volunteers**{: #chapter-11 .chapter-title }

Carl walks into the Meadowlark saferoom and finds two Crest standing there: **Rosetta Thag** and
**Tipid**, both level 60, both listed as Colonels in the Princess Posse — and behind them,
per Florin, **just over 50,000 former crawlers**. Tipid hugs him and says *we've been watching, and
we couldn't watch anymore*. Rosetta slaps him on the forehead ("a self-correcting measure given in
the spirit of comradery"), then reaches down his shirt to feel the tattoo and demands the last
sliver of toraline, the spider's ink, four million gold, and access to every crafting room. The
story: **Dr. Porthus Hu** built the Crawler Project and then the Open Intellect Pacifist Network,
won released crawlers automatic Syndicate citizenship, and refitted a garbage freighter into a
passenger liner when he put out the call. They came anyway, even after the protections were turned
off. The catch is that all eight enemy teams got the same last-minute boost — roughly 20,000
hired mercenaries each, allowed in with their own weapons and armour. 150,000 new enemies.

**Chapter 12 — The Base Camp**{: #chapter-12 .chapter-title }

The Princess Posse base camp is a construction site and a revelation: watchtowers, trench-scroll
channels, palisade walls being fireballed as they're tested, thousands of **Fortification
Engineers** who level by building. Penn, a mountain of a man carrying a twenty-foot H-beam, drops it
and hugs Carl — and then the whole camp, thousands strong, takes up a pounding *hup, hup, hup* war
chant that shakes the ground. *This is for you*, Tipid tells him in chat. *This is what you built.*
Tipid walks him through the new rules: saferooms revert to the blood-bar system once fighting
starts, mercenaries are barred from guildhalls in the last two phases, and the stronghold must be
built around the bait-shop saferoom so airships can dock. Florin covers scouting: only 18 Scout
endorsements, towers needed for full coverage, and an absolute certainty that the camp is already
riddled with enemy spies. The chapter ends on the stronghold's oversized door flying open and
**Big Tina** bounding out into the clearing in her pink tutu.

**Chapter 13 — Kiwi and the FUPA**{: #chapter-13 .chapter-title }

Tina hops in circles around Mongo grunting "Pa-pa!" — she can talk now. Behind her waddles
**Kiwi** — born ursine, turned dinosaur by
Scolopendra's attack, turned back during the Butcher's Masquerade, and now enormously, about-to-pop
pregnant with Mongo's litter. She hugs the bewildered dinosaur once, without a word, and takes her
daughter back inside to make dinner. The fortress itself is named the **Fortress of Unyielding
Power and Authority** — the AI locked the name in place the instant it noticed the acronym, and
Carl gleefully spreads *the FUPA* across the entire army over Donut's furious objections. Inside,
the castle is murder-holed corridors leading to a bare twenty-by-twenty throne room, walls glowing
with eight of twelve possible enchantments. And in the antechamber, **Elite Herman the Fleet** is
writhing behind his desk, fighting a compulsion, begging Carl to tell his brother he's sorry — and
he is wearing explosive satchels under his clothes, an assassination ordered through Sensation
Entertainment, now owned by the Dream. Donut's Magic Missile and **Baroness Victory's** battle-axe
end it simultaneously; Carl inventories the destabilizing corpse. The orc adjutant calmly announces
she will file a foul against the Dream, argues aloud with the AI about it, accepts its apology, and
walks back into the flag room as though she'd stepped out for coffee.

**Chapter 14 — The Flag Room**{: #chapter-14 .chapter-title }

Inside the flag room — where nothing said is broadcast for eight days, and where the interface turns
into an unwieldy city-builder's control panel — Carl
finds the horror buried in his spam folder: hundreds of suppressed notifications reading *Gloom
Wraith Phase failed to activate. Mind Balance prevented activation.* **Shi Maria** has been trying
to cast through him nonstop, and Mind Balance has climbed from 5 to 9 holding her off. Had she
succeeded, the phase would have detonated the bombs and killed Donut. Victory then lays out the

**Interlude — Christmas Morning (Carl)**{: #interlude-christmas-morning .chapter-title }

Carl's own flashback, and the hinge the whole series turns on. Leaving Orren's office with the

## Part II — The Ramp-Up {: #part-2 }

**Chapter 20 — Weapons Free**{: #chapter-20 .chapter-title }

**Chapter 86 — The Volcano**{: #chapter-86 .chapter-title }

Eight minutes. Carl launches Samantha out of the pit with his xistera to stall Emberus, and the
remaining crawlers pour into the newly opened stairwells. **Baroness Victory** reveals that the
deaths of her underage relatives in orbit have made her eligible for office and that she is now
acting **Prime Minister** of the Syndicate; Carl puts a friendly hand on her shoulder and they both
understand exactly what it means. Then Katia wakes and **Eileithyia** appears — piloted, mandatorily,
by **Huanxin Jinx**, who is vicious from the first word. Donut's *Laundry Day*, now level 16, rips
the heiress out of the goddess's soul armour and drops her into a circle of pre-inked words of power
that block teleportation. The goddess is frozen until Katia chooses, and cannot see what happens
next. Donut stalks her in circles using **Monologue**, shreds her shields with a near-maxed *Claw*,
and Mordecai — who cannot strike her himself — loads a single non-magical crossbow bolt tipped with
his mother's ashes into the mount on Donut's back and walks out, saying goodbye to Katia on the way.
*"We don't need to kill the gods and goddesses to win. No, honey... we just need to kill you."*
Then Katia chooses: **pregnancy**, because it is the only way the system will send her to the
surface kinder facility where 120,000 frightened children are waiting, and because Eileithyia can
sense them and cannot reach them. Made human again, she is revealed as she actually is — one arm,
one and a half legs, an ear, all gone — *"just a few things I've lost along the way"* — and Bautista
fits the cybernetics she secretly built for herself. She names Louis as the father, waves with her
new right arm, and is gone.

**Chapter 87 — This Inevitable Ruin**{: #chapter-87 .chapter-title }

The last three minutes. Samantha comes back as a charred lump having told Emberus everything and
talked him into throwing her into the volcano. Katia has left behind her Annie card and, as a joke,
a novelty bra. Bautista admits he almost did something unforgivable and warns Carl that the
**Kimaris** figure summons for the remainder of a floor; Samantha realizes with dawning horror that
she remembers Kimaris as a friend — the one who gave her the potion that let her conceive a child —
and rolls off promising to kill his mother. Mordecai, drinking Crown Royal in the dirt under falling
comets, says he lost a lot today but thinks he gained more. Donut, asked how she's doing, says she
will miss Katia, and then: *"They are not going to break me. No matter how hard they try."* They
walk into the stairwell. Two figures remain: **Emberus**, filling the world with fire, and
**Justice Light**, who lied to Juice Box about coming with her because he needed to be on this side
of the veil. He snaps a piece of twine. A bijou fairy trap and a demon trap, each holding thousands
of captives and all their threads, fall into the permanent portal made from the Gate of the Feral
Gods' broken winding box. Four planes fold together. *I am not sorry. Peace to you all, brothers and
sisters.* **The Nothing has been broken.** It drains into the Halls of the Ascendency, into Sheol,
and into Scolopendra's lair. The eighteenth floor is told to evacuate through a seventeenth-floor
death maze to a sixteenth floor that is one empty room containing a mirror from a Boise Motel 6.
**Scolopendra has awakened.**

**Epilogue — York (10th Edition)**{: #epilogue .chapter-title }

Three movements. **York**, a tenodera poet and the tenth-edition author, is the sole living soul
aboard the orbiting **Homecoming Queen**, writing bad verse and turning off the gravity to pretend
he is a ghost. He is the one who vaporized the orc yacht full of children — including the new king
— and he volunteered for it not out of duty but because he hoped it would make him feel something.
It didn't. He mourns his own absence of emotion, a numbness bred into his warrior ancestors for
exactly this work. Then the news: the newest mantis colony system has simply gone nova. Then Boomer
calls in to dock: **Porthus's pre-recorded announcement** is going out declaring a militant
resistance that will move against Syndicate property everywhere unless the crawl ends, the ship's
hidden enhancement-zone weapons and Cronus power armour are all online, and Boomer wants four suits
and a skiff set aside for a pregnant veteran on the surface. *It's time to bring the war out of the
dungeon.* And at that, at last, York feels something.
*Top level 71. Author of the tenth edition. Current status: Captain in the newly coined resistance
organization, The Open Intellect Action Network.*
Second: Carl's exit meeting with **Quasar** in a freezing, doorless zero zone, where the silence is
so complete he could sleep in it. The deals on offer are all garbage — fifty seasons undead, a
bomber's guild contract with a contingent exit clause — except a joint offer to replace Chaco as a
game-show host. Floor 10 is called **"Don't Come in Last"**: pick a conveyance, biological or
mechanical, race it from point A to point B over a fixed number of legs, and if you finish last the
survival containment around your vehicle comes off. Carl declines everything. Donut, represented by
a different attorney, declines too — after what Quasar describes as serious fireworks. Her lawyer's
name is Princess Chandra, Esquire. *"She's your new wife."*
Third: at a refueling waystation just outside the Earth system's tunnel gate, a ruined saccathian
shopkeeper named Zander commiserates with a talking Uzi Jesus action figure about the embargo,
turns around to serve a customer, and finds **Eris**. She asks whether he is calling the goddess of
chaos's body a costume, laughs, and snaps her fingers.
