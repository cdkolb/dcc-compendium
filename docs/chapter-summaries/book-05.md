**Chapter 1 — Collateral for the Gate**{: #chapter-1 .chapter-title }

Floor 6, "the Hunting Grounds," opens with the Gate of the Feral Gods already a liability: hauled before the hooded, worm-headed Valtay liaison **Orren**, Carl learns the Syndicate can't legally confiscate the artifact but won't let him keep it either, given the danger it poses on a floor about to be flooded with hunters. Orren lays out three options — lock down every portal on the floor, "accelerate" (kill) Carl outright, or have him voluntarily surrender the gate for a receipt, redeemable on the ninth floor. Carl demands a lawyer, halting the meeting cold. Shunted into a stadium-sized staging arena for mandatory class specialization, he learns via chat that Donut has picked a new class, **Legendary Diva** (a singing bard subclass), over Mordecai's objections, and that Katia got a HAZMAT endorsement for her Monster Truck Driver class. Waiting in line, he meets an amputee crawler named Ajib and hears the floor's opening announcement: hunters, bumped to level 50, will be loosed in under 28 hours. A Bush Elf attendant, Ian, warns him in passing about a mantis hunter named **[Vrah](../characters/vrah.md)** who killed his brother and wears the heads of her kills — the first mention of the antagonist who will define the floor. The chapter ends with Carl specializing into **Agent Provocateur**, the bomb-focused class upgrade.

**Chapter 2 — The Legendary Diva**{: #chapter-2 .chapter-title }

Carl arrives at a dryad settlement outside Zockau and is immediately ambushed by an invisible pack of night weasels that paralyze two level-95 Funeral Bell guards in seconds. His new **[Ping](../spells/ping.md)** spell and a thrown sack of gunpowder reveal the creatures just in time for Donut, Mongo, and Chris to drive them off before Carl himself is paralyzed and dragged into the personal space's saferoom. There, Mordecai's Floor 6 NPC form is revealed as "Pocket Kuma," a comically small and cuddly bear completely at odds with his usual gruffness, reducing Carl to helpless laughter. The chapter ends with an unexpected knock at the personal space door — the first delivery from the Gate negotiation.

**Chapter 3 — Quasar's Condom Speech**{: #chapter-3 .chapter-title }

**[Quasar](../characters/quasar.md)**, a foul-mouthed Nullian, introduces himself as Carl's court-appointed attorney, cutting Orren's meeting off cold with a memorable metaphor: he can't stop Carl from "getting fucked" by the Syndicate, but he's the condom. He warns Carl off "naked" (bodiless) Valtay like Orren and explains liaisons exist to manufacture leverage for the system. Together they settle on surrendering the gate as collateral rather than giving it up outright — Carl gets it back on the ninth floor. Back in the personal space, the mercenary package itself arrives: four cretin bodyguards (Bomo, the Sledge, Clay-ton, and Very Sullen) split across the party, plus two bonus spells taught to the mercenaries rather than to Carl directly — a **Teleport to Stairwell** spell for Bomo and a mysterious spell called **[Zerzura](../spells/zerzura.md)** for the Sledge, Quasar's own suggestion. Katia returns from the previous floor's climax at level 52 with a new Steely Skin passive.

**Chapter 4 — The Advanced Bomber's Studio**{: #chapter-4 .chapter-title }

Carl buys the Advanced Bomber's Studio, a room that lets him simulate explosive yields on virtual targets and dissect ordnance he doesn't own. Donut's five new Legendary Diva spells are cataloged: *Standing Ovation*, *Entourage*, *Encore*, an offensive psionic song, an ice song, and a bonus spell, *[Laundry Day](../spells/laundry-day.md)*, that strips armor off a target — none of them useful yet, since her singing can't hold pitch. With the timer on the Hunting Grounds' 30-hour hunter grace period running down, Carl executes **Predator**, an invisibility-and-infrared recon move, and has Bomo cast the stairwell-teleport spell to jump himself and Donut directly into the hunter capital of Zockau before the hunters are even released.

**Chapter 5 — The Zockau Raid**{: #chapter-5 .chapter-title }

Carl's first strike against the hunters is a bloodbath. He mines the alleys around Zockau's Desperado Club entrance with barrel bombs, then uses the **Ring of Divine Suffering** — looted earlier, worn for the first time — to mark a drunk hunter, Chin'Dua, as living bomb-bait before beating him to death by hand. Spotting Vrah's flying sister **Xindy** overhead, he activates his toe ring's **Super Spreader** ability to copy his own Left to Fester mark onto her from a distance, then finishes her with a five-missile homing salvo that blows her out of the sky. The barrel bombs detonate, leveling a city block and trapping half the Desperado Club's occupants inside. Fleeing on foot, Carl and Donut are hit by a pursuing archer-hunter's crossbow bolts — one buries itself in his leg, another in his shoulder, both carrying a bleeding **Blood Trail** debuff. Carl collapses at the city border from blood loss and a Woozy-induced coma, saved only when Miriam Dom, Prepotente, and Bianca happen across them on the road outside.

**Chapter 6 — Miriam and Bianca**{: #chapter-6 .chapter-title }

Carl wakes in the back of Miriam Dom's cart, badly wounded but alive, having racked up 34 confirmed hunter kills from the raid. He meets **Bianca Del Ciao**, [Miriam Dom](../characters/miriam-dom.md)'s Hellspawn familiar — a formerly ordinary, formerly-asshole pet goat reborn as a horned, flame-wreathed demon after her soul was corrupted despite a protection spell — and learns only that Miriam herself is now a vampire, without yet learning how. Carl accidentally insults Prepotente (calling him by his hated nickname, "Pony," then telling him to "suck my dick"), who nearly attacks him in a rage before Miriam scolds them both into an awkward, forced hug.

**Chapter 7 — The Belching Termite**{: #chapter-7 .chapter-title }

Carl and Donut reach a bombed-out bugbear settlement and take refuge in a saferoom disguised as a Whataburger. Mordecai, furious over the reckless Zockau raid, tries to have "the talk" with Donut, who preempts him with a rehearsed speech: yes, Carl was reckless, no, she's not leaving him, and no, they are not doing a will-they-won't-they storyline on top of Katia's. Katia, Louis, Firas, and Britney are all gathered in the space; Mordecai instructs the group to open their boxes and settle in for the recap before the chapter ends.

**Chapter 8 — The Butcher's Masquerade, Announced**{: #chapter-8 .chapter-title }

Opening a Gold Junkie's Box from his first ring-kill lands Carl a second, much larger tattoo on his elbow — a sigil granting access to something called the **Guild of Suffering**, which Mordecai identifies as an obscure cult built around a "memorial crystal" and a demigod called the Night Wyrm, tied to anyone who uses a divine suffering artifact. Carl's 34 Hunter-Killer boxes turn out to contain severed hunter hands and escalating piles of gold (595,000 total) — revolting to Donut, who gets a single hand and a description explaining these are tickets to a prize counter at an end-of-floor event called **the Butcher's Masquerade**. That night's system message announces private crawler chats are no longer broadcast live (a lawsuit win Quasar had warned Carl not to get his hopes up about, since the hunters'-outside-intel problem is a separate, much shakier case), formally launches the guild system (parties capped at 30, guild charters starting at 500,000 gold), and reveals the Masquerade itself: an invite-only, top-50 party with a pet beauty contest, talent show, and hunter-trade prize booth. Donut immediately begins plotting Mongo's beauty-pageant campaign. The floor's top-10 list shows Carl at #1 with a bounty of 1,000,000 gold.

**Chapter 9 — Bombs for the Bridges**{: #chapter-9 .chapter-title }

With the hunters about to be released, Carl and Katia study the map: the Hunting Grounds spans roughly 100,000 square kilometers, bisected by a single winding river. Carl orders every bridge but one destroyed to funnel hunters into predictable chokepoints. Back at the sapper's table, he builds his largest bomb yet — a five-foot, egg-shaped thermobaric device nicknamed **"Just Wait Until Your Daddy Gets Home"** — designed for Bomo to wheel out as a suicide-and-respawn attack, over Donut's objections. Mordecai supplies **Nitro Sludge**, an oxygen-amplifying byproduct of his shield-potion research, as the payload.

**Chapter 10 — Point Mongo**{: #chapter-10 .chapter-title }

Carl and Donut cross into a large Ursine settlement and are turned away at the gate by a "no dinosaurs" town rule expressly written to keep Mongo out (along with bans on magic, dancing, singing, and naiads). Carl bribes the elderly mayor, Elmer, into stepping close enough to take payment, and Donut shoves him down the riverbank slope with *Astral Paw*; he dies from the fall itself, and a passing naiad drags his corpse into the water before they can loot it. The town falls to Donut, who renames it **Point Mongo**. Just before the hunter release deadline hits, a note arrives pinned to the personal-space door: **Vrah**, having somehow learned Carl's exact location despite the communication bans, promises a slow, ritual death in retaliation for her sister Xindy. Carl writes back "Come and get it, bitch" as the system announces the hunters are loose.

**Chapter 11 — Mongo's People**{: #chapter-11 .chapter-title }

Donut's new mayoral collar charm grants her the **Snitch** skill (summons town guards) alongside stat buffs, and she uses her new authority to strip Point Mongo of its more puritanical ordinances over the town cleric's objections — though Carl talks her out of also scrapping the "bugbears must bathe in the river" rule once he realizes it's a naiad death trap. A trap Carl left on the road nets a hunter kill and a Gold Trapmaster box (a pack of alarm-triggering **Fail Safe** mods). While arguing with the cleric about reinstating anti-dinosaur ordinances, a new quest, **The Recital**, appears — something is drawing escalating dinosaur attacks to Point Mongo — and Mongo suddenly bolts from town, drawn by instinct, straight into a semicircle of forty wild velociraptors led by the pack matriarch **Kiwi**. Carl and Donut nearly trigger a massacre trying to pull him free, until it becomes clear the standoff is a courtship ritual, not an attack — Mongo and Kiwi mate in front of the horrified party.

**Chapter 12 — No Matter What**{: #chapter-12 .chapter-title }

Back in town, Donut suffers a genuine panic attack after nearly losing Mongo, blurting out that she's terrified of being abandoned again (as Katia left, and as Carl keeps risking his life) before shutting down mid-confession about "the ninth floor." Zev interrupts with pointed small talk — an unsubtle warning to drop the subject before their scheduled visit to Odette later that day. Carl finally tells Donut directly, for the first time, that he will never leave her behind, no matter what, sidestepping for now the real problem underneath: the Crown of the Sepsis Whore curse still binding her to the Blood Sultanate's royal line on the ninth floor. The moment is interrupted by Mongo's triumphant, exhausted return from the jungle.

**Chapter 13 — Bravvo in the Tree**{: #chapter-13 .chapter-title }

Elite **[Signet](../characters/tsarina-signet.md)** reappears at Point Mongo's bridge with a new entourage: the ogre Areson the Wise and six were-creature warriors riding a giant caterpillar. They've found a hunter, the yenk **Bravvo**, immobilized in a tree after Point Mongo's guards shot him out of the sky, and offer him to Carl to finish off as a professional courtesy — the first hint of the "hunting rights" turf war developing between rival factions over Carl and Donut.

**Chapter 14 — The Were-Castors**{: #chapter-14 .chapter-title }

Carl marks and kills Bravvo with the Ring of Divine Suffering, leveling it up. Signet introduces her six warriors — **were-castors** (transformed beaver-people), survivors of a genocide by then-Princess Imogen decades before Scolopendra's attack — and their caterpillar guardian, Nadine, a transfigured schoolteacher. Signet's own backstory unfolds: a naiad princess's bastard daughter by the high elf king, exiled and cursed so water refuses to touch her, forced into circus life after her mother's murder in a naiad coup.

**Chapter 15 — Vengeance of the Daughter**{: #chapter-15 .chapter-title }

Areson delivers another hunter kill and a hand-drawn map, scrawled on the back of a kids' diner menu, revealing the hunter factions have divided the Hunting Grounds into claimed territories and drawn up a target list — Carl and Donut are double-claimed by both the Dark Hive and the Skull Empire, while the Dream has fixated obsessively on Louis for stealing a photo of their leader's mother to have Juice Box impersonate. Signet formally recruits Carl and Donut into her storyline, **The Vengeance of the Daughter**: help her retake her mother's naiad throne from the usurping Confederacy at their stronghold, Fort Freedom.

**Chapter 16 — Clint and Holger**{: #chapter-16 .chapter-title }

Two were-castors, bald Clint and mulleted Holger, escort Carl and Donut back toward Point Mongo, bickering and brawling constantly despite genuine affection, then fighting off a night-weasel pack with terrifying, teeth-and-claw efficiency once real danger arrives. Carl receives his third and final sponsor, **the Apothecary**, shared suspiciously with most of the current top-10 crawlers — a company Carl suspects is a front for Krakaren's collective-mind faction.

**Chapter 17 — Samantha's Cousin**{: #chapter-17 .chapter-title }

Back at base, **Samantha** throws a screaming fit on learning Donut and Carl are working with Signet, whom she claims (via a tangled royal lineage) is her third cousin and thus a possible source of a new physical body. Carl and Donut are then yanked to an orbital security checkpoint en route to Odette's show, tumbling in near-zero gravity as Earth spins beneath them, before a pair of shade-gnoll mercenaries pat them down and clear them for transfer.

**Chapter 18 — Beatrice Is Alive**{: #chapter-18 .chapter-title }

In Odette's production trailer, Odette drops the floor's first real bombshell: **[Beatrice](../characters/beatrice.md)** survived the initial collection, was rescued by Odette from black-market bounty hunters intent on selling her to Borant as a repurposed country boss, and has been alive and hidden off-world this whole time. Odette wants to bring her onto the show as a ratings coup before a rival program can leak the story first. Carl is furious, suspecting Odette engineered the whole situation; Donut, shaking, insists on going through with the reveal on her own terms rather than let Carl shoulder it alone.

**Chapter 19 — All Eyes on Bea**{: #chapter-19 .chapter-title }

Beatrice appears on a pre-taped segment of Odette's show, thin, drugged, and hollow-eyed. Donut plays it cold through most of the segment before finally breaking character to deliver a devastating, unscripted monologue: she loved Bea unconditionally and was going to be given away anyway, and Bea doesn't deserve the closure of explaining herself. Carl backs her up and ends the reunion outright. Moments later, a system-wide broadcast announces the Valtay Corporation has forcibly acquired 51% of the Borant Corporation from the kua-tin government — a political earthquake Donut appears to have had a hand in, hinting at a secret alliance with Zev and a resistance cell.

**Chapter 20 — Viva la Revolución**{: #chapter-20 .chapter-title }

Aftermath of the Valtay takeover: Odette privately warns, via her assistant Lexis, that the incoming ownership will want to "wipe out and start fresh" with a top-10 of their own choosing — meaning Queen Imogen, the still-unrevealed Floor 6 country boss, is being positioned as a season-ending trap, and Carl and Donut should go down the stairs early rather than attend her looming party. Donut casually reveals she's been smuggling Valtay secret agents to Zev's resistance cell, the **Borant Emancipation Front**, without realizing the political fallout, leaving Carl (and Lexis) speechless.

**Chapter 21 — CrawlCon Fan Box**{: #chapter-21 .chapter-title }

The recap episode shows Vrah decapitating crawlers mid-hunt and mounting their heads, plus glimpses of Lucia Mar, Eva, and the Meadow Lark splinter team pursuing separate storylines across the floor. A fan box gives Carl a CrawlCon pass for three mandatory virtual appearances (an art-contest judging panel, a "Crawling Through the Ages" panel, and an autograph session) without Donut, sparking her indignation. The guild system launches properly; Katia's team forms **Safehome Yolanda** under guildmaster Imani. As the party discusses upgrades, a real attack begins: unseen elven artillery starts shelling Point Mongo with poison-gas boulders.

**Chapter 22 — The Elven Catapult**{: #chapter-22 .chapter-title }

Under bombardment from a hidden Dream-faction trebuchet, Carl improvises a guided missile out of **Samantha**, duct-taping seeking rockets to her head and lobbing her toward the artillery crew with a Major Charlie intelligence potion boosting his *Ping* range. The strike kills one elf outright, and a recall on Samantha's xistera drags a second, unconscious survivor back with her, clamped onto her teeth. Carl marks and interrogates the survivor, **Akland**, a self-described "accountant" drafted into the Dream's hunt for Louis, before deciding to kill him with the Ring of Divine Suffering regardless.

**Chapter 23 — The Bomb Patch**{: #chapter-23 .chapter-title }

Killing Akland levels the Ring of Divine Suffering up to two stat points per kill and earns Donut a looted **Fireball** bracelet, which she immediately equips and threatens to misuse indoors. Aftermath of the catapult fight: Samantha, now covered in gore and freely rolling around like a feral pinball, unsettles even Donut with how quickly her strength is growing beyond what a "harmless" withering spirit should have. Carl reflects on the Vengeance of the Daughter storyline's likely tragic ending and Herot's cookbook advice about surrounding himself with competent allies rather than dead weight.

**Chapter 24 — The Rock Chucker**{: #chapter-24 .chapter-title }

Doubling back to loot the first Dream elf's corpse — killed outright by Samantha's opening missile strike, and carrying a doctored photo of Louis — and to claim the abandoned trebuchet itself, Carl discovers the "Venomous Elven Rock Chucker" — a lightweight, poison-cloud-imbuing siege weapon built from magically-conjured bamboo and suspiciously high-tech metal plates, evidence the hunters are cheating with off-world tech disguised as enchantment. He claims it as siege equipment for later use. Signet's assault team gathers for the Fort Freedom raid; Carl reflects on the political dominoes still in motion — Sensation Entertainment's contested ninth-floor extension, and the growing certainty that the Vengeance storyline is building toward Signet's own death.

**Chapter 25 — The Sacrifice**{: #chapter-25 .chapter-title }

Elite Areson delivers a captured Skull Empire orc, "Future Huntress," for Signet's pre-battle blood sacrifice. Just before Carl marks her, word arrives that a hunter has claimed a top-10 bounty, killing Bogdon Ro along with archer Langley and the whole team who'd cornered him (later revealed to be **Vrah**'s doing). Grief-stricken, Carl kills the sacrifice anyway, reasoning she never should have come. Signet's squad — paper-cutout tattoo monsters brought fully to life — wades into the naiad-held river toward Fort Freedom while Carl and Donut's ground team, plus Samantha thrown in as a distraction, hold the shore.

**Chapter 26 — Into Fort Freedom**{: #chapter-26 .chapter-title }

Samantha splashes into the water bearing a rigged alarm trap, triggers the castle's defenses on cue, and successfully draws the confederate guards toward the front gate while Signet's amphibious squad slips in from the rear. The castle assault initially goes smoothly, but a sudden lightning bolt and a wave of naiad and paper-warrior corpses floating to the surface signal that something has gone badly wrong deep inside.

**Chapter 27 — Claude Sludgington the Fourth**{: #chapter-27 .chapter-title }

Investigating the chaos, Carl accidentally teleports the fight's true obstacle — **[Claude Sludgington the Fourth](../bosses/claude-sludgington.md)**, a Level 65 Eryops Borough Boss who's squatted in the throne room for years — directly onto the shore via Samantha, who'd bitten into the amphibian mid-rescue. The lizard-frog boss immediately kills Miss Nadine the caterpillar guardian with a lightning bolt aimed at her as she attacks it, narrowly missing Carl in the process. In the ensuing brawl, Carl's *Protective Shell*, cast too close, accidentally launches Claude straight into the sky instead of flipping him prone; Donut's first-ever **Fireball** spell burns a hole clean through his exposed belly as he falls back to earth, pinning him against the trees. Carl finishes the job by diving bare-fisted through the wound with Mongo, punching straight through the creature's body and out the other side as it explodes in gore around them. The fight costs Miss Nadine and, offscreen, the were-castor Clint, both killed in the chaos before Claude even fully manifested.

**Chapter 28 — The Blood and Ink Tattoo**{: #chapter-28 .chapter-title }

With Claude dead and the castle secured, Signet declares Fort Freedom liberated and the naiad Confederacy defeated, completing **Vengeance of the Daughter, Part One**. Part Two arrives immediately: kill Signet's half-sister, Queen Imogen, at the still-unnamed high elf party everyone already knows is coming. The camp mourns Miss Nadine and Clint in stunned silence as the were-castors search fruitlessly for Clint's body.

**Chapter 29 — Miss Nadine's Memory**{: #chapter-29 .chapter-title }

Signet's elder tortoise companion, Edgar, performs a ritual "blood and ink" tattooing on Signet's skin using Miss Nadine's remains, explaining that her fallen companions live on as sentient "blood and ink elementals" fused into her tattoos rather than true resurrections. The finished tattoo, unexpectedly, brings Clint's memory along with it — the caterpillar teacher had loved him enough that his form survives inside her portrait even without a body to draw from, giving the were-castors a bittersweet keepsake of both losses at once.

**Chapter 30 — The Art Contest**{: #chapter-30 .chapter-title }

Carl's first mandatory CrawlCon appearance is judging a children's art contest, seated alongside a jaded club designer, Hurk, and a barely-conscious camel playwright, G'valt. He fields a stream of unsettling submissions — including a depiction of his own gruesome hypothetical death and a trio of eerily red-eyed goat children from the Plenty faith — and strikes a bargain with Hurk to secure a good score for the head of security's granddaughter in exchange for a plug of the Desperado Club's interior design.

**Chapter 31 — Judging Little Monsters**{: #chapter-31 .chapter-title }

The contest wraps with word that the reigning champion — a slug-child whose portrait of Donut usually wins and took the previous day's round — is expected to riot when it loses this round. Hurk warns Carl, half-joking, half-not, to "be careful" before revealing the real headline event: Carl's upcoming panel will be moderated by the mother of the hunter he just killed, and fandom is buzzing over how badly it's expected to go.

**Chapter 32 — The Ultimate Training Room**{: #chapter-32 .chapter-title }

Zev debriefs Carl on the panel ahead, revealing Circe is a Dark Hive businesswoman rather than actual mantis royalty. Given a four-hour gap before the panel, Carl exploits the production facility's tier-four training room — far beyond anything available in the dungeon this early — to fast-track upgrades to his sapper's table and Powerful Strike skill, and briefly meets an NPC engineer, Dr. Ratchet, setting up his own demonstration table before being ushered toward the stage.

**Chapter 33 — Circe Took's Panel**{: #chapter-33 .chapter-title }

The "Crawling Through the Ages" panel goes exactly as badly as promised: Circe attacks the (illusory) table in front of Carl with her bladed arm, delivers a droning lecture nobody wants, and is repeatedly baited by Carl until, drugged without his knowledge, he hijacks the entire panel to crowdsource tactics against Vrah and the Dark Hive in front of a live galactic audience. A mysterious cloaked woman — a representative of Carl's new sponsor, the Apothecary — publicly intervenes to keep the mic on him before departing, further muddying who exactly is pulling strings on his behalf. Once the panel ends and the studio empties out, Carl finds Dr. Ratchet's level-5 Automaton crafting table and instruction manual left behind on the stage and claims both for himself.

**Chapter 34 — Crowdsourcing a Mantis Kill**{: #chapter-34 .chapter-title }

The panel audience volunteers a wealth of mantis-killing intelligence — vulnerable necks, antidote rings, the fact loud sounds hurt them, and the existence of the pacifist-but-fracturing Nebular Sin Patrol hunting faction, who've begun poaching the Dark Hive's claimed targets out of ancient rivalry. **Chapter 74 — Cage Fight Extreme**{: #chapter-74 .chapter-title }

With the castle gone, everyone remaining — crawlers, hunters, Queen Imogen, and the newly-exposed, still-mid-birth **Diwata** — is rebranded by the AI as a four-way **boss battle royale**, plummeting together into the castle's exposed, mantis-choked basement pit as "Thunderstruck" blares. Donut's maxed *Laundry Day* spell strips Diwata's divine armor away, letting Circe Took's own newborn mantis children tear their mother apart in a berserk feeding frenzy. Imogen falls to a coordinated, beat-synced barrage of blunt-force attacks, finished off by Prepotente smashing a potion directly into her face.

**Chapter 75 — We Are Goddamned Alive**{: #chapter-75 .chapter-title }

The cost of the battle is brutal: **Firas**, **Gwen**, **Horton**, and both **Popov brothers** are killed outright (though the Popovs' nodling deaths split them into two rescued, ineligible infant crawlers who are immediately pulled from the dungeon), Tran loses both legs, and Britney's face is permanently scarred saving the group with her butterfly jacket. Katia finds and finishes off a buried, barely-alive Eva off-page, earning a second player-killer skull. In the shaky aftermath, Carl talks Donut through her guilt over not grieving hard enough, the group claims two open Faction Wars slots for **The Princess Posse** (Carl and Donut's own newly-sponsored team) and Princess D'Nadia's Prism Kingdom, and Carl uses his last moments on air to publicly shame the other faction-wars sponsors into a vote on removing the "safety protections" that keep non-crawler teams from real danger.

**Epilogue — The Great Race**{: #epilogue .chapter-title }

Floor 6 collapses into Floor 7, "The Great Race," dropping every crawler into an individual glass starting pod arranged in a spiraling stack of stairwell portals. **Prepotente**, having pieced the floor's true nature together from his late mother's item-lore boots and an overheard aside on a CrawlCon panel, deliberately shatters his own pod's reinforced glass with a hammer-and-rod combo — collapsing the entire level's Prince Rupert's Drop-style maze structure before it can properly begin, over the AI's own panicked, in-voice objections. Squads are capped at five and locked for the floor. Given ninety seconds to choose a starting location on a spinning globe of Earth, Donut — squad leader by default — picks the Caribbean instead of the intended Bahamas, to Carl's exasperation. Elsewhere, a shaken Katia hunts down and personally kills Eva, only for Eva's dying hand to trigger a **Crown of the Sepsis Whore** hidden on her own armor — the same crown Donut lost back on Floor 3 — which binds itself to Katia's bare head instead (the protective hat that would have stopped it is the one she'd handed off to Carl mid-battle), meaning only one of them will now be allowed to leave the ninth floor.
