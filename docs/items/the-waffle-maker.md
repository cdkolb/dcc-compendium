# The Waffle Maker and No Disassemble

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Maker:** Carl
    - **The Waffle Maker:** A **thermobaric** bomb the size of a thermos. Incinerates everything
      within **100 metres**. The direct descendant of Book 5's "Just Wait Until Your Daddy Gets Home"
    - **No Disassemble:** The dungeon's **EMP equivalent** — square, shield-and-tech-busting, with a
      pulse radius considerably wider than its blast
    - **Delivery:** Both are the standard payload of
      [Party Planner](party-planner.md), which adds **+50% yield**
    - **Body count:** Between them they take the Khepri temple guard, the Reaver honeycomb castle's
      robot force, and **Warlord Fang**

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **Neither device has a system description block.** Both are player-built, and everything below is
    Carl's narration.

## The Waffle Maker

Carl's lineage of increasingly portable atrocities reaches its most compact form here:

> The bomb was something I'd been tinkering with for a while now, and it was currently the
> second-largest explosive I had in my inventory. **The first version of this thing had been called
> "Just Wait Until Your Daddy Gets Home," and we'd used it on the sixth floor.**
>
> This version packed about the same punch, minus the addition of *Fear*. **The tradeoff was this
> version was self-contained in a much smaller device. The first had been so big it had to have
> wheels. This one was the size of a thermos.** The result was **a thermobaric explosion that would
> absolutely incinerate anything within 100 meters.** Louis currently had fifty of these things on
> Party Planner. **The only reason he didn't have more was because the explosive dust was a pain to
> make.**
>
> **The AI had helpfully called the bomb The Waffle Maker.**

Same yield, one wheeled cart's worth of hardware compressed into something that fits in a jacket
pocket. The trade was dropping the *Fear* component. The binding constraint on production is the
explosive dust, not the casing.

Physically it is round and tube-shaped, and it makes a heavier clunk than a No Disassemble when it
drops.

**First use, chapter 54:** detonated inside Khepri's temple against three of the god's guards. The
blast also flattens Carl into "*a boxer-clad amalgam of flesh and hair*," which is not a figure of
speech — he survives it on invulnerability and healing, not on distance.

## No Disassemble

Square, and built for a completely different job: killing electronics and shields rather than people.

> **The No Disassembles were basically the dungeon's equivalent of an EMP. They exploded, and you
> couldn't just drop one at your feet, but the real damage came from their pulse, which had a much
> wider range than the explosive itself.**

Which is the tactical distinction between the two weapons in one line. The Waffle Maker's radius is
its blast. The No Disassemble's radius is its **pulse**, and the pulse is much bigger than the bang.

Colonel Boomer's reason for pushing the disastrous chapter-66 bomb run in the first place was the
absence of these: "*We don't have No Disassemble missiles to kill their electronics.*"

## The Reaver Honeycomb Castle

This is the payoff, and it takes fourteen chapters to arrive.

**Chapter 66.** [Party Planner](party-planner.md) is torn in half by a gravity-magic dragon. Louis
dumps his bombs unarmed before he goes down and crash-lands on the flat roof of the Reaver honeycomb
castle.

**Chapter 67.** He collects what he can from the roof — "*a handful of unexploded No Disassembles and
Waffle Makers into his inventory*" — and Carl notes the problem: "*He had no way of setting them off
without blowing himself up. They had to be unstable as fuck. Louis did not have invulnerability.*"

**Chapter 68.** Louis, trapped inside the castle and being shot at by Reaver sentry dogs: "*They have
these little dog robot things that shoot darts… I have some No Disassemble robot killers, but the
explosions are too big. I can't do it where I'm stuck.*"

**Chapter 80.** Carl solves it from outside. Louis still has "*a ton of no disassembles and waffle
makers*" in his inventory. Carl tells Juice Box: "*Louis has No Disassembles in his inventory. Drop
them and leave! They'll kill the robots!*" — and then opens his own **crafting menu**, finds the No
Disassembles page, sorts by unexploded, and **triggers them remotely.** He built them, so he can set
them off from anywhere.

The pulse wipes the Reaver robot force. The collateral is instructive:

- **Jamal's mechanical legs** are destroyed — they were unshielded and sat on the edge of the pulse
  radius
- **Louis's replacement heart and lungs are shielded**, and survive. Carl's note afterward: "*if we
  figure this out and he has to keep those organs, we can't use those bombs anywhere near him ever
  again*"
- The transport is wrecked by a separate **Mini Tactical Nuke**, not by the EMP

**Warlord Fang** dies in this sequence. He survived Donut's *[War Crime](../spells/war-crime.md)* in
chapter 76 specifically because he is biological material inside a robot; the No Disassembles are the
thing that finally reaches him.

## The "Shit Just Hit" Folder

Chapter 71 contains the clearest single illustration of Carl's late-book inventory, dumped all at once
toward the Piccadilly Theater:

> approximately **20 waffle makers**, **two no disassembles**, a pair of the anti-personnel **cheese
> cutters**, approximately **500 impact hob-lobbers**, and more sticks of dynamite than I could count

He keeps them pre-sorted into a folder named for the circumstance in which he would need all of them
simultaneously.

## Scaling

Neither device has a level. The relevant multipliers are:

- **[Party Planner](party-planner.md)'s +50% yield** on anything dropped from its bomb bay
- **Production limits** — the Waffle Maker's explosive dust is the bottleneck, not the design
- **Remote detonation via the crafting menu**, available to Carl as the builder, for any unexploded
  unit anywhere in the world
