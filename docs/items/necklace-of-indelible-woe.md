# Necklace of Indelible Woe

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Looted from:** **King Rust**, chapter 39, after Rosetta lassos and beheads him mid-parley
    - **Effect:** Swaps one of the wearer's **own** spells for one of a target's. Kill the target and
      you get one of their **five highest-level** spells
    - **Restriction:** Works **only on crawlers** (and on NPCs like Tipid)
    - **Status:** **Informally banned** among Faction Wars combatants
    - **Used in Book 7:** **Never.** It sits in Carl's inventory for the whole book

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **No system description block exists for this item in Book 7.** Everything known comes from
    Tipid's chapter-32 explanation, quoted below.

## What It Does

Tipid explains it while working out how King Rust, at level 78, knows dozens of spells with several
at level 11 to 15 — including a level-15 *Protective Shell*, *Tripper* and *Puddle Jumper*:

> "That's easy," Tipid said. "He likely has a **ring of Divine Suffering** for the stats—and the
> levels that come with charging the ring—and a **necklace of Indelible Woe** for the spells."
>
> "Indelible Woe?" I asked. "Is that like the tattoo thing Rosetta is trying to make us do?"
>
> "Similar. Indelible Woe is in the same collection as the Divine Suffering ring. **It trades out a
> random spell of your own for a spell of a target's. If you kill the target, it'll be one of their
> five highest-level spells.** Like the Divine Suffering ring, **it only works on crawlers.** And
> people like me. **It's not all that useful unless you're a complete psychopath with a bunch of spells
> you can afford to lose.** The necklace was popular a long time ago, but **they all agreed to stop
> using it because combatants were farming crawlers to power it up, and it was a little... messy.**"

Two things are established there.

**It is a trade, not a theft.** Every use costs the wearer a spell of their own, chosen at random. It
is only worth owning if you are carrying spells you are willing to lose sight-unseen, which is a
narrow and unpleasant profile of person. Tipid's word for it is "psychopath."

**It is informally banned.** Not by rule — by agreement. Faction Wars combatants stopped using it
because the optimal strategy was farming crawlers for their best spells, repeatedly, which made the
games "a little... messy." It is one of very few things in the series banned by the *participants*
rather than the system, which says something about both.

## The Contrast With Tattoos

Tipid draws the comparison himself, immediately after, and it is the cleanest statement of why the
[toraline tattoo system](toraline-tattoo-transfer.md) was worth building:

> "Rosetta's tattoos will help you share skills and spells **without actually killing anyone.** It
> just needs to brew for a little longer."

Same outcome — moving an ability from one person to another — with the kill requirement and the
random self-cost removed. The tattoos are the humane version of this necklace, and the book puts them
side by side on purpose.

## Why It Is Never Used

Carl acquires it by accident. Rosetta kills King Rust in chapter 38, Carl pulls "*the entirety of King
Rust's heavy, leaking corpse*" into his inventory, and finds he now owns a second Ring of Divine
Suffering and this.

His verdict, in chapter 40:

> **Donut: WHAT DID YOU DO WITH KING RUST'S RING AND THE NECKLACE?**
>
> **Carl: Don't worry. I can only wear one Ring of Divine Suffering at a time. I checked. Besides, it
> turns out two rings can't mark the same person on this floor anyway. And we don't need that
> necklace.**

The second Ring of Divine Suffering goes to **Li Na** — Donut privately begs Carl to give it to her.
Li Na asks about the necklace too, and turns it down for a specific and excellent reason:

> Li Na had also asked about the Necklace of Indelible Woe but ultimately decided she didn't want it,
> **especially now that she had several level-15 dreads that she could possibly lose if she used the
> necklace.** **It remained in my inventory for now.**

Which is the trade-off arriving exactly as Tipid described it. Li Na has just spent an entire assault
grinding four **[Dreads](../spells/li-nas-dreads.md)** to level 15. The necklace would put every one of
them into a lottery. Nobody with anything worth keeping can afford to wear it.

It stays in Carl's inventory, unused, through the end of the book.

## Scaling

No level. The only stated variable is the **quality of the take**: a swap against a living target
yields a random spell, while **killing** the target yields one of their five highest-level spells. The
wearer's loss is random in either case.
