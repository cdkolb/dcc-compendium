# The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Slot:** Not equippable — a readable/writable book kept in inventory
    - **Obtained:** A prize-carousel "joke" reward on Floor 3, widely assumed by the party to be worthless
    - **Format:** A living, crowd-sourced field manual, edited and annotated by a chain of prior crawlers going back at least 24 editions

## What It Is

Far from a joke prize, the Cookbook turns out to be Carl's single most valuable item of Book 3: a
running compendium of survival knowledge — power-supply mechanics, portal theory, deity-summoning
rules, crafting recipes, monster lore — left behind by 24 previous crawlers who owned it before
him, each annotating and correcting the last. Carl becomes its 25th author. He also discovers it
comes with a hidden, mentally-accessed "scratchpad" feature that lets him add his own notes
without physically opening the book, which he uses constantly to record observations he then
passes off to Mordecai as his own deductions.

## Effect

No combat stats or equip slot — its value is purely informational. Passages quoted from prior
editions (credited to named past crawlers like Coolie, Forkith, Tin, Milk, Azin, and Drakea) cover
topics as varied as indestructible "quest-critical" NPCs, portal behavior, and the full rules
behind deity summoning (see [Enchanted War Gauntlet of the Exalted Grull](war-gauntlet-of-the-exalted-grull.md)
for how Carl applies that last one).

## Scaling

Not applicable — the book doesn't scale with a stat or skill; its usefulness scales with how
much Carl reads and adds to it over time.

## Role in the Story

The Cookbook becomes Carl's primary information advantage for the rest of Floor 4, repeatedly
supplying him with knowledge (power cell mechanics, portal classification, deity lore) well ahead
of what Mordecai or anyone else on the floor has access to — enough that
[Elle McGibbons](../characters/elle-mcgibbons.md) tells him: "Sometimes I think you're cheating,
Carl. How do you know all this stuff? It's like you have one of those teacher's editions with all
the answers in the back." By the end of the floor, Carl writes his own entry — the book's 25th —
closing not with a plan to free NPCs, but with a darker escalation: killing mobs and NPCs is
framed as mercy ("actually the best thing we can do for them"), but Carl vows that the 24
crawlers before him all failed to go further than talk — he commits to actively fighting back
against the system itself ("They will not break me... I will break them all"), without yet
knowing how.

## Book 5: New Marginalia and a 25th-Edition Log

By Floor 6, the Cookbook is shown being actively annotated by dozens of past crawlers, cited by
edition number (e.g. "Crawler Forkith, 20th Edition," "Crawler Everly, 5th Edition") — reinforcing
its nature as a living, multi-author artifact rather than a static reference. Several new
marginal notes recur across the book, quoted directly:

- **Herot (16th Edition)** — contributes theory on manipulating NPC belief systems ("Show them the
  lie... find the pebble and place it on the path") and on castle typology, both of which Carl
  applies directly against Signet and while planning the Butcher's Masquerade infiltration
- **Everly (5th Edition)** — a multi-part, increasingly bitter entry describing a sponsor who
  seemed helpful before deliberately steering her toward a broadcast-friendly death; Drakea's
  22nd-edition follow-up confirms it was her final entry
- **Ikicha (11th Edition)**, **Milk (6th Edition)**, **Volteeg (7th Edition)**, **Rosetta (9th
  Edition)**, and **Allister (13th Edition)** — shorter entries on love, loss, vampires, and
  country-boss treasure maps, several marked by Drakea as their authors' final contributions
- **Drakea (22nd Edition)** — recurs constantly as the book's most active recent annotator,
  bridging older entries with fresh Floor 6 observations (elite/hunter dynamics, faction
  insurance schemes) and consistently urging future authors to document exactly how they die

Still credited as the book's 25th author (a status he earned back in Book 3), Carl opens a new,
dated entry midway through Book 5 that documents, in real time, the entire Butcher's Masquerade
plan — page by page, with running annotations signed by Katia, Imani, Elle, Donut, Florin, Louis,
Firas, Li Na, Bautista, and Chris — explicitly framed (per Drakea's earlier advice) as a record in
case the plan gets them all killed.

## Known Authors & Their Editions

Books 6 and 7 confirm the book's full authorship model. Every marginal note is signed by
"Crawler [Name], [Ordinal] Edition," and Book 7 goes a step further: nine of its part-openers are
**interludes** — short chapters narrated in the third person, each from the point of view of one
cookbook author, appearing at the moment they wrote (or were about to write) their entry. The
table below lists every author confirmed in the text, in edition-number order, with what they
personally contributed — a quoted note, a theory later used in the main plot, or a summary of
their interlude — rather than a full biography. Where a character already has a full page or a
supporting-cast row, the "Who" column links there instead of repeating it.

Two editions before Carl's own are never named on the page (**1st** and, so far, **23rd** and
**24th**) — the book's status quote to Carl calls it "at least 24 editions," but only 21 distinct
predecessors (2nd–22nd) are ever credited by name. Porthus's own note in Book 4 implies there was
no author before him at all: he calls himself "the first to receive these words, and now, as the
author of the second edition," suggesting the book started as a bare, uncredited set of recipes
(an unofficial "1st edition") before he became its first actual contributor.

## Future Enhancements

Confirmed by later books: Book 6 establishes the interlude-free "Crawler [Name], [Ordinal]
Edition" marginal-note format as an ongoing device through Floor 8, and Book 7 adds the
first-person interlude structure for nine of its authors (see table above). Book 8 confirms Herot
(16th edition) survived and is running the Pineapple Cabaret. Not yet confirmed: any further
detail on the 1st, 23rd, or 24th editions, or whether the book itself (as opposed to Carl's
scratchpad) appears again after Book 7.
