# Enchanted Crown of the Sepsis Whore

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Princess Donut
    - **Slot:** Head
    - **Rarity:** Legendary, *Fleeting*, and *Unique*
    - **Obtained:** Donut's very first Legendary Quadruped Box (awarded for being the first cat to enter the dungeon)

## Effect

**+5 Intelligence.** **+5 to the "Good First Impression" skill.** All of the wearer's attacks,
including magic, gain a 15% chance to inflict the **Sepsis** debuff.

## Scaling

No skill-level scaling described in Book 1 — the crown's bonuses (Intelligence, Good First
Impression, the 15% Sepsis proc) are all presented as flat, one-time enchantments rather than
something that grows with a skill level. The Sepsis debuff itself is shown in action (a Scatterer
hit with it wanders off and eventually collapses), but no stronger/weaker variant of the debuff is
ever described.

## The Catch

The crown is tagged **Fleeting** — it crumbles to dust the instant it's removed, at which point a
new one is generated somewhere else in the dungeon for another crawler to find. Its bigger
consequence is structural to the whole series' faction politics: putting it on **permanently**
enrolls the wearer in the royal line of succession for the **Blood Sultanate**, one of the
factions active on Floor 9. Removing the crown afterward does **not** undo this status. Members of
the Blood Sultanate's royal line are required to kill the reigning Sultan and every other member
of the royal family before they're permitted to descend past Floor 9.

Donut puts it on within seconds of receiving it — before Mordecai can finish reading her the
warning — because it's purple, her favorite color.

## Why It Matters

This is one of the first hard plot commitments made in the series: Donut is now locked into a
Floor 9 storyline regardless of anything else that happens between now and then. Prince Maestro
later confirms, mockingly, that the Blood Sultanate is a perennial underdog faction ("almost
always the first of the nine to get eliminated") in the recurring "Faction Wars" — implying Carl
and Donut are headed into one of the more dangerous political situations in the dungeon, sight
unseen.

## Book 5: The Crown Reforms and Finds Katia

Book 5 confirms the crown itself regenerated somewhere in the dungeon after Donut's copy
disintegrated in Book 2 (per the Legendary Boxes' single-owner mechanic) and, at some unspecified
point, ended up in the possession of **[Eva Sigrid](../characters/eva-sigrid.md)** — carried openly
on the back shoulder of her breastplate the whole time, disguised as part of the armor's own
decoration, never actually worn. Carl privately reflects mid-book that Donut's succession
obligation to the Blood Sultanate persists "which she didn't even have anymore," confirming this
page's original note that removing the physical crown never lifted the underlying curse.

At the very end of the book, a dying Eva — killed by [Katia](../characters/katia.md) during the
Butcher's Masquerade's aftermath — pulls the hidden crown from her armor with her lower hand as a
final, deliberate act of spite. Eva dies before the crown even finishes equipping, but it still
finds its way onto Katia's bare head moments after — Katia's sponsor had sent her a hat that
would've blocked this, but Katia had given it to Carl, leaving her own head uncovered. The crown
instantly enrolls Katia in the same Blood Sultanate line of succession Donut has carried since
Book 1. The text is explicit about the resulting stakes, realized by Katia herself in this same
private, Katia-only closing scene: with two members of the same royal line now present, only one
of Katia or Donut will be permitted to take the stairs from Floor 9 down to Floor 10. Neither Carl
nor Donut learns any of this — the crown's reappearance, Eva's death, and the succession conflict
it creates — before the book ends.

## Book 6: The Transfer Confirmed, and an Escape Hatch Prepared

!!! success "Book 5's open question, answered"
    This page previously asked where the curse went after Book 5. **Book 6 confirms it: Katia
    carries the Crown for the entire book**, physically on her head, and tells Carl so directly
    mid-floor — "*I have the Crown of the Sepsis Whore on my head, but there's a way out.*" Carl's
    reaction is exactly the one the Book 5 page anticipated: "*How? How did she have the Crown of
    the Sepsis Whore on her head? The implications were staggering.*"

    **The succession problem itself is unchanged at book's end.** Only one of Katia or Donut may
    descend from Floor 9 to Floor 10. What Book 6 adds is not a resolution but an **escape route**
    — one the party deliberately chooses not to take.

### The Orchid of Eileithyia's Grace

Katia's celestial prize box, earned in Book 5 for opening the Gate of the Feral Gods and flooding
Larracos, turns out to be the **Engaged Lock Box of the Night Wyrm**, attuned to her alone. Visible
inside it, behind slats she cannot open, is a pink orchid bud: **The Orchid of Eileithyia's Grace**.

Three seals must be broken to open the box:

| Task | Status |
|---|---|
| Kill someone you are currently or formerly partied with | ✓ Eva Sigrid (Book 5) |
| Kill a country or level boss | ✓ Queen Imogen (Book 5) |
| Find a Guild of Suffering and complete an assassination quest | ✓ Astrid killed ch. 32; quest marked **"Job Complete"** and the Orchid received ch. 33, after the body is delivered to the guild |

Eating the orchid renders the user unconscious for one hour at 1% health with no healing possible.
Survive that, and the goddess Eileithyia grants **one of three permanent boons, at her discretion**:

1. **Triple base Constitution** — all future Constitution points count triple, all Constitution
   bonuses tripled. Stops working if Eileithyia dies or the user passes Floor 12
2. **Celestial Attendant** — immediate transfer to Floor 12 as a temporary, non-combatant NPC until
   the Ascendency games begin, at which point the user may exit the dungeon or continue. Progress,
   sponsorships and inventory all freeze; the user leaves all parties
3. **Revert to your birth race and be made pregnant** (father of the user's choosing, same race).
   Under this season's ruleset this **disqualifies** the user, exporting them to the **Kinder
   Facility** in Mumbai

**Boons 2 and 3 both solve the Crown problem the same way — by removing Katia from the game
entirely.** Katia intends to take Celestial Attendant. Boon 1 does nothing for the succession.

### The Deal Behind It

Katia worshipped Eileithyia on an emergency scroll from Princess Formidable, and only then
discovered the goddess was sponsored by **Huanxin Jinx**. Huanxin's offer was transactional:
Huanxin needed Yemaya resurrected (Yemaya being Eileithyia's retainer for the Ascendency games), so
Katia was to talk Carl into repairing Yemaya's shrine, and Carl was to secure
[Odette](../characters/odette.md) as the Princess Posse's Faction Wars **adjutant**. In exchange,
Huanxin would "*clear this issue with the Crown of the Sepsis Whore*" and guarantee Katia a boon
that let both her and Donut survive.

Carl delivered the shrine. Odette, on air, announced she would not be anyone's adjutant this
season — she is entering the Ascendency as an immortal goddess herself. **Huanxin honoured the deal
anyway**, and the third seal broke; Katia has the orchid.

### Where It Stood at the End of Book 6

Katia still has the Crown, and the orchid is **uneaten**. Carl, Donut and Bautista have all
turned against the plan, precisely because Odette's entry into the Ascendency has made
Huanxin's motives unreadable:

> Using the flower would require all of us to trust in the word of Huanxin Jinx. She promised
> she would take Katia to the 12th floor and protect her. We worked so hard to make it happen,
> and now none of us wanted to use that path. I understood Huanxin's original motivations —
> one, to win the Ascendency throne, and two, to get revenge against Odette — but now that we
> knew Odette was actually going to be playing an immortal goddess herself, it changed
> everything.

Mordecai's warning is the concrete version: Celestial Attendants are high-value former crawlers
surrounded by the Maestro, hostile sponsors, and the families of every hunter Carl killed —
"*You'd be walking into a viper den.*" Katia's own position is that it's "a death sentence if I
*don't* use it." Donut's, on the last page: "*Carl, I don't want Katia to eat that flower. I
don't want her to go away.*"

The decision is explicitly deferred. **Book 6 establishes the escape hatch and then leaves it
unopened.**

## Book 7: The Curse's Two Clauses

The curse turns out to have *two separate clauses*, resolved in two separate operations forty
chapters apart — and the second is the harder of the two. The clean statement of both comes from
Tempest's Floor 8 recap, before chapter 1:

> "Look, the important stuff you need to remember is that on this upcoming ninth floor, **neither
> Donut nor Katia can leave until all the Naga are dead. And even once that happens, only one of
> them can leave.** … It's because they both put on some tiara."

### Clause One: Operation Snake Pit and the death of Princess Vinata

The reigning Sepsis Whore is **Princess Vinata**, a six-armed white naga who is, outside the
dungeon, the daughter of the naga sultan, a war veteran and her father's lead advisor. In-game she
play-acts a historical queen from her own people's history — the original Sepsis Whore, who poisoned
almost all of her sons — and, per Carl, "*she took the gig very, very seriously.*" Her introduction
in chapter 42 is disarming: "*My name is Princess Vinata. But for these games, you're welcome to
call me the Whore.*" Her reputation is that she is "*a ghost. Untouchable.*" The naga lose Faction
Wars every season, and she has never once been killed in the game.

The curse clause does not require killing all eleven naga on the Blood Sultanate team, only the
**blood relatives** in the line of succession. Tipid and Rosetta supply the list, and Carl's chapter-46
briefing names exactly four targets:

1. **Princess Vinata**, the sultana
2. Two female assistants titled **Ikbal**, both Vinata's real-life cousins
3. The **head of her castle guard**, also a cousin

The other seven naga players are friends, subjects, or executives from the kingdom's tourism
marketing agency — not blood, therefore not in the succession, therefore not targets. The lone
surviving naga, **Rishi**, is explicitly exempt for the same reason.

The assassination plan is **Operation: Snake Pit**, named in chapter 39 and launched at the close of
chapter 40. Its real purpose is not a straight fight: "*We just needed the hornet's nest kicked. We
needed Vinata's escape plan to get triggered on our schedule.*"

**Vinata dies in chapter 55**, and not to any of it. She has Carl pinned, slamming his head into the
ground while burning alive, when a sluggalo Carl did not know was riding inside him bursts out of
his back:

> I was about to drop the next bomb when I felt the sluggalo burst out of my back. He—actually, a
> she—slipped out from under my shirt, whooped loudly, and planted a hatchet right between Vinata's
> eyes, killing her.
>
> "Yeah, bitch! Right in the nugget!" the sluggalo woman shouted.

She is **Kandy "Bigs" Newton**, level 29, the size of a bulldog, with a hatchet growing out of the
top of her head. The Sepsis Whore of the Blood Sultanate — untouchable for as many seasons as anyone
can remember — is killed by a level-29 slug with a head axe, as an afterthought, in the middle of
somebody else's fight.

The system messages follow immediately, in order:

> **System Message: The Sepsis Whore of the Blood Sultanate has fallen. Credit for the kill has been
> given to Kandy "Bigs" Newton of the Princess Posse.**
>
> **System Message: The Blood Sultanate has been defeated.**
>
> **System Message: All assets of the Blood Sultanate, as per prior arrangement, have been split
> evenly with the surviving blood relatives of the Sepsis Whore, Warlord Princess Donut and General
> Katia Grim, both of the Princess Posse. As per prior arrangement, all assets have been forfeited
> to the Princess Posse.**

### Clause Two still stands — and it is worse than remembered

Killing the Whore satisfies "all the Naga must die." It does nothing at all about the second clause,
and chapter 56 restates it in a form the earlier books had not made explicit:

> I didn't want to think about the third option, which would mean only one of them, Donut or Katia,
> would be able to go down the stairs. And in fact, **it was more insidious than that. Neither of
> them would be able to go down the stairs as long as the other was still alive.** I refused to even
> entertain the thought.

That is the trap in its final form. It is not "one of you may pass." It is "neither of you may pass
until the other is dead." Chapter 84 puts it plainly: "*Donut wouldn't be able to leave until after
Katia was whisked away. That was the price of the Crown of the Sepsis Whore.*"

### Chapter 84: Katia eats the orchid

With 65 minutes left before the floor collapses and the orchid taking exactly 60 to work, Carl gives
the order in the middle of an evacuation:

> "Eat the orchid. You have to do it now. Now, now, now!"

Katia climbs onto a table, eats it, and drops unconscious at 1% health with **Waiting for the
Goddess** floating over her head.

> **System Message: The Orchid of Eileithyia has been consumed. The goddess has been summoned.**

### Chapter 86: the second ambush

Eileithyia arrives — level 250, invulnerable, and wearing her sponsor **Huanxin Jinx** inside her
soul armor. Katia has three boon choices in front of her and Huanxin, who controls which one the
goddess grants, opens with a threat: "*Choose already. Choose or I will kill the cat and Carl.*"

The party has spent the entire floor preparing for this exact two-minute window. The exploit,
explained afterward by Baroness Victory, requires a choice-option boon *and* a sponsored god:
"*She can't move until Katia makes her choice. It's a little quirk of the system. She's not even
seeing this, I don't think.*" For as long as Katia does not press the button, the goddess is frozen
and the CEO inside her is exposed.

The kill chain, in order:

1. **Donut casts *[Laundry Day](../spells/laundry-day.md)*** — level 15, enhanced to 16 — and rips
   Huanxin Jinx bodily out of the goddess's soul armor. Eileithyia remains standing. Huanxin hits the
   floor and slides to a stop inside a **pre-drawn circle of words of power**. No teleport was
   needed to place her; the spell delivered her into it.
2. Carl explains the circle: "*That was a last-minute addition. You can't teleport away while you're
   standing on that. Your tech shields will work, yes, but no magic will.*" Huanxin clicks the ring
   on her finger and nothing happens.
3. **Donut shreds the tech shields with *[Claw](../abilities/claw.md)***, a natural cat-race skill
   she has quietly trained from level 4 to nearly 15 on her new
   [knee pads](enchanted-spiked-knee-pads-of-kina.md). Stacked with the Eileithyia proximity buff
   (500% female melee) and Carl's party bonus (another 500%), she crosses the circle faster than Carl
   can see and "*the shields around Huanxin were now just simply gone.*" Her own assessment: "*that
   wasn't necessary. I'm just showing off a little.*"
4. **The [Scorpion's Bite](index.md#book-7-item-catalog) quadruped weapon mount** appears on Donut's
   back — a gold Apothecary benefactor box item, "*non-magical bandolier accessory*" — and **Katia's
   [Enchanted Repeating Crossbow of the Scavenger Mother of Mothers](enchanted-repeating-crossbow-of-the-scavenger-mother-of-mothers.md)**
   forms on it, unloaded.
5. **Mordecai walks in with the bolt.** He is a non-combatant NPC and cannot touch her — "*No, I
   can't. Not directly*" — so he does not have to:

    > "You see this?" He flicked the tip. "**My brother died because of you and because of Odette
    > and because of Chaco. But mostly because of you. Some of my mother's ashes are built into the
    > tip of this bolt. It's a funeral rite in my culture. To make a weapon with the ashes of those
    > you lost. My mother died before the crawl came to my world, and I'm grateful for the
    > opportunity to allow her to avenge her son.**"

    He loads the bolt into the crossbow on Donut's back, says "*Goodbye, Katia. Be safe, kid,*" and
    walks out of the tent.

6. Donut, circling, delivers the line the whole ambush was staged for, and the crossbow fires
   between the alien's multiple sets of eyes:

    > "You're strong, yes. But we are getting stronger every day. And you know what? We don't need
    > to kill the gods and goddesses to win. No, honey..."
    >
    > **The crossbow on her back fired, and the bolt slammed right between the multiple sets of eyes
    > of the alien.**
    >
    > **"...We just need to kill you."**

Victory's verdict: "*It was all within the rules, but they won't care. Congratulations on your
victory.*"

### Katia refuses the escape hatch and takes the exit instead

With Huanxin dead and the goddess free to act on her own judgment, Katia does **not** take Celestial
Attendant — the boon the entire Book 6 arc was built around. Eileithyia offers it again directly:
"*If you come to assist me and Yemaya in my court, I promise you will be kept safe.*"

Katia asks for the pregnancy instead, and her reasoning is the point:

> **"The rules of this world are strange," Katia said. "If I am pregnant, I get sent somewhere else.
> There are no children or pregnant women allowed here in this place. At least not of my kind. But
> there are several children, up there," Katia said, pointing upward. "And they are mostly alone. I
> think they're in trouble. They all need help, and this is the only way I can get to them."**

The children are real and the number is known: Zev tells Carl in chapter 73 that roughly **120,000**
children — those who tried to enter the dungeon and were routed into one specific wing — are being
held physically on the Earth's surface in the **kinder facility**, along with pregnant women.
Eileithyia can sense them once Katia points, and cannot reach them: "*There are so many of my
daughters there, all alone and afraid. Why couldn't I sense them until now? I... I can't reach
them.*" Donut supplies the answer — "*You can't get there. But Katia can, and she can protect them
for you.*"

Katia is not escaping the dungeon. She is redeploying.

### What she had been hiding

Eileithyia reverts her to human first, and what that reveals is the book's quietest gut-punch:

> **Her entire right arm was gone, all the way to the shoulder. Her left leg was gone. Her right leg
> was gone up to the knee. She was missing an ear as well.**
>
> **Katia smiled sadly from the bed. "Just a few things I've lost along the way."**
>
> "I had no idea," I said.
>
> **"How could you? We've all become experts at hiding what we've really lost."**

Seven floors of accumulated injuries, invisible for as long as she could shape-shift over them.
**Katia had built the cybernetic replacements herself, in anticipation of this moment**, and left
them in the crafting room; **Bautista** finished them and carried them in ("*I got them done*"), and
the whole party fastens them on while the goddess waits.

Eileithyia requires a human father. She looks at Carl and rules him out — "*I see who you wish for it
to be, but it cannot be so*" — and Katia asks **Louis Santiago**, who agrees after checking that he
still qualifies as human.

> **"It is done. It will be a boy."**
>
> **"Spoilers," Louis muttered.**
>
> **We all backed up, and the word Ineligible appeared over Katia's head.**

She waves with her new cybernetic right arm and disappears.

### Where It Stands at Book's End

**Both clauses are discharged.** The naga blood line is dead, Katia is out of the dungeon and tagged
**Ineligible**, and Donut is free to descend without anyone having to fight anyone. There is no
system message announcing it — the resolution is simply that in chapter 87 Carl and Donut walk into
the stairwell together, and nobody stops them.

> "I'm going to miss her," she said. "But you know what, Carl?"
>
> "What's that?"
>
> **"They are not going to break me. No matter how hard they try."**

!!! note "Exact-verification notes"
    - The book never prints a "succession obligation lifted" notification. Donut's freedom is
      established by Katia's **Ineligible** tag and by the unimpeded descent in chapter 87, not by
      an explicit clearance message.
    - Mordecai's brother is referred to only as "**my brother**" in Book 7. The name *Uzzi* does not
      appear anywhere in this book.
    - The prosthetics were **made by Katia**; Bautista finished and delivered them.
