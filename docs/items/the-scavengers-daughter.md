# The Scavenger's Daughter

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Slot:** Back patch, affixed to the
      [Enchanted Anarchist's Battle Rattle](enchanted-anarchists-battle-rattle.md)
    - **Rarity:** **Divine**, **Unique**, **Fleeting**
    - **Obtained:** A **Celestial Predator Box** — the reward for the "Apex Predator" achievement,
      earned for killing the most hunters on Floor 6 *and* killing the very last one (a Book 5
      event; the box is opened at the start of Book 6)
    - **Grants:** Doubled patch stat bonuses, +25 Dexterity, +25 Strength, deity-proximity alerts,
      **Climb**, **[Soul Reaper / The Daughter's Kiss](../abilities/daughters-kiss.md)**, and the
      **[Mysterious Bone Key](mysterious-bone-key.md)**

## The Patch Itself

A thick black rectangle of fabric embroidered with a white pile of skulls from various creatures,
stacked into a pyramid. Running across the whole face of it, invisible until it matters, is a layer
of **clear stitching**, "almost like it was sewn with fiberoptic wire" — an image Carl can feel but
not see.

Its pre-installation description tells him nothing:

> **This patch depicts a pile of scary skulls, all stacked up nice and neat. I wonder if there's a
> story there?**
>
> **If this upgrade patch is affixed to an eligible garment, it will imbue the following upgrades:
> (Hidden until it is affixed and then worn.)**

Affixing it also earns the achievement **"Fashion of the Gods!"**, which explains what a divine
item is: something that "might've once been worn by a god. Or sneezed on by a god." Divine items
hide their effects until equipped, and gain new powers — or nasty debuffs, with "no rhyme or
reason to it" — in the presence of divinity. Mordecai is unbothered: "*Divine items are the best
items.*"

Carl sews it on himself, badly, over Donut's loud objections about his stitching.

## Base Effects

> **All patch stat point benefits are now doubled.**
>
> **Plus 25 points to Dexterity.**
>
> **Plus 25 points to Strength.**
>
> **You will receive a notification every time a deity enters the area.**
>
> **The *Climb* benefit.**
>
> **The *Soul Reaper* benefit.**
>
> **The *Mysterious Bone Key* benefit.**
>
> **This patch will imbue additional, varying benefits when it is in the presence of divine
> entities.**

The Battle Rattle already grants +1 to every stat per affixed patch; doubling that across Carl's
five smaller patches, on top of the flat +25/+25, makes this the single largest equipment upgrade
he receives in the book. It also adds a new **Soul Essence** bar to his interface, sitting directly
below his mana in the slot the Floor 6 blood bar used to occupy.

Mordecai's reaction to the middle benefit is the tell — he blinks his single eye and says "*Oh,
shit. Soul Reaper?*... *it's good all right. But you gotta be careful with it.*"

## Divine Proximity Effects

The patch "opens her eyes" whenever a deity or demon prince enters the area, and the effect varies
entirely by who shows up. Every instance seen in Book 6:

| Entity | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Ogun**, god of blacksmiths (lvl 250) | All Smash-related attacks **+300%** |
| **Yemaya**, the river mother | All healing skills and spells **cost no mana and have no cooldown** |
| **Eileithyia** | All damage Carl deals to **female** opponents is **quartered**; all **female** party members strike with melee power of **500%** ("Girl power, bitches!") |
| **Amayon**, prince of Sheol | All of Carl's fire spells use **Sheol Fire** instead of regular fire |
| **Ysalte**, goddess of hopelessness and insanity | All potions Carl quaffs or physically handles **outside his inventory turn to vinegar** — and return as a *more powerful* version of themselves upon her banishment |

Ysalte's is the book's clearest demonstration that a divine "debuff" on this item is usually a
delayed gift: Carl deliberately pulls potions out and drops them on the floor mid-fight to be
vinegared, earning the **"Kombucha!"** achievement, and when the patch looks away it announces
that "all of your vinegar-infused potions outside your inventory have been enhanced." One of them
regrows Mongo's burned-off wing.

Amayon's effect does the heavy lifting at the climax: with every fire attack upgraded to Sheol
Fire, Carl's [Martyr's Path](../abilities/the-martyrs-path.md) release bathes a demon prince in
hellfire rather than ordinary flame.

When a deity leaves, the buff "fades and will completely dissipate over the next ten minutes."

## Scaling

The patch's own numbers are flat. What grows is the **Soul Essence** bar it adds: the bar's
capacity increases every time it is filled and fully drained, so the longer Carl uses it the bigger
each charged attack becomes. See
[Soul Reaper / The Daughter's Kiss](../abilities/daughters-kiss.md) for the full mechanic. By
mid-book Carl has topped and drained it repeatedly and notes the attacks are "noticeably stronger
already."

## The Hidden Stitching

!!! warning "Foreshadowing — unresolved"
    The clear stitching resolves on the final page of the book. As Carl and Donut are being lifted
    off Cuba, Donut sees the jacket glowing and finally makes out the image: "*It's not an aardvark
    like I thought. It's a giant centipede! The image is of a centipede crawling through all the
    skulls!*"

    A centipede is a **Scolopendra** reference — the same entity behind the "Scolopendra levels"
    of *[Zerzura](../spells/zerzura.md)* and, per in-dungeon rumour, connected to the Bedlam Bride
    herself ("*one dwarf say she is the daughter of the great centipede. Another say she's the
    sister. Another claimed she's the mother*"). Book 6 does not explain the connection.

    At the same moment a voice speaks in Carl's mind: *"You did not diffuse your soul power from
    the Scavenger's Daughter. That was a mistake. You should never allow it to linger within you.
    I need you alive, Carl. I need you reasonably sane. We have so much work to do."* The speaker
    is not named. Carl had indeed forgotten to spend a full essence bar, which the item's own
    description had warned about in a joke he was told to ignore.

## Book 7: Soul Poisoning

!!! danger "New mechanic — the warning was real"
    Book 6 closed with an unnamed voice telling Carl "*You did not diffuse your soul power from the
    Scavenger's Daughter. That was a mistake. You should never allow it to linger within you.*"
    Book 7 opens by making that a **condition with a name and a progression bar**. Letting the Soul
    Essence bar sit full now actively damages Carl, and it gets worse the longer he leaves it.

The condition surfaces in chapter 1. Carl feels "*a sudden pang… like a stomach cramp, but much
more burning*," and his
[Emberus ring](enchanted-paupers-ring-of-the-steadfast-emberus.md) flashes and warms. The health
menu reads:

> **Soul Poisoning.**
>
> **The effects of this condition are being partially blocked by your Pauper's ring of the Steadfast
> Emberus.**
>
> **This condition is worsening.**
>
> **This condition causes jolts of pain.**
>
> **This condition has additional effects that will soon be revealed if you don't do something
> about it.**
>
> **You are suffering from a very minor case of stop-carrying-stored-souls-on-your-back. Seriously.
> Do you know why soul crystals explode when they get overloaded? Do you remember that whole thing
> on the previous floor when too many souls flowed through Sheol? And you're just walking around,
> giving a piggyback ride to a bunch of corrupted souls stuck in something made out of fabric?
> Yeah, good luck with that. You better smush something soon, dumbass.**
>
> **Oh, and don't try unequipping that jacket while your soul power is full. That won't go well for
> you or anybody nearby.**

That last line converts the jacket from an asset into a hostage: while charged, it cannot safely be
taken off.

### The escalation

| Stage | Chapter | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | 1 | **Jolts of pain**, partially blocked by the Pauper's Ring |
| Reminder | 20 | "*You are now suffering from Soul Poisoning. Activate the Daughter's Kiss skill to disperse the effect.*" |
| Worsening | 24 | "*Your Soul Poisoning is worsening. You will now suffer from **Post Coital Blues**.*" Carl's entire reaction: "*What the hell does that mean?*" |
| Worst | 25 | **Post Nut Clarity** — using the Daughter's Kiss now knocks Carl unconscious |

Imani, over chat, after he buries the squad in dirt:

> "The second you did that attack, you had a debuff called **Post Nut Clarity**. You were out for 25
> seconds. You buried us in dirt. Don't do that again when we're that close."

Carl's own summary: "*The Post Nut Clarity was, apparently, a debuff from my soul poisoning that
knocked me out once I activated my Daughter's Kiss. That was dangerous as fuck. I needed to make
certain I used the skill from now on before the debuff activated.*"

So the patch's incentive structure inverts. In Book 6 the bar rewarded patience — hold the charge,
hit harder. In Book 7 holding it invites a condition that makes the very act of spending it
dangerous, and the correct play becomes discharging early and often, on whatever is nearest. Chapter
1's discharge is on an already-dead Concierge Shark, which earns the achievement **"Sushi Smoothie"**
and the system's note: "*Next time use your foot.*"

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **Post Coital Blues is never defined.** The system names it, Carl asks what it means, and the
    book never says. **Post Nut Clarity is a consequence of the worsened condition**, not a baseline
    cost of every Daughter's Kiss — it only appears once the poisoning has escalated.

### Divine proximity on Floor 9

The deity-proximity mechanic is unchanged and fires constantly — Floor 9 has gods walking around in
the open — but Book 7 clarifies its duration rules. The patch "opens her eyes" when a deity is in
range, and closes them when the deity leaves, at which point "*your boon is timed and will run out
on its own.*" There is also an explicit range limit: "*This deity is too distant to activate your
Scavenger's Daughter,*" and a partial state, "*The Scavenger's Daughter has blinked her eyes, but
she does not know what she sees.*"

Every effect seen in Book 7:

| Entity | Chapter | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Khepri** | 52 | "*Uh, please wait while I look something up…*" → **immortal for the next 90 seconds** |
| **Harpocrates** | 70 | **Level 15 Pied Piper skill** (later made *permanent* by Eris — see [Pied Piper](../abilities/pied-piper.md)) |
| **Yarilo** | 72 | "*Temporary Effect from Yarilo: Please wait. / Oh shit, we hadn't fixed that one…*" |
| **Meatus**, a feral god | 76 | "*Your dick will fall off if you get an erection.*" Made **permanent** when Meatus is "slap-chopped all over the realm" |
| **Eileithyia** | 86 | Damage against female opponents quartered; all female party members strike at **500%** melee power. "*Girl power, bitches!*" |

Chapter 54 is where this becomes a liability rather than a lottery. With gods appearing all over the
battlefield at once, Carl works out why he has worms coming out of his nose: "*My Scavenger's
Daughter patch. **Every new god gave me either a buff or debuff**.*" The simultaneous stack —
Never-Ending Mana, Shit-Faced, Stiff Legs, suction cups on his fingers, screaming worms — is what
finally **overloads the Pauper's Ring**.

!!! note "Still unanswered"
    In chapter 78 Carl asks Mordecai outright who the Scavenger and the Scavenger's Daughter
    actually are. Mordecai deflects — "*in-dungeon one or the fairy tale?*" — and the question is
    never answered. The Book 6 centipede/Scolopendra stitching is likewise not explained in Book 7.
