# The Tiara of a Thousand Lights and Accompanying Charge Pillow

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Princess Donut
    - **Slot:** Head
    - **Rarity:** **Celestial Benefactor Box**, **unique**
    - **Obtained:** Chapter 3, from Donut's sponsor **Long Haul Biological Waste Management
      Solutions** — a crest-owned company Mordecai has never heard of sponsoring anyone
    - **Base effect:** **+10 Charisma. That is all.**
    - **Real effect:** **99 Absorption Jewels** — a permanent buff-storage battery, filled by
      draining other enchanted hats on the accompanying **red charge pillow**
    - **Second pillow:** A **purple universal charge pillow** from a separate Platinum Apothecary
      benefactor box, same chapter, which drains *any* magical item but only transfers to a
      like item

This is Donut's **third** tiara, and it does not replace either of the first two in the way one
might expect. It is a container. The
[Enchanted Tiara of Mana Genita](tiara-of-mana-genita.md) stays in inventory, and the
[Enchanted Tiara of the Inebriated Dragonfly](enchanted-tiara-of-the-inebriated-dragonfly.md)
is deliberately *destroyed as an enchanted item* on the purple pillow so that its powers can be
poured into this one.

## Delivery

The benefactor box arrives as a box-shaped spaceship covered in flashing lights, with the Long Haul
logo on its side, which then folds in on itself:

> The ship started to move, folding in on itself like it was a giant robot. Donut squealed with
> delight as it turned into a massive, mechanical hand. And sitting upon that hand were two items.
> A red, fluffy pillow with a glittering, shining, opalescent tiara placed upon it.

Mordecai's reaction is split between suspicion and approval — "*That's a crest-owned company. I've
never even heard of them sponsoring anybody. Much less giving a Celestial Benefactor box. It
doesn't make any damn sense*" — followed immediately by "*Wow. That's a lot of absorption crystals.
Good. This is a good one.*"

The pillow is velvet-textured and much heavier than it looks. The tiara is "covered in about a
hundred, tiny jewels, each sparkling a different color."

## The Description

> **The Tiara of a Thousand Lights and Accompanying Charge Pillow.**
>
> **This is a unique item.**
>
> **This item was made especially for Crawler Princess Donut by her sponsor, Long Haul Biological
> Waste Management Solutions.**
>
> **This particular tiara is imbued with 99 Absorption Jewels.**
>
> **The tiara itself has no inherent magical properties, and in its base state it gives no
> additional benefits or buffs except plus 10 to Charisma. However, it has the ability to be
> permanently charged with up to 99 different buff items.**
>
> **Any single enchanted hat item of any quality you place upon the accompanying charge pillow for
> a period of ten hours will lose all of its magical properties. These properties will be stored in
> the pillow until the tiara is placed upon the charger, upon which time, all of the buffs and
> magical enhancements will be instantly transferred to the tiara. If a hat item contains five
> separate buffs, it will use up five of the 99 jewels. Similar buffs from different hat items, like
> two different intelligence boosts, will not combine and will take up two jewels.**
>
> **With some careful planning, one can turn this tiara into one of the most powerful items in the
> history of Dungeon Crawler World.**

The item's lore section is a Long Haul origin myth about a lost crest habitat, a scavenging young
engineer and "*a bioengineered farm animal called a cockrent — basically a space chicken that
breathes methane*," whose jettisoned spheres exploded into colored light and drew a Syndicate
rescue squad. The joke is buried in a parenthetical, and it matters for reasons the book does not
flag until much later:

> **(They, of course, were fined so much money that the habitat was eventually repossessed and the
> vast majority of the adults were forced to indenture to pay off the debt, but this part is
> usually left out of the retellings of this legend.)**

## The Warnings

These are the operative rules, quoted in order:

> **Warning: Temporary effects will remain temporary, and upon expiration will burn out the
> absorption jewel.**
>
> **Warning: Items with curse effects may or may not transfer to the tiara depending on multiple
> factors, so be wary. As such, curse effects may or may not be removed from items that have been
> drained of their magical properties.**
>
> **Warning: Certain Celestial, god-blessed hat items may or may not be properly drained or
> transferred.**
>
> **Warning: Items carrying a debt cannot be absorbed into the pillow.**
>
> **Warning: This is a big one. Hat items placed on this pillow take ten hours to fully transfer,
> however if you remove the item after one hour, it will still lose its magical abilities.**

Four things follow from that list and they are all load-bearing:

1. **A temporary buff is worse than useless.** It occupies a jewel, expires, and burns the jewel out.
2. **There is no safety window.** Pull a hat off after one hour and the hat's magic is gone anyway,
   with nothing stored. The ten hours is the transfer time, not a trial period.
3. **Curses are a coin flip in both directions** — they may ride across into the tiara, and they may
   or may not be scrubbed off the drained item.
4. **Debt-bearing items are categorically excluded.** See
   [the debt cross-reference](#the-only-hint-about-debt-bearing-items) below.

There is also no completion notification, which Mordecai calls a long-standing bug — you have to
set your own timer, and you cannot tell whether it worked until you peel the drained item off.
Items "click" audibly onto a pillow and then stick like a magnet.

This is the third tiara referenced on [Magic Missile](../spells/magic-missile.md) and
[Tiara of Mana Genita](tiara-of-mana-genita.md) — its "advanced targeting" doesn't originate with
the tiara itself, but is absorbed into it from a hat; see
[Where the "advanced targeting" actually comes from](#where-the-advanced-targeting-actually-comes-from).

## The Second Pillow — Purple, Universal

Minutes after the Long Haul box, a **Platinum benefactor box from the Apothecary** arrives in the
same chapter. Donut expects a hat; it is a second charging pillow, nearly identical to the first but
purple instead of red, with the same ten-hour transfer and the same one-hour point of no return. The
difference is a single appended clause:

> **Warning: This will drain any magical item, but it will only transfer to a like item. If you
> drain a ring, the stored buff will only be transferred to an absorption crystal affixed to a ring.
> If you attempt to transfer to an incompatible item, the buff will be lost.**

Carl's read on it:

> "So, this one is almost like a universal charger," I said, turning the purple pillow over in my
> hand. "Be sure they're listed as hats before you use it, Donut. But now that you have two, you can
> fill up your new tiara twice as fast. We'll need to start cataloging all your magical hats ASAP and
> see what buffs we want to transfer over."

In practice the purple pillow serves two roles: it doubles the party's charging throughput (two hats
can cook at once, even though only the red pillow feeds the tiara), and it is the tool used to strip
non-hat gear.

## The Inebriated Dragonfly Is Sacrificed

Donut loads both pillows for the first time in chapter 3: **the new boonie hat on the red pillow,
and the [Tiara of the Inebriated Dragonfly](enchanted-tiara-of-the-inebriated-dragonfly.md) on the
purple one.**

> Donut grumbled as she put the pillows on the counter. She placed her new boonie hat on the red
> pillow and her Tiara of the Inebriated Dragonfly on the other. Each item made an audible click the
> moment they touched the pillow. There was no other notification.

The dragonfly tiara is therefore deliberately and permanently drained — its 50% potion cooldown
reduction, +10 Intelligence, poison protection, inebriation clause and level-10
*[Hover](../abilities/hover.md)* stripped out of it — so that those buffs can be re-housed in the
Thousand Lights tiara. The physical tiara survives; Donut keeps all her drained hats for her
collection, which she considers the best part of the whole arrangement:

> "Do you know how many hats I have with magical abilities, but I can't wear them because they only
> have one or two? Or because they're ugly? And you know what the best part is? Even though it
> drains the original item of all its magic, it doesn't destroy it! So I can keep the hats for my
> collection. It is just divine."

!!! note "Hover survives the migration — confirmed to chapter 75"
    Mordecai briefly argues *against* spending a jewel on *Hover* when the party is deciding which
    single skill to push to level 15 ("*Hover is a little different, and I don't think it would be
    worth it*"), but Donut does have it afterward, while wearing the Thousand Lights tiara. Chapter
    75, planning the Nest collapse: "*I also have Hover, Carl. If the tower falls, I won't. But in
    case I do, I have the potion*," and in narration, "*would it break in time for her to use her
    Hover to save herself?*"

    **Exact-verification note:** the book never prints a "Hover transferred" notification, and
    chapter 75 is the *last* mention of *Hover* in Book 7. Continued possession at the very end of
    the book is an inference from the tiara still being worn in chapter 86, not an explicit
    statement.

## Where the "Advanced Targeting" Actually Comes From

The Book 6 flag pointed at "a tiara granting advanced targeting." The chain is longer than that.

Donut's Floor 9 class is **Deathbed Hellcat**, a long-range sniper/assassin class she picks off-page
during the floor transition. It quadruples the range of all her existing spells, raises her
Dexterity, improves her instakill odds, grants at-will invisibility — and it ships with an
accessory kit containing a **ghillie suit** and a **boonie hat**. The hat is what carries the skill:

> The floppy hat added a Targeting skill, which allowed her to zoom in and examine mobs from afar.
> It was similar to the gnomish farseerer, but a lot less awkward. It also increased the range of
> all her spells, even more than they were already buffed.

Worn together with the ghillie suit, the two stack: "*She'd be able to snipe line-of-sight enemies
from well over a mile away.*"

Mordecai's very first instruction on receiving the pillow is to drain that specific hat first:

> "The first thing you'll want to put on that pillow is that hat you just got with the targeting
> ability. And set a timer in your own interface. I know these things, and they don't give any sort
> of notification when the transfer is complete. It's been a bug for a very long time. So set a
> timer for like ten and a half hours to be safe. You can't see if it worked until you pull the
> drained item off, so you have to be extra, extra careful."

By **chapter 32** the transfer has landed — "*Donut was examining the orc prince via her new
Targeting skill which had just been added to her crown*" — and by **chapter 61** it produces the
exact line the Book 6 flag was reaching for:

> Crack! That was a new sound, and it took a moment for me to realize what it was. It was Donut
> casting *Magic Missile*. **With her tiara's advanced targeting skills and Donut's elevated
> position, along with her Deathbed Hellcat class, she could hit anything she could see with any of
> her long-range spells.** It was louder than expected.

That single capability changes the party's siege engineering. The Nest tower was going to be built
as a dedicated sniper tower to give Donut exactly this; because the tiara already provides it, the
tower's synergy slot is freed up and reassigned to **Atrocity-class spells** instead — which is what
makes *[War Crime](../spells/war-crime.md)* viable from the top of it.

## Fill Rate and the Running Tally

| Chapter | State of the tiara |
|---|---|
| 3 | Received. Empty — "*The tiara didn't have any powers yet, but it glittered magnificently.*" |
| 32 | *Targeting* confirmed transferred and in use |
| 40 | **23 of 99** jewels lit. This session adds **+10 Constitution**, a **+2 Dodge upgrade** (taking Dodge to 15), and a **1% Intelligence** buff. **Steady** is queued on the pillow via a plastic baseball hat |
| 56 | **35 of 99**, with *Perfect Night Vision* newly added, and "*two more by the time the timer expired*" |
| 73 | Glowing "*like a lighthouse beacon*" |

The chapter-40 passage is also the clearest illustration of the mechanic's waste problem — Katia is
running the swaps, and one jewel is spent on nothing useful because it rode in on the same hat as
something Donut wanted:

> On the tiara, three of the absorption jewels started to sparkle, joining the twenty other
> sparkling gems. Twenty three buffs added, 76 to go. The tiara had just gained another 10 to
> Constitution, a plus two enhancement to Donut's Dodge skill—which should bring it to 15—and a 1%
> buff to her Intelligence. **The intelligence buff was a waste of a crystal, but it was on the same
> hat that had the Dodge upgrade and couldn't be avoided.**

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    The figure **37** does not appear in Book 7. The only number after 35 is the forward-looking
    "two more by the time the timer expired" in chapter 56, from which 37 can be inferred but is
    never stated. No final count is ever given.

## The Liability

The tiara's brilliance is a tactical problem, and Carl says so as soon as it becomes obvious:

> The tiara itself glittered brilliantly like a star upon Donut's head, which I didn't like. She,
> predictably, loved it.

The concrete version, same chapter: "*Especially since she wore a glittering, here's-where-my-head-is
tiara.*" By chapter 73 the contrast is absurd — Donut is in the top level of the Nest tower wearing
a **ghillie suit**, the single most camouflage-oriented item she owns, with a tiara that "*glowed
like a lighthouse beacon*" on her head, marking the exact position of her skull for every enemy
gunner on the field.

## The Only Hint About Debt-Bearing Items

The fourth warning — **"Items carrying a debt cannot be absorbed into the pillow"** — is the **only
place anywhere in Book 7** where debt-bearing items are acknowledged as a mechanical category.

That matters because Carl is still carrying one. The
[Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus](enchanted-paupers-ring-of-the-steadfast-emberus.md)
has an explicit "**This ring has a debt attached to it**" clause and a 990,000-gold Temple of Emberus
loan outstanding since Book 6. Book 7 never mentions the loan, the asterisk on "interest-free," the
curse clause, or Pater Coal. This warning line is the closest the book comes to touching the thread
at all, and it does so sideways, in a different item's fine print, about a different character.

## Scaling

The tiara has no level. Its power is a pure function of **how many of the 99 jewels have been filled
and with what** — which makes it the only item in the series whose ceiling is set by logistics rather
than by loot. The constraints on filling it are:

- **Ten hours per hat**, one hat per pillow, and only the red pillow feeds the tiara
- **One buff per jewel**, with no stacking of duplicates
- **No partial-drain recovery** — an aborted charge destroys the source hat's magic for nothing
- **Temporary buffs self-destruct** their jewel on expiry
- Donut's supply of hats, which is the one resource she has in genuinely absurd abundance
