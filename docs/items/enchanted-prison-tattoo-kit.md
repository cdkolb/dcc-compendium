# Enchanted Prison Tattoo Kit

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Obtained:** A **Gold Quest Box** from the ongoing **Vengeance of the Daughter** storyline —
      the quest whose advertised reward (looting the High Elf castle) had been rendered worthless
      when the castle was *[Zerzura](../spells/zerzura.md)*'d down to Floor 9
    - **Type:** Stick-and-poke tattoo kit
    - **Contents:** A long sharp needle, ink, a few sticks, and something that looks like a piece of
      sandpaper

## Description

> **This kit allows an artist to create a tattoo of their choosing upon the skin of a willing or
> unwilling target. Tattoo artistry is a skill that can imbue all sorts of magical buffs and
> upgrades. Or it can just make certain that your next mugshot goes viral. It all comes down to the
> talent of the artist.**

The preamble is a bleak little riff about prison resolutions ending with "*letting SkullFuck from
the next cell come over and etch a poorly-drawn dragon on the side of my face.*" Donut is offended
on a different axis entirely — "*Hey, the dungeon stole my joke! I said the viral thing when you got
one of your other tattoos!*"

Carl has no idea what to do with it — "*What the hell am I supposed to do with this?*" — and puts it
away.

## What It Is, In Series Terms

This is the same class of item **Edgar** the shell-mage tortoise used on
[Tsarina Signet](../characters/tsarina-signet.md) in Books 3–4, performing the "blood and ink"
ritual that bound her fallen companions into her skin as sentient blood-and-ink elementals. The kit
is not inherently sinister; the two operative clauses are "**willing or unwilling target**" and "it
all comes down to the talent of the artist."

## Scaling

The kit has no level of its own. Its output scales entirely with the **tattoo artistry skill of
whoever is holding it** — a talented artist can imbue magical buffs and upgrades; an untalented one
produces a bad drawing. Carl has no such skill. The one person who uses it in Book 6 has a great
deal of it.

## Use — Chapter 71

The kit's only on-page use is its worst possible one. While **Shi Maria** is controlling Carl's body
during the Amayon climax, she rifles his inventory, finds this kit, and uses it to apply
[The Eye of the Bedlam Bride](the-eye-of-the-bedlam-bride.md) to his sternum — an unwilling target
on both counts, since Carl is both the artist and the canvas and consents to neither.

The ink is brewed in front of him: a sliver of toraline root vegetable in a looted beaker,
superheated by a forced *[Self-Immolation](../spells/self-immolation.md)*, mixed with **Li Jun's
eye**, which Carl is made to gouge out with the kit's own stick and eat. Shi Maria's parting note as
she finishes — *"You will likely be doing more of this soon with the remaining ink"* — suggests the
kit and the leftover ink both survive into Book 7.

## Book 7: The Payoff Is a Whole System

!!! success "Flag resolved — and it is much bigger than a payoff"
    This page previously noted only that the kit and Shi Maria's leftover ink "*suggest*" they
    survive into Book 7, and asked whether that goes anywhere. **They do, and it does.** The
    leftover toraline ink becomes the foundation of a full skill-and-spell-transfer mechanic
    developed across Floor 9 by **Rosetta**, **Edgar**, **Mordecai** and the crawler **Milk** — a
    system large enough that it has its own page:

    **→ [The Toraline Ink Tattoo System](toraline-tattoo-transfer.md)**

    Shi Maria's line was not a throwaway. She was telling Carl what the ink was for, and he spent
    four chapters of Book 7 not understanding it while Rosetta — who understood immediately — was
    already demanding it from him.

The kit's own role in Book 7 is what it always was: it is the tool, not the magic. The magic is in
the ink. In one-line summary:

- **Chapter 11.** Rosetta asks for the toraline and the ink within minutes of the party's arrival on
  Floor 9, before she explains anything: "*Give it to me, and give me the ink the spider used to make
  that tattoo.*"
- **The rule.** Blood **freely given** lends a spell or skill temporarily. Blood **taken by
  violence** steals it permanently — which is the retroactive explanation for Carl already owning
  Li Jun's **Walk on Air**, acquired the night Shi Maria made him eat Li Jun's eye with this kit's
  own stick.
- **Three tattoos** are inked with it: Li Na's *Profane Iron* onto Carl's forearm (Katia, ch. 40),
  Carl's *Tripper* onto Imani's ankle (Katia, ch. 47), and *Bijanbi* onto Donut's flank (Edgar,
  ch. 74).
- **The ink runs out.** Katia's chapter-40 estimate — "*We'll have just enough to do Imani's small
  tat next*" — proves exactly right.
- **It is superseded.** Edgar and Milk develop a new ink that does not need toraline at all and can
  extract abilities from the *dead*, and Milk gives Carl 825 ml of
  [Stayfast Ink and an Adept's Fountain Pen](stayfast-ink-and-adepts-fountain-pen.md) as the
  replacement path, explicitly tied to
  [The Filthy Little Crawler's Book of Voodoo](the-filthy-little-crawlers-book-of-voodoo.md).

Note that **Edgar** — named on this page since Book 6 as the artist who performed the "blood and ink"
ritual on Tsarina Signet — turns out to be exactly the right person, and says so himself. Asked to
do Donut's tattoo, he distinguishes the two crafts: Signet's work was "*stabilizing and preserving a
memory*," while this is "*something new, and I honestly don't know what will happen once it's
complete.*"

Katia, who has never tattooed anyone in her life, does the first two because Edgar is away scouting
naga territory. The system rates her chain dragon **expert quality**.
