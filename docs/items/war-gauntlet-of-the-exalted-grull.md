# Enchanted War Gauntlet of the Exalted Grull

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Slot:** Right hand (wrist bracer that transforms on command)
    - **Obtained:** Gold Brawler Box, awarded at a training guild right after The Juicer fight
    - **Deity link:** Grull, a war god worshipped by equine-class creatures (centaurs, tikbalang, and — after Floor 3 — the Bad Llama's kin)

## Effect

- **+3 Strength** (fist mode only)
- **+1 Dexterity**
- **+2 skill levels to Iron Punch**
- **+1 skill level to Powerful Strike**
- **2% chance to Stun** an enemy on a successful hit
- Does **not** negate Carl's bare-knuckle skill bonuses — it stacks with them rather than replacing them

The item is worn as an ordinary wrist bracer and takes about two seconds to transform into a
spiked, black orcish-steel gauntlet when Carl clenches his fist or holds a weapon for more than
two seconds. It reverts to bracer form when released.

## Scaling

The gauntlet's own bonuses (+3 Strength, +1 Dexterity, 2% Stun chance) are flat and don't scale —
but the skill levels it grants feed into skills that do scale on their own:

- **Powerful Strike:** per Mordecai, each level of this skill *multiplies* unarmed damage — at
  level 3, for example, every kick or punch does three times normal damage. The gauntlet's +1
  skill level is a direct, permanent boost to that multiplier.
- **Iron Punch:** the book doesn't state an explicit per-level formula, but Carl's own natural
  Iron Punch level climbs independently through combat (observed at levels 3 and 5 elsewhere in
  Book 1), so the gauntlet's +2 skill levels stack on top of whatever he trains up separately.

By late Book 1, the combination of Iron Punch, Powerful Strike, natural Unarmed Combat, and
Pugilism lets Carl's bare fists match a weapon as strong as Brandon's lightning maul.

## The Grull Warning

The item carries an unusual warning: striking a worshipper of Grull with it carries a 1.5% chance
of transfiguring the target into the god Grull himself. Mordecai clarifies that Grull "isn't a
real god" — a rare moment where an NPC volunteers that the dungeon's "deities" are constructs
rather than literal beings, at least as far as he's willing to say on the first couple of floors.

## Role in the Story

This becomes Carl's signature weapon for the rest of Book 1 — the piece that finally gives his
bare-knuckle build a proper offensive multiplier without abandoning his refusal to carry a
traditional weapon. It's directly responsible for one of his first successful Stun procs, which
lets him land a killing blow on the Frenzied Gerbil boss "Ralph."

## Book 3: Deity Summoning Rules and the Death Challenge

Book 3 finally lays out the dungeon's full deity-summoning mechanics, explained to Carl by
Mordecai after Carl starts asking pointed questions about the gauntlet's involuntary-summon
warning. There are three summon types — **celestial boons** (a worshipper prays and the god
fights for them), **indentured summonings** (a mage compels a god against its will), and
**involuntary summonings** (accidental, via a trap, spell, or item like this gauntlet). All
require a physical "vessel," usually a mob of the god's own worshipper race. An involuntarily
summoned deity lasts a number of seconds equal to the vessel's level plus the god's own level, and
grows until it fills the chamber it's summoned into; a captured or re-triggered summon can be
"stolen" from one vessel to a second one with no cooldown.

Carl deliberately exploits this on Floor 4, beating a captured mantaur neighborhood boss into
vessel status and pushing it through a portal to summon **Grull** directly in front of a
threatening city-boss mimic — turning the whole encounter into a broadcast **Death Challenge**
gambling event, since the "god" is secretly being sponsored and piloted by the previously
presumed-dead Prince Maestro. Carl survives by decoying Grull with a body double and re-summoning
a second mantaur at the last possible second to yank Grull (and Maestro) out of the fight and into
the abyss — winning the encounter without the gauntlet, or any weapon, landing a single blow on
either monster. See [Carl](../characters/carl.md#book-3-the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook) for the
full sequence.

## Book 4: The Platinum Sharpening Stone

After Carl and Katia detonate the *Wasteland* from a hijacked ambassador's balloon, Carl's
platinum quest box reward includes 5,000 gold, twenty healing scrolls, and a **Platinum
Sharpening Stone**. Mordecai identifies it immediately as a Book-4-era enhancement item — "you're
starting to get items that will enhance your existing items" — and directs Carl to apply it right
away, since it carries a short shelf life. Its full description:

> "Apply this to any spiked offensive weapon to receive the following buffs. Plus (2 x current
> level)% damage to all attacks. Plus 1 to all current stat buffs. Does not add new buffs if they
> do not exist. It also makes the weapon look extra oily and mean looking."

Carl rubs the stone along the gauntlet's spikes immediately (in the desert outside Hump Town,
mid-mission). The spikes visibly lengthen and the gauntlet glows; the effect resolves as **+1
Dexterity and +1 Strength** on top of its existing bonuses, plus the scaling damage buff. This
remains the gauntlet's most recent confirmed upgrade as of the end of Book 4.
