# Carl's Doomsday Scenario

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Type:** Unstable custom explosive (quest-generated)
    - **Obtained:** Formed mid-quest on Floor 3 from a massively overloaded soul crystal and a
      Sheol Glass Reaper Case
    - **Status:** Explosion Imminent — cannot be stabilized or safely removed from inventory

## Effect

Per its in-game description: "An explosion large enough to rattle the teeth of a god... powerful
enough to level an entire city and all the suburbs around it." Its full flavor text credits its
creation to "a man who murders babies and steals rare collectibles from his elders" — a pointed
jab at Carl's own reputation following the Goblin War Chieftain incident and the "Pork Boy" saga.

## Scaling

Not applicable — this is a singular, quest-bound item rather than a skill-scaling piece of gear.

## Role in the Story

Carl is forced to snatch the device into his inventory bare-handed in the two seconds before it
would have otherwise detonated, badly burning himself in the process. This happens at the climax
of a separate, later quest chain — "The Sex Workers Who Fell from the Heavens" — investigating
serial krasue (vampiric flying-head ghost) killings in a skyfowl settlement, which traces back to
the settlement's true ruler, [Miss Quill](../bosses/miss-quill.md), and her undead husband
Remex. After Carl and Donut destroy Quill and corner Remex (a "Soul Leech Capacitor" tethered to
an overloaded soul crystal amplifying the town's guardian constructs), the resulting wild-magic
backlash forces Carl to trap the swelling soul crystal inside a **Sheol Glass Reaper Case** —
which does not stop the explosion, but merges with it, leaving Carl holding the resulting
unstable device. Because it can never be safely removed, it effectively becomes a permanent,
ticking liability he carries with him — the quest's own escalating "precursor bursts" (which
strip crawlers of their magical gear in stages before the main blast) are the direct result of
this device's containment failing in slow motion. The book itself is named after it.
