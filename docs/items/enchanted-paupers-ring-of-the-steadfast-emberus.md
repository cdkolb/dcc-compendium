# Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl
    - **Slot:** Finger
    - **Made from:** Carl's original Strength ring — "the first ring I'd received in the dungeon"
    - **Made at:** Blessed Sun Enchantments, inside [Club Vanquisher](../locations/club-vanquisher.md),
      by a satyr jeweler who also worships Emberus, with high priest **Pater Coal** brokering
    - **Cost:** 1,000,000 gold — 10,000 down plus a **990,000-gold interest-free loan** from the
      Temple of Emberus
    - **Achievement:** **"Columbia House!"**

## What It Does

> **When working properly, this ring will remove most health-seeping and disease-based debuff
> effects. Has a variable numbing effect on various other debuff types. This will not remove the
> debuff itself. Too many active debuffs of any kind may temporarily mute or overheat this ring.**

Three warnings follow, and they are the actual content of the item:

> **Warning: This ring may only be worn by a worshipper of Emberus. This will turn into a cursed
> item if worn by a non-worshipper.**
>
> **Warning: This ring's true nature may only be seen by a worshipper of Emberus. It is disguised
> as an enchanted Strength ring.**
>
> **Warning: This ring has a debt attached to it. You may not sell this ring while this debt is
> attached. If you lose this ring, the loan payment will be accelerated. If you are expelled from
> the church while you are wearing this ring, the curse effect will activate.**
>
> **Warning: This ring carries a curse effect. Non-worshippers who attempt to wear this ring will
> find themselves infected with every debuff suffered by anyone within 50 meters of this ring.
> That's... that's a bad one.**

The jeweler's own caveat when he hands it back: "*This isn't the most powerful base ring, but it
will work for your slug condition, and it will help against a few other debuffs as well, but if it
gets too overwhelmed, it might not work as intended.*"

## The Debt

The system's own notification is unusually blunt:

> **You have taken an interest-free\* loan of 990,000 gold from the Temple of Emberus. You will no
> longer be allowed to renounce your religion while you are under debt. You may make payments
> directly to the Emberus shrine.**

Carl asks what the asterisk means. Nobody answers. Instead the **"Columbia House!"** achievement
fires — a long riff on twelve-albums-for-a-penny negative-option billing that closes with: "*you
have taken on a financial debt. You got the albums. Now you gotta pay. Or don't. Maybe there won't
be any consequences. Maybe there will.*" He immediately regrets the whole transaction.

The binding clause is the one that matters strategically: **Carl may not renounce his religion
while in debt**, which locks him into the Emberus storyline for the foreseeable future.

## Scaling

No level scaling. The ring has a *capacity* rather than a level: it removes most health-seeping and
disease debuffs and numbs others, but stacking too many active debuffs will "mute or overheat" it.
This is shown in play — after being perforated by a porcupine deckmaster, Carl carries **Woozy**,
**Sore as Shit** and **Bonked** simultaneously and feels the ring *burn* on his finger while it
struggles.

## Narrative Placement

The purchase is told **out of order**. Donut's prologue recap mentions "*that weird ring Carl got
that I think is giving him headaches*" before the reader has seen it, the ring is first shown
working during a chapter-42 card battle, and the shop scene itself is not dramatised until chapter
56, which opens with the header "**Several Days Earlier.**" Donut only clocks that it isn't a
Strength ring when she sees it glowing while healing him.

## Book 7: The Ring Works Hard. The Debt Is Never Mentioned.

!!! warning "Checked — and the answer is that nothing happens"
    This page previously asked whoever processed Book 7 to check whether the loan is repaid,
    called, or weaponised. **It is none of those things. It is simply not addressed.**

    Literal, case-insensitive search results across the whole of *This Inevitable Ruin*:

    | Search term | Hits |
    |---|---|
    | `990,000` / `990000` | **0** |
    | `interest-free` / `interest free` | **0** |
    | `church loan` | **0** |
    | `tithe` | **0** |
    | `loan` | 2 — neither is Carl's (Tipid's real-life pension loan; a Donut trash-talk joke) |
    | `debt` | 5 — none is Carl's (see below) |
    | `Pater Coal` | 3 — all narrative, zero financial content |

    **Pater Coal does not appear in Book 7.** He is *referred to* three times, entirely as memory or
    quest-pointer: Carl recalls him being taken over by the AI (ch. 6); a situation is "*just like
    with Pater Coal from the Emberus temple*" (ch. 37); and Emberus sends a message "*telling me I
    needed to find Pater Coal and go talk to somebody*" (ch. 54), which goes nowhere within this
    book.

    **The 990,000-gold debt remains outstanding. The asterisk on "interest-free" remains
    unexplained. The curse clause remains armed. The renounce-your-religion lock remains in force.
    The flag stays open for Book 8.**

    Do not infer a resolution from the silence. Carl never thinks about the money, no one demands
    payment, and the Temple of Emberus's only Book 7 interactions with him are about worship and
    smiting, not accounts.

### The one mechanical acknowledgment that debt-bearing items exist

There is exactly one line in Book 7 that touches the *category*. It is in the description of
Donut's [Tiara of a Thousand Lights](tiara-of-a-thousand-lights.md) charge pillow:

> **Warning: Items carrying a debt cannot be absorbed into the pillow.**

That is the whole of it — a fine-print exclusion in a different character's item, which happens to
confirm that the dungeon tracks debt-encumbered gear as a class. This ring carries the clause the
warning describes ("**This ring has a debt attached to it. You may not sell this ring while this
debt is attached**"), and would therefore be categorically ineligible for the pillow. Nobody in the
book connects the two.

### What the ring actually does on Floor 9

Named in full exactly once — inside the chapter 1 Soul Poisoning readout — and thereafter only as
"the Emberus ring" or "my ring." It is in constant, load-bearing use, and it is visibly struggling
for most of the book.

**Partially blocking the new Soul Poisoning condition (ch. 1):**

> **The effects of this condition are being partially blocked by your Pauper's ring of the Steadfast
> Emberus.**

See [The Scavenger's Daughter](the-scavengers-daughter.md) for that condition. "Partially" is doing
real work: the poisoning still escalates through Post Coital Blues to Post Nut Clarity regardless.

**Absorbing stacked debuffs, and losing:**

- Ch. 1, first onset: "*My Emberus ring flashed and started to get warm.*"
- Ch. 24, mid-battle with buffs and debuffs flickering on and off: "*My Emberus ring burned.*"
- Ch. 40: "*On my finger, the Emberus ring was a little hotter than usual.*"
- **Ch. 54, the overload.** With gods appearing all over the battlefield and the Scavenger's Daughter
  handing him a fresh effect from each one, the stack finally breaks it: "*My finger burned. It was
  the Emberus ring. It was overloaded.*" Immediately: "*I still had slugpox. I never had Imani cure
  it, and now that the ring wasn't working, I had a boil on my back.*"
- Ch. 55, recovery: "*Thankfully, the Emberus ring seemed to catch up as some of the other debuffs
  were contained, including the slugpox.*"
- Ch. 65, cooling after holding a line: "*'No need,' I said as the Emberus ring on my finger cooled.
  In my interface, dozens of mind-based spells crashed against me, but they didn't find purchase.*"

Book 6's stated capacity rule — "too many active debuffs of any kind may temporarily mute or
overheat this ring" — is therefore confirmed in play, and Floor 9 is the environment that finds its
ceiling. It is the difference between a working item and an item whose failure mode you have to plan
around.

## Book 8: Removed, Then Renounced, Then Confessed

At the Floor 11 arena climax, Carl springs a trap on his own god — see
[Emberus](../bosses/emberus.md#renounced-and-confessed) and
[Carl's own page](../characters/carl.md#renouncing-emberus) for the confrontation itself (using
[Khepri](../bosses/khepri.md) as a decoy accusal, then Katia's Bolt of Ophiotaurus to strip
Emberus's invulnerability). This page covers only the ring's own part in it, which the text gives
in a precise, unambiguous order.

### The exact sequence

> I reached over and pulled the Emberus ring off my finger, and I put it into my inventory. I moved
> into my menus, clicked over, and made my selection.
>
> *Here we go.*
>
> **Uh, Carl? Are you fucking sure? Holy shit.**
>
> I clicked Yes.
>
> **Y-y-you have left your faith, and you no longer worship Emberus. You have abandoned your god.
> Prepare for a smite. Something tells me this is gonna be a bad one.**
>
> Both of the tattoos on the back of my hands burst into burning, searing flames. A notification
> appeared regarding the ring I'd just shoved into my inventory, and I waved it away.

So the order is fixed: **ring off and into inventory first, then the renounce-faith menu
confirmation, then the confession** ("The other guy to kill your worthless smear of a son was
*me*..."). The smite lands on the confirmation, not the confession — it burns the sun-shaped
tattoos on the backs of both hands into permanent scars the system calls the
**[Mark of the Damned](mark-of-the-damned.md)** (see that page; also covered on
[Carl's own page](../characters/carl.md#renouncing-emberus)).

### The debt, the curse, and the asterisk: still explicitly unresolved

!!! warning "Deliberately ambiguous — do not resolve this"
    The text goes out of its way to raise the debt/curse question and then decline to answer it.

    - **A notification about the ring fires the instant he renounces, and Carl waves it away
      unread.** The book never shows what it said.
    - Later, in the arena dirt immediately after looting Emberus's Memorial Crystal and finding the
      Kyryap's tracking dart, Carl checks his own debuffs for something unrelated and finds this
      instead:

      > There *was* something new, but it had to do with my Emberus ring, not this, and it was
      > something I'd have to deal with later.

      That is the last word Book 8 has on the subject. **What the new ring-related effect actually
      is, is never stated.**
    - Book 6's warning was explicit: *"If you are expelled from the church while you are wearing
      this ring, the curse effect will activate."* Carl removed the ring **before** renouncing, so
      the letter of that clause (wearing it at the moment of expulsion) was arguably not met — but
      the book never says this dodge worked, never confirms the curse fired or didn't, and never
      revisits the 990,000-gold debt, the accelerated-payment clause for losing the ring, or what
      the "interest-free\*" asterisk means.
    - Whether the debt is still owed, whether the curse is now armed against Carl's own inventory
      (since the ring is sitting in it, unworn, not sold, not destroyed), and what the deferred
      "something new" debuff turns out to be are all **left open on purpose**. Do not guess at any
      of them. This is explicit Book 9 setup, not an oversight.
