# Enchanted Night Wyrm's Ring of Divine Suffering

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Owner:** Carl — looted from Frank Q's corpse; [Prepotente](../characters/prepotente.md)
      acquires an identical second copy in Book 5
    - **Slot:** Ring
    - **Status:** Worn in earnest as of Book 5, once Floor 6's bounty-hunter war begins

## Effect

A flat **+5% bonus to all stats** while worn, plus access to a **Marked for Death** skill:
charging the ring up through kills lets its owner gain massively increased power with each
subsequent kill, scaling exponentially the more it's used.

## Scaling

The ring's stat bonus is flat and doesn't scale, but its Marked for Death skill compounds with
use — per Mordecai, a combatant who charges one of these rings up sufficiently gains power
"exponentially" per kill, which is why every historically victorious army in the Floor 9 faction
wars has included a Divine Suffering ring-bearer.

## Danger and Mordecai's Warning

Every season, several of these rings are seeded into the dungeon specifically as bait: their
owners become known, hunted targets, since the ring can't otherwise be brought in from outside the
game and factions competing in the Floor 9 wars are desperate to acquire one. Crawlers who equip
one are typically the first to be tracked down and killed by hunters once they reach the Floor 6
hunting grounds. Mordecai reacts to Carl wearing it with unprecedented alarm — slapping him on
the side of the head, an out-of-character loss of temper he immediately apologizes for (Carl
privately notes his own real father used to hit him "much harder") — and threatens to keep Donut
away from Carl once they reach Floor 6 if he doesn't get rid of it.

## Role in the Story

Carl loots the ring from **Frank Q**'s corpse and wears it on and off for a while, mostly out of
stubbornness, before Mordecai's confrontation convinces him to remove it for good. He agrees to
"talk about selling it on the fifth floor, before we get to the sixth," but keeps it in his
inventory for the remainder of Floor 4.

## Book 4: Kept as Bait

Carl never sells the ring on Floor 5. Amid a growing conflict with the Skull Empire (whose agents
send both an Infiltrator-possessed Chris Andrews and later a summoned demon after him) and having
just become the dungeon's #1-ranked crawler, Carl instead decides to lean into the target the ring
paints on him. At the very end of the book, standing at the stairwell down to Floor 6's hunting
grounds, he pulls the ring from inventory, holds it up, and publicly dares any Floor 6 bounty
hunter to come and take it — vowing that "every single hunter who dares to set foot on the same
floor as us will be dead" by the time that floor collapses. The ring itself remains unequipped and
unused mechanically; its role here is purely as bait for a war Carl is deliberately starting.

## Book 5: Worn in Earnest

Carl finally wears the ring throughout Floor 6, using its **Marked for Death** skill exactly as
Mordecai warned: every kill made while a target is marked grants a stacking, escalating number of
free stat points, split according to the victim's own highest stat. Confirmed thresholds from the
book: 1 point per kill at the ring's base level, jumping to 2 points after enough kills, then 3,
then 4, then 5 points per kill by the book's end — each jump announced by the system message
"**Congratulations, Murderer.**" A separate, related mechanic (the "Guild of Suffering," unlocked
by a companion tattoo the ring's use grants — see [Carl](../characters/carl.md)) is introduced but
never explored before the floor ends. Marking an NPC or elite is explicitly impossible; only
crawlers, hunters, and regular mobs can be targeted.

Two major complications emerge:

- **Left to Fester.** Left to Fester is inflicted on the *wearer* of the ring, not the marked
  target — it won't lift until the wearer's mark is dead. When Prepotente's own,
  separately-acquired copy of the ring activates involuntarily during a magical feedback loop with
  the vampire-hunting elite Viscount Fog, it marks [Miriam Dom](../characters/miriam-dom.md) as
  Prepotente's target, saddling *Prepotente himself* with the debuff. Because the same magical
  bounce-back has also left Prepotente frozen and Fragile (one hit from death) and unable to act,
  Miriam — who is doomed regardless, having been caught by her elite hunter and now facing sunrise
  as a vampire — asks Carl to end her life so Prepotente can be freed once she's gone. Carl
  secretly marks her a second time with his own ring just as dawn breaks, inflicting Left to Fester
  on *himself* too. This isn't what kills her — the sunrise does that on its own, as it would to
  any vampire caught outdoors at daybreak — Carl marks her only so he can also collect the ring's
  stat-point reward for a kill that was already guaranteed, and hides the act from Donut, Miriam,
  and Prepotente. A system notification afterward pointedly calls this out: "Someone should
  probably ask Carl why he suddenly has three new points to his intelligence stat. Suspicious!"
- **Super Spreader.** A separate toe ring Carl already owns — the **Enchanted Toe Ring of the
  Leprous Bandit**, looted from a Grull's-blessing quest box during Book 4 (its first ability,
  Sticky Feet, lets him briefly walk on ceilings) — turns out to have a second, previously unused
  ability during Floor 6: it can copy the Ring of Divine Suffering's active mark/debuff onto a
  second target simultaneously.

Prepotente, after Miriam Dom's death, retrieves his own copy of the ring from the ground and
swallows it whole rather than loot it normally — its fate as of the end of Book 5 is unresolved.
