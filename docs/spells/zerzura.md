# Zerzura

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** "The Sledge" — a Cretin mercenary bodyguard, installed with the spell specifically
      because casting it is too taxing to give to a crawler
    - **Effect:** Transfers an entire town or city (buildings and NPCs) down the dungeon —
      normally one floor, but skips floors entirely on "Scolopendra levels"
    - **Obtained:** Negotiated as part of Carl's mercenary-bodyguard package with a kua-tin Borant
      admin, in exchange for surrendering the Gate of the Feral Gods as collateral — Quasar's own
      suggestion
    - **Danger:** Landing location is random and uncontrollable, and the transferred town is
      cursed with an unstoppable, self-propagating revenant horde on arrival (see below)

## Overview

Zerzura is introduced as a rumor — Donut hears the name secondhand and is immediately curious,
and Carl privately recalls "the crazy story Quasar told [him] about Remex the Grand and what he'd
done with the *Zerzura* spell" — well before anyone actually explains what it does. Casting
requirements alone are demanding: it can only be cast at the end of a floor, takes about five
minutes to complete (during which the caster needs active protection), and cannot be re-attempted
if it miscasts. It's also "pretty hard on the caster," which is why Quasar has it installed into a
mercenary (the Sledge) rather than given to Carl or Donut directly.

The basic transfer is simple:

> "It takes a full city or town, NPCs and buildings and all, and it transfers them one floor
> down. It transfers the town itself and all the NPCs. That's it. No mobs or enemy, red-tagged
> NPCs. No stairwells or any other special rooms, either, like prize rooms."

On certain floor types called "Scolopendra levels," the spell skips floors entirely rather than
moving down just one — this is what happened to the historical crawler Remex the Grand, whose
town-cast jumped straight from Floor 6 to Floor 9, skipping 7 and 8.

## The Real Danger: The Children of Inpewt

The spell's popularity with viewers/factions comes from a much darker mechanic Quasar only
reveals after the deal is already made. The destination cannot be steered, and it "almost always"
drops the entire town directly into the worst possible location — typically a monster nest or
boss room — killing everyone on impact. Every one of those deaths immediately resurrects as a
**Child of Inpewt**: a powerful revenant that retains whatever spells/abilities it had in life.
Everyone from the transferred town, plus the caster, is permanently "marked," and the revenants
actively hunt the marked down; anyone they successfully kill also becomes a revenant. The horde
doesn't stop until every marked person — villagers and caster alike — is dead. Remex used the
spell repeatedly to farm easy levels off this mechanic, until his own unlucky Floor 6→9 skip got
his mercenaries and NPCs slaughtered by both revenants and Faction Wars combatants simultaneously.

Carl reacts to the full explanation with open horror, weighing it against the alternative of
simply letting a group of endangered NPCs (including children) be lost to the collapsing floor
instead. The plan settles on casting it on the one remaining town in the area — the High Elf
castle — once a group of refugee changelings has been maneuvered inside it disguised as elves. In
practice, most of those changelings end up escaping separately (shape-shifting into birds and
flying off) after the flying house ferrying them is overrun by mantis nymphs. It's the Sledge
himself, along with the rescued gnome Bonnie and two orphaned Ursine cubs — none of whom make it
onto the flying house — who ends up riding the castle down via *Zerzura* when he finally casts it
in the battle's last moments ("Casting now. One minute to chant spell. Five minutes to cast."),
successfully teleporting all of them to Floor 9 as the castle and its grounds are overrun. Carl's
parting warning to the Sledge — "there might be some zombie things attacking you" — confirms the
"Children of Inpewt" mechanic is indeed expected to trigger, though what actually happens to them
on landing isn't shown within Book 5's pages; the castle itself is last seen having "traveled away"
and landed "half-destroyed" somewhere on Floor 9.

## Scaling

No stated per-level formula for the spell itself. The revenant threat scales with the size and
composition of whatever town is transferred (more NPCs/monsters killed on landing means more
Children of Inpewt), not with the caster's own skill level.

## Book 7: The Second Cast — and How the Princess Posse Wins Faction Wars

The Sledge casts *Zerzura* a second time in **chapter 85**, and this time it is not a desperate
improvisation to save a handful of refugees. It is the win condition.

> "Starting to cast," The Sledge said. It would take five minutes for the spell to work.
>
> He glowed. He had cast *Zerzura*.
>
> **Rosetta: It worked. Shanty Town is included in the glow. It successfully combined the two
> towns.** Moving to the saferoom now with Tipid and the others. I have Samantha.
>
> **The entire funnel city of Larracos—and Shanty Town—was about to get transferred to the 12th
> floor. To the Court of the Ascendency.**

### What moves

The cast takes the whole **funnel city of Larracos plus the newly-built Shanty Town ring**, combined
into one settlement for the spell's purposes, and drops them on a green hill overlooking miles of
cotton fields on the **12th floor**. Because Floor 9 is a Scolopendra level, the spell skips floors
rather than moving down one.

| Moves with the city | Does not move |
|---|---|
| The thousands of NPC residents of Larracos | **Hired mercenaries**, including the Sledge himself and the surviving cretins — returned to "storage" or the mercenary market |
| The rescued **changelings** | **Mordecai** |
| The **Semeru dwarves** | The strippers and sluggalos, deliberately kept and sent back to the saferoom barracks |
| Everyone who moved down from the 6th floor | |
| **Ferdinand**, **Juice Box**, and little Ruby | |

Over **50,000** former crawlers had come to the city; roughly **28,000** leave the dungeon entirely
at the moment the spell triggers.

The Children of Inpewt mechanic is still armed. Anyone crushed by the landing city becomes one.

### Why it wins the war

This is the mechanism, and it is a rules exploit rather than a battle. Ferdinand and Juice Box are
the two surviving warlords of **Team Retribution**. By riding the city out, they abandon their posts:

> **System Message: Both Warlords Ferdinand and Juice Box have left the realm. Under the current
> ruleset, they have abandoned their posts and have forfeited their position.**

Team Retribution is defeated by forfeit. The Princess Posse wins Faction Wars because the enemy
warlords voluntarily got on the bus.

The plan is not a secret by the end — Epitome Tagg says so directly in chapter 83: "*We know what
you're planning on doing with the NPC team. With the Zerzura spell, which will take the city of
Larracos to the 12th floor. I will go with them.*"

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    Book 7 contains **no system description block for *Zerzura***, and only four mentions of the
    spell by name. The Book 5 mechanics above are not restated, contradicted, or expanded. What
    happened to the Floor 6 High Elf castle and the Children of Inpewt horde that followed it down
    is likewise never revisited.
