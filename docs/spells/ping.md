# Ping

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Carl
    - **Obtained:** Book 4 — a platinum quest box tome, awarded after saving Hump Town from the
      *Wasteland*'s bombardment
    - **Mana cost:** 5 mana per cast
    - **Type:** Detection/hunting utility, not offensive

## Effect

Also nicknamed "Here piggy, piggy" or "The Night Dread," *Ping* sends out an audible pulse that
reveals the distance and location of every non-crawler, non-red-tagged mob in range — including
targets outside the caster's normal minimap radius. Anyone hit by the ping hears an audible noise
but can't tell where it came from.

> "Sends out an audible ping that gives the distance and location of all non-crawlers and
> non-red-tagged mobs in a circle around you. It will mark targets beyond the range of your map."

The spell is popular among the elven gunnery officers of a faction called the Dream, and — per the
tome's own description — is dreaded by anyone hiding in a trench, since it reveals their position
without ever revealing the caster's.

## Scaling

- **Range:** 1 kilometer around the caster, plus 500 meters for every 10 points of Intelligence
  (environmental factors and obstacles can increase or decrease this) — confirmed again in Book 5,
  ch. 22: "*My Ping spell had a reach of one kilometer plus 500 meters for every ten points of
  intelligence.*" Book 6, ch. 4 reconfirms the resulting range as "*a little more than three
  kilometers*" by that point, consistent with the same Intelligence-scaling formula, though Carl
  notes city terrain (tall buildings) shrinks the effective range in practice
- **Duration:** Instantaneous; the pulse travels at the speed of sound
- **Cooldown:** 5 minutes (confirmed again Book 5, ch. 32: "*I cursed myself for having wasted my
  Ping spell when the map was down. It had a five-minute cooldown.*")
- **At skill level 5+**, the caster may imbue the ping with [Fear](fear.md) — per the tome's own
  text at acquisition; Carl explicitly plans around this threshold in Book 5, ch. 8 ("*Once I
  trained it up to five, I'd be able to combine the two spells*"), tracking *Ping* at only level
  two at that point. No later book states the moment he actually crosses level 5

No formula or number is ever given for how *Ping*'s effective radius scales with its own skill
level (as opposed to Intelligence) — every range figure on record traces back to the Intelligence
formula above, not to a stated per-level bonus.

The spell explicitly does **not** work on mobs or crawlers, making it most useful against NPCs —
Carl notes it's primarily intended for locating elites and hunters on Floors 6 and 9, rather than
anything on Floor 5 itself.

## Role in the Story

Carl receives the tome as part of a Book-4 platinum quest box and picks it up specifically with
Floor 6's hunting grounds in mind, anticipating the wave of bounty hunters he expects after
publicly declaring war on them at the end of the floor.

## Book 5: In Regular Use

Carl casts *Ping* extensively on Floor 6 exactly as anticipated — locating hidden hunters,
confirming a stuck bounty hunter's exact position in a tree, and coordinating multiple
raid-and-ambush operations against Zockau's satellite towns. He also discovers a countermeasure:
casting a **Major Charlie** ("Brain Juice") intelligence potion beforehand temporarily extends
*Ping*'s range to a full three kilometers, at the cost of a subsequent five-minute Intelligence
penalty once the potion wears off. The spell fails entirely at least once when an unrelated
magical dampening field blankets an entire village, confirming it can be blocked by area effects.

## Book 6 and Beyond

*Ping* stays in regular rotation through Floor 8 (Book 6), used the same way — locating NPCs and
checking whether an area is clear — but with no further mechanical changes shown. By Book 7, the
spell has spread beyond Carl entirely: Florin describes staffing border towers with "*a group of
four or five folks with proper observation and Ping spells*" as a standard Faction Wars scouting
tactic, confirming it is a known, teachable spell rather than something unique to him. Carl's own
Floor 9 hunting plans for it, floated when he first obtained the tome in Book 4, are never shown
paying off on-page in the specific way he originally anticipated.
