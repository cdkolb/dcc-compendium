# Why Are You Hitting Yourself?

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Princess Donut
    - **Obtained:** Granted automatically with her Floor 8 Character Actor class,
      **Master Telephone Psychic**
    - **Type:** Offensive charm / self-harm compulsion
    - **Effect:** Makes the target attack itself
    - **Debuff icon:** A head with little wavy lines over it

## Overview

Donut picks Master Telephone Psychic over Mordecai's other suggestion (Agony Urchin) on the
grounds that the urchin class "changes the way I look, and I will never pick a class that changes
the way I look." Mordecai sells the psychic option on two points: it improves her mapping, and it
gives her "some offensive charm spells." Donut's own summary of the haul, shouted in chat: "**I
GOT A SPELL CALLED *BRAIN FREEZE* AND ONE CALLED *BAD ATTITUDE* AND ANOTHER CALLED *WHY ARE YOU
HITTING YOURSELF?* THAT ONE IS GREAT! IT MAKES PEOPLE STAB THEMSELVES IN THE FACE!**"

It does roughly what the name promises. The target howls, spins, and starts savagely biting or
punching itself while its own health drains — no damage contribution required from the party. The
first test subject, a beret-wearing "legendary creature" mob on the Havana streets, collapses on
its side while Carl watches and thinks: *Donut did that, and she barely had to lift a paw.*

## Training on Spiders

The spell gets an unusually large amount of practice because of an unrelated quest. Huanxin Jinx's
spider-hunting quest makes every tiny spider mob in the region visible on the party's map, and only
requires five kills a day between Carl and Donut. Donut kills them by the dozens anyway,
specifically because casting *Why Are You Hitting Yourself?* on a spider and watching it spin in
circles trying to bite itself delights her. Carl's assessment: "*It was kind of fucked up, but it
was also training the spell.*"

## Notable Uses

- **Benchmark for how bad things are** — when the boss Shi Maria turns half a group of reindeer
  puppets on each other with no warning, Carl's frame of reference is that they look "like [they'd]
  been hit by that enraged debuff and Donut's *Why Are You Hitting Yourself* spell at the same
  time"
- **Amayon, prince of Sheol (ch. 70)** — the spell's high-water mark. It lands on a **level-250**
  demon prince in his true, full-power form, and it *works*: mid-sentence, Amayon balls his fist
  and punches himself in the face hard enough to shake the world. It holds long enough for Carl to
  finish an entire negotiation while dangling in the demon's hand, and its expiry is announced as
  its own beat — "*The Why are you Hitting Yourself? spell timed out*" — after which the demon
  sighs with relief and immediately resumes trying to kill him

## Scaling

No numeric formula appears. Duration is clearly finite and is treated as a countdown Carl has to
talk fast inside of. The Amayon cast is the important datapoint: the spell landed on a target
roughly 190 levels above the caster, which is far outside what any of Donut's other charm effects
manage in this book.
