# War Crime

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Princess Donut
    - **Type:** **Atrocity** — a formal spell class (see
      [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for Atrocity-class spells generally)
    - **Obtained:** Chapter 29, from the Legendary **1914 box** awarded for killing Princess D'Nadia
    - **Cost:** **1 mana point**
    - **Cost, actually:** Casting drops the caster to **1% health**
    - **Uses:** **3 times in a lifetime**, once per floor, and only on Scolopendra levels
      (3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18)
    - **Starting level:** 5, rising by 5 with each cast
    - **Cannot kill.** Not "usually doesn't." Cannot

*War Crime* combines two of the caster's own existing spells into a single effect, in a way the
dungeon chooses. It is the most expensive spell in the series by every measure except mana, where it
costs almost nothing — which is the joke, and the warning.

## Acquisition

Donut opens the 1914 box in chapter 29 and reports the contents over chat in her usual register:

> **Donut: DID YOU OPEN YOUR 1914 BOX YET? I GOT A SPELLBOOK CALLED WAR CRIME. I'M NOT SURE IF I
> SHOULD READ IT OR NOT. THE BOOK ITSELF IS BLEEDING AND CRYING AND IT'S DISGUSTING.**

The same box gives Carl *[Run, Little Günter, Run](run-little-gunter-run.md)*. Both books share an
unusual property noted in chapter 40: unlike every other object in the dungeon, they do not register
on the map at all — "*It doesn't appear as a monster or NPC on the map. It doesn't appear at all.*"

Carl's own reaction to the description is dread, and it is about Donut rather than about the enemy:

> When I had first read it, the AI had been especially enthusiastic, which immediately set off my
> alarm bells. The more I thought about the spell and its implications—both direct and implied—I'd
> been overwhelmed with a sense of horror. **Horror at how using this spell might affect Donut**,
> especially if we used it to its full potential.

He also looks the word up, in Florin's bullet-holed 1800s dictionary: an atrocity is "*an act of
fiendish cruelty.*" His conclusion is that the real atrocity was handing the spell to Donut.

## The Precedent

The description then supplies the only other recorded use of the spell in the history of Dungeon
Crawler World, and it is the passage that explains what "atrocity" means in practice. An Operatic
warlord, standing on a circle of power, combined **Fireball** and **Paralysis** and hit roughly 200
mantid-team mercenaries at once.

> The victims were paralyzed permanently. They were set on fire permanently. They were each blown
> off in an arc away from the caster where they didn't stop until they hit something. For some of
> those poor mercenaries, that was very, very far.
>
> They remained there, paralyzed and on fire and undying for the remainder of the floor. **The
> caster of the spell received an experience point update for every hour or so of their suffering.**
>
> Incidentally, the wuss of an AI in charge of that season decided to put the on-fire NPC
> mercenaries out of their misery once the floor finally collapsed about two weeks later.

The description closes with a coda about that caster needing an identity reset afterward and part of
his essence eventually forming into the Hortense Leadership Unit — "*I'd like to think that's why he
has a twitch to him*" — which Carl notes did not appear for anyone but himself.

## Scaling

| Cast | Level | Spells combined |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 5 | **2** |
| 2nd | 10 | **3** |
| 3rd | 15 | **4** |

The spell cannot be trained. Level is purely a function of how many times it has been cast, and
there are only three casts, ever. Component spells must be **at least level 8**; anything above that
is ignored, so there is no benefit to feeding it a level-15 spell over a level-8 one.

The compensation for losing the two component spells for the rest of the floor is permanent: on
later floors those spells can be **chained and cast simultaneously for the cost of one**, and every
spell added by a later *War Crime* cast joins the same mix-and-match pool.

## The Nest Tower

*War Crime*'s stated area of effect is small. What makes it a weapon of mass destruction in Book 7 is
a **synergy tower**.

The party built the **Nest** intending it to be a sniper tower, to give Donut long-range targeting
from height. The [Tiara of a Thousand Lights](../items/tiara-of-a-thousand-lights.md) made that
redundant — she could already hit anything she could see — so the synergy slot was reassigned:

> The Nest was instead built to have synergy with something called **Atrocity-class spells**.
>
> Donut had several, including her new one. War Crime. **It was the one part of today's plan I
> prayed we wouldn't have to use.**

Carl's summary of the combination:

> Donut's *War Crime* spell was both simple and horrific in both usage and scope. And while the area
> of effect was relatively low, **the synergy tower's ability to upgrade Atrocity spells and allow
> them to be cast over a wide area made War Crime a true spell of mass destruction.**

## The One Cast — Chapter 76

**Components: *[Bijanbi](bijanbi.md)* + *[Astral Paw](astral-paw.md)*.**

The choice of *Bijanbi* is why Donut gets a tattoo at all. The plan needs a mass-drag spell at level
8 or better and she does not own one; Rosetta's team extracts *Bijanbi* from a fallen cleric of
Wepwawet and Edgar inks it onto Donut's right flank, after which Milk boosts it from level 2 to 15
with Words of Power calligraphy. See
[The Toraline Ink Tattoo System](../items/toraline-tattoo-transfer.md).

Rosetta's design intent, laid out in chapter 74, includes the finisher the spell's no-kill clause
demands:

> "With them both amplified by War Crime, it should separate the enemies all out and drop them into
> a ring of fire. Fleshers are fire resistant, but symbiotes aren't, so if any enemies resist, they
> should be pounced upon by the fleshers, turned to symbiotes, and then killed. **That's the
> finisher since War Crime keeps them alive.** It'll kill the enemy and any potential baby war
> mages."

The cast itself:

> "Casting now!" Donut shouted.
>
> **System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has committed a third Atrocity.**
>
> **System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has received a new class.**
>
> **System Message: Warlord Princess Donut will now be referred to as The Oak Fell.**
>
> **System Message: The Oak Fell has been named Champion of Nekhebit.**

The effect: the world freezes, "Break Stuff" plays, and thousands of frozen elves, gnolls and Reaver
cyborgs — plus the feral god **Meatus** — float upward beneath "the massive, glowing outline of a
cat's paw," covered in fleshers and dissolving. Donut's health drops to 1% and Carl heals her with a
scroll.

The finisher does not go to plan. Li Na builds a lattice of chains and messages *"Donut, this is all
I can build. Do it."* Donut has to close the paw herself:

> "It's the finisher... **I shouldn't have picked Astral Paw. I have to do it myself.**" … "**I can
> feel them, Carl. I can feel them under my paw.**"
>
> She closed her eyes, and she swiped downward with three quick baps.

Carl's dread in chapter 74 — horror at what the spell might do *to Donut* — is the part that lands.
Elle's verdict afterward is the counterweight: "*You are a goddamn hero.*"

## Aftermath

- Red ash rains over Shanty Town for some time afterward (ch. 78)
- **Warlord Fang survives**, because he is biological material inside a robot (ch. 81)
- King Rust's harem women were pulled into the ball but "*not captured as enemies by Donut's War
  Crime spell*" (ch. 82)
- Casting three Atrocities in one floor triggers Donut's new **Oak Fell** class, which Mordecai and
  Rosetta both say they have never seen awarded that way

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    The system message says "**a third Atrocity**" — *War Crime* is Donut's third atrocity *cast* of
    the floor, after *[Rolling Battle Formation](rolling-battle-formation.md)* and
    *[You're Not Done Yet](youre-not-done-yet.md)*. It is the **first and only** cast of *War Crime*
    itself. Two of its three lifetime charges remain.
