# Profane Iron

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Original owner:** **Li Na**
    - **Borrowed by:** Carl, via a **chain-shaped dragon tattoo** on his right forearm, inked by
      **Katia** in chapter 40
    - **Level:** **13**
    - **Frequency:** Once a day
    - **Effect:** A dozen-plus prehensile chains erupt from the user's own flesh
    - **Classified as:** A defensive skill. Nobody uses it that way

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **No system description block exists for this ability in Book 7.** The narration of its one use,
    quoted in full below, is the only description the book gives.

## Acquisition

*Profane Iron* is the first ability transferred by
[the toraline ink tattoo system](../items/toraline-tattoo-transfer.md), and the transfer that
establishes its rules. Li Na gives blood **freely**, which makes the copy temporary and costs her
only a reversible five levels:

> "She says it didn't hurt at all. It's not permanent, unlike what happened with her brother. She
> lost five levels temporarily, but she can still use it. **Intention matters.**"

Because the source ability is level 13, the tattoo has to be large: "*Since this is a level-13 skill,
it has to be pretty big. I have to use almost all the ink in the vial, otherwise you won't get it at
full strength.*" Carl picks the design himself — "*It has to be a chain. A chain shaped like a
dragon*" — and the system grades Katia's first-ever tattoo as **expert quality**, the second-highest
rating available.

Donut, following by Voodoo book, is unimpressed: "*You're starting to look like one of those white
rapper doodle notebook guys, Carl. And it's a little crooked!*"

## What It Does

Carl activates it exactly once, in chapter 55, in a corridor full of mantaurs:

> I pulled two more *Black Nimbus* potions as I activated the skill I'd received from Li Na.
>
> ***Profane Iron.***
>
> **I felt my own skin tear as dozens of chains ripped from my flesh. I took a tiny bit of a hit to
> my own health.** Two of the chains reached down and plucked the potions from my hands. Each one
> shot out in a different direction and smashed into a different mantaur… A third chain wrapped
> around the neck of another, and a fourth tripped two more. Two more struck, scorpion-like at the
> closest, piercing directly into the guard's flesh at his clavicles…

The fight is over in seconds, which is fortunate, because the skill does not last long. The
comparison against the original owner is the useful part:

> **Li Na had several chain spells and skills, and this was different from her regular ones. This
> was a once-a-day, multiple chains at once skill. I could only do it for about thirty seconds. She
> said she could now do it for about a minute and a half. I could only truly control two of the
> chains. She could control ten. She used it to attack multiple enemies at once, but it was actually
> designed as a defensive skill, like a type of shield.**

That paragraph is the clearest statement in the series of what a *borrowed* ability actually costs.
Carl has the same skill at the same level and gets roughly a **third of the duration** and **a fifth
of the control**. The chains all appear; only two of them do what he tells them to. The rest thrash.

It also **damages the user** on activation — the chains tear out through his own skin — which is a
small health hit but a permanent feature of the ability, not a side effect of borrowing it.

## Scaling

No per-level table is given, and the spell is never mentioned again after chapter 55 — including
no appearance anywhere in Book 8. What the text establishes is that level is not the whole story:
at identical level 13, the owner and the borrower get materially different results in **duration**
and **number of controllable chains**. Practised familiarity, not skill level, is the variable, and
there's no stated formula connecting level to either duration or chain-count — just the one
paired data point (Carl vs. Li Na, both level 13) above. No level-15 cast or cap-break is recorded
for this skill.

## Intended Use vs. Actual Use

The last line of Carl's assessment — "*it was actually designed as a defensive skill, like a type of
shield*" — is worth holding onto. Li Na uses it offensively. Carl uses it offensively. Nobody in the
book uses it as a shield. The original plan for the tattoo, before events overtook it in chapter 55,
was explicitly offensive too: "*to activate Li Na's tattoo skill—which I still hadn't used—to kill
all the nagas.*"
