# Tripper

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Carl
    - **Type:** Trap-detonation utility — item-based spell, no mana cost
    - **Source:** [Drakea's Enchanted Kerchief of Disorder](../items/drakeas-enchanted-kerchief-of-disorder.md)
      (the bandana), Book 4 — "**Wearer may cast a level-15 Tripper spell once every five hours**"
    - **Item-granted level:** Flat **15**, fixed to the item, 5-hour cooldown
    - **Underlying spell level:** Trained independently to **7** by Book 7, discovered only when
      the bandana came off — 10-minute cooldown at that level
    - **Also lent out:** Tattoo-transferred to **Imani** in Book 7 at an effective **level 9**
      (15 − 6, both numbers stated on-page; the resulting "9" itself is simple arithmetic, not a
      quoted figure)

## Effect

Instantly triggers every trap within the spell's area of effect at once — landmines, alarm
modules, snare traps, and any other device, regardless of who placed it or whether a target has
stepped on it. It works just as well on traps Carl never set himself: he uses it to clear a path
through an unfamiliar minefield of hunter-planted alarm traps around the Zockau stairwell on his
very first day on the floor, and later to blow through a nest of naiad castle traps he'd never
seen before while riding in Miriam Dom's cart.

## Scaling

*Tripper* turns out to have **two separate levels running at once** — a discovery Carl doesn't
make until Book 7, ch. 47:

| Level | Book / ch. | What's confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| **15** (item-granted, flat) | Book 4, ch. 28 (grant); reconfirmed Book 7, ch. 33 | The bandana's own text: "*Wearer may cast a level-15 Tripper spell once every five hours.*" At level 15 the radius is described as "very wide." This level is fixed to the item and doesn't reflect Carl's own trained skill |
| **7** (underlying spell, trained) | Book 7, ch. 47 | "*when I took off the bandana, I learned my Tripper spell had raised on its own to level 7*" — Carl's actual spell-list entry had been training up independently the entire time, invisible behind the item's flat-15 display. Cooldown at this level is **10 minutes**, versus the item's 5 hours — the two versions can't share cooldowns |
| **90% detonation chance** (external data point) | Book 7, ch. 33 | Not Carl's own cast, but the same spell at the same flat level: King Rust's level-15 *Tripper* gives "each trap a 90% chance to blow anyway" even against traps built to resist it — the clearest number the text gives for what a level-15 cast can do against hardened defenses |

!!! note "Confirmed formula: none stated"
    Unlike *Wisp Armor* or *Bang Bro*, no per-level radius or duration formula is ever given for
    *Tripper* — level 15's "very wide radius" and the 90% figure above are the only concrete
    numbers on record, and there's no stated rate connecting level 7 to level 15's performance.

### The Imani Transfer (Book 7, ch. 47)

When Katia's toraline tattoo system makes borrowing possible, Carl can't just hand over the
bandana (fairy-class crawlers can't wear it), so he tattoos the *spell* onto Imani instead:

> "*I couldn't give Imani the item, but I could still let her borrow the spell in the form of a
> tattoo. Since it was a lower level spell, the tattoo didn't need to be big. When I drank the
> associated potion and selected the spell, I lost six levels when Katia drew the blood. That was
> irrelevant because the bandana itself brought the spell up to 15 either way.*"

Carl's own item-independent copy drops from 15 to 9 (a flat **–6** on the blood draw, both numbers
stated on-page — the "9" is arithmetic, not a quote) but the bandana's flat grant papers over the
loss for him personally. Imani receives that computed level-9 copy, which "would slowly lose
potency" and eventually fade entirely, while Carl's own dip "heals" back over **four days**. This is the same borrowing mechanic documented on
[Profane Iron](profane-iron.md), and it's the clearest confirmation that *Tripper*'s two levels
(item-flat-15 vs. trained-spell-7) are tracked completely separately by the system.

## Role in the Story — Book 5

Carl casts *Tripper* repeatedly on Floor 6 as an offensive weapon, most notably during raids on
hunter-held towns: cast alongside [Ping](ping.md), it sets off dozens of alarm and landmine traps
simultaneously to disorient a group of hunters right before Carl's seeker spider automatons move
in. He also uses it mid-escape from his opening solo raid on Zockau, deliberately triggering an
unavoidable alarm trap along with every other trap nearby rather than being caught by just the one.
While riding in Miriam Dom's cart toward a village, Prepotente warns of a set of decapitation traps
guarding the town's entrance — and insults Carl in the same breath — prompting Carl to cast
*Tripper* on the spot, both clearing the traps and startling Prepotente into a scream.

By Book 7, Faction Wars has turned it into a recognized threat rather than just Carl's personal
trick: border defenses are built specifically to be "Tripper-proof," Florin recruits a trap-master
NPC explicitly to counter it, and Carl still runs into traps flagged "**This trap is Tripper
proof**" when the system decides it should fail.

## Level Cap — Not Broken

Neither of *Tripper*'s two tracked levels ever exceeds 15, and Carl's own trained copy (level 7)
never gets close. Contrast this with [Mind Balance](../abilities/mind-balance.md), which the wiki
documents as the first skill in the series pushed **past** the level-15 training cap by sustained
battlefield conditions — *Tripper* is a useful case of the *opposite*: an item quietly holding a
skill artificially at the cap while the "real" version underneath trains up slowly and separately,
never threatening to overtake it on-page.
