# Laundry Day

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Princess Donut
    - **Obtained:** A separate, ordinary spell gifted by Donut's Floor 6 "spellbook-of-the-floor
      club" — not part of her Legendary Diva bard kit, and not sung
    - **Type:** Armor-stripping debuff

## Effect

A spell that strips a target's equipped armor away. Its scaling thresholds are explicit:

| Spell level | Effect |
|---|---|
| 5 | Strips a target's chest armor |
| 10 | Strips a target's entire outfit |
| 15 | Can strip multiple targets at once |

Crucially, when cast on a mortal host possessed by a god, the spell can treat the god's own
presence/possession as the target's equipped "armor" rather than trying to strip gear off the god
directly. Stripping actual equipment off the god itself would need the spell at level 20 (which
Donut can never reach, capped at 15) and simply isn't feasible — but treating the god as the
"armor" worn by the person underneath only requires the ordinary level-15 threshold for stripping a
*person's* gear. That distinction is the whole trick: the level-15 cast leaves the god itself
completely unharmed while banishing it from its host and exposing the now-unprotected mortal
underneath.

## Scaling

Donut trains the spell to level 9 on her own through Book 5, then uses a **Pawna's Cries** potion
(+3 to a chosen spell) to push it to 12, and finally, during the climactic battle, a rarer
**Pawna's Tears** potion (+5) to reach level 17 — overshooting the level 15 actually needed,
following an explicit instruction from Carl's sponsor to use exactly this combination for a
specific upcoming fight.

## Role in the Story — Book 5

At the Butcher's Masquerade's climax, Donut casts *Laundry Day* (boosted to level 17) on the
possessed form of **[Circe Took](../characters/circe-took.md)**, who is wearing the goddess Diwata
as a borrowed divine "armor." The spell strips Diwata's protection away from Circe's mortal mantis
body without harming the goddess herself, forcing Diwata to return to the Halls of the Ascendency
and exposing Circe to be killed moments later by her own children.

## Book 6: Half of "Phantasm"

By Floor 8 the spell sits at level 15 and acquires a permanent partner. The moment Donut reads the
tome for *[Legionnaires of the Damned](legionnaires-of-the-damned.md)*, Mordecai spots the
combination: strip the armor off a group of mobs with *Laundry Day*, then reanimate that same armor
with wailing spirits and set it on them — "*they will tear through the unarmored monsters like
wildfire.*"

Carl names the two-spell move **Phantasm**. Donut objects to the name every time he says it. Its
signature use is the Asojano fight, where the "armor" stripped is a purpose-built
**Scrapheap Breastplate** Carl assembled out of loose metal and trash-tier daggers and put on the
cretin mercenary **Bomo** — engineering a room full of freshly-unequipped, individually-animatable
pieces on demand.

A totem's version of the same effect appears later in the book: **Jola the Yule Cat**'s
***Fashion Critic!***, which explodes every item off a target at once. Carl's assessment is that
it's "pretty much the same spell as Donut's *Laundry Day* except the cat was going to literally eat
the clothes if we let him."

## Book 8: Ejecting the Maestro from Grull

!!! note "Corrected — not Emberus"
    An earlier draft of this catalog's brief misremembered this scene as involving Emberus. It does
    not. The god possessed/ridden in this scene is **Grull**, and the possessing entity ejected is
    an orc called **the Maestro** — not a mortal wearing a god's power like Circe Took in Book 5, but
    the reverse: an orc apparently lodged inside the god's own chest.

Cornered by Grull mid-race on Floor 10, with the god about to kill the mantaur Corcunda for breaking
faith, Donut casts *Laundry Day* directly on the god himself:

> Grull raised his axe over his head. "I'm gonna enjoy this, Meat!" Bubbles started to form around
> his shoulders. "After all this time, and you're going to die like the little bitch that you . . .
> Gah!"
>
> Donut finished casting *Laundry Day*.
>
> The orc was ejected from the god's chest. He dropped like a rock into the river of metal below.

The Maestro can't be killed here — Floor 10's rules don't allow tourist deaths on this floor, so he
simply regenerates a few minutes later, presumably back at Grull's own quarters. Grull himself is
entirely unharmed and unslowed by the cast; the spell only strips the orc riding inside him, buying
the party a brief window rather than ending the fight. This is functionally the same
armor-vs-possession distinction established in Book 5 (stripping a god's presence from a host
without harming the god) run in reverse — here it's the god's own body that gets something stripped
*out* of it, rather than a mortal host losing a god's protection.
