# You're Not Done Yet

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Princess Donut
    - **Type:** **Atrocity**
    - **Obtained:** Chapter 3, from a Legendary Quest box for the "Hell Comes to Crawler Town" quest
    - **Effect:** Rips the skin off nearby corpses, creating autonomous **fleshers** that hunt and
      attack anyone marked as an enemy
    - **In-universe significance:** This spell is the stated origin of **war mages** as a creature
      type

Mordecai's one-line summary is the closest thing to a definition the book gives: "*It's similar to
Donut's [Second Chance](second-chance.md), but it's a lot more insidious.*"

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **No system description block for this spell exists in Book 7.** Everything below is drawn from
    narration, Mordecai's explanation, the AI's war-mage trivia interstitial, and the cast itself.

## The Book

The tome arrives in the same chapter-3 unboxing as everything else Donut gets for Floor 9, alongside
a cat-sized kilt, and it is the only item in the sequence that frightens Mordecai:

> The book was a black-hued tome that clanked heavily to the table. It looked as if it had been
> burned. Purple wisps of smoke rose off it.
>
> "My word," Donut said, crinkling her face. "It smells just awful. Like whenever you tried to burn
> your microwave popcorn on purpose, Carl."
>
> "Fuuuck," Mordecai whispered, voice filled with awe. **He took a step back, as if he was afraid of
> the book itself.**
>
> "What is it?" I asked. Little black tendrils, like roots, started to spread out from the book,
> moving across the table.
>
> "It's similar to Donut's *Second Chance*, but it's a lot more insidious." He gave a shiver. "It's
> called *You're Not Done Yet*."

Carl half-recognises the name and cannot place it. The book "*let out a little screech*" as it
disappears into Donut's inventory.

It is one of four spellbooks Donut picks up in chapter 3, the other three being the far more
mundane *Flak*, *Shield* and *Sentry*.

## What a Flesher Is

The spell's output is a level-1 mob called a **Flesher**. The AI's chapter-61 trivia interstitial is
the only full account of what they are and what they become, and it is also the passage that makes
this spell historically important:

> Want to know some interesting trivia about war mages? These guys have been around since the very
> early days of Dungeon Crawler World, but they are one of the few, **true dungeon-born entities**.
> They weren't dreamed up by a sweaty, caffeine-addled writer, nor were they developed by one of my
> kind. **They just sort of happened, a result of too many combatants having access to You're Not
> Done Yet.**
>
> The final form a war mage takes is entirely dependent on the combination of its first two forms…
> **The skin of a corpse is ripped off of a body and turned to a creature named a flesher. The
> flesher then consumes a living being, melting the original skin off and re-sleeving the creature.
> The flesher casts *Boned*, reanimating the skeleton, and the two become a symbiote.**
>
> Symbiotes are extremely attractive targets to other fleshers. And because young symbiotes are slow
> and awkward, they are oftentimes quickly re-sleeved by fresh fleshers. **Each iteration results in
> a more powerful being.**
>
> It's rare for a symbiote to be resleeved at least a dozen times, but when it happens, **the magic
> itself can become sapient. When this happens, the flesh finally fuses to the reanimated skeleton,
> turning the three creatures—the skeleton, the flesher, and the sapient well of magic—into a single
> being. A war mage.**
>
> This process usually takes at least 12 iterations, but there's no hard rule about it. Sometimes it
> takes up to 15 or 16 iterations. It depends on the latent magic in the skin of the fleshers.

So the spell does not summon war mages. It creates the raw material from which war mages
*accidentally accrete*, over enough iterations, and the entire war mage faction on Floor 9 exists
because enough people cast this spell enough times across enough seasons. Fleshers and war mages are
covered in more detail on the Mobs pages.

Fleshers are **fire resistant**; symbiotes are not, which is the basis of the finisher used later
with *[War Crime](war-crime.md)*.

## Use — Chapters 65 to 69

The party plans around it for several chapters before casting. Every guild donates graveyard corpses
to Bodi and the 103rd; the bodies are hauled out and dumped across the battlefield in advance so
there is something to skin. Donut's constraint, in her usual all-caps:

> **YOU'RE NOT DONE YET ISN'T GOING TO WORK IF THERE ARE NO TARGETS… THE BACK SIDE OF THE MADNESS
> CASTLE IS JUST PAST MY RANGE FOR ATROCITY MAGIC.**

The cast, chapter 69, with the Nest tower's Atrocity synergy applied:

> At the same moment, Donut cast the spell. **She did, indeed, cast a tower-enhanced version of
> You're Not Done Yet.** And the moment she did, the entire world in front of us burst into
> activity. The Xs on the map all started to blink.
>
> **System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has committed another Atrocity. Wow. What a naughty
> kitty. I sure hope she isn't forced to cast a third.**

Thousands of fleshers swarm outward, seeking anything marked as an enemy, including the enormous
rolling ball created by
*[Rolling Battle Formation](rolling-battle-formation.md)*. The AI's parting sentence is a
foreshadow: she is forced to cast a third atrocity seven chapters later, and it is
*[War Crime](war-crime.md)*.

Carl makes one grim intervention during the sequence: he kills the flesher-wrapped **Nami** with a
hob-lobber specifically to stop another war mage being born out of her.

## Scaling

No level is ever stated and no per-level behavior is described. The two variables shown in play are:

1. **Corpse supply** — the spell is worthless without bodies, which is why the party spends chapters
   stockpiling them
2. **Range**, which Donut can extend enormously by casting from an Atrocity-synergy tower
