# Conscript

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** **Doctor Bones**, the skin-skellie bard and Desperado Club strip-show announcer,
      hired into Carl's mercenary barracks after Astrid's death
    - **Type:** Military charm
    - **Effect:** Forces the target to fight on the caster's side
    - **Duration:** "If they're weak, it pretty much lasts forever" — permanent on the
      weak-minded, shorter or ineffective on stronger subjects
    - **Limitation:** Does not work on anyone already pledged to a specific army

## Overview

Carl catches Doctor Bones practising "that mind control thing" in the mercenary arcade and asks
what it is. The skeleton's answer is matter-of-fact: **"It's a type of military charm. It only
works on the weak minded. That is why I am training with it."** He is deliberately honing it
upward so it will hold against stronger subjects.

Asked how it compares to Donut's *[Minion Army](minion-army.md)*, Doctor Bones draws the line
cleanly: *Minion Army* is battlefield sabotage — effective, but slow to cast and temporary.
*Conscript* is neither. It is slow to train and hard to land, but when it lands on the right target
it simply never wears off.

## Why It Matters: Faction Wars

Carl's reaction to hearing the name is "a sudden sense of revulsion," because he already knows the
spell from the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook. This is the spell used against crawlers on the ninth
floor:

> It was used quite a bit against crawlers on the ninth floor. It wouldn't work if they'd already
> pledged themselves to a specific army. Most factions spent a lot of resources making sure they
> had somebody who could cast that spell at level-15. **Part of the reason why we bought the
> Faction Wars team was to negate their ability to basically enslave crawlers to their cause.**

That last clause retroactively explains one of the book's biggest expenditures. Buying the Princess
Posse's own Faction Wars team slot was never only about getting a seat at the table — a crawler
pledged to a faction is immune to *Conscript*, so owning a team is the only reliable defence
against the other nine factions simply charming the remaining crawlers into their own armies.

## Carl's Sluggalo Infantry

Having learned what the spell does, Carl immediately turns it around. He locks Doctor Bones and
the "strippers" — the Club Vanquisher dancers hired as mercenaries — into the mercenary arcade
together and has the bard *Conscript* a squad of slug mobs born inside the arcade, building an
infantry force of roughly forty hatchet-wielding **sluggalos** out of nothing. He buys the
five-million-gold barracks upgrade (20 → 100 slots) to hold as many of them as possible.

His own assessment of what he's bought is not flattering:

> **The *Conscript* spell is far from perfect. It makes them fight on our side, sort of. It doesn't
> really change their attitude, and they can be rebellious. It's kinda like turning them into
> totems. We'll probably have to kill the highest level ones, but the rest are going into our
> infantry.**

This is borne out at the FOCSA tower, where the entire sluggalo squad ignores the plan, screams
"Chop, chop, bitches!" and charges a mass of demons unprompted, then routs and runs back into the
city. Carl notes one thing the cookbook authors before him — including Priestly — apparently never
knew: during an actual Faction Wars, a warlord can open the warlord menu and **force conscripted
troops back into line**, which would have prevented exactly that.

## Scaling

No numeric formula. Level matters in two stated ways: factions invest heavily in having a
**level-15** caster, which is treated as the practical ceiling; and the target's mental strength
sets whether the effect is permanent, temporary, or fails outright. Doctor Bones' training is
aimed squarely at raising the tier of subject he can hold.

## Book 7: Level 18, and It Works on Gods

Book 6 set *Conscript* up as the reason Carl had to buy a Faction Wars team at all. Book 7 makes it
one of the central plot mechanisms of Floor 9 — and blows past the level-15 ceiling the previous book
treated as the limit.

### The ordinary spell on Floor 9

Faction Wars conscription in practice, as Book 7 documents it:

- Any crawler **not already recruited** when the ceasefire ends is vulnerable (ch. 1)
- The effect is control, not loyalty: conscripts "*wouldn't be able to attack officers directly. We
  could force them to follow orders. We would get a notification if they ran away*" (ch. 27)
- Conscripted crawlers show as a **light blue glow** on the map (ch. 68)
- Refusing to fight while conscripted inflicts "*a nasty, no-healing debuff*" — Tuba nearly bleeds out
  from it (ch. 68)
- Carl knows workarounds: "*The Conscription spell was far from perfect, and I knew of several tricks
  one could employ to hinder an army's efforts even with the debuff active*" (ch. 58)
- The war mages break their own allegiance with "*a clever combination of Pull spells*" to kill Prince
  Stalwart (ch. 63)
- Non-combatants — King Rust's harem, for instance — cannot be conscripted at all (ch. 82)

The one hard personal defence Carl has is **Mind Balance**, from his
[Enchanted Toe Ring of the Well-Balanced](../items/enchanted-toe-ring-of-the-well-balanced.md):
"*Mind Balance, Level 5. Allows protection from Enslavement and Conscription.*"

A Faction Wars ruling in chapter 68 also extends the spell to a new claimant:

> "Action item two. '**The War Mage Rebellion be given full access to the Conscription spell and all
> its benefits.**' … This request has been granted. The Conscription spell will now allow
> unaffiliated players to be conscripted into the War Mage Rebellion."

### The Hell-Kissed Potion, level 18

The escalation arrives as a **potion**, not a spell, and it is the only verbatim description Book 7
gives for anything in this family. See
[Hell-Kissed Potion of Conscription](../items/hell-kissed-potion-of-conscription.md) for the full
item page.

> **Hell-Kissed Potion of Conscription, level 18.**
>
> **This works like a regular potion of Conscription, but it has been hell-kissed. At level 18, it
> will work against pretty much any non-affiliated entities, regardless of their protections,
> including most deities. Most.** It was brewed in the dark recesses of the Nothing by **Krakaren
> Prime**, who is said to yearn for domination, which was why she'd been banished there in the first
> place. She prepares for a war she considers inevitable. There's an irony that the in-dungeon
> version of Krakaren has supplied these potions to the War Mages…
>
> **Conscription potions only work on the 9th, 12th, 15th, 17th, and 18th floors.**

Baroness Victory's read on the risk, which is the reason this is not simply a free win:

> "They're using potions, not the spell. They'll have to be hell-kissed versions. Those will be level
> 18. But, it also means **using the potion is likely suicide for the caster if the level disparity
> is too high.**"

### Three gods conscripted

| Deity | Chapter | By whom | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yarilo** | 69 | An unnamed war mage | Worked — and "*the moment he cast the spell, the war mage guy just blew up!*" |
| **Lucia Mar** | 71 | **Carl**, smashing the potion against her head | Worked. **Under Orders** appears over her; she is physically prevented from attacking him |
| **Harpocrates** | 72 | **Li Na** | Worked, and she survived. "*Warlord Message: Harpocrates has been successfully conscripted. Harpocrates, level 250 is now a member of the Princess Posse.*" |

Carl gets his potion because Li Na **duplicated** the one looted from the war mage who attacked
Harpocrates, using Li Jun's scroll of potion duplication. She used the original herself.

Carl's read on Lucia afterward: "*There were ways to break conscription, as the war mages figured out,
but it was all but impossible during the first few days. Especially with this version of the
Conscription spell.*" And the hard limit: "*The conscription only lasts until the end of this
floor.*"

Chapter 74 shows the strain — Li Na is "*literally riding Harpocrates like he was a mount… the god
was clearly enraged by this, but so far the conscription held*" — and the practical constraint: "*I
think Harpocrates is fighting the Conscription, so we need to do this now… he's harder to control when
I'm not near him.*"

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    Book 7 contains **no system description block for the *Conscript* spell itself**. The level-18
    potion's description is the only quoted text. The spell's own rules in Book 7 are reconstructed
    from scattered prose.
