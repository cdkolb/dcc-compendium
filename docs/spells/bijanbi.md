# Bijanbi (formerly "Heel")

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Caster:** Princess Donut
    - **Obtained:** Tattooed onto her right flank by **Edgar** in chapter 74 — see
      [The Toraline Ink Tattoo System](../items/toraline-tattoo-transfer.md)
    - **Original owner:** A **cleric of the god Wepwawet** who fell defending the FUPA. The spell was
      granted to him through worship
    - **Effect:** A wide-area drag — pulls multiple enemies toward a designated location
    - **Level:** Arrives at **2**; boosted temporarily to **15** by Milk's Words of Power calligraphy
    - **Renamed:** "*It used to be called Heel, but apparently the translation makes it sound like
      another spell, so this season it's called Bijanbi.*"

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    **There is no system description block for this spell anywhere in Book 7.** The only definition
    is Carl's inference by comparison, quoted below. The text also spells it **"Binjabi"** in chapter
    76, where the spell is actually used; **"Bijanbi"** in chapter 74 is the spelling Rosetta gives
    when naming it. Both spellings appear once each.

## What It Does

Carl works it out by triangulating against two spells he already knows:

> *Hamzo* was a spell that **stunned and then summoned an enemy to your location**. Katia had it.
> *Dinner Bell* was similar, but it **worked in a wide range over multiple enemies at once, and you
> could designate a location. It didn't stun them.** I didn't know, exactly, what *Bijanbi* did, but
> **it was clearly something that dragged enemies toward you**.

Rosetta calls it "*a Dinner Bell variant*," so the working definition is: mass, ranged, targeted
drag, no stun.

## Why Donut Needed It

Purely as ammunition for *[War Crime](war-crime.md)*. The endgame plan requires two component spells,
each at **level 8 or higher**, and Donut's arsenal has no mass-drag effect at all. Rosetta explains
the substitution in chapter 74:

> "We were going to go with **Hamzo**, but even with the Synergy tower's effect tied in with the
> bonus from the *War Crime* spell, it won't be a wide enough area. **So we went with a Dinner Bell
> variant from a cleric who fell defending the FUPA. It's a spell that came from him worshiping his
> deity, but it appears it's going to work. Edgar extracted it properly, and with some ink tips from
> Milk, we don't need the toraline anymore to do what we need.**"

This is also the first demonstration of Edgar's new ink, which can pull an ability from **a corpse**
— something the original toraline ink, which required a living donor, could not do.

## The Tattoo

Edgar picks the placement and Donut objects at volume: "*You will not be shaving my ass!*" He needs
"*a spot about as big as my foot*" on her right flank, and works fast enough that the artwork is not
his best. The result:

> **It was a simple, rather adorable line art tattoo of a dog. As I watched, the tattoo did, indeed,
> move. It lifted its leg and started to lick itself.**

Edgar's professional assessment: "*I do not believe the art is sentient, as it hasn't been imbued
with anything other than the spell itself. I imagine this is all it can do.*"

The full negotiation, including Donut's condition that Carl get a matching one, is on
[The Toraline Ink Tattoo System](../items/toraline-tattoo-transfer.md).

## Levels 2 to 15

The transfer lands short. Donut, immediately after:

> "**I did, but it's only level two!** My word... this is quite a good spell. **It says it came from
> a god called 'Wepwawet.'** What kind of name is that? **It gives me the option to worship him
> now.**" She gasped. "Carl, Carl, it says he's in the '**Court of Nekhebit**!' and I can worship
> Odette now, too!"

Level 2 is useless for *War Crime*, which requires 8. **Milk** fixes it by writing calligraphy
directly over the finished tattoo:

> "**Not quite yet, child. We must get it temporarily above level eight. This may sting a little**,"
> Milk said. She came forward and produced a pen, dripping with black ink… She wrote some words atop
> the tattoo with a flourish as Donut hissed with pain. I didn't recognize the script, but when I
> examined the characters, **the system simply said, Words of Power.**
>
> "**It's a magic style that I haven't seen used often in the dungeon. The calligraphy guild teaches
> it.**"

The overshoot is dramatic — "*The spell went up to level 15!*" — and explicitly temporary: "*As the
ink fades, the power will decrease, but if you practice using the spell at that high of a level, it
will assist greatly with training.*"

## Use — Chapter 76

*Bijanbi* is one of the two component spells Donut feeds into *War Crime* from the Nest tower, the
other being **Astral Paw**. Combined and tower-amplified, the two produce the effect that ends the
battle of Larracos: thousands of frozen enemy soldiers, plus the feral god Meatus, dragged up off the
field and gathered beneath a colossal glowing cat's paw.

Per the *War Crime* rules, Donut loses access to both component spells for the remainder of the
floor, and gains the permanent ability to chain them together for a single cost on later floors.

## Side Effects

The transfer comes with unwanted extras. Donut receives "*two very rude achievements. One called me a
traitor and another just gave me something called a stolen catalytic converter as a prize*" —
consequences of taking a spell that belongs to a god she does not worship. It also opens **Wepwawet**
as a worship option, which turns out to matter: he sits in the **Court of Nekhebit**, and Donut is
named Champion of Nekhebit at the end of the same sequence.

## Scaling

No stated per-level formula. The observable facts are that **level 2 is too low to be combined into
*War Crime*** (the floor is 8), and that the level can be inflated well past the donor's own by
overlaying **Words of Power**, at the cost of the boost decaying as the ink fades.
