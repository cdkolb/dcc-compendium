# Travel the Path and Welcome Home

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Light-god worship spells — granted on worship, not learned from tomes
    - **Available from:** 6 of the roughly 15 light deities in the Ascendency pantheon grant
      *Travel the Path*; **4 of those 6** also grant *Welcome Home*
    - ***Travel the Path*:** Turn into light and travel an existing path at the speed of light for a
      few seconds. **Not classified as teleportation by the system**
    - ***Welcome Home*:** **Once ever.** Combined with *Travel the Path*, follow a Bijou fairy's
      worship tendril directly to the strongest connected temple
    - **Why it matters:** This pair is the mechanism behind the Sepsis Whore's legendary
      disappearing act

!!! note "Exact-verification note"
    Neither spell has a system description block. Everything here comes from Carl's chapter-49
    25th-Edition notes, which are the party's reconstruction of how the trick works.

## The Bijou Mechanic

Neither spell makes sense without the infrastructure they run on, and Book 7 explains that
infrastructure for the first time.

> When one worships a god, a link is formed between the adherent and the immortal. **This link comes
> in the form of an actual, physical, whisker-thin connection made of invisible light.** Light that
> can penetrate almost any substance or spell. After a new adherent pledges themselves, **a tiny,
> non-corporeal fairy mob called a Bijou grabs a tendril of light from the god and drags it to the
> new worshiper and attaches it to the adherent's heart.**
>
> Here's the important part. **This line isn't direct. It goes from the god to the strongest temple
> on the floor and from the temple to the worshiper.** This whole process takes about two minutes.
> **From the third floor until the 11th floor, the strongest temple will almost always be inside of
> Club Vanquisher.**
>
> When a person moves around, other tendrils also grow, connecting the adherent to other temples and
> in some cases, other worshipers. These connections, too, are facilitated by the Bijou fairies.
>
> This strange, little quirk of the game usually has no impact on anything, and Rosetta seems to
> think it's actually some sort of workaround the original designers developed to add the whole
> deity aspect to the game. **Nobody has ever killed or caught or stopped the Bijou**, though
> Mordecai says in theory it should be possible to catch one with a certain type of trap.

The Bijou fairies themselves are covered on the Species/Mobs pages. What matters mechanically is
that worship leaves behind a **physical route** from the worshiper to a temple, and that route runs
through Club Vanquisher.

**Edgar** is how the party detects them. His level-15 **Sense Movement** skill lets him see
non-corporeal motion:

> In Edgar's case, he can catch their movement. He can see upward momentum from the ground. **This is
> a sign that someone nearby has just worshiped a god for the first time.**

## Travel the Path

> There were approximately 15 different deities in the Ascendency pantheon who were gods and
> goddesses of light. All 15 of those immortals had temples inside of Club Vanquisher, and, unique
> amongst the light gods and goddesses, was a powerful spell called *Travel the Path*. **It looked
> and acted like a mix between a phase and a teleportation spell. It was neither. It allowed someone
> to temporarily turn into light and follow a path.** They could usually only do it for a few
> seconds, but **they could follow that path at the speed of light**, making travel almost
> instantaneous. **It was a dangerous spell to use and oftentimes ended with the caster getting
> splattered or cut in half by an unexpected shadow blocking their route. It was not considered
> teleportation by the system.**
>
> Of those 15 gods, six gave their adherents some version of this spell immediately upon worship.

Two clauses carry all the weight:

1. **It is not teleportation.** Which means every teleport ban, teleport suppression field and
   anti-teleport ward in the dungeon simply does not apply to it.
2. **A shadow across the route is lethal.** The path has to be clear of obstruction the whole way, at
   the speed of light, with no ability to react.

## Welcome Home

> Of those six, **four also included a skill called Welcome Home. This could usually only be used
> once. Ever.** This skill, combined with *Travel the Path*, would allow the adherent to **follow the
> bijou fairy's light tendril path directly to the strongest connected temple**, which was invariably
> inside of Club Vanquisher.
>
> **Even if we destroyed the entrance to the temple, the spell would work.**

So the combination is: worship a light god, receive a permanent light-tendril route to Club
Vanquisher, and hold a one-shot ability to ride that route there instantly, through walls, immune to
teleport bans, from anywhere on the floor.

That is a perfect escape hatch, and it is the answer to a question the party has been chewing on for
most of the book.

## The Sepsis Whore's Disappearing Act

**Princess Vinata**, the reigning Sepsis Whore, has never been killed in a Faction Wars game. Not
once, across every season the naga have played and lost. The party needs to know how she leaves.

Rosetta's theory is the light-god escape. Two problems have to be solved for it to hold:

- **Nobody ever sees the sultana worship a god**, which implies the worshiper is somebody else — the
  always-conveniently-absent guard
- **Seven naga escape on a single worship event**, which a one-person spell does not explain

The answer to the second is a church-recruitment quest. Two of the four candidate deities issue a
quest requiring a new worshiper to bring someone in, via a **"Prospect Congregation"** day pass —
which works like a party: everyone piles into the worshiper's pocket and transfers together. Carl's
description of the experience is that prospects cannot leave the temple for at least an hour, making
it "*the dungeon equivalent of going to a timeshare pitch.*"

That narrows the field to two gods. **Theia** is sponsored, and a sponsored deity would smite Vinata
for the abuse, which leaves **Khepri** — and Khepri's temple inside Club Vanquisher becomes the
target of the assault that eventually gets Vinata killed.

The twist, in chapters 52–53: "*To my surprise, I saw Vinata was actually the one who'd worshiped
Khepri, which was a break in tradition.*" She had not delegated it this time. She had summoned Khepri
into his own temple, as a trap for the party.

## Scaling

No levels given for either spell. The only stated limits are *Travel the Path*'s duration ("*usually
only… a few seconds*"), its lethal intolerance of obstruction, and *Welcome Home*'s single lifetime
use.

## Coda: The Bijou Trap

Mordecai's throwaway line that it "*should be possible to catch one with a certain type of trap*"
pays off on the last page of the book. In chapter 87 a **bijou fairy trap** is deployed along with
"*the thousands of threads that connected the fairies to the 12th floor*," and "*the unbreakable
lines of the bijou fairies tightened, creating a flower pattern.*" Then:

> **System Message: The Bijou Trap has deactivated. All captured Bijou have been freed.**
