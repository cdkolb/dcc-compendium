# Ghommid

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** *Not a single race* — a catch-all term for any other-realm spirit creature visiting
      from the land of the dead
    - **Variety:** "There are all sorts, and many of them are one-of-a-kind"
    - **Where they settle:** Anywhere the veil between worlds is thin — cemeteries in particular
    - **Danger level:** Mostly harmless by default; Floor 8's ghommid horde is a *corrupted*
      population, not a normal one
    - **Book 6 appearances:** The **Medium Ghommid Settlement** on the Cuba floor, and the specific
      [Mobs](../mobs/index.md) generated there

## What the term means

The AI is unusually direct about the taxonomy when Carl enters the settlement:

> "A 'Ghommid' is a sort of catch-all term for just your regular, run-of-the-mill other-realm
> creature. Visitors to this world from the land of the dead. There are all sorts, and many of them
> are one-of-a-kind. And like all those weird bugs and microbes in that drop of water, they do
> different things. Most are harmless. Some are cute. Some are angry. Some will give you explosive
> diarrhea just for fun. They often settle in areas where the veil is thin. Like cemeteries."

In other words, "ghommid" sits alongside terms like *undead* or *fae* rather than alongside
*Porsuk* or *Tigran*. It describes an origin — the other side of the veil — not a shared biology.
Two ghommids may have nothing in common with each other at all.

Carl's own observations bear that out: the horde he fights includes toddler-sized minotaurs, a
six-legged fox, a round fish-like thing, a bug-headed creature, a donkey-headed humanoid whose head
shatters like pottery, and "a naked, massively obese man with Dumbo-sized ears walking on his
hands," his ears dragging on the ground and on fire. At night they emanate an ethereal blue glow
they don't have during the day.

## Faded gods become ghommids

The most consequential piece of ghommid lore in Book 6 is that the category is where *dead
religions* end up:

> "As the worshippers of these gods grew more and more extinct, their temples crumbled, and the
> power of these former deities waned. Eventually, they faded into nothing, slipping away beyond
> the veil, becoming nothing more than any other ghommid, only able to visit the physical world on
> occasion."

This is the backstory of the orisha **Asojano**, the Lord of Smallpox, whose fall from god to
ghommid drives the Floor 8 settlement quest — see [Asojano](../bosses/asojano.md).

## The Medium Ghommid Settlement (Floor 8)

The town Carl clears is a cemetery settlement whose ghommid population has been driven mad. The
cause is not the ghommids themselves:

> "An Orisha is the source of the ghommid infection. He used the last remaining power of his shrine
> to enter town, and he's unable to leave. The vile power of his presence has altered the minds of
> the town's residents, turning them mad. Remove him to free the town."

Practical notes from the fight, for anyone cross-referencing the mob entries:

- The ordinary corrupted ghommids run around **level 30** and cast no spells, but attack in
  effectively unlimited numbers and hit with a powerful **freeze/ice attack** on flesh contact —
  Carl can punch them safely only through his gauntlet.
- They are treated as **undead** for damage purposes: anti-undead missiles, holy grenades, blessed
  oil and healing-potion smoke all work on them, and a blessed door chock repels them.
- Killing them fills a soul-essence bar with a distinctive **black** charge, as opposed to the
  sickly green of duendes.
- They are **cured, not destroyed, by the quest resolution.** Repairing Asojano's shrine liberates
  the settlement, and the surviving ghommids revert to ordinary NPCs — one of them immediately
  falls to his knees weeping for his goddess, and the graveyard's tombs turn into shops.

Individual named ghommids from the floor — including the level-30 **Unsettled Ghommid** Kipper and
the **Ghommid Splitter** Skylar Spinach, who becomes one of Donut's totem cards — have their own
entries under [Mobs](../mobs/index.md). This page covers only the general category; see those for
stat blocks and card behaviour.
