# Semeru Dwarves

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Dwarven people of the **ninth floor**
    - **Built:** The castle of **Larracos**, raised along the roots of **the All Tree**
    - **Religion:** The only worshippers of the goddess **Ysalte** anywhere in the dungeon; she has
      a single temple in the entire crawl
    - **Book 6 status:** Holding the castle submerged and sealed off after the Larracos flood,
      letting nobody near that part of the city

## Overview

The Semeru are a Floor 9 people who become relevant to Book 6 in advance, because Carl's team
floods Larracos and then inherits the consequences. They are named in the quest text that ushers in
the book's endgame:

> "This usually happens here in the dungeon, but only upon the battlefields outside Larracos, where
> the Semeru Dwarves built their castle along the roots of the All Tree, seeking the very gods who
> disdain them."

"The very gods who disdain them" is the whole character of the people in one clause. They built at
the roots of the world-tree specifically to be near deities who want nothing to do with them.

## The All Tree

The **All Tree** is carried over from Book 5: per [Tsarina Signet](../characters/tsarina-signet.md),
it is a massive tree that "grew through all the worlds at once, connecting everything," and her
husband Grimaldi — transformed into a vine by Scolopendra — is now connected to it. Carl makes the
link himself on Floor 8 when a tree motif comes up in a quest.

## Ysalte

The Semeru are the reason [Ysalte](../bosses/ysalte.md) exists as an encounter at all. Per the
Bahamas book:

> "The Semeru Dwarves of the ninth floor all only worship a single goddess, who inevitably makes an
> appearance near the end of the battles as the final teams approach the gates of the castle of
> Larracos. She only has a single temple in the entire dungeon. I have never seen or heard of a
> crawler worshiping her. I don't know if that's even possible."

The AI adds that "the earliest citizens of Larracos always worshipped Ysalte," and that she takes
no new worshippers beyond the citizens of Larracos. She is the Goddess of Hopelessness and Insanity
— formerly of the Dirt, then of Tears — and is better known to crawlers as **The Downward Spiral**
or **The Vinegar Bitch**.

That exclusivity has a hard consequence in Book 6. When the crawler Paz Lo kills Ysalte on Floor 8,
the system announces that **all worshippers of Ysalte have permanently been inflicted with the
*Despondent* debuff** — which, given the Semeru are her only worshippers, lands squarely on the
entire people whose castle Faction Wars is about to be fought over.

## Book 6 posture

Odette's report on the state of Larracos after the flood:

> "The Semeru aren't letting anyone near that part of the city, and they're keeping the castle
> submerged. Mercenaries and players alike are getting fed into that pit on the daily."

## Book 7: the Semeru on-screen

Book 7 is where the Semeru stop being a rumour. Carl meets **War Leader Arief**, *Commander of the
Larracos Defense Force Infantry*, at the foot of a tower on the campus of the
Larracos College of Magic, and gets the series' first proper look at one:

> "This was the first time I'd gotten a chance to really examine a Semeru dwarf. All the dwarves
> we'd met so far were more like the traditional, angry Scottish variety. His body was squat and
> wide, but that's where the similarities ended. This guy was darker complected, Asian looking, with
> a round face and flat nose. Thick, black hair peeked out from under his large hat. **He did not
> have a beard**, though he did have a long, thin mustache."

Their war gear is the other surprise:

> "His armor was almost samurai-like, but not quite. It was like a wide, open-chested metallic vest
> with flared shoulders. The bottom of the metal vest moved outward, like a bell, ending just below
> his waist. His squat legs were covered in what looked like chainmail with loose, flowing, red and
> yellow fabric strips along the sides."
>
> "His helmet was a rounded skull cap, but it was decorated with **multiple feathers** of various
> kinds, reaching straight up into the air. In the front of the helmet was a metallic ornament,
> featuring a wide-leafed fern, which curled at the tip, making the helmet look a little ridiculous.
> His whole getup made him appear as if he was getting ready for **a parade, not a battle**."
>
> "**The wide sickle slung over his shoulder was no joke, however.** And everything the dwarf wore
> was enchanted. Everything, including the individual feathers in his helmet, all let off a glow of
> various colors."

The narration later calls the look by name: "A group of Semeru dwarves, all with their **ornate
parade armor**, stood at the front gate to the college, pushing back a rush of the shadowy mobs who
were trying to fight their way inside." Sickles are standard issue — elsewhere a running formation
jogs past "carr[ying] hammers and sickles over their shoulders."

## The full history

The AI finally tells the story properly, and it is the bleakest origin in the series:

> "Anyway, of all the peoples in this story, there's an argument to be had that the Semeru had it
> the worst. This land, the volcanic level in which Larracos stands, was once a haven for their
> people... The Semeru people lived here in relative peace. They farmed. They mined. They learned."

They dug toward the gods out of love for Ysalte; a city grew in the pit; scholars came from
everywhere and Larracos became "a rich, cultural paradise." What they found at the bottom was not
gods but the **roots of the All Tree** — and they stopped:

> "They built their castle at the very bottom, along the thick roots. But it's more than a castle.
> **It's a monument. An apology.** In discovering these roots, they had an epiphany... Some things
> are best left alone. Some things should never be woken up."

Then Scolopendra's nine-tier attack:

> "When Scolopendra's nine-tier attack came, the Semeru got it the worst of all. It wasn't their
> fault. But they were there, and they were mortal, and they were devastated. **They numbered in the
> millions before that day. Today, there are less than 10,000 left.**"
>
> "And in the ultimate irony, their great structures weren't even touched... The remaining dwarves,
> defeated, took it upon themselves to be stewards of the great, empty city."

## The Despondent debuff, in numbers

The consequence of Ysalte's death in Book 6 is now quantified. Every Semeru Carl meets has it
blazing over their head:

> **Warning: This NPC is Despondent because his goddess is dead. He is unable to worship another
> deity, and all of his stats have taken a 15% hit. In addition, those who worship other deities
> inflict 10% more damage against him.**

Carl: "That despondent debuff was pretty brutal. Still, it didn't seem to bother him too much."
Arief greets the two crawlers who flooded his city by bowing deeply and calling it an honour to have
them within his borders — Donut, watching: "CARL DOES THIS GUY KNOW YOU AND KATIA ARE THE ONES WHO
FLOODED THE CITY IN THE FIRST PLACE? HE'S BEING REALLY NICE."

## The Jatismara

Book 7 introduces a term for Semeru who carry memory across crawl seasons. Arief, asked whether he
remembers previous seasons:

> "I don't remember this, but there are some with **the memory quickened**. I believe I am new, as
> are most of us. But some have persisted. My uncle remembers dozens of seasons now. Not as many as
> Juice Box, but he's one of the **Jatismara**, and he sits upon her war council."

!!! warning "One sentence is all there is"
    That quotation is the *only* occurrence of "Jatismara" in the series, and the only occurrence of
    "quickened" in this sense. What can be said with confidence: some Semeru retain memory of past
    crawl seasons, they are called the Jatismara, and at least one of them sits on Juice Box's war
    council. Anything beyond that is unsupported.

## Dwarven diggers

The Semeru pilot enormous excavation machines that become a major asset for
[Team Retribution](../dungeon-factions/team-retribution.md) during the siege:

> "The massive dwarven digger, which was basically a boxy, god-sized robot covered in all directions
> with spiral digging arms and goblin-murder-dozer-like spinning wheels, sat idle. **A pair of Semeru
> stood atop it, doing something inside a control panel on the shoulder of the thing. They looked
> like bugs standing upon it.**"

The floor is honeycombed for them: "They had passages like this intersecting the entire floor. They
were built so the massive, dwarven diggers could get from point A to B quickly."
