# Species

Sapient races and peoples encountered in the series — distinct from corporations/governments
(see [Factions & Organizations](../factions/index.md)) and from in-dungeon businesses/clubs
(see [Dungeon Factions](../dungeon-factions/index.md)). Named monster-type creatures with their
own dedicated stat blocks live under [Mobs](../mobs/index.md) instead.

## Full pages

- [The Kua-Tin & The Bloom](kua-tin-and-the-bloom.md) — aquatic species that owns the Borant Corporation, and its ruling political party
- [Primal](primal.md) — ancient, vanished precursor species; also a selectable crawler race (Carl's choice)
- [City Elves](city-elves.md) — banished High Elf offshoot; worship the skyfowl as angels
- [Changeling](changeling.md) — shapeshifting species stranded on Floor 5; see also the
  [Changeling Principals](../dungeon-factions/the-changeling-principals.md) cult
- [High Elves](high-elves.md) — near-immortal ruling elf caste, first named in Book 2; shown in full on Floor 6
- [Naiad](naiad.md) — river-dwelling species central to Tsarina Signet's Book 5 storyline
- [Were-castor](were-castor.md) — Chee genocide survivors turned were-beavers, allied with Tsarina Signet
- [Gondii / "Residuals"](gondii-residuals.md) — homeless hyperspatial collective-mind race that
  infiltrates any world with a Primal Engine; Book 6's biggest species reveal
- [Hobbledehoy](hobbledehoy.md) — proportionally small humans; Pater Coal's race
- [Ifrit](ifrit.md) — smoking, white-eyed demons drawn up from Sheol; Damascus Steel's race
- [Zebani](zebani.md) — briefly-seen crawler race; Emir Akbas's race
- [Porsuk](porsuk.md) — badger-headed race; the Desperado Club's bartenders, plus the crawlers
  Burcu and Charles Ellis
- [Grixist](grixist.md) — six-eyed, hairy race of the Grixist Swarm; Huanxin Jinx's race
- [Sai](sai.md) — bipedal rhinoceros samurai, ex-firefighters, guards of the Desperado Club's
  Larracos level
- [Ghommid](ghommid.md) — catch-all term for other-realm spirit creatures from the land of the
  dead, not a single race
- [Semeru Dwarves](semeru-dwarves.md) — builders of the castle of Larracos along the roots of the
  All Tree; the only worshippers of Ysalte; shown in full, with the Jatismara, in Book 7
- [Nullian](nullian.md) — "the Null," the galaxy's grey aliens; Quasar's race, with his niece
  Tempest as Book 7's second on-page nullian
- [Bune](bune.md) — small winged dragon-people, "the elves of the lizard world"; a very common
  former-crawler race, and the Madness's adjutant Opiee
- [Tenodera](tenodera.md) — smaller, flightless mantis cousins bred for emotionlessness; York's race
- [Xenopus](xenopus.md) — winged, clawed frog-people; Milk's chosen crawler race
- [Vesper](vesper.md) — Milk's bat-like birth race, named only in contrast to Xenopus
- [Sturnus](sturnus.md) — Volteeg's songbird birth race
- [Gargoyle](gargoyle.md) — Volteeg's crawler race; stone-skinned, long-sleeping, and unable to sing
- [Tripod](tripod.md) — basketball-bodied, three-legged, eye-covered and famously emotionless;
  Fopsy's race
- [Shadow Mimics](shadow-mimics.md) — Book 8: near-undetectable sapient mimics that grow without
  limit in intelligence and power; the counter-species to the [Changeling
  Principals](../dungeon-factions/the-changeling-principals.md), revealed via the boss
  [Mitch](../bosses/mitch.md)

## Other Book 6 species

Races named or shown on Floor 8 that don't yet warrant a full page. For the Faction Wars teams
these races field, see [Factions & Organizations](../factions/faction-wars-teams.md).

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| Viceroy | The people behind the Faction Wars team **the Madness**. Bulky bodies, long thin bone-white necks, and a blank white mask "like something out of a creepy anime" over a bone-white, demonic, tusked face. Their race is "somewhat of a mystery" — before Book 6 nobody had ever seen one die, so it was an open question in the cookbook whether they were even biological; one is decapitated on-page and bleeds red. Epitome Noflex notes he **regenerates at sunset**, at least until the games begin. They specialize in "horrific battlefield spells and necromancy" and are Faction Wars perennials. Off the playing field, their system is a destination for cheap medical procedures for non-citizens and people outside the inner system. **Book 7:** the anatomy under the robes is finally shown — when **Architect Houston** leaps up mid-hearing, "his robes flung back, revealing a horrific, bug-like body and **six arms**, two of which were holding a rifle." Later, unmasked: "the horrific, multi-limbed, half bug, half monster that he was." Their culture is not medical but scientific — "Viceroys have no passion for healing. Their passion is science. Understanding the universe. Understanding everything from the atom to the tree, no matter the cost" — and Houston is "the prime of the Viceroys not because he was elected so... but because he's made the most scientific discoveries." Their medical arm runs a Reaver-like debt trap: sign yourself over to "their post-biological form studies corp" and the bills are wiped. Note that many masked "viceroys" on the battlefield, including the invisible **Monkeywrenchers**, are ordinary mercenaries in Viceroy masks |
| Reavers | Machine people and the megacorporation/system government of the same name, fielding the Faction Wars team **the Reavers** under Warlord Fangs. Like the Nebulars, they began as several different biological races who augmented themselves with machines — but the Reavers "took it a step further and kept only their brains," which is why they look like robots. Shape-shifting at will; normally plastic-skinned android soothers, though their rep appears to Carl as a 1960s-movie tin robot for his benefit. They live on a **Dyson Sphere**. Once related to the [Nebulars](../factions/nebulars.md); frequent orc allies and multiple-time Faction Wars winners. **Book 7:** the **citizenship-by-cybernetics** system is laid out in full. Unless you are "a vat-born progeny of **the Initial**," Reaver citizenship — which carries profit-sharing and heavy financial benefits — requires losing your biological form first. That first upgrade is free; after that there are only two routes. Pay yourself, which almost nobody can ("the only companies smart enough to hire a moron who allowed the Reavers to cut off a perfectly good hand... are the space equivalent of a Wendy's or the interstellar carwash"), or **work for the Reavers for free**: food, housing, unlimited healthcare and one more upgrade per three-cycle deployment, dropped "feet-first into the fire" evicting settlers off land leases so the Reavers can expand their factories. Carl's rule of thumb from the AI: "If they have more than two or three cybernetics installed, you know they've voluntarily re-upped at least once." One mercenary, formerly Jake Tortokai, is 23 enhancements into "his 400-cycle journey to gain Reaver citizenship." **The Initial** is a physical chunk of Warlord Fang's brain kept on a Reaver world, "and every year, they peeled microscopic parts off it to build new citizens" |
| Operatic Collective | Opaque, quivering green blobs "shaped into three tiers of decreasing size, like a Jell-o mold from the 1970's," with mouths at the very top that speak upward into the air. They breed by splitting apart and can merge back into one another — their Faction Wars rep is "Voting Admin Cell Mass Number Three," three individuals in one body, including one named Hortense. Donut renames him "Green Jiggly." Their later representative, Commander Stockade, is eaten by sharks in the flooded Larracos. **Book 7:** consistently called "the slimes." Their collective has a formal **identity-reset and re-absorption** cycle: one Operatic who overreached on a spell "supposedly had to go for an identity reset after the crawl and was re-absorbed. Part of his essence eventually formed into **Hortense Leadership Unit**" — the Faction Wars warlord — which the AI offers as an explanation for his twitch, adding that "collective minds should really have learned by now how to peel off their own... impurities." Economically they are bankers, running the **Operatic Banker's Federation** and shown "bomb[ing] settlements back to oblivion if they couldn't pay their mortgages." Their armour is the **Weeper-class tank** |
| Lemig | Green, humanoid, "like a cross between a goblin and a nullian alien." The majority race of the **Lemig Sortion**, a genuinely direct democracy in which the whole population votes on everything and an 80% supermajority is needed to reverse any standing decision — which is why they can't quit Faction Wars despite having soured on it. Their actual leader is a different alien, "a fuzzy caterpillar-looking thing." Mordecai: their system is "in a constant state of disarray." Their Faction Wars rep, Luke, talks like a surfer. **Book 7:** their team competes as **the Democratic Sortion** and is the first eliminated. They are confirmed as goblins ("Corpse of Luke. Level 64 Lemig Goblin"), and their warlord **Commander Stockade** — the "giant, fuzzy thing... an upright caterpillar" with an aged-surfer voice — is the caterpillar leader Book 6 alluded to, not a Lemig himself. Luke was his partner; Stockade dies of grief and an Insanity spell. Victory's verdict on Syndicate politics, late in the book: "The council is just as bad as the Lemig Sortion these days. It's total gridlock." **Note:** the six-armed, insectoid body under robes seen in Book 7 belongs to the **Viceroys**, not the Lemig |
| Mexx | Disc-shaped robot race. **U56KL** — whom Donut immediately renames "Urkel" — runs the Faction Wars preproduction meetings, seating the warlords and logging action items. **Book 7:** mexx robots continue to run the warlord councils — seating the warlords, announcing accepted action items — but a different unit each time; Donut names one "Carla." No new physical or cultural detail is given, and the designation "U56KL"/"Urkel" does not recur |
| Taurine | The race that owns **Hereford War Machines, Inc.**, a manufacturer "known to outfit some of the most elite mercenary squads in the galaxy." Donut's Book 6 personal shield, **The Bull Rush**, is theirs — a small opalescent longhorn-cow pin. No physical description of the Taurine themselves is given. **Book 7:** effectively nothing new. A single "taurin mercenary" dies in the assault on the Madness castle, and Hereford is not mentioned at all |
| Lutin | Gnome-like race specializing in support magic. **Randall**, one of Imani's squad of former Meadowlark residents, chose it as his crawler race; he dies during a totem battle after a possession attack |
| Wisp | Crawler race; **Yasmine**, a level 75 Wisp who worships the goddess Áine, is the secretary of the Club Vanquisher Temple Portal in Book 6 |
| Hills Cyclops | **Mordecai's own Floor 8 transformation form** (level 50), *not* a separate NPC race in this book — large, pink, bald, with a single blinking eye in a cone-shaped head. Per the AI: the weakest and dumbest of the cyclops-style monsters, usually shepherds, "always disheveled because they can't see their own reflections in mirrors," and given to bar fights and drinking contests |
| Pocket Kuma | Likewise **a Mordecai transformation form**, the "tiny, cute creature" he wore on Floor 7, referenced in Book 6 only in contrast to his much larger Hills Cyclops body. Neither form represents an NPC species Carl meets on this floor |
