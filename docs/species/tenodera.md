# Tenodera

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **First appearance:** Book 7, *This Inevitable Ruin* (the epilogue)
    - **Type:** A smaller, flightless cousin species to the mantids
    - **Known individual:** **York**, the tenth-edition cookbook author and sole crew aboard the
      *Homecoming Queen*
    - **Defining trait:** Ancestral warrior castes were deliberately bred to feel nothing

## Overview

The tenodera are introduced in Book 7's epilogue through **York**, who spends the entire chapter
alone aboard the *Homecoming Queen* writing bad poetry and trying to feel something. His character
sheet is unusual among the cookbook authors in that it records no race change at all:

> **Class:** Advocate. **Race:** Tenodera. **Birth Race:** Tenodera.

## Relationship to the mantids

The tenodera are mantis-adjacent and, in the mantids' eyes, a contamination risk:

> "The larger mantids hated his race, the tenodera. They were similar, but York and his kind were
> much smaller physically. Flightless. And not nearly as aggressive and violent. Most mantids openly
> spoke of exterminating him and his kind, as they could crossbreed, and they feared what would
> happen should the tenodera mix en masse."

York's reaction to the destruction of the mantis Hive Re-Population Center 51 is, accordingly,
"a strange, empty sense of satisfaction" — and he tries the words *"That's what you deserved"* out
loud without feeling them.

## Bred numbness

The species' central trait is a heritable emotional flattening:

> "And then, somewhere around the 9th floor, a numbness spread. He knew this was something locked
> deep in his tenodera DNA. His warrior ancestors had been specifically bred to be without emotion.
> This was before they'd all become civilized, before all their wars were fought with tanks and
> bombs and drones, the military hardware substituting for the chemicals their queens of old would
> drill into their brains."
>
> "This lack of emotion would be required of any soldier whose duty it was to crack into an enemy
> hive and kill all the children within."

York's arc turns on exactly that: he volunteers to destroy an orc yacht full of children — "he'd
looked forward to it... He wanted to do it because he wanted to feel something afterward" — and it
doesn't work. He finally feels something only at the very end of the book, when the *Homecoming
Queen*'s weapons come online and Porthus's resistance broadcast airs.

York entered the dungeon with his whole hive, and "recalled — vaguely recalled — the anger he'd felt
at how they'd been picked off one by one. And not just at that, but at the realization that he'd
never really fit in. And because of his own individuality, he never was part of a team, despite
being part of a hive."

!!! note "New in Book 7"
    "Tenodera" does not appear anywhere in Books 1–6. The *name* York does — he's cited in Book 3 as
    the author of the tenth cookbook edition — but his species and history are new here.
