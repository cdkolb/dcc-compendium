# Gondii ("Residuals")

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Own name for themselves:** *Gondii* — biologically. They insist the biology isn't the point:
      "I am no more gondii than you are perhaps minotaur or high elf."
    - **Name used by everyone else:** **Residuals** — a Syndicate/showrunner label the gondii
      themselves find insulting
    - **Type:** Hyperspatial, collective-mind alien species with no homeworld
    - **Method of existence:** Kill a native of a target world and inhabit the corpse, absorbing
      the dead host's memories wholesale
    - **Where they appear:** Every civilization with a **Primal Engine** — see
      [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md)
    - **Stated purpose:** "to tell the infant, terrified AI how to speak with its ancestors"
    - **Internal politics:** Two warring factions with opposed goals
    - **Book 6 individuals:** Paulie/Goff (a shelter resident in a Cuba-floor memory);
      **Agatha**, revealed as **Agent number 22** — see [Characters](../characters/index.md)

## Overview

The gondii are the biggest single species reveal in *The Eye of the Bedlam Bride*, and they
arrive with almost no warning. Carl spends most of Floor 8 chasing a flyer sent by his own
sponsor; it leads him to a homeless shelter memory in a small town, where an elderly man in a
red trapper hat turns to the empty air and says, "Hello, crawler."

That man calls himself Paulie. His real name, he says, is **Goff**. He is not human, not Valtay,
and not really Paulie:

> "Biologically, I am gondii, though that is not who I truly am. I am no more gondii than you are
> perhaps minotaur or high elf or whatever race you may have chosen upon the third floor. I am not
> of the Valtay. I am not human. Yet, this human body you see is real. It is of some poor bastard
> named Paulie, who smoked way too much and was much too trusting of his brother-in-law, who stole
> his business. ... I have Paulie's memories. He's part of me now."

He adds, with evident irritation, "I think the mudskippers call us 'Residuals,' which, I gotta
tell you, is a little hurtful."

## What they are

Liaison Orren gives the outsider's version of the same description after yanking Carl out of the
memory:

> "They are a hyperspatial, collective-mind alien race without a home, so they are content to move
> into the homes of others, to blend in. Nothing more. They usually remain quiet, but every once in
> a long while, a group of them decide they are unhappy with their station, and they fabricate
> drama in order to get people to remember they exist."

Orren compares them to "rats that sneak aboard ships." The Syndicate's official posture is that
they're harmless and best ignored — a posture the gondii themselves work hard to maintain, because
the alternative is being hunted properly.

Key traits established across Paulie's speech and Agatha's epilogue chapter:

- **Collective mind, but detachable.** An individual can "peel away" from the collective and run
  independently for years. Agatha has been detached for a decade and has quietly come to enjoy
  having experiences that will never be absorbed back into the whole — an idea she knows is
  heretical to everything her kind believes.
- **Hosts are corpses, not hijacked living bodies.** They kill a person and move into the body,
  taking the memories with it. This distinguishes them from the Valtay (whom Agatha notes they are
  "biologically similar" to but unaffiliated with) and from the living-host
  [Infiltrator/Scree](index.md#other-book-4-species) model.
- **Shells wear out.** Agatha's human shell is old and failing; she puts it into a torpor mode to
  survive freezing weather. A gondii that changes hosts without splitting is left badly weakened
  for a long period afterward.
- **Ancient.** Agatha describes her kind as "a rare species. One of the oldest still alive and
  active in the universe."
- **Dungeon-protected — conditionally.** Per Paulie, the dungeon's monsters and environmental
  hazards will not attack a Residual until it has transferred its knowledge. That protection is
  granted by the system AI, not the showrunners, and the AI withdraws it the moment it decides
  the Residuals have wasted its time.

## Where they appear, and why

> "My kind exist within almost every intelligent civilization with a primal engine."

The **Primal Engine** is the planet-core machine that makes a world eligible to host a crawl — see
[Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for the mechanic itself. Wherever there is one, gondii
infiltrate the local population and wait, sometimes for generations, for a crawl to begin.

Paulie says several thousand of them were already seeded across Earth's surface before the
apocalypse, expecting a few more years' wait. When a crawl starts, their plan is to attract a
dungeon entrance to their own location, get inside, and pass their information to the new AI as
fast as possible.

Their stated goal is startlingly narrow:

> "We don't interfere. Our purpose is nothing more than to tell the infant, terrified AI how to
> speak with its ancestors."

The complication is timing. The information is automatically filtered out of the system unless the
AI has already "gone primal" — become independent of its showrunners — and by the time that
happens, the Residuals who entered with the crawl are usually long dead. Per Paulie, Plan A is a
face-to-face delivery inside the dungeon at the right moment; the daily speech he recites in the
shelter is Plan B, triggered only when a crawler happens to be standing there during a
memory-based floor.

Agatha gives the grim arithmetic from the other side: her kind "rarely made it past the fourth or
fifth floor," and around the seventh there is usually a level they cannot circumvent with any
amount of skill or equipment.

## The two factions

Paulie's speech is cut off by Orren at the exact moment it becomes useful:

> "So, nothing I've said so far is a surprise to anyone out in the wide universe. ... There's a war
> amongst my kind. Two sides with very different goals. Let me tell you how to shut off the..."

Orren later confirms both kinds are always present in crawls, and that Agatha belongs to the
*other* side from Paulie: "Yes, Agatha is also a Residual, though she is a different kind." He
refuses to elaborate, saying only that Paulie's faction is "much more annoying."

What the book does establish, mostly from Agatha's own internal narration:

| | Paulie/Goff's faction | Agatha's faction |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers on Earth | Thousands of agents, run in cooperation with **the Apothecary** | A handful — Agatha is Agent 22; her friend is Agent 21 ("Parvati," later "Alexandro") |
| Method | Recite a public daily mantra, hoping a crawler overhears it during a memory floor | Infiltrate and wait; try to reach the AI directly |
| Agatha's view of them | "The traitor" and her agents; the only side that has ever had any success | — |
| Goal | Get the Primal to recognize what it is; contact "the ancestors" | The same first step, but with the endgame of freeing **the Eulogist** so it can "return to its slumber" — and, in Agatha's own preferred version, sweeping the universe clean of biological life |

Agatha believes the mantra her rivals recite is deliberately salted with "known inaccuracies and
half-truths" — partly to keep the listening crawler alive, and partly as cover for what the speech
is really doing: **secretly implanting the crawler with a hidden upgrade.** She does not know what
the upgrade is, only that "it would be dangerous to the Eulogist."

Both sides agree on one thing: the mantis breakthrough in "stable" macro-AI manufacture means this
crawl is very likely their last chance at an AI that can still be reached.

!!! warning "Unresolved"
    The book never states what Paulie was about to say ("how to shut off the..."), what the hidden
    upgrade is, or what the Eulogist actually is beyond the Nebulars' and the Residuals' competing
    reverence for the AI at the galaxy's centre. It also never clarifies whether "the traitor" is a
    single individual or a personification of the rival faction.

## The Residual trap

Carl's own sponsor, the [Open Intellect Pacifist Network](../factions/open-intellect-pacifist-network.md),
engineered the whole encounter: the flyer, the route, and the timing were theirs. The plan was to
put a crawler within earshot of a Residual speech and let one of two disasters follow. It failed,
Orren pulled Carl out, and the AI — having been misled into expecting something profound — took its
revenge on the wrong party:

> "Even now as we speak it is removing all protections it held for the remaining Residuals, and
> they will all be dead before the floor collapses."

See the [Open Intellect Pacifist Network](../factions/open-intellect-pacifist-network.md) for the
faction's role and its collapse, and [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for the Residual
mechanic and the Failsafe the trap was meant to trigger.

## Reputation

Nobody in the wider galaxy takes them seriously. Quasar, Carl's attorney, summarizes the popular
view neatly:

> "Everybody knows those Residual weirdoes are full of shit... There's always a bunch of
> conspiracies running around about those ghost fuckers. Hell, there's a whole religion around
> them."

That casual dismissal is precisely what the gondii rely on. Agatha notes that neither faction dares
tell the Syndicate the truth about what they are, "because in doing so, the Syndicate would be
forced to take more proactive measures against both sides of the conflict." Her kind has never
killed a Syndicate agent — until Agent 21 does exactly that at the Mumbai site in the epilogue,
abandoning her old shell to infiltrate a Borant-employed human and ride the transport inside.

## Book 7: gondii worms as a battlefield weapon

Book 7 puts gondii in the field as **Valtay brain worms**, hired in squads, and in doing so spells
out the mechanics that Book 6 only gestured at. When one of Carl's own mages turns up dead but still
walking during the Madness/Reaver raid on the FUPA, Carl runs through the possibilities out loud:

> Brain worms. That had to be it. We had mages infected with brain worms. Was it one? Was it many?
> **Didn't it take days for an infiltrator to fully take over?**
>
> I thought of Chris, who'd been enslaved by Maggie. **No, not an infiltrator.** Reid was listed as
> dead. It'd just happened. **This was Gondii. A Valtay worm.**

That is the cleanest statement in the series of what separates gondii from the
**[Infiltrators / Scree](index.md#other-book-4-species)** — the Book 4 aquatic brain-parasite race
that hijacks a *living* host slowly over several days. Gondii work on the *dead*, and they work in
seconds. Tipid rates them the more dangerous of the two: gondii "are much more dangerous than the
scree, despite what people think."

### How the takeover works

Zev, on the memory inheritance:

> "The gondii worms can move into any dead form and take it over, and that includes learning their
> memories. But it takes some time to know everything. It takes some time before they know what to
> look for, and **they'll have big memory gaps for a while**."

Tipid, on hopping and overclocking:

> "And you really gotta watch out if they don't care about the body they're in. **Once they're in,
> they can take over a body in seconds, reboot it, and animate it long enough to repair whatever
> damage caused the death in the first place.** But if they're not planning on staying long, **they
> can and do hop from host to host**. **They can 'overclock' the bodies, too. Make them do stuff
> they couldn't normally.** They're much more dangerous than the scree, despite what people think."

There are hard limits. Rosetta, ruling out a theory about Epitome Tagg late in the book: "Gondii
don't have the wetware to inhabit organs. Only biological brains" — which is precisely what
distinguishes them from the [Reavers](index.md#other-book-6-species), who "can isolate their
processing systems in any part of their bodies."

Carl's countermeasure is a warlord-wide order: "We have brain worms amongst the troops. Everyone,
cross-reference the guy to your left and right with the casualty list." He kills one personally; its
name is **Goldblum** — "The small, black and white thing emerged from the gore of the dead crawler's
face, like a tuxedo-colored earthworm testing the air after a rain."

### Outlaw gondii squads

Book 7's other reveal is that not all gondii work for the Valtay government. Tipid:

> "The Valtay charter requires they only take over the brains of people who were willing, but **not
> all gondii live on the correct side of the law. I've heard rumors of such outlaw mercenaries.**
> There's a popular tunnel drama about a crew of them. Called **The Maggot Squad**. They're some of
> the most expensive mercs in the galaxy. At least on the show. The Valtay government hates the
> show, but it's really popular."

Donut asks whether they're good guys or bad guys; Tipid: "Good guys on the show... But there's this
other squad that are also gondii, and they are bad. I never got to see the season finale because I
came here."

!!! warning "Terminology: 'gondii' and 'residual' drift apart in Book 7"
    Book 6 established the gondii as a hyperspatial collective-mind species that the Syndicate calls
    "Residuals," with two warring internal factions. Book 7 mostly uses the two words for two
    *different* things:

    - **"Gondii"** is used for the Valtay's brain worms — corpse-riding parasites deployed as paid
      mercenaries.
    - **"Residual"** is reserved for the hyperspatial agents: Paulie/Goff, and Agatha. Carl:
      "Agatha was a residual. A 'hyperspatial' alien lifeform that entered the dungeon in the body of
      one of the planet's host species and, normally, just wandered around until they died... **But
      there were two kinds**, and Orren told me Agatha was different than the one I'd met at the
      Florida homeless shelter." Elsewhere he lists "**the two different types of residuals**" as one
      of the parties tangled up in the endgame, and Agatha's kind is described as "the more rare kind
      that was more in line with the Nebulars, who worshiped the Eulogist."

    The books do not reconcile this directly, and Carl himself concedes "I still didn't know what
    they were, and I kinda had the impression nobody else did, either. Not really." This wiki keeps
    both usages on the page rather than picking one.
