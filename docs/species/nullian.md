# Nullian

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **First described:** Book 5, *The Butcher's Masquerade*
    - **Also called:** "the Null"
    - **Type:** Short, grey, large-eyed, bulbous-headed aliens — the galaxy's own version of the
      Earth "Grey"
    - **Best-known member:** **[Quasar](../characters/quasar.md)**, Carl and Donut's attorney
    - **Book 7:** Quasar's niece **Tempest** appears on-page as "the young nullian," putting a
      second nullian in the series and confirming Quasar's family

## Description

Book 5 introduces the race directly:

> "He was a gray alien... A Nullian, though they were mostly just called 'the Null.'... This race
> was similar to the much-taller Forsoothed, but this guy was only about four and a half feet tall.
> He looked just like a stereotypical, cartoon, Area-51 alien with the gray skin, large eyes, and
> the bulbous head."

Book 6 is where Quasar himself is explicitly tied to the race — the [Lemig](index.md#other-book-6-species)
are described as looking "like a cross between a goblin and a nullian alien, **like Quasar**" — and
the cookbook gives an extended entry on the Null, including their association with "Nightgaunts" as
pack animals.

## Book 7: Tempest

Book 7 opens with **Tempest's Floor 8 Recap School Report**, in which Quasar's niece interviews him
for a school assignment on "perceptions of the crawl":

> The young nullian rolled her eyes. "I told you, Quasar. I have a report due on perceptions of the
> crawl, and I'm interviewing people, having them give me recaps, and I was assigned Carl and Donut.
> Obviously my teacher assigned me them because I'm related to you, and if I don't interview you
> about it, I will fail..."

She is the first nullian relative of Quasar's shown on-page — she references her father (Quasar's
brother) and "Aunt Nova," Quasar's ex — which is the closest the series comes to a direct on-page
statement of Quasar's own species during Book 7 itself.

!!! note "Sourcing caveat"
    Book 7 never says "Quasar is a Nullian" in so many words; the identification within this book is
    by way of his niece. The explicit statement lives in **Book 6**. A later Book 7 line describing a
    goddess's "distinctly alien, almost nullian-like half of her face" is a simile about her
    appearance, not species lore.
