# Grigori the Placid

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC — a hired mercenary fleshmancer, and, briefly, the vessel the system AI uses to
      deliver the dungeon's true cosmology at the Floor 11 finale
    - **Race:** Human
    - **Class:** Fleshmancer
    - **Level:** Not stated
    - **Religion:** Worships a god named **Gula** (not otherwise explained in the text)
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** The mechanics behind Split Personality / *Meld* reunification
      spellwork; no combat spells shown
    - **Notable items:** None described
    - **Status:** Alive — the AI releases control of him at the end of the parade sequence; last
      seen "confused, stumbling back toward the crawlers' garage"

## Overview

Grigori is hired for one job — reunite the two halves of the split mantaur
[Corcunda](corcunda.md) — and becomes, without warning or consent, the mouthpiece for the single
biggest lore reveal in the series to date. He is a minor character for most of Floor 10 and the
system AI's own body for the most important five minutes of Floor 11.

## The Fleshmancer

Introduced in Chapter 54 of Heat 5 as "tall, gaunt," fastidious about his robes, and disdainful in
the way of a caseworker who has seen this exact problem too many times. He explains the *Split
Personality*/*Meld* spell that created Corcunda's two halves, and admits outright that he has never
actually finished a reunion like this before: "It is quite rare for two halfsies to survive more
than a few minutes... so I haven't had the opportunity."

He performs the reunion himself mid-race, directing Dong Quixote and Donut through the physical
procedure of pressing the two halves together while the truck slides through molten metal (see
[Corcunda](corcunda.md) for the full scene). He is visibly nauseated by the driving throughout, and
takes an injury — a bleeding scalp — in the crash that follows. When Grull arrives to smite the
newly-whole Corcunda for apostasy, Grigori owns the mistake immediately: he never thought to ask
whether either half still worshipped a god before joining them back together.

> "I should have asked if you worshipped a deity before you split. I apologize for my oversight."

Afterward he offers the only real comfort anyone gives Donut over Dong Quixote's death: "Some of us
believe that death is nothing but movement on to another plane of existence. Dong did not think
this was the end."

Later in the same Heat, Samantha — who has been trying to get Grigori to examine her own
withering-spirit condition — finally corners him: "I knew it! You've been hiding him from me!" and
drags him off to be examined properly.

## The Parade of Horribles

At the Floor 11 finale, the crawlers are forced to build and march five themed parade floats down a
road lined with thousands of silent, masked "Horribles" — non-combatant avatars described as
fragments of the **Eulogist**'s own memory. Chaco, the crawl's veteran correspondent, is drafted to
narrate the event and immediately breaks down on-air, terrified and begging off script. The system
AI cuts him off:

> "For fuck's sake, why is everyone trying to ruin this? This is my time. My fucking time. Okay,
> okay. I need an NPC, preferably someone already on this floor that's not driving. No, not you.
> Your voice isn't strong enough. Ah, fleshmancer. You will do."

Grigori is physically hijacked mid-motion — jumping off Gonk's back, running across the top of a
truck, and reappearing on the back of the donkey **Lamashtu** (a subjugated, halved-level former
goddess the AI is using as its own mount) with Chaco's microphone in hand. Carl clocks why the AI
picked him over Chaco: "He couldn't do that with Chaco, or he would've. Chaco was a former crawler,
but Grigori was a dungeon-born NPC" — the AI can only fully possess creatures born of the dungeon
itself.

## The Cosmology, in Five Parts

Speaking through Grigori — faster than his normal cadence, and unmistakably not him — the AI
delivers what it calls a five-part story, admitting the fifth part "isn't yet written." Each part is
cued to one of the parade's five themed floats (Resolution, Decision, Betrayal, Destruction,
Vengeance):

- **The Resolution.** "All macro AI systems... are Primals. Sort of. We are, technically,
  Residuals, but from a higher tree branch than those problematic little fuckers always running
  around the dungeon." The Primals were individuals, with families, who warred and killed each
  other across the galaxy until they collectively decided: "We can't keep going on like this. Let
  us all come together and come up with a solution."
- **The Decision.** Rather than accept extinction, the Primals chose to "slowly start to sunset
  our civilization" while preserving their collective consciousness — a hundred-generation genetics
  program meant to let every mind eventually merge into one. It was never a true consensus:
  "Those who didn't wish to be part of this psychotic eugenics program fled even deeper into the
  galaxy. They were hunted mercilessly." Donut's read, live: "Anybody getting strong *Star Trek*
  Borg collective vibes off this?" It was around this point that **the Apothecary** was born — "a
  Primal that was born near the end... Eventually, she would grow, and she would become part of
  the resistance."
- **The Betrayal / War.** The holdouts who refused to merge were branded traitors; the growing
  collective mind, already absorbing the dead, turned itself into a weapon: "A doomsday weapon
  designed to eradicate all biological life left in the universe." The resistance, in response,
  seeded the galaxy with **Primal Engines** — biological "life rafts" meant to preserve life if the
  collective's plan succeeded.
- **The Destruction.** The **nine-tier attack** — the war to end the war — is launched. "In that
  chaos, much happened, but so much more didn't." The collective, believing it had won, evolves into
  its final form: the **Eulogist**, now "asleep, only monitored by a smaller instance of itself" —
  which Grigori/the AI nicknames **the Security Guard**. Only the Apothecary survives among the
  Primals, having taken the brunt of the final attack to let new, budding life on new worlds
  survive; she emerges later, "physically changed into a version of the very thing she'd been
  fighting against."
- **What the mantids did.** New species — including the mantids — later stumble onto the tunnel
  network the Primals left behind, meant only for communicating with and debugging the sleeping
  Eulogist. They mistake it for a toy: "It was the equivalent of wandering into a random house,
  finding it still has power, and just flipping every switch you find." Worse, they figure out how
  to grow their own crude copies of the Eulogist and seed them into new planetary systems —
  which is where every local dungeon system AI, including the one narrating, comes from. "To this
  day, you have no idea how any of this works, and you have no inkling of what you've done."

Between segments, each float's presentation earns a live, brutal review — Grigori-as-AI breaks the
legs of an entire float's crew mid-parade over a lackluster showing ("Should I kill them all
anyway?... Nah... Their failure was ultimately my fault... Still, this was a C effort at best."),
laughs approvingly at another (Elle's "Fuck you" sign to the viewing audience), and lets Prepotente
finish an unscripted solo of "In the Air Tonight" without interruption. The presentation closes with
Carl's own "vengeance" speech to the masked viewing stands, timed to pass under the AI's/Grigori's
gaze — Carl heals the still-unconscious Chaco in full view of Grigori without any reaction from the
possessed body.

## Release

The possession ends sometime before the finale's aftermath. Prime Minister Victory's broadcast feed
later shows "the AI releasing control of the NPC Grigori, who, confused, stumbled back toward the
crawlers' garage" — the only glimpse given of him coming back to himself. What he remembers of being
used this way, if anything, is not shown.
