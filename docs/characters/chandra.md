# Princess Chandra, Esq.

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC — a Naga lawyer and newly-minted royal, and the book's clearest recurring
      antagonist
    - **Race:** Naga
    - **Class/Subclass:** Not applicable — an attorney, not a combatant
    - **Level:** Not stated
    - **Role:** Newly appointed **Princess** of the Naga Sultanate (her uncle seized the throne in a
      coup after Vinata's death); self-appointed attorney to both Carl and Princess Donut; Carl's
      wife, via **Widow's Rights**
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None — operates entirely through Naga legal custom, Syndicate
      court filings, and hired muscle
    - **Notable items:** None described
    - **Status:** Alive, at large, and still litigating — she flees the guild after Donut's threat
      partway through the book and is not seen on-page again, though Quasar confirms she is "still
      out there, still tossing filings out into the void" by book's end

## Overview

Chandra resolves Book 7's epilogue cliffhanger — Carl's mysterious, unexplained marriage — on the
opening page of Book 8, and spends the rest of the book trying to convert that marriage into
control of both Carl's and Donut's fortunes. She never wins a fight with Donut outright, but she
also never loses badly enough to be written off, and the book ends with her case against the
marriage's legality still pending.

## Widow's Rights

Chandra is introduced in an interlude before Heat 1, aboard the Naga warship *Ventilator* in the
aftermath of a coup that put her uncle on the Naga throne. She is coldly ambitious and openly
contemptuous of her own people's low status in Syndicate society — "Chandra was sick of how her
kind were treated" — and her entire plan turns on a quirk of Naga inheritance law:

> This was the old Naga law that stated a Naga royal widow could claim her husband's assassin as
> her new husband. This right usually only extended to the "primary" wife, but because that bitch
> of an obsidian had died alongside Vinata, Chandra had moved to the primary slot, even if it had
> been only for a few hours.

Her late husband was **Rishi** — killed by Carl at the end of Book 7 — and with Vinata (the actual
primary wife) also dead, Chandra briefly outranks Rishi's five other widows and becomes, by the
letter of the law, entitled to claim Carl as her replacement husband. A Naga court approves it,
treating the whole thing as a public joke at Carl's expense: the marriage is announced as an
accomplished fact before Carl or Donut are even told it happened, and it is the marriage Quasar
references at the very end of Book 7 without explanation.

## The Conservatorship Play

Chandra's real goal is money. Book 7 established that Donut's fan-funded fortune (the Princess
Posse's accounts, plus royalties from unauthorized merchandisers like the "Donut Holes" group) is
enormous and unmanaged. Chandra's opening move is a legal claim that Donut's species "was not yet
recognized as a legally competent racial entity by the Syndicate," which would place all of Donut's
assets under a conservatorship — with Carl, as Chandra's own new husband, named custodian in name
only, and Chandra herself installed as Donut's attorney of record with practical control of both
fortunes:

> And then, after a short conversation and a discreet payment to a district judge's reelection
> campaign, Chandra had found herself assigned as Princess Donut's attorney, effectively giving
> herself access to both Carl's and Donut's financial empires.

She arrives in the guild's segregated safe room and lays the paperwork in front of Donut directly —
the conservatorship order, a lawsuit rerouting all future Princess Posse royalties into her own
trust account, a seizure of the Posse's existing accounts (including the Faction Wars prize money),
a challenge to the Donut Holes' sponsorship, and a subpoena demanding to see the Donut Holes' books.

## "What a Sad Little Snake You Are"

Donut dismantles her without raising her voice. When Chandra tries to justify the seizure with
pre-collapse Earth paperwork Carl's family once used against him, Donut turns the same category of
document back on her — a matching set of Carl's father's own arrest records, a marriage certificate,
and financial disputes — to make a point about character rather than law:

> "They paint a very distinct picture of who Carl's father was as a person. Much the same way these
> papers paint a picture of who you are, *Princess* Chandra." Donut let out another sigh. "What a
> sad little snake you are. I pity you."

Chandra, humiliated and furious, threatens Donut back — and is immediately undercut by an incoming
message on her own interface. Her hired Taurin mercenaries, tasked with killing Carl's attorney
**Quasar**, are asking for her coordinates in person. Realizing the media pass covering the meeting
might be watching everything, she panics and gathers her papers to flee. Donut's parting line makes
the threat explicit without raising a hand:

> Donut leaned in toward Chandra and let out a low growl, like she was going to pounce. The crawler
> whispered, "Since you're new to this, let me give you your first lesson on being a princess.
> Lesson one, don't put yourself in situations where you're so afraid that you piss yourself. It
> shows a lack of decorum."

Later on Floor 11, Donut recounts a second, off-page confrontation to Carl in the same register —
Chandra tried to force an exit deal onto Donut without her consent, backed off the instant some
notification frightened her, and vanished: "The whole thing took, like, two seconds!" This is the
scene the book's outline points to as Donut "intimidating her into fleeing" — the text gives it to
the reader secondhand, through Donut's own recap, rather than as a directly staged confrontation.

## The Taurin Contract

Chandra's retaliation for the "hit" Donut places on her is to send **Taurin mercenaries** after
Quasar's own family — an attack fended off by his niece **Tempest** (see
[Tempest](supporting-cast.md), already established from Book 7) using an illegal blaster Quasar had
given her. Quasar identifies the client behind the attack immediately and vows revenge; see
[Quasar](quasar.md) for his side of the subplot.

## Unresolved

Chandra never appears on-page again after fleeing the safe room, but she does not go away. By the
Floor 11 chapters, Quasar's own status report treats her as an ongoing, active threat rather than a
closed one:

> Princess Chandra. Donut's lawyer. The Naga bitch had disappeared the moment Donut had put the
> "hit" on her, but she was still out there, still tossing filings out into the void left and
> right. Quasar was scheduled to argue against her in a hearing in a few days regarding the
> legality of the marriage certificate. He was going to lose, but the filing had put a pause button
> on a lot of her other actions.

Book 8 ends without resolving either the marriage or the conservatorship claim. Both are explicit
open threads — Chandra remains Carl's legal wife, remains Donut's attorney of record, and remains,
per Quasar, likely to win at least the pending hearing.
