# Louis Santiago

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Human Crawler. Still human as of Book 7, per Eileithyia's own ruling — despite a
      transplanted naiad heart and lungs and a permanent set of **gills**
    - **Class:** Pest Exterminator → **Extermination Professional** (Book 5, Floor 6
      specialization) — grants a 0.5% insta-kill chance on same-level-or-lower mobs
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** 22ish (Book 4) → 42 (Book 5); not stated in Book 7
    - **Rank (Book 7):** **Army Air General** of the Princess Posse — a rank unique to him
    - **Marital status:** Engaged to [Juice Box](juice-box.md) from Book 4; **married** to her in a
      changeling "small wedding" in Book 7 and **divorced** by her before the floor ends. Becomes the
      father of [Katia](katia.md)'s son as part of her exit from the dungeon
    - **Party:** Katia's splinter team
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** *Tent the House* — an area-of-effect potion-delivery spell,
      castable once every six hours, expanded further when cast from an airborne platform; a poison
      cloud attack used repeatedly in Book 7
    - **Notable items:** A trap-module boombox rig (rigged from Carl's own trap modules); the airship
      ***Party Planner***, a *Casket in the Sky*-class tilt-rotor stealth light bomber (Book 7, from a
      celestial box — **destroyed** in Chapter 66), plus the **Airship Portal** guild upgrade that
      hangars it
    - **Status:** Alive. Five player-killer skulls from Book 6; vivisected by the Reavers and rebuilt
      by Mordecai in Book 7; descends to Floor 10 with the party

## Overview

Louis is a comic-relief lieutenant who becomes one of Carl and Donut's most consistently loyal
allies from Book 4 onward, alongside his best friend Firas. Both become the party's dedicated
pilots for the flying house (*the Twister*) on Floor 5, and Louis proposes to Juice Box by the end
of that book.

## Role in the Story — Book 5

Louis fights with Katia's splinter team through Floor 6's hunter war, using his *Tent the House*
spell to deliver Mordecai's anti-undead herbicide against the floor's world-quest "Odious
Creepers." He co-produces (with Donut) the Butcher's Masquerade's viral pet-show entrance for
Mongo — complete with a smuggled trap-module boombox and a full moonwalking reveal — and, while
stuck at the party, coordinates by chat with the Sledge during the desperate effort to smuggle a
refugee changeling community (disguised as elves) into the High Elf castle's sentinel towers. The
operation goes badly: the *Twister* itself is boarded and overrun by mantis nymphs, and the
changelings aboard it shape-shift into birds and flee under their own power rather than escaping
with it. It's the Sledge alone — along with the gnome Bonnie and two rescued Ursine cubs, who
never make it aboard the *Twister* at all — who ends up riding the High Elf castle itself down to
Floor 9 once he casts *Zerzura* on it in the battle's final moments.

He's devastated when the
Sledge, forced to abandon the doomed *Twister*, instead crashes the airship into the last two
active castle turrets still firing on him, Bonnie, and the cubs on the castle roof — and again
when his best friend Firas dies in the battle's climax, having secretly been planning to be
Louis's best man at Louis and Juice Box's eventual wedding (and had asked Katia to secretly sew the
tuxedoes as a surprise).

## Role in the Story — Book 6

Book 6 gives Louis the best supporting arc in the book.

### Christmas

Two hours after fighting a hydra head wearing his own mother's face, Louis throws a Christmas party
in the guild hall. He dates it off Chris's iPod (March 20th), wears "an idiotic Christmas sweater
featuring a mooning Santa Clause," and bribes Mordecai's changeling kids with Vietnamese candy the
strippers helped wrap.

Carl asks whether this isn't all a little inappropriate, and Louis — for the first and only time in
the series — gets angry at him:

> "Oh my god. Look, man," he said, suddenly serious. There was something else there, something I'd
> never seen from the large man. Anger. He had tears in his eyes… "You think I don't know? That last
> party sucked, man. My best friend died. And after today… Imani's squad doesn't have a key.
> Tserendolgor doesn't have a key. Lots of squads don't. I'm not stupid. None of us are. We all know
> what happens next. It's going to be terrible, and some of us are going to die. And if we survive?
> We go down to the next floor, and we do it all over again. I have a whole, entire army of elves who
> want to kill me on the ninth floor. Britney can't stop crying. Tran just watched a memory of his
> own mother tell him he's not welcome home anymore. Katia is struggling every day, and Bautista is
> having a nervous breakdown. **And every time I look at you, I get scared that you're going to go
> insane and kill everybody in the room because your eyes are getting wilder and wilder by the day.**"
>
> He lifted a shaking finger and pointed it into my chest. "**But not tonight. None of that is going
> to matter tonight. I don't care what you say or what you think. I don't care what day it really is.
> Tonight it's *fucking* Christmas, and we are going to have fun and drink some goddamned eggnog and
> open presents.**"

Carl folds, in the only way he knows how:

> "This isn't Christmas," I said, turning to face Louis. He swallowed as he met my eyes. I pulled a
> turkey corpse from my inventory and held it out. "**Not until we eat one of these.**"

Carl and Imani are both still carrying their unopened presents from Santa when the floor collapses.

### Five Skulls, and a Sixth

Louis gets his first player-killer skulls on Floor 8 — **five of them, all from one attack** — and,
per Katia, is not handling it well.

Which is why, when Carl drops [Sister Ines](sister-ines-quiteria.md) unconscious with a *Nighty-Night*
wand and the party has to finish her, Louis shoulders him out of the way:

> "I'll do it," Louis said, pushing me aside. "**I already have five player skulls and none of you
> guys do. Trust me. You want to put that off as long as possible.**" Before I could object, he
> pulled a dagger from his inventory. "Sorry cat lady. I never met you, but you need to go if we want
> to save everybody else." He took the knife and plunged it directly into her chest.

A marked-for-death indicator — a spinning squiggle like a tornado — appears over his head the instant
the knife lands, and music starts to play.

> "Uh-oh," Louis said.
>
> **The death of the bard has angered her celestial Patron. She is coming to seek revenge.**
>
> **The Goddess Ysalte has entered the realm.**

Ysalte hunts him personally through the rest of the battle — "Find that one with my mark upon him,
and kill him!" — and he survives her. [Paz Lo](paz-lo.md) kills the goddess, and Louis collects on the
curse:

> **Louis: Dude! I got a Celestial box for outliving a goddess who has a marked for death curse on
> me!**

He finishes the book carrying an unconscious Carl through the burning city in his arms.

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Louis gets married, gets divorced, gets shot down, gets cut open, gets a naiad's heart and lungs, and
ends the book as the father of [Katia](katia.md)'s child. It is the most that happens to anyone in
the book who is not a warlord.

### Army Air General

The Princess Posse's warlord menu gives him a rank nobody else has: **Army Air General**. His
celestial box from Floor 8 pays out in two pieces — a tier-three guild upgrade called an **Airship
Portal**, an elevator from the guild hall to a dimensional hangar, and the airship itself.

> Casket in the Sky. Party Planner. Tilt Rotor Stealth Light Bomber.
>
> This aircraft is owned by crawler Army Air General Louis Santiago 2.

*Casket in the Sky* is the model name; ***Party Planner*** is what Louis calls his. The system
describes it as "a lighter-than-air, long-range death delivery system" and is candid about the
tradeoff: forward and aft anti-air guns, rudimentary shields, and nothing else. "Seriously. It would
take a pilot with gonads the size of bowling balls to fly this thing into a heavily fortified area."
Every bomb dropped from it gains 50% yield. Donut's verdict on the name is a flat "Casket in the
Sky?"; Carl's is "Jesus, Louis. This thing looks like a death trap."

He flies it low and fast on bomb runs across every front of the war, carrying fifty of Carl's
thermobaric charges at one point, and using it to deliver the opening barrage that cracks the
southern trench line — bombing the ground *in front of* the enemy trenches rather than the trenches
themselves, to strip their traps and bury their mages in dirt.

### The Small Wedding

Chapter 10, in the grimy entrance bar of the Desperado Club, after Clarabelle has banned every
crawler in the dungeon from the club proper.

Changeling weddings come in two parts. The **small wedding** is the legally binding one: no rings, no
officiant, no singing, no ring bearer, to Donut's considerable disgust. The **Confirmation** a month
later is the party. Mordecai explains the catch — part of the small wedding is signing divorce
papers, which expire in a month if unfiled. For that month either party can walk away with no
consequence.

Louis is not exactly swept up in it. He agrees because the marriage "bonds the houses" and keeps the
NPC team on side:

> Louis: She thinks it's important. We need them on our side, and if this helps, I'll do it. If they
> turn against us, we're screwed. This will help. We need each other.

Donut coaches him into the proposal by chat ("DON'T BE ALL CARL ABOUT IT. BE ROMANTIC"), and he
manages it, after asking [Juice Box](juice-box.md) to change into someone else first. The ceremony
itself takes almost no time; the vows are whispered in each other's ears, to be repeated aloud at the
Confirmation that never happens.

> "I wish my mom was here," he whispered.

### Shot Down

Chapter 66. On his second sortie of the general offensive, a gravity-magic dragon over Reaver
territory tears *Party Planner* in half. Louis reports it himself, mid-fall, in the chat:

> Louis: A little busy now, Carl. Feather falling... Party Planner is dead. Ripped in half... Hit the
> dragon with missiles, and it just pissed it off... It cast a spell, and I tried to dodge. It ripped
> Party Planner down the middle. Dropped the bombs before they were armed. I'm sorry... They didn't
> go off. I'm falling through the air right now. Gonna land right on the Reaver castle... I think my
> door gunners are dead... Glad Bonnie and Skarn weren't here... He was right... That cop's
> husband. I'm sorry... The feral god is still there a mile west of the castle. I see him just
> sitting there. Dragon still flying above... I'm going to hit the castle by myself. Tell Katia when
> she wakes up that I'm sorry to be such a fuckup...

"That cop's husband" is a Book 7 reveal, delivered second-hand by Katia: as a child, Louis was
protected by a police officer who was shot and killed doing it, and at her funeral her husband said
something cruel enough to Louis that he has carried it his whole life.

### What the Reavers Did

He lands in the middle of the Reaver castle, is tracked by its security, and disappears into a
cell for the better part of the floor — unconscious, health bar untouched, because he is hooked to a
machine keeping him alive. Juice Box, reaching him first, describes what she found:

> "They were doing surgery on him. He was... cut open. His heart has been replaced. His lungs have
> been replaced. It looked like they were getting ready to replace his brain, but we got to him in
> time. I had to heal him, but when I did it, it didn't regrow his heart or lungs."

The replacement organs are mechanical with biological threads woven through them, and the biology
isn't his:

> "It's not Louis. It's... It's pieces of Tagg."

**[Epitome Tagg](epitome-tagg.md)**, warlord of the Dream, has grafted himself into Louis's chest
cavity and controls his vital organs from inside them — the same trick Warlord Fang used to survive
inside the dragon. Tagg's leverage is stated plainly in the warlord chat: "Fuck off. I control his
vital organs. You try to remove me, and I will shut him down." See Tagg's own page for the cyborg
reveal and how it resolves.

Juice Box gets him out by taking a **hunter wraith** form, possessing his unconscious body, walking
it to a teleport, and dumping his No Disassemble charges behind her; the detonation kills Warlord
Fang and finishes the Reavers.

### Heart, Lungs, and Gills

Chapter 84, with Emberus counting down the floor's last hour, the party performs open-chest surgery
on Louis in a field tent — thirty minutes they do not have.

The donor organs come from **Princess Lunette**, the naiad flesh golem in Carl's inventory: Signet's
mother, originally one of Edgar's tattoos, handed to Carl at the end of the Butcher's Masquerade as
the body [Samantha](samantha.md) was eventually going to inhabit. It is Samantha who proposes
spending it, knowing exactly what it costs her:

> "If we do this, that means you won't get your body back. At least not for a while."
>
> She nodded solemnly. "I know. It's okay. Louis deserves it more than I do."

[Mordecai](mordecai.md) cuts and sews. Edgar directs. Donut pours the healing potion and Mordecai's
Special Brew. Carl and Tipid hold the ribcage open against the healing trying to close it. Imani,
Rosetta and Elle extract the naiad organs. Juice Box, shifted into a Fleshmancer form, casts the
spell that makes them take. Bucket Boy yanks the old organs free — Mordecai can't, without it
counting as an attack. Mongo eats the discarded plastic heart and lungs, and the system credits him
with killing Tagg.

The complication is that Lunette was a full naiad, so the organs are water-only. Mordecai's fix is a
tiny device from Carl's inventory:

> "It was the kua-tin rebreather I'd taken from Loita's body after I'd assassinated her."

He slits the bronchial, sews the rebreather inside it, casts Water Breathing from a scroll, and grows
Louis a set of **gills**, tubed directly to the transplanted organs and held open with stops until
they set. They are permanent. Louis spends the rest of the book with a hand around his own throat,
coughing water.

!!! note "The Loita reference is the same Loita"
    This is the Borant administrator Carl assassinated on Floor 5 with the rigged Veriluxx toy in
    Book 4 — not a namesake. Book 7 states it twice: "Loita the kua-tin I'd assassinated on the fifth
    floor," and, at the surgery, "the kua-tin rebreather I'd taken from Loita's body after I'd
    assassinated her." Carl looted her rebreather off her corpse at the time and has carried it since.
    Loita was kua-tin and wore the rebreather because kua-tin are aquatic, which is why it fits a
    naiad's lungs.

### Divorced, and a Father

Juice Box files the divorce papers inside the month, and she does it because of what the wraith form
showed her while she was saving him. The **Inevitable Ruin** is a curse laid on hunter wraiths: when
they possess someone, it tells them the truth about what they possessed. What it told her about Louis
was that "he's terrified of me. The truth is he's not in love with me," and that he believes he has a
special ability that makes NPCs fall for him, so he will never trust what she feels. She leaves
rather than be another monster in his life. Louis's account of it is three sentences:

> "Juice Box left me," Louis said, coming to sit down in the tent… "She gave me divorce papers."

Minutes later, Eileithyia tells Katia she must name a human father for the child that will carry her
out of the dungeon, and that it cannot be Carl. Katia asks Louis. His first concern is whether the
gills disqualify him.

> "Uh," Louis said. He reached up and touched his neck. "Am I still human?"
>
> "That's actually a good question," Donut muttered. "The last thing Katia needs is a baby with
> flippers."
>
> Eileithyia smiled, and she put her hand on Louis's hand. "Of course you are. And a good soul at
> that."
>
> "Then sure, I guess. I mean, if she'll have me."

The goddess confirms it will be a boy. Louis's reply: "Spoilers."

He walks into the Floor 10 stairwell mourning his airship — "I really wish I could've kept Party
Planner" — and Carl tells him Florin stole a plane.
