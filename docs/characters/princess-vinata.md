# Princess Vinata

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC warlord — **the Sultana**, playing the historical role of **the Sepsis Whore**,
      leader of the **Blood Sultanate** in Faction Wars. Outside the dungeon she is the real-life
      crown princess of the naga, daughter of the king, a war veteran and lead advisor to her father
    - **Race:** Naga — and an unusual one. Six arms rather than the usual four, “half again as big as
      any” other naga Carl has seen, with solid red eyes that have no pupils or slits. In-game she is
      a brilliant, glossy white with gold along the edge of her cobra hood; the white is makeup
    - **Class:** None stated — warlords are not given character sheets
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** Not stated
    - **Religion:** Worshipper of **Khepri**, God of Rebirth and the Sunrise, Level 250 — a break in
      naga tradition, and the centrepiece of her trap
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** **Travel the Path** and **Welcome Home**, the light-god spell
      pair that lets her vanish instantly from her own throne room; a temporary **Immortality** boon
      from Khepri’s presence; Invisibility and Speed potions; glowing daggers in her lower hands
    - **Notable items:** A golden breastplate embossed with a tree branch bearing a single flower;
      golden bracelets on each of her six arms, “that jingled and sang with each wave-like movement”
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — killed at the end of Chapter 54 in the lobby of Club Vanquisher by a
      hatchet to the skull from the sluggalette [Kandy “Bigs” Newton](kandy-bigs-newton.md)

## Overview

Vinata is the reigning Sepsis Whore: the target [Donut](princess-donut.md) has been contractually
locked into killing since Book 1, when she put the
[Crown of the Sepsis Whore](../items/crown-of-the-sepsis-whore.md) on her head before Mordecai could
finish reading the warning. She is also, unusually for a five-book payoff, a genuinely frightening
person in her own right.

[Carl](carl.md) meets her at the warlord council in Chapter 42, where she materialises coiled beside
him with no chair, “bigger than I was expecting, like a building had just suddenly appeared to my
left,” smelling of sweet flowers.

> I’d seen several nagas at this point, but I’d never seen anything quite like this woman. Her very
> presence was electric, and I had to make an effort to close my mouth. I hadn’t had a reaction like
> this since I’d first seen Signet on the third floor, but that had been a magical charm. And that
> had been a physical attraction. Such effects were not active here in this room. Still, I felt this
> overpowering sense of awe. I felt... small in her presence. This was how a mouse felt when it was
> placed into the cage with a snake.

Her introduction is a handshake Carl’s hand passes straight through:

> “Carl,” she said, her voice sounding surprisingly feminine for such a large creature. It was like
> we were the only two in the room. “I don’t believe we’ve properly met.” She held out her lowest arm
> and offered it to me, palm down. “My name is Princess Vinata. But for these games, you’re welcome
> to call me the Whore.”

The chapter opens with a warning from an earlier cookbook author:

> The naga. Beware the naga. They are cunning, and sometimes I wonder if they can read one’s mind.
> The others say that the mantids are the masters of strategy, but I do not believe this. Even though
> everything seems to be going wrong for the nagas this season, I cannot shake the idea that this all
> isn’t part of some grand scheme. Underestimate them at your own peril.

## The Ghost of Faction Wars

The Blood Sultanate are perennial losers who have never once lost their Sultana. Their castle is
almost always the first to fall, at ruinous cost to the NPCs and mercenaries left holding it — and
every single season, by the time the invaders reach her inner sanctum, she is simply gone.

> Viewers would carefully watch the naga feed to see where she went. They’d watch the castle get
> invaded while the sultana would remain in her chambers, and when the invaders got close, the veiled
> naga would calmly get up from her nest of pillows, go through a door, and then she’d simply be
> gone. And not just her, but her entire elite honor guard of six nagas.

The vanishing act is deliberate branding. The naga’s continued participation in Faction Wars is a
tourism campaign: after she disappears, the feed fills with advertisements for the “exotic” naga
system, the ancient palace, the ziggurats, the Forbidden Bazaar. Whole tunnel communities argue about
how she does it. Nobody has ever infiltrated the naga inner sanctum — not even
[Juice Box](juice-box.md)’s changelings, and the awakened NPC Arief will not try:

> “The nagas are not very good at mass warfare, and they say they never win this game. It is said
> theirs is a court of assassins, not warriors. If they are to win, it is with a poisoned blade in
> the dark, not with the marching of a thousand feet.”

## “I wonder how you will react”

At the council she spends the meeting whispering to Carl alone, with a polite, serene smile never
leaving her face. She opens by telling him a piece of non-public news — that Cascadia’s family have
just been killed in the Borant system — and watching him watch the kua-tin fall apart. Asked how she
could possibly know that, given adjutants and warlords are cut off from outside information, she does
not answer. Instead:

> “I wonder how you will react when you learn your entire family is dead as well?”

Carl says his family is already dead. She clarifies, and it turns into the only real offer anyone
makes him all book:

> “That’s not the family of which I speak, Carl. You and I both know you will have to make your move
> soon. Perhaps it’s happening now, while we are up here. We are expecting you, and we will be ready.
> They will all die, and after, what will happen? Will you become catatonic like Cascadia? When you
> are forced to choose between Donut and Katia, what choice will you make? How will the other
> survivors, if any, react? Say the word. Simply nod, and we will take care of your Katia problem for
> you. We do not have to be enemies, Carl. We can both survive this, and perhaps, gain something in
> the process.”

And then the recruitment pitch:

> “I would very much like to make peace with you. I would like for you to join us in our system when
> this is all done. My father’s court could always use someone like you. We have great plans. We, too,
> are survivors. That is how we are the same, you and I.”

The whispering is also cover. The council is a stalling operation: while Vinata holds Carl’s
attention, Odette blunders into revealing that the Sultanate owns Blood Hunter Holdings, and Carl
works out that the missing naga army is not hiding — it is dead, wiped out by
[Prepotente](prepotente.md) at the start of hostilities, with the Sultanate quietly pretending
otherwise. Donut addresses her directly for the first and only time:

> Donut looked Vinata up and down, and for the first time, addressed the naga princess directly. “Oh,
> honey. And I thought I was going to be the one who killed you.” She licked her paw and groomed
> herself for a short moment. “In the eternal words of my mulleted friend, Holger. You done stuck
> your pecker in the wrong beehive.”

## Operation: Snake Pit

Carl’s plan to free Donut and [Katia](katia.md) from the crown runs roughly from Chapters 44 to 55
under the codename **Operation: Snake Pit**. It required killing the four blood members of the Blood
Sultanate’s line of succession out of the eleven naga playing this season:

1. **Princess Vinata** herself, the Sultana.
2. and 3. Two female assistants holding the title **Ikbal** — in real life, Vinata’s cousins. One is
   married to **Rishi**, the temple guard. The AI’s boss-battle commentary describes the pair as a
   graphic designer and a PR agent.
4. The male **head of her castle guard**, also a cousin, and — per rumour reported by
   [Rosetta](rosetta-thagra.md) — her lover. Carl calls him “the Sultana’s real-life boyfriend”; the
   AI, during the temple fight, calls him her “secret husband.”

!!! note "No names given"
    The text never names the two cousins or the head of the castle guard. Only Vinata and Rishi (who
    is not a blood member and therefore not a target) are named among the naga.

The castle guard dies first, killed by [Elle](elle-mcgibbons.md)’s squad in the naga stronghold while
Carl is still at the council. That leaves three.

The rest of the operation exists purely to make Vinata run on Carl’s schedule — a full-front push by
[Florin](florin.md) in the north, a feint by Tran in the south, and then a wave of sluggalos sent to
swarm the castle “face-first, inexpertly killing and getting themselves splattered like a bunch of
idiots.” As Carl puts it: “We just needed the hornet’s nest kicked. We needed Vinata’s escape plan to
get triggered on our schedule.”

## How She Disappeared

Rosetta’s theory, which the whole plan is bet on, turns on a quirk of the light gods.

Fifteen deities of light in the Ascendency pantheon keep temples inside
[Club Vanquisher](../locations/club-vanquisher.md). All fifteen grant **Travel the Path**, a spell
that turns the caster into light and lets them follow a path at the speed of light — “It was a
dangerous spell to use and oftentimes ended with the caster getting splattered or cut in half by an
unexpected shadow blocking their route. It was not considered teleportation by the system.” Six grant
it immediately on worship. Four of those six also grant **Welcome Home**, a once-ever skill that,
combined with Travel the Path, lets the worshipper follow a Bijou fairy’s light tendril straight to
the strongest connected temple — which is invariably inside Club Vanquisher. It works even if the
temple’s physical entrance has been destroyed.

Two of the four gods come with a recruitment quest that can smuggle the rest of her guard in with
her: **Theia**, who turns prospects into motes of light that fit in a pocket, and **Khepri**, who
turns them into literal beetles that the female worshipper must swallow whole and later give birth to
on a buffet table. Rosetta’s reading of past seasons — a year Vinata never appeared at all after the
Viceroys’ summoned goddess buried the temple entrance, another year she arrived late and two guards
short during a demon eviction event near that same temple — points at the club.

Theia is off the board this season because she is sponsored, and by the Pacifist network. That leaves
Khepri. Carl’s team uses Edgar the tortoise and his level-15 *Sense Movement* skill to watch for the
upward flick of a Bijou fairy from inside the naga castle. When Edgar reports “Non-corporeal, Coming
from below and moving up,” the theory is confirmed and the raid goes.

## The Trap

It was a trap, and Carl sees it the instant he is inside.

The one thing Rosetta’s model got wrong was who did the worshipping. Traditionally a guard takes the
god so that the Sultana never shows any sign of worship. This time:

> To my surprise, I saw Vinata was actually the one who’d worshiped Khepri, which was a break in
> tradition.

> It all hit me at once. Vinata knew we’d have to come for her no matter what. We’d assumed that she
> wouldn’t anticipate we’d find her secret hiding place. But, unlike so many others, she hadn’t
> underestimated our determination, and she’d prepared for this eventuality. She’d been the one to
> worship Khepri, and she’d somehow prepared a method to summon him into his own temple. She’d waited
> to see if we’d find her, and when we did, she’d summoned the bug god, knowing we’d storm right into
> the deity’s lap. As trespassers into the god’s own temple, we’d be fucked.
>
> It was the perfect trap, and we’d walked right into it.

What she could not price in was that being in the presence of a summoned deity hands Carl a
**temporary Immortality** buff of his own — 90 seconds against her 63. The boss-battle card goes up:

> God of Rebirth and the Sunrise. Khepri! Level 250.

The AI also broadcasts what she was actually protecting, which is not the game at all:

> Breaking news, folks. Princess Vinata is about to inherit her own solar system. That’s no secret,
> but what you don’t know is that after a few hundred cycles pretending to be destitute, her entire
> kingdom was about to surprise everyone. But now her secret husband is dead, and her kingdom’s plans
> to pull a Fred Johnson and seize all the shipbuilding factories are being leaked. As we speak, all
> factory owners are moving to protect their assets from the slippery snake folk.

The moment the countdown ends, her three guards charge on Speed potions, the two cousins flee
invisible down the endless banquet hall, and Vinata goes invisible and charges. Carl drops **The
Waffle Maker** — a thermos-sized thermobaric bomb that incinerates everything within 100 metres — at
his own feet, inside a long, narrow room that acts “like a cannon barrel.”

> If not for the Immortality struggling to keep my very atoms together, I would’ve been just gone.
> There wouldn’t have been anything left.

Both cousins die in the blast. The three guards die. Carl is flattened, burned and turned inside out,
and reforms. Vinata, immortal, does not.

## Death

She had been wrapped around Carl since the explosion blew them both out of the temple portal, and
neither of them realised it for the better part of a minute. When he finally works out what is
screaming in his ear, she has her middle hands around his throat, her upper hands slamming his skull
against the floor of Club Vanquisher, and glowing daggers going in and out of him “like a piston.”

> “Everything. You’ve ruined everything,” she cried as she stabbed me over and over, tears streaming
> down her snake face. She wasn’t white anymore, but a dusky gray.

> “We were almost free. We were almost free,” she cried. I had no idea what she was talking about.
> “You ruined it the moment that fucking cat put that tiara on. We would’ve been gone. We would’ve
> been done. We were leaving this madness, and you had to ruin it. You killed my people. All of them.
> Do you think I really want to be here? I was going to have children with him.”

Carl’s shirt had been blown open in the blast.

> In my choking, confused, inebriated state, I realized my shirt was open. The eye on my chest was
> open, and Vinata was staring directly into it.
>
> She had been driven mad by the Eye of the Bedlam Bride.

See [the Eye of the Bedlam Bride](../items/the-eye-of-the-bedlam-bride.md). She never stops. Khepri
is banished by another god; a third god, Hellik, sits down beside them cross-legged and delivers a
message to Carl while she is still beating his head into pulp; the immortal burning bugs swarm over
both of them. “Even as her body burned, she slammed me over and over.”

What kills her is a level 29 sluggalette bursting out of a slugpox boil on Carl’s back:

> I was about to drop the next bomb when I felt the sluggalo burst out of my back. He—actually, a
> she—slipped out from under my shirt, whooped loudly, and planted a hatchet right between Vinata’s
> eyes, killing her.
>
> “Yeah, bitch! Right in the nugget!” the sluggalo woman shouted. She looked down at me as Vinata’s
> lifeless body slipped off of me and to the side. “I wish I had feet. I’d make me some snakeskin
> boots!”

> System Message: The Sepsis Whore of the Blood Sultanate has fallen. Credit for the kill has been
> given to Kandy “Bigs” Newton of the Princess Posse.
>
> System Message: The Blood Sultanate has been defeated.
>
> System Message: All assets of the Blood Sultanate, as per prior arrangement, have been split evenly
> with the surviving blood relatives of the Sepsis Whore, Warlord Princess Donut and General Katia
> Grim, both of the Princess Posse. As per prior arrangement, all assets have been forfeited to the
> Princess Posse.

Katia’s reaction, in chat, is the joke the whole five-book thread lands on: “Who the hell is Kandy
Newton, and where the hell are you, Carl?”

Carl takes her corpse into his inventory, and only then sees what she actually looked like:

> Her white scales were actually gray with a hint of red along her neck. The white had been some sort
> of makeup or effect. An effect that had been eradicated in the explosion.
>
> In death, she didn’t look so big.

## What Her Death Bought

Killing Vinata completes the four required kills in the Blood Sultanate’s line of succession, which
is the hard prerequisite the crown imposed on Donut and, through the succession rules, on Katia. The
mechanics of the crown belong on its own page —
[Crown of the Sepsis Whore](../items/crown-of-the-sepsis-whore.md) — but the book is clear that her
death is only half of it: “In order to save Donut and Katia from the Sepsis Whore issue, we first had
to kill the entire line of succession of the Blood Sultanate.”

The remainder is the succession problem between the two surviving blood relatives, which is not
settled until much later, when Katia eats the Orchid of Eileithyia’s Grace and the deal with Huanxin
Jinx has to be forced. “Donut wouldn’t be able to leave until after Katia was whisked away. That was
the price of the Crown of the Sepsis Whore.”

The surviving naga, **Rishi** — married into the family but not a blood member, and therefore never
part of the quest — buries himself in the dirt outside the castle, waits out the fighting, surrenders
to Donut, and declares himself leader of the new Naga Sultanate. He speaks well of her:

> “I agree. And I know you don’t believe it, but Vinata was a firm believer in this philosophy. She’d
> seen war on alien soil, and she firmly believed it was all for nothing. Her father insisted she
> participate in Faction Wars. We needed the tourist money. We needed to keep the shipyards staffed
> and running. We were going to steal the entire fleet and run. We were going to travel, as a whole,
> for an entire generation, off in a direction that none of the tunnel threads point. Off to a place
> where it would be a very, very long time before we were found. That was our Great Consensus,
> engineered by Princess Vinata. To leave.”

That is what “We were almost free” meant.
