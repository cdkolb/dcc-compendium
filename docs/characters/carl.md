## Background

Carl enters the dungeon in the same clothes he was standing in when the Earth was harvested —
including a pair of ill-fitting Crocs, which he's self-conscious about ("painfully aware that I
was wearing Crocs that didn't fit"). He's accompanied by his ex-girlfriend's cat, **Princess
Donut**, whom he's holding when the apocalypse hits.

## Class Situation

Unlike most crawlers, Carl does not get a class assigned right away. His character sheet reads:

> **Class: Not yet assigned.**

This persists across multiple checks early in Book 1, which becomes a running point of tension —
other crawlers and NPCs comment on it, and Carl compensates by leaning entirely on unarmed
combat. Mordecai notes that Carl's bare-hand attack has scaled to the point that it's
"just as good right now as any unenchanted weapon."

## Relationship with Princess Donut

Carl and Donut form a party very early on — **The Royal Court of Princess Donut** — with titles
assigned by Donut herself:

- Donut: **Grand Champion Best in Dungeon**
- Carl: **Royal Bodyguard**

Donut is characterized as vain and status-obsessed (insisting on her full title, "GC, BWR, NW
Princess Donut the Queen Anne Chonk"), while Carl treats her partly as a responsibility he didn't
ask for and partly as his only real connection to his old life. Their dynamic — bickering,
protective, occasionally very funny — is a throughline of the early books.

## Background (continued)

Before the collapse, Carl was 27. After a stint in the US Coast Guard as an MK (machinery technician) aboard a cutter, he'd left the service and was working as a civilian marine tech, fixing electrical systems on rich people's party boats. He lived with his girlfriend Beatrice ("Bea") in Seattle; they'd broken up days earlier after he learned she'd cheated on him in the Bahamas, and he was looking after her show cat, Donut, when the apocalypse hit. His Coast Guard background explains his above-average hand-to-hand combat training and why his starting skill sheet already listed **Unarmed Combat: 3** alongside firearms proficiencies he can't use (he entered with no weapons).

## Signature Fighting Style

Carl never picks up a real weapon for long. His build centers on bare-knuckle combat, reinforced
by a stack of unarmed-combat skills (see Quick Facts above for the full list). By the end of
Book 1 these skills, combined with gear like the [War Gauntlet](../items/war-gauntlet-of-the-exalted-grull.md),
make his fists hit as hard as an enchanted weapon.

## Personal Rules

Carl adopts "**You will not break me**" as a private mantra after being forced to kill goblin infants in a boss room (Book 1's darkest moment). He also insists — even when it costs time and safety — on destroying corpses left behind on the second floor once he learns corpses spawn endless waves of Brindle Grubs, and on trying to protect Meadow Lark's group of elderly evacuees even though it's tactically costly.

## Book 2: Race, Class, and the Manager Scheme

On Floor 3, Carl picks **Primal** as his race — a species so rare and mysterious even Mordecai
doesn't fully understand it; legend holds Primals were the first civilization to conquer the
galaxy before vanishing entirely. He picks it specifically because it's the only race/human
option that unlocks training skills past their normal level-15 cap, at the cost of starting five
effective levels behind (-1 to all stats, missing the +10 human bonus).

For class, he takes **Compensated Anarchist** — an explosives/trap/social-media hybrid class
described in-game as "the Agent Provocateur" — over the AI-recommended Bomb Squad Tech and
Prizefighter, largely because it comes with the hidden **Escape Plan** skill, which unlocks
access to a secret **Dungeon Locator** system Donut's own build had flagged as available.

Per [Odette](../characters/supporting-cast.md)'s advance coaching, Carl and Donut deliberately
engineer Mordecai into choosing the hidden **Manager** benefit (tied to Donut's Former Child
Actor class) by making it look like an accident — Donut "misclicks" her floor specialty in a way
that locks it in. Carl goes along with the deception despite real guilt about it, since a manager
who can personally accompany the party (rather than being confined to a guildhall) is too
valuable to pass up. Mordecai remains furious about it through most of Book 2 without learning
it was deliberate.

## Book 3: The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook

Carl's Floor 3 "joke" prize from a fan-vote prize carousel turns out to be the single most
valuable item he's ever received: **[The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md)**,
a living, editable record of survival knowledge left behind by 24 prior crawlers to have owned it.
Carl becomes its 25th author, using it (and a hidden mental "scratchpad" feature that lets him add
notes without physically opening the book) as his primary source of dungeon knowledge for the
entire floor — including power-supply mechanics, portal theory, deity-summoning rules, and
crafting recipes he passes off to Mordecai as his own deductions.

### The Iron Tangle Collapses

Floor 4 is an underground subway-and-trainyard superstructure called the Iron Tangle, run by
gnoll/hobgoblin NPC staff and infested by a "Krakaren" ghoul-generator system. When a group of
crawlers destroys a soul crystal at a ghoul generator, it triggers a cascading, franchise-wide
system failure: the Krakaren (a real, license-parodied alien "Apothecary" species per Mordecai)
floods the Tangle with monsters, hundreds of thousands of crawlers are stranded across the
railway, and the level's collapse timer accelerates. Carl becomes the de facto architect of the
floor-wide rescue effort:

- Reverse-engineers hobgoblin **interdiction carts** (rail vehicles with built-in, dial-a-destination
  portal "scoops" meant for clearing wrecks) into weapons and rescue vehicles, using them to teleport
  entire crowds of stranded crawlers directly to safety and to funnel Krakaren monster waves into
  the abyss
- Farms severed hands from an indestructible, self-resurrecting gnoll bartender NPC named
  **Growler Gary** — 14 hands total — to bypass a two-person security lock on the carts — a plan
  he narrates with open self-disgust. The first several hands are taken while Gary is frightened
  and resisting; Carl stops partway through to explain the full reasoning, after which Gary gives
  resigned consent to the remaining ten
- Builds and detonates a hobgoblin-dynamite train bomb by sending the salvaged Vermillion engine
  backward down a monster-choked line, timed to a hob-lobber trigger pole — his explosives handling
  skill hits 11 and he levels to 35 off the resulting kill credit
- Rigs a rapid-response cart into a **suspended, upside-down portal** directly over a stairwell
  station's collapsing floor — correctly guessing, with Katia, that the Iron Tangle's track layout
  mirrors a hidden, upside-down duplicate level connected by a "noodle" of interior escape tunnels —
  plugging the hole the Krakaren pour through and saving the entire station

### The Ring of Divine Suffering

Carl inherits the **[Enchanted Night Wyrm's Ring of Divine Suffering](../items/enchanted-night-wyrms-ring-of-divine-suffering.md)**
from Frank Q's corpse and wears it briefly for its passive +5% stat bonus. Mordecai reacts with
genuine alarm bordering on violence — smacking Carl for the first time since his own father used
to — explaining that faction-war armies on Floor 9 always include a Divine Suffering ring-bearer,
that the item exists specifically to lure crawlers into becoming farmable power sources for the
factions, and that its owner becomes an immediate, universally-known target the moment word gets
out. Carl removes the ring and agrees to sell it before Floor 6, though he keeps it in inventory
for now.

### The Grull Gambit and the Death Challenge

Cornered by the floor's city-boss mimic while trying to clear a path for stranded ally
[Bautista](supporting-cast.md), Carl exploits his [War Gauntlet's](../items/war-gauntlet-of-the-exalted-grull.md)
involuntary-summon mechanic on purpose for the first time: he beats a captured mantaur neighborhood
boss into "vessel" status, drops the transforming body through a portal to summon the war god
**Grull** — unknowingly piloted, live, by the supposedly-dead **Prince Maestro** as a sponsored
guest deity — directly in front of the mimic. The confrontation is broadcast as a full **Death
Challenge** gambling event (50-to-1 odds against Carl's survival). Carl survives by hiding,
decoying Grull with a Katia-disguised body double, and re-summoning a second captured mantaur at
the last second to yank Grull (and Maestro) out of the arena and into the abyss — winning without
landing a single hit on either monster. He ends the floor at Level 41, having also helped
orchestrate the deaths of Hekla (indirectly, via [Katia](katia.md)) and dozens of soul-crystal
ghoul generators floor-wide.

## Book 4: The Gate of the Feral Gods

Floor 5 traps roughly 1,172 crawlers each inside a self-contained "bubble" world with four
quadrants (Land/Sea/Air/Subterranean), each hiding a castle that must fall before the bubble's
stairwell unlocks. Carl and Donut's bubble is a desert bowl ruled by a dromedarian/changeling
refugee town (Hump Town) under siege by an airborne dirigible-gnome dreadnaught, the *Wasteland*.

### The Air Quadrant and the Fall of the *Wasteland*

Carl builds increasingly refined guided rockets (with Mordecai's help) to fight the floor's
aerial threats, killing the borough boss **Ruckus** (a cyborg chicken hawk) for its buzzsaw
weapon, then engineers an elaborate hostage-swap con — disguising Katia as a corpse, a chair, and
a resurrected zombie gnome simultaneously — to hijack a gnomish ambassador's balloon mid-negotiation
and detonate the *Wasteland* from the inside with smuggled missiles, saving Hump Town from
bombardment (**"Squeeze out the Juice Box"** quest). The strike enrages the System AI for reasons
Carl doesn't understand at first (it wanted him to personally stomp a frenzied gerbil, per its
long-running fixation from Floor 2's Ralph fight) and nearly triggers a punitive "acceleration"
before Carl relents and kills one by hand. He and Donut go on to finish the air quadrant by
defeating the indestructible **Denise the Feral Goose Mother** in a no-magic, no-physical-damage
boss fight (solved by shoving her head into a garbage disposal), rescuing the orphaned NPC child
**Bonnie** in the process.

### Chris, Maggie, and the Infiltrator

Carl encounters a changed **[Chris Andrews](chris-andrews.md)**, who turns out to be puppeted by
**Maggie My** — revealed to have taken an **Infiltrator** (Scree) race, a full-body brain parasite
that locks its host out of their own actions entirely. Unable to save Chris without killing him,
Carl has Donut portal him into a sealed, flooding chamber rather than let him be executed on the
spot. At the very end of the floor, using a Mordecai-brewed Phase Lava Rock potion, Carl reaches
directly into Chris's skull mid-battle to extract the worm; it burrows into Carl's own arm before
Mordecai's double-healing potion forces it out through his neck, and Mongo finishes it off. Chris
is freed and joins the party for the trip down to Floor 6, in Brandon An's memory.

### Killing Administrator Loita

After Donut's merchandising deal repeatedly humiliates her, and PR admin **Loita** threatens to
have Mongo fed to a mob if Donut talks back again, Carl engineers Loita's death disguised as an
accident: rigging a defective RealPet-toy's tamper-proof self-destruct panel to fail at the exact
moment Loita stands up from a couch, using residue from an infused "Hobgoblin Disco Ball" smoke
bomb (learned from the Cookbook) to corrode the panel's seal. A Syndicate liaison, **Orren**,
interrogates Carl and Donut afterward but cannot prove intent and is overruled by an unnamed kua-tin
faction that intervenes on Carl's behalf — Carl learns fifteen admins have already died to crawlers
this season, all with known methods, making his the season's only unsolved case.

### The Gate of the Feral Gods

Carl assembles all three pieces of a celestial-tier artifact — two enchanted pocket watches and a
winding box, together called **[the Gate of the Feral Gods](../items/gate-of-the-feral-gods.md)**
— capable of opening a one-way portal to any dialed coordinate, at the cost of also opening a
temporary two-way gate to "the Nothing" that releases an imprisoned feral god. He uses it twice:
first, repeatedly, to evacuate roughly 2,300 stranded crawlers out of sealed, doomed bubbles
(intentionally unleashing a feral god into each abandoned bubble as the price); second, to send a
gnomish/changeling ally, **[Juice Box](juice-box.md)**, safely through to Floor 6's city of
Larracos while flooding its ninth-floor faction-war marketplace with sharks, explosives, and
seawater instead of a god — deliberately crippling the resupply capability of seven factions who'd
sued to stop him. He keeps the artifact rather than destroying it, over Mordecai's objections.

### Becoming a God's Acolyte

Cornered mid-air-battle by the rival crawler **Quan Ch** — who is trying to kill an innocent,
Celestial-tier puppy named Orthrus for a bounty — Carl throws a sponsor-gifted celestial grenade
without realizing he has no deity to summon, is prompted to pick one, and accidentally becomes a
worshipper of **Emberus**, the fire god whose lost pet the puppy turns out to be. The resulting
Divine Intervention buff saves him and Donut from the ensuing god-fight; afterward he rips Quan
Ch's arm off in a separate confrontation and loots two enchanted rings from it. He ends the book
publicly declaring his intent to hunt down every bounty hunter sent after him on Floor 6, having
just become the dungeon's #1-ranked crawler.

  end — she splits off to protect Eva Sigrid's scattered former "daughters" before Eva can pick
  them off one by one
- Ends the book with Chris Andrews as a new party member and Katia operating independently

## Book 5: The Butcher's Masquerade

Floor 6, the Hunting Grounds, is a jungle Over-City variant where sponsored bounty hunters are
released to hunt the top crawlers by name — 360 were registered as of the pre-release broadcast,
with the announcement itself warning that number was expected to double or triple by the actual
release. Carl treats it as the war he promised at the end of Book 4.

### The River

Book 5 introduces a recurring internal motif: Carl repeatedly hears/feels "a river" or a running
tap at the back of his mind, growing louder under stress and only quieted by rest or violence. It
is tied, obliquely, to a childhood memory of hearing running water, going down to the basement, and
finding a note shortly before his mother arrived — the text never states outright what happened to
her, but the implication (and Carl's private mantra shift to "*I will break you all*") suggests her
suicide is the source of both his numbness around death and his growing willingness to escalate.

### Opening the Hunt

Forced to surrender the Gate of the Feral Gods as collateral (returnable on reaching Floor 9) in a
negotiation with Syndicate liaison **Orren**, Carl gains an attorney, the Nullian lawyer
**[Quasar](quasar.md)**, and four mercenary "cretin" bodyguards (Bomo, the Sledge, Clay-ton, Very
Sullen). Before the hunt officially opens, he teleports solo into Zockau with Donut via Bomo's
*Teleport to Stairwell* spell and runs a "Predator"-style invisibility raid — barrel bombs packed
with gunpowder and hobgoblin dynamite planted by hand along the club district's alleyways, plus a
volley of rocket missiles — that kills roughly 35 hunters (earning a separate "Mass Casualty
Event" achievement for over 250 additional non-hunter NPC casualties from the blast), including
hive matriarch **[Circe Took](circe-took.md)**'s mantis daughter **Xindy** (Vrah's sister, finished
off by missile fire).
<!-- VERIFY: only Xindy is explicitly named as a Circe Took daughter killed in the opening
Zockau raid; the text doesn't confirm a second daughter dying that night. -->
The raid nearly gets him killed by crossbow fire and is what first draws
**[Vrah](vrah.md)** — Circe's remaining daughter and heir — into a personal vendetta. Carl then
builds a second, far larger thermobaric bomb — nicknamed by the system **Just Wait Until Your
Daddy Gets Home** — and he and Donut plan to use her *Clockwork Triplicate* spell to send a copy of
Bomo out to wheel it back into Zockau's streets for a second strike, but they scrap that plan after
finding a taunting note from Vrah warning him off further explosives. The bomb itself doesn't see use until
much later, when Carl kicks it out a saferoom door to blow apart the hunters and mantises chasing
him and Donut out of Alucarda, a village that Lucia Mar had separately seized and slaughtered
earlier in the floor.

### Conquering Towns, Killing Hunters

Carl and Donut spend the floor taking over a string of small Ursine settlements (Point Mongo,
Ferdinand Peak, Nipton) as forward bases, training the local guards to fight off hunters with
missile launchers, and running coordinated raids with Katia's splinter team using seeker-spider
automatons, siege weaponry, and Carl's expanding trap network. He personally kills or marks dozens
of hunters using the newly-worn [Ring of Divine Suffering](../items/enchanted-night-wyrms-ring-of-divine-suffering.md).
By the Butcher's Masquerade prize counter, he's collected roughly 66 trophy hands — 34 short of
the 100 needed for the counter's priciest item, a **Memorial Crystal: Apito** (Queen Imogen's own
necklace, offered as the party's prize). Nobody affords it at the counter, so it reverts to
Imogen — but when she dies in the floor's final battle, a ranger crawler named Osvaldo (his race,
Curupira, is drawn from Brazilian folklore) loots
it straight off her corpse, immediately triggering an update to Prepotente's own "Find Out Who
Killed My Son" quest and setting the two of them up for a dispute over it. Separately, at the
50-hand tier, he's one hand short of buying both a skill upgrade and a case of potions in the same
trip and has to choose.

### Vrah, the Arrow, and Diwata

After Vrah nearly kills him in single combat, Carl improvises a weapon out of a looted **[Arrow
of Enthusiastic Double Gonorrhea](../items/arrow-of-enthusiastic-double-gonorrhea.md)**, jamming
it into her neck moments before she can flee — deliberately choosing infection over an outright
kill to keep her out of the fight without killing a would-be hive queen outright. The gambit
backfires spectacularly: Circe Took turns out to have sponsored the shapeshifting fertility deity
**Diwata** into the realm to cure her daughter — Diwata first manifests through a sacrificed
hunter used, unknowingly, as a birthing vessel, then shapeshifts into a male mantis to personally
cure Vrah by mating with her before turning female and birthing an endless wave of mantis nymphs
into the Hunting Grounds. Carl marks
Vrah for real during the climactic battle at the Butcher's Masquerade; [Mongo](mongo.md) and Kiwi —
a raptor pack leader Mongo mated with earlier on the floor, later revealed to be a cursed,
transformed Ursine woman — tear off her limbs together before Carl sends Kiwi back to the stage and
has Mongo finish her off.

### The Miriam Dom Mercy Kill

Summoned by an endangered **[Miriam Dom](miriam-dom.md)**, Carl secretly marks her with his own
Ring of Divine Suffering and lets her burn to death in the sunrise rather than intervene — the only
way to cure the floor-wide vampirism curse she'd unknowingly spread and to free her paralyzed
adoptive son, [Prepotente](prepotente.md), from a magical stalemate. Only Donut is told the details
firsthand, though a floor-wide world-quest announcement publicly flags the suspicious timing of
Carl's resulting stat boost to everyone else on the floor. He also quietly pockets a couple of
Miriam's abandoned potion scrolls for himself before honoring the rest of his promise to hand her
remaining stockpile over to Prepotente.

### The Butcher's Masquerade

Invited to Queen Imogen's mandatory top-50 party inside the High Elf castle, Carl helps engineer a
multi-front plan — smuggling disguised changelings through a secret underground tunnel to seize the
castle's four sentinel towers on foot, charming Queen Imogen's cat familiar **Ferdinand** through
Donut, and infiltrating the security control room via **[Samantha](samantha.md)** — that has to be
reworked on the fly the moment the party turns out to be sealed under an unbreakable,
Apito-enforced peace ward that traps everyone inside, including Imogen herself, until someone
breaks it. When the plan falls apart, Carl privately resolves to kill fellow crawler **Eva Sigrid**
and break the seal himself as a last resort, sparing his friends the choice — before
**[Tsarina Signet](tsarina-signet.md)** volunteers to sacrifice herself instead, casting a
one-person *Ink Marauder* spell that shatters the peace seal and triggers the floor's final,
catastrophic battle. In the ensuing "Cage Fight Extreme" boss battle against Queen Imogen and
Diwata simultaneously, Carl leads the assault on Diwata, using Donut's *Laundry Day* spell (boosted
past level 15 with a Pawna's Tears potion) to strip away Diwata's divine "armor" — which, since
Diwata is inhabiting Circe Took's own mantis body as her vessel, exposes the ordinary, mortal Circe
Took underneath, stripped of her god-tier protection and immediately torn apart by her own mantis
children. Separately, the Sledge — trapped inside the castle with the gnome Bonnie and two rescued
ursine cubs as everything collapses — casts the long-planned *Zerzura* spell at the last possible
second, teleporting the entire castle (and everyone still inside it, including Ferdinand) straight
down to the ninth floor.

### Losses

The final battle kills **[Signet](tsarina-signet.md)**, **[Gwendolyn Duet](gwendolyn-duet.md)**,
Firas, Horton the mushroom bard, and roughly thirty crawlers who'd stayed behind to help sow
chaos outside the castle, among many others. Carl privately tells a shell-shocked Donut, holding
her as she realizes she isn't grieving properly, that she's wearing an emotional "mask" that will
protect her until it's safe to take it off — a rare moment of him comforting her rather than the
reverse.

### Faction Wars and the Ending

In the wreckage after the battle, Carl and Katia dig two unconscious baby nodlings out of a pile
of corpses and gore, and Carl and Donut discover that the "**Ineligible**" ruling protecting
pregnant/child crawlers has been quietly used to smuggle the Popov brothers to safety as newborn
nodling yearlings. At the floor's close, Carl publicly reveals that the Princess Posse — the crowd-funded
fan organization Donut had been building since Book 4 — has bought a Floor 9 Faction Wars seat,
making the pair **Co-Warlords**, and uses the broadcast to publicly pressure the other eight
factions into voting to remove Faction Wars' sponsor-safety protections. He also learns, via a
surprise, staged reunion on Odette's show, that **Beatrice** — his ex and Donut's original owner —
survived the Earth's collapse and was secretly rescued by Odette; both he and Donut coldly reject
her on-air. Floor 7's entire maze is then instantly destroyed by Prepotente before Carl and Donut
so much as see it properly, and the two of them are already choosing their Floor 8 starting
location (a globe-spinning mishap lands them in the Caribbean instead of the Bahamas Carl wanted)
when the book closes on a rare private point-of-view scene revealing that Katia has separately
hunted down and killed Eva Sigrid — and that the reforming **Crown of the Sepsis Whore** has passed
from Eva's dying grip onto Katia's own head, a secret Carl and Donut do not learn before the book
ends.

## Book 6: The Eye of the Bedlam Bride

Floor 8, "The Ghosts of Earth," is the floor where Carl's entire past is put on public record and
then rebuilt into a boss monster and pointed at him. He enters it ranked **#3** with a 2,000,000-gold
bounty, at **level 64/65**, and leaves it at **level 73** with another consciousness living in his
chest.

### The Shadow Boxer Interview

Zev books Carl and Donut onto ***Shadow Boxer***, an interview show about crawlers' pre-crawl lives,
hosted by the former crawler [Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md). Her opening line to him is "So,
comrade. Let's talk about your dead mother."

What comes out — most of it for the first time, in front of the entire galaxy and in front of Donut:

- On Carl's **fifteenth birthday**, his mother poisoned his father, left him for dead, went down to
  the basement and hanged herself from the pipes. Carl found her. His father survived, barely
  injured. She left a note; it was a letter to Carl, and its contents have never been made public.
- A few months later his father walked out and never came back. Carl lived alone in the house,
  eating whatever was left, until the power went out and someone noticed. He spent a night in an
  interrogation room being asked whether he'd killed his father, took his first cigarette from the
  detective, and — he admits to himself — *wanted* to be suspected: "I was here, goddamnit. I was
  real." Then his father turned up alive and drunk in Wisconsin and the interest evaporated. "I was
  just another sad, abandoned kid. A victim. I was not their problem. I was nothing."
- Two years at the **Anacortes Boys' Ranch**, a high school equivalency certificate, a month on a
  couch in Seattle, and a recruitment centre where the Coast Guard desk was the only one manned over
  lunch.

Donut had been told, for years, only that his mother "left." Her reaction is one of her best moments
in the book: she gets on his lap, asks one question, decides not to push, and leans her head against
his. Carl's closing line on it: "It's in the past. I don't want to be defined by things I have no
control over."

The interview is also a delivery mechanism. Rosetta plants a coded "milk and potatoes" message
pointing Carl at Crawler Milk's 6th-edition cookbook notes on magic ink, and the Valtay liaison
**Harbinger** cuts the broadcast mid-word, recalls Carl's attorney, bans Rosetta from all future
crawler interviews, and tells Carl to his face: "I am actively working to have both you and the
caprid disposed, and I will eventually succeed."

### The Father, and Asher

Floor 8 replays the crawlers' own memories. Carl's takes him into his father's trailer in Iowa to
watch a man he hadn't seen in over twelve years die of liver failure, 140 pounds and yellow, with a
Mariners game on. He finds a small boy in the hallway.

His half-brother **[Asher](asher.md)**, about six, son of his father and his stepmother
**Tami-Lynn**. Carl never knew he existed. Beatrice did — Tami-Lynn had written asking for help,
and Beatrice buried the message, a fact Donut reveals by accident two chapters later. It is the first
time Carl has ever actually been angry at Beatrice.

Carl talks at his unhearing father for a while — "You're broken, you're alone, and you're a nobody.
My only regret is that you lived long enough to inflict yourself onto my poor brother" — and leaves
before the end, because "You don't deserve for anybody to watch you die." Tami-Lynn kills the man
with a lethal dose of morphine she'd been hiding in the flowerpot outside, after sending Asher to a
neighbour's house.

!!! note "Carl's father is never named"
    Donut asks directly: "What was your dad's name, anyway? I don't think you ever said." Carl:
    "**It doesn't matter. He doesn't deserve to have one. Not anymore. It's done.**" No name for him
    appears anywhere in the text.

!!! note "Tami-Lynn's motive is implied, not stated"
    The book shows the morphine and shows her protecting Asher from the scene; Carl says outright
    that he doesn't know what her plan was. Her later line as a hydra head — "I wrote you, and you
    never answered! I needed help. Look what you made me do!" — is the strongest evidence, but the
    text never states the motive directly.

Among the things Carl loots out of the trailer before setting the curtains on fire: his father's
Glock, his motorcycle keys, three packs of Marlboro Reds, court documents, and an arcade cabinet.
"It wasn't Frogger, but Centipede."

### The Reminiscence Hydra of Malicious Compliance

The burning trailer produces the floor's boss: a stitched flesh-ball with **nine screaming heads**,
each one the face of somebody Carl knew, all of them dead in the collapse. Asher, Tami-Lynn,
Monobrow Sam, Bea's mother, Donut's cousin Sugar Cube, Carl's boss Dick, his landlord Mr. Roth,
Sally from billing, and the cat-show judge **Judge Lucian** as the deckmaster. The AI admits on air
that it swapped the boss at the last minute — the original was going to be Carl's father — and that
these things used to be called **Scolopendra Nymphs**.

Carl gets through it by refusing to take the writing seriously: "The heads were clearly saying things
the real versions of these people would never say… the dungeon was trying too hard to be edgy, which
in effect turned the whole thing into a parody."

The consequential moment is silent. Half-dead and on fire, Carl asks his own totem for a mercy —
*Asher. Get him out of here. Kill him fast. He's suffered enough* — and **Shi Maria** grants it, and
opens her third eye on him doing it:

> *Ah, yes. I see now. Don't worry, Carl. I will not blind you. Not in the way you're thinking.*
>
> ***There, Carl. The echo of your kin has been muffled. He rests. I have done something for you.
> Soon, soon you will do something for me.***

Also in the hydra's deck: **Leveled-Up Frank**, a level-90 Blood Assassin Night Elf — the resurrected
[Frank Q](frank-q.md). Carl's immediate reaction is not to the fight but to the precedent: "The
existence of Frank as a card opened up a new, horrific possibility I hadn't even considered… There
were a lot of people who wouldn't be able to handle that."

### Devotee of Emberus

Carl came into the book already worshipping Emberus. The hydra fight promotes him:

> **Baptism by Fire.**
>
> **You have grown closer to your god, Emberus. He is pleased by your suffering.**
>
> **Your new rank: Devotee.**
>
> ***Warning: because you are not a Cleric or a Paladin, this is the highest rank you may obtain.***

It upgrades his Burn immunity so that heat and lava environments (Sheol included) do no environmental
damage, grants a once-a-day level-10 ***Self-Immolation*** — a sphere of Sheol fire 1.5 metres in
every direction, including down — and raises his tithe from 5% to 10%. *Self-Immolation* is the
spell Shi Maria fires off inside a crowded room in Chapter 71.

Later, in Club Vanquisher, the high cleric **Pater Coal** and a satyr jeweller sell him an
**Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus** to survive slugpox — a million gold, 10,000
down, no interest "to our martyrs," financed by the church:

> **You have taken an interest-free\* loan of 990,000 gold from the Temple of Emberus. You will no
> longer be allowed to renounce your religion while you are under debt.**

Carl: "Uh… What the hell does that asterisk part mean?" The jeweller: "I don't understand." The
achievement is called **Columbia House!** and the collateral is the strength ring Carl has worn since
Floor 1.

### Quan Ch

Quan Ch — level 70, still one-armed, sixty-plus player-killer skulls — holds both of the region's
exit keys. Carl teleports into the Capitol stairwell chamber on a spider automaton and takes him
apart. Quan, blind and dying, asks for something back: not his stolen cloak, but a plain gold ring
reading **"For Daddy"** on the inside, still attached to the severed arm in Carl's inventory. Carl
digs it out and puts it in his hand.

> He took in a deep, rattling breath. "She'd be ashamed of what I've become."
>
> I didn't answer. I formed a fist, but before I could finish him off, he died.

The system gives the kill, the bounty, both keys and a **golden player-killer skull** to Donut.

### The Eye of the Bedlam Bride

In the last fight, Carl is temporarily *combined* with a totem — and the totem drives.

Shi Maria takes his body, rifles his inventory, finds the cookbook and the toraline root and the
tattoo kit, and starts building something. She casts his *Self-Immolation* to burn through a floor,
maiming Mongo's wing and Katia's arm in the process. When **Li Jun** slides down a chain to help,
she uses Carl's hand to drive the tattoo stick into his eye, and makes Carl eat it, for ink.

> *If you won't bring this card to the next floor, I will have to attach myself to something you
> can't discard so easily.*
>
> *I am with you now, Carl, until you decide to free me. I will no longer be a card you can abandon,
> one you can rip away. **I am a part of you, and you will have to decide. Keep me with you and gain
> my power, or set me free. Either way, I win.***

A second, male voice intervenes during all this and is shouted down — "*You killed me, and now I am
here*" / "*That is a lie!*" — and gets one warning out to Carl: "*Don't trust her, boy. Get her out
of you as soon as you can. Don't ever trust her.*" The book never identifies the speaker.

The result, in the middle of his chest, is a tattoo of an eye that blinks once and then closes and
vanishes into his skin:

> **Tattoo. The Eye of the Bedlam Bride.**
>
> That's all it said. It gave no other description.

Donut rips Shi Maria's card the moment the battle ends — "She's not going to hurt Mongo ever again" —
and it changes nothing. The escort quest to deliver Shi Maria to Larracos is still in Carl's log, and
Shi Maria is still in Carl's head.

### The Ending, and the Centipede

Carl talks the AI into scouring the entire planet with a hell portal, which lets every surviving
crawler ride a stairwell chamber into the sky rather than be hunted through the last four days. The
crawler count goes from 38,532 at the start of the floor to **33,804** — a number Carl calls, with
wonder, "shockingly low."

He burns his first Emergency Action Item to hand all remaining Faction Wars votes to the audience.
The audience passes **Remove Safety Protections For Faction Sponsors** at **98%**, and a tenth,
NPC-populated team — the **Home Team**, based in Larracos, co-warlorded by [Juice Box](juice-box.md)
and Sir [Ferdinand](ferdinand.md) — at **52%**. His speech at the ceiling:

> "We are coming. There is nowhere for you to hide. It is going to be a bloodbath the likes of which
> has never been seen."

And then, in the last seconds before the floor collapses, a mistake he didn't notice making.

The **Scavenger's Daughter**, a divine back patch he won early and sewed onto his Battle Rattle,
charges a **Soul Essence** bar off every melee kill and is supposed to be drained after every fill
via the **Daughter's Kiss**. Mordecai's instruction was explicit: "Just make sure you drain the
essence bar each time it fills up… Just don't store the stuff for too long." The patch's own warning
compared carrying it around to asbestos, then told him to forget it said anything.

> "Carl, your jacket is glowing! It's not an aardvark like I thought. It's a giant centipede! The
> image is of a centipede crawling through all the skulls! It's disgusting!"
>
> At the same moment, a voice spoke in my mind.
>
> *You did not diffuse your soul power from the Scavenger's Daughter. That was a mistake. You should
> never allow it to linger within you. **I need you alive, Carl. I need you reasonably sane. We have
> so much work to do.***

!!! warning "Unresolved — the Scolopendra hook"
    The book never explains the centipede image and never says the word "Scolopendra" at this
    moment. What it has established elsewhere: **Scolopendra** is the final boss of the whole
    dungeon; the Reminiscence Hydra is a rebranded "Scolopendra Nymph"; a cookbook note speculates
    that Shi Maria is the great centipede's daughter, or sister, or mother; the galaxy's wealth is
    gathered in something called the **Scolopendra Club**; and the arcade cabinet in Carl's father's
    trailer was Centipede. None of this is resolved in Book 6.

Carl's last thought as Cuba is stripped off the face of the Earth beneath him:

> I thought of Asher, the brother I never knew. I needed to make him proud… I wasn't there for him
> while he lived, but I would be damned if I allowed his death to be for nothing.
>
> *You will not break me. You will be avenged. I swear it. I swear it to you all.*

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Floor 9 is **Faction Wars**, fought across the plains outside — and down inside — the funnel city of
**Larracos**. Carl enters it as the dungeon's number-one-ranked crawler and Co-Warlord of **The
Princess Posse**, and spends most of the book running an army instead of a party: nine rival
factions, a ten-team alliance board, four named offensives and a staff of officers. It is also the
book in which he quietly does the single most consequential thing any crawler has done in the
series, and never tells anyone he meant to.

!!! note "Level endpoints"
    The book prints only two hard levels for Carl. At the Club Vanquisher raid (Ch. 51) the mantaur
    guards are "ranging from 75 to 80, which was a little worrying as I was only level 75." At
    Chapter 77 he notes "even I had leveled a few times—up to level 81—despite me not doing
    anything." **81** is the last level stated for him in the book; no start-of-floor or
    end-of-floor figure is given, and he ended Book 6 at 73.

### Mayor of Shanty Town

In Chapter 10, negotiating with Queen Imogen's cat familiar Sir [Ferdinand](ferdinand.md) — now
co-warlord of the NPC **Home Team**, renamed **Team Retribution** — Carl trades Quan Ch's looted
black bowler hat (plus gold the system demands to complete the transfer) for the mayorship of
**Shanty Town**, the shanty ring built around the lip of Larracos:

> "I want to be mayor of that town. Shanty Town. You'll have to transfer your mayorship to us,
> which means that jewel on your necklace will go away. You'll get this hat, and we'll trade it back
> for your turban in a day or so. ..."

The trade also renames the two factions' truce to "**The Good Guys**." Shanty Town becomes the
Posse's third front, the site of the **Nest** sniper tower Donut fights from, and the ground over
which her third Atrocity is cast.

### Christmas Morning, and the button he actually pressed

The book's biggest secret is buried in two matching interludes set on Floor 8 — the Prologue and
the **Christmas Morning** interlude that closes Part One, just before Chapter 20. Both are memories
replayed on the eighth floor's Ghosts of Earth, and in both Carl can move and act while the memory
around him stays frozen.

In the Prologue, a homeless man named **Paulie** — a gondii residual wearing a human — speaks to
Carl through a frozen moment and talks him into drinking a doctored cup of coffee. It installs an
illegal Valtay-built **Containment Interface** in Carl's head. Paulie explains what it is for: the
system AI's containment carries a "fuse," a failsafe that turns the system's star nova if five or
six conditions trip. Paulie's people want Carl to blow it on purpose.

> "Good. This controller in your interface will allow you to pop the fuse. You will, unfortunately,
> die. But the good news is you'll kill everyone else on the way out."

Paulie then breaks his own mission to add a warning: "If you make this decision. Be sure. Be sure
it's your only option. This is more than just a failsafe. It's the end of everything."

The Christmas Morning interlude is the other half. The handshake completes — *Containment Interface
is now available for use* — and Carl asks the liaison Orren to transfer him back to a gas station he
had passed, so he can sit with the memory ghost of a sleeping dog, Lightning Lou, and think. There
is no single "Boom" button. There are six tabs of untranslated menus. It takes him about ten minutes
to find the Fuse page, which lists five benchmarks with only two marked **Conditions Met**, and one
control: **Override Conditions And Activate Fuse. Warning. Warning.**

He wants to press it. The interlude is explicit that he wants to press it "more than anything in the
world." He does not, for two reasons: he does not believe the AI would have permitted him the power
if it actually wanted to die, and he cannot make the choice for everyone else.

> I did want to hit the button. I wanted it more than anything in the world. My anger at the powers
> that made this happen were bright. So bright.
>
> I miss you, too, Donut.
>
> ...
>
> But not bright enough.

He keeps reading, and at the very end of a list of twenty status notifications he finds the only
other control in the interface:

> Disconnect Containment. Warning. This will disengage all metrics and controls, including the fuse
> system. It will render the Containment Unit inert. The failsafe will no longer function. Most
> controls will cease to function.

He weighs three options — do nothing, blow the fuse and die, or switch the whole apparatus off — and
frames the decision as "Which of those three options would hurt the Syndicate the most? And which of
those three options would protect my people the most?"

> We already were under the thumb of the psychotic AI.
>
> I pressed the Disconnect Containment button. The entire new interface blanked out, leaving nothing
> but the map. Already, the little red dot over Earth started to grow.
>
> "Welcome to the party, pals," I said.

The consequences land in Chapters 34–39. Skull Empire King Rust reveals his daughter **Princess
Formidable** has hijacked a cruiser and intends to manually trigger the failsafe from an orbital
monitor station, killing everyone in the system. Carl already knows it will not work:

> I knew it wasn't going to work because I knew the failsafe was already deactivated. I knew the
> failsafe was deactivated because I was the one who had turned it off.

Orren interrogates him about the implant with ten minutes on the clock, and by the end of the scene
the [System AI](the-system-ai.md) has demonstrated reach out past the star. Orren, defeated: "All
right. You've gone fully primal, and we can't stop you. But beyond that, you have broken free of your
containment." Afterwards he asks Carl directly:

> "Carl," Orren said after a few moments. "Is that what you did? You helped break the AI from its
> containment? Did you do it deliberately?"
>
> "What?" Donut asked. "What's this?"
>
> I just looked at him. I didn't answer.

Orcs at the monitor station later pull the trigger themselves and nothing happens. The showrunner
Cascadia spends the rest of the book managing a season with an AI that has officially "gone primal"
and a system with no off switch — which is also the premise Rishi builds his treaty offer on, and the
reason the galaxy's tunnel node to the Aryl system went dark.

### Party leader of the Royal Court

In Chapter 27, immediately after Empress [D'Nadia](princess-dnadia.md) dies, the system reassigns the
party:

> Due to your recent upgrades, your total base stat count has made you the strongest member of your
> party. As a result, you have been designated as the new Party Leader of the Royal Court of Princess
> Donut.

Donut has held the role since Book 2 on combined base stats. Her objection is entirely for the
cameras, which Carl notices and is sad about:

> "This is not acceptable," she said with a harumph. She jumped onto my shoulder. "If you change the
> name of the party, I swear I will leave, Carl. Is that what you want? Mongo to come from a broken
> home?"

He keeps the party name and the titles. The arrangement lasts about fifty chapters: when Donut jumps
to level 135 in Chapter 77 she takes it straight back — "At least I'm back in my rightful spot as
leader of the party."

### The Ring of Divine Suffering

Carl wears the [Ring of Divine Suffering](../items/enchanted-night-wyrms-ring-of-divine-suffering.md)
through the whole of Faction Wars, and the book treats it as an addiction rather than an item. He also
loots a *second* ring off King Rust's corpse, which he eventually gives to **Li Na**.

The worst moment comes in Chapter 26, standing over a dying D'Nadia. The anger is explicitly
unprecedented — "The anger that washed over me was so brilliantly white, all three of my eyes
burned. I'd never felt anything like this" — and the river in his head comes back with it. Donut
talks him down over party chat while stalling out loud:

> Carl: Donut, I changed my mind. I want to do it. Step aside.
>
> Donut: No, Carl. Please, no. I need you to step back. I need you to calm down right now. I got
> this. It's okay.

He steps back. The book gives the moment its own paragraph — "I saw it, clear as can be. Right there.
I stood upon it, one foot stretched out over open air... You can find clarity when you need it most."
— and he lets Donut call the kill instead, which Mongo takes. Afterwards she presses him again:

> Donut: You do know what came over you. I want you to throw that ring away, Carl. It's not worth it.
> It's doing something to you. You're scaring me.

*You're scaring me* lands on him as "How many times had my mother said that to my father?" He still
refuses: "Not yet. I need it for just a little longer."

He finally lets it go in Chapter 85, in the rebuilt ruins of Larracos with Faction Wars already won.
The temptation is specific — he could mark and kill the surrendered enemy mercenaries and max his
base stats at 300 the way Li Na just had:

> One look at Li Na, and I knew that path, that slope was too steep. Survival had more than one
> meaning.
>
> Still, I didn't want to let it go.

So he destroys it in the only way that guarantees he cannot change his mind. He holds the ring up in
front of Donut's nose, she asks "Are you sure, Carl?", and she swallows it.

> "I almost choked to death, Carl. That would've been quite ironic, don't you think?"

### New skills, boons and borrowed magic

Every acquisition on this floor comes from somewhere other than a level-up. (Ability and spell pages
carry the mechanics; this page records only how each was obtained.)

- **Walk on Air** — already in his skill list when the book opens, which he only notices when a toe
  ring's description says "(already obtained.)" It is **Li Jun**'s skill, taken permanently when Shi
  Maria used Carl's hand to tear out Li Jun's eye at the end of Book 6 and made him eat it. Rosetta
  later confirms the rule: "Spells and skills taken against the owner's will were much more powerful
  and resulted in the owner losing them permanently."
- **Gloom Wraith Phase** — level 13, from a Sheol-flavoured potion out of one of his Floor 8 celestial
  boxes, identified and handed over by Mordecai in Chapter 6. It is the skill he kills Architect
  Houston with.
- **Profane Iron** — a once-a-day chain skill borrowed from **Li Na** by Rosetta's toraline-ink
  method: Li Na gives blood freely, temporarily losing five levels, and **[Katia](katia.md)** inks the
  resulting chain-dragon tattoo across Carl's right forearm (his first tattoo on that arm; the system
  rates it expert quality). He reciprocates, giving **Imani** a borrowed *Tripper* via a small
  barbed-wire ankle tattoo of her own.
- **Heathen Scald** — level 15, a **Celestial Boon** from **Emberus** for completing the church quest
  *Kill the Blasphemer* in Chapter 51, announced after "a good ten seconds of uncontrolled, absolutely
  unhinged, shrieking laughter in Emberus's voice." Adds its damage to any punch attack, for the
  remainder of the floor.
- **Speedster** — not permanent and not from Eris. It is one of four thirty-hour buffs granted by the
  **Enchanted Hairbrush of the Beefmaster**, a celestial item from the Club Vanquisher raid haul, which
  requires 25cm of hair and screams while in use. Mordecai has never seen the benefit awarded that way.
- **Pied Piper** — level 15, a proximity effect from the god **Harpocrates** in Chapter 70. It becomes
  permanent by coin flip. In Chapter 76 the chaos goddess **Eris**, newly freed of her sponsor, spins
  a two-item wheel in front of him: *Dick will fall off!* (from the feral god Meatus) and *Level-15
  Pied Piper!* It lands on the first, "hovered for a second, then ticked over" to the second. It is
  also, as Carl has to explain to Donut, nothing to do with rodents — "the skill allows me to play
  musical instruments."

### Mind Balance, trained to 16

The Legendary **Enchanted Toe Ring of the Well-Balanced** (left pinky toe, non-negotiable) gives him
**Mind Balance** at level 5, protection from Enslavement and Conscription. It immediately reports
itself *currently triggered* — Shi Maria, tattooed into his sternum, is trying to cast through him,
and the log fills with hundreds of identical entries:

> Gloom Wraith Phase failed to activate. Mind Balance prevented activation.
>
> Your attempt to disable Mind Balance was prevented by Mind Balance.

The skill levels every time she pushes. It peaks in Chapter 65, when the Madness hit him with a
psionic barrage that buries him in a root of the All Tree and floods him with Self Harm and Intrusive
Thoughts, and an unidentified friendly male voice — a dwarf, he thinks — talks him through it:

> Your Mind Balance is now Level 16.
>
> New Achievement! You now tread in the realm of gods!
>
> You've trained a skill past 15 using battlefield conditions, as opposed to just taking a bunch of
> potions to do it. That's quite the accomplishment.

This is the first skill in the series trained past 15 by battlefield conditions rather than potions.
It is also where Carl finally sees what the river in his head actually is — "Voices. Screams.
Nightmares distilled to light. Souls." — standing in it, barefoot, in an All Tree root.

### Rishi, and the offer to make him leader of Earth

**Rishi**, a Blood Sultanate temple guard, survives the destruction of the naga faction by hiding
rather than dying with his wife and princess, and is conscripted into the Princess Posse. In
Chapter 58 Carl visits his cell to find out why. Rishi is smiling, armless and entirely unbothered:
he hid so he could make an offer.

> "So I could implore upon you our plight and make an offer to you. It was a gamble, but I suspected
> Vinata would fall. I have in my inventory a signed and AI witnessed document for you, the de-facto
> leader of Earth."

The claim is real, and petitioned for on Carl's behalf without his knowledge: with no working
government left, Syndicate law lets a planet's native leader be assigned, and "the fact you are a
warlord and the number one crawler on the leaderboard makes your claim legitimate." The terms:

> "I'm sure your attorney can explain it to you. But the document does nothing unless you sign it. It
> gives Earth natives citizenship rights in all naga territories, plus five Naphil-class generation
> ships. This is in exchange for allowing us to terraform and settle upon the other planets in your
> system post crawl once there is no longer a quarantine."

Rishi has signed it himself, because his brothers are at that moment deposing the king, the prime
minister and the entire House of Greens. The signature reads **Sultan Rishi the Usurper, First of his
Name of the Naga Uprising**. Carl's verdict is that he is "just like all the others, like Empress
D'Nadia. A power-hungry asshole. It was oddly disappointing." He asks one question — what happens to
the contract if Rishi dies before it is signed — and then renegotiates:

> "This is what I want. I want you to shut up. I want your people to take those ships you offered us,
> to get into them, and to drive yourselves into the nearest star. But first, I want you, Mr. new
> Sultan of the naga, to know something."
>
> ...
>
> You have successfully marked Rishi of the Princess Posse.

The chapter ends on the mark and Carl's line to the naga system's audience, "Rishi here is just the
opening act."

!!! note "The killing blow is off-page"
    Chapter 58 closes on the completed mark and Carl's promise. The text never shows the kill itself
    and no system message for Rishi's death appears; he is never mentioned again.

### Killing Architect Houston

**[Architect Houston](architect-houston.md)** — Viceroy prime, warlord of the Madness, and the only
creature King Rust admitted to fearing — is Carl's personal objective in **Operation Ruin**. Carl and
Elle enter Madness territory through the Gate of the Feral Gods and reach the surgical theater beside
the throne room in Chapter 68.

What he finds is Houston already on his own operating table, robes and mask off, having the machine
physically graft him to a dead changeling suspended above him. The room is wired with fireball traps
on a **Containment Breach Trap Mark IV** carrying a dead man's switch keyed to Houston himself. The
crawler **Burcu** is bound to a second table.

Houston is not fighting. He is begging for time:

> "Carl. Do not... Do not press that button. I can stop it all," Houston called. "You don't
> understand. It is called the Beautiful Place. I can get there. They are the key. The changelings.
> This world. Why do you think we come here, cycle after cycle? You've helped make the greatest
> discovery in history, and you don't even realize it. It was here all along, right under our
> mandibles."

He gets there. His last words while the machine kills him are "I found you. I found you. After all
this time. You were right here all along. It's really you, isn't it? Not just an avatar. It's you.
It's you." Then the dead man's switch fires and Carl, mid-*Gloom Wraith Phase*, goes straight through
him:

> I passed through the naked Houston, unraveling him.

Carl escapes injured, having struck something magical on the way out that "felt like a dream." Burcu
does not.

> System Message: Architect Houston of the Madness has fallen. Credit for the kill has been given to
> Warlord Carl of the Princess Posse.

### Warlord Fang

The Reaver warlord's death in Chapter 81 is split three ways, and the AI enjoys itself about it:

> System Message: Warlord Fang of the Reavers has fallen. Credit for the kill has been given to...
> well, that's a complicated one. We'll give it to Warlord Carl for detonating the bomb that was
> dropped by a wraith-possessed Crawler Louis Santiago 2, who was under control of Warlord Juice Box
> of Team Retribution. By this point, I guess it doesn't matter, but we're giving 51% experience to
> Carl, 1% to [Louis](louis-santiago.md), and 40% to [Juice Box](juice-box.md). The rest is going to
> the now-deceased Fang for hiding in the worst possible place, which was inside the dragon.

Fang had survived Donut's *War Crime* earlier by being mostly robot around a scrap of biological
material, and had hidden inside the cybernetic drake **Calliope**.

### Operation Snake Pit and the ninety seconds of Immortality

**Operation Snake Pit** is the campaign to exterminate the Blood Sultanate down to the last naga —
mandatory, because neither Donut nor Katia can leave Floor 9 while any survive (see the
[Crown of the Sepsis Whore](../items/crown-of-the-sepsis-whore.md)). It kicks off under cover of the
warlords' roundtable and immediately goes sideways: most of the naga army is already dead, killed by
Prepotente's hellspawn Bianca, and Princess **Vinata** has gone to ground.

The endgame (Chapters 51–55) is a four-person break-in through the temple of Dagda, into
[Club Vanquisher](../locations/club-vanquisher.md), and then into the **Temple of Khepri** — an
endless buffet table where Vinata has taken shelter under a Prospect spell. Vinata has anticipated
them and summoned the bug god **Khepri** into his own temple, which should have been fatal. The
proximity effect saves Carl instead:

> Temporary Effect from Khepri:
>
> You are immortal for the next 90 seconds.

Vinata gets the same buff with a 27-second shorter clock. Carl spends his window detonating **The
Waffle Maker** — the AI's name for his thermos-sized successor to *Just Wait Until Your Daddy Gets
Home*, a thermobaric charge that incinerates everything within 100 metres — inside a narrow room, at
point-blank range, on himself:

> I was turned completely flat, inside out, and into a boxer-clad amalgam of flesh and hair.

Khepri leaves the realm. Carl survives, barely, and the Blood Sultanate falls; the final kill on the
Sepsis Whore goes to the sluggalette **Kandy "Bigs" Newton**. The raid also produces the Club
Vanquisher haul — seven celestial boxes awarded floor-wide, over thirty loot boxes for Carl, a
permanent ban from Club Vanquisher, and "friend of the church" status with nine deities including
several he is supposed to be at war with.

**Operation Ruin** is the four-pronged final offensive that opens the Open Hostilities phase, with
Rosetta as theater commander, Florin on the orcs, Boomer on the Reavers, Donut on the Dream and Carl
on the Madness — "followed by what Donut calls the 'Coup De War Crime.'" It wins Faction Wars.

## Book 8: A Parade of Horribles

Floor 10, "Don't Come in Last," is a seven-heat vehicle race across Floor 10, followed by Floor 11's
"A Parade of Horribles" — a mandatory float parade culminating in a boss fight against Scolopendra
and Krakaren Prime. Carl spends the floor married to a woman he's never met, drops the biggest
public reveal of the series live on caprid television, kills two friends on purpose to save a third,
and ends the book having renounced his own god on the record.

### Married, Without His Knowledge

Book 7's cliffhanger resolves in the opening interlude, which is not even Carl's own POV: **[Princess
Chandra, Esq.](chandra.md)**, Donut's newly assigned attorney, invoked **Widow's Rights** — a Naga
custom letting a royal widow claim her husband's killer as a replacement husband — the moment Carl
killed her husband Rishi and the primary wife, Vinata, died alongside him. Chandra spends the book
trying to leverage the marriage and a parallel conservatorship claim into control of both Carl's and
Donut's fortunes; see her own page for how that goes, and for the fact that neither the marriage nor
the conservatorship claim is resolved by the end of Book 8.

### Live on *Plenty of Plenty*

In Chapter 28, Carl and Donut are pulled onto the caprid interview show ***Plenty of Plenty***,
where a panel of goats — unbeknownst to Carl, "the leadership of the Plenty," the species that
controls the galaxy's tunnel network — judges them one question at a time. Liaison Orren and Prime
Minister Victory patch into his HUD mid-broadcast, off the record, to explain what's actually
happening: the escaped system AI has been attacking targets well outside the solar system, mobs are
appearing where they shouldn't, and a large number of unsponsored gods have slipped their leash
entirely, "at cross-purposes" with the AI and with each other. Orren feeds Carl a careful, diplomatic
version of a question to ask the Plenty leadership on the record. Carl asks his own version instead,
live, in front of the entire audience:

> "Here's my question. Does the public know that the gods have escaped from the dungeon? And that
> the enhancement zones of the current AI have jumped into your tunneling system? Do the people know
> they're in danger? Worse danger, perhaps, than us crawlers? Do they know that they need to fucking
> run?"

> **Prime Minister Victory:** Gods-fucking-damnit, Carl. You just said that on live. And the AI let
> it go out.
>
> **Orren:** And now the party really begins.

The broadcast triggers galaxy-wide panic over both the escaped gods and the compromised tunnel
network — a consequence the book never lets Carl walk back.

### Killing Jasha and Radoslav

Near the end of Heat 6, with **[Prepotente](prepotente.md)** immobile and near-suicidal inside an
AI-generated vision and only minutes left on the race clock, Carl detonates a bomb he had already
planted — shortly before the heat started, using *Oozy Form* while Samantha distracted them — inside
the magical beer cooler of **[Jasha and Radoslav](jasha-and-radoslav.md)** —
the bugbear musicians of Team Free Love, allies since Heat 1 — killing them both instantly so their
team's elimination absorbs the time cost of the rescue. It is planned in advance, not a battlefield
accident, and the book gives Carl no reprieve from how it feels afterward: "They were our friends,
and I felt like an utter piece of shit. That was the point, of course. But it still hurt." See their
own page for the full scene.

### Freezing Mitch as a Dog

During the Heat 7 Desperado Club ambush by a **Shadow Mimic** posing as the casino's croupier
("Mitch"), Carl — transformed into a dog by one of Scolopendra's nine-tier-attack curses — is cornered
by the reforming mimic. He reflexively opens the **[Eye of the Bedlam Bride](../items/the-eye-of-the-bedlam-bride.md)**,
tattooed on his chest since Book 6 (relocated to his forehead in dog form) and never willingly used
before this:

> I'd reflexively opened the eye in the center of my forehead, and he froze in place.
>
> "No," Mitch said. His entire form started to sparkle like someone had poured glitter into him.
> "No. Don't do this. It's too cold... It's too cold!"

Donut finishes Mitch off with *Magic Missile* while he's frozen. Carl confirms afterward, privately,
that he kept control the whole time and shut the Eye again ("*No. You stay where you are. You are
trapped, and you will stay trapped. I am in control.*"), and makes sure it's fully closed by the time
Floor 11 opens.

The same ambush is where the system AI, mid-fight, delivers its own version of the "Big Six"
dungeon-power factions — a taxonomy [Akuma](akuma.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) had already been
circling around earlier in the book:

1. **The Crawlers** — "But this group also contains former Crawlers, like that Herot guy, that
   Forkith dude, and so forth."
2. **The standard NPCs** — including offshoot rebellions like Hamed's.
3. **The Gods** — "the most fractured and dumbest of all the groups... This is also the group I
   despise the most."
4. **The sapient mobs** — Shadow Mimics, the Ogre Imperium, non-deity boss monsters like Krakaren
   Prime, and the War Mage Rebellion.
5. **The demons in Sheol** — "real wild cards... the most organized. Or maybe not."
6. **Group Six** — deliberately withheld: "You survive this fight, you make it to the 11th floor,
   you'll meet group six."

### Renouncing Emberus

At the Floor 11 climax, Carl pulls off the **Enchanted Pauper's Ring of the Steadfast Emberus** and
publicly renounces his own faith, then confesses — on the record, mid-smite — to a crime his church
quest has been dancing around since Book 6: he is the one who killed Emberus's son, **Geyrun**.

> "The other guy to kill your worthless smear of a son was me. That's right. I killed him with my
> bare hands, and as he died, I said, 'I'm doing this because your dad is a little bitch.'"

The smite burns the sun-shaped Emberus tattoos off the backs of both his hands and replaces them with
permanent scars the system names and nothing else:

> The tattoos on the back of my hands no longer glowed. The sun shapes remained, but they now were
> raised, angry scars. When I examined them, they both had the same description with no further
> explanation. **Mark of the Damned.**

No mechanical effect is ever given for the Mark of the Damned in Book 8 — an explicit open thread for
a future floor.

!!! warning "The confession is false; the renunciation is real"
    Carl genuinely leaves the faith — ring off, menu confirmed, smite taken, scars permanent. The
    confession he shouts at Emberus is a deliberate lie told to enrage him. Carl says so in
    narration in the same scene: "*I actually had no idea for certain who killed Geyrun, though I
    now knew this was something — if I survived this — I would still have to figure out.*" Who
    actually killed Geyrun is still unknown at the end of Book 8; Carl only suspects that "one of
    the key details of the whole mystery was a lie." Do not record the murder as solved.

### Scolopendra, Krakaren Prime, and the Bait

At the Floor 11 finale, Carl and the other 22 surviving crawlers jointly request the summoning of
**Scolopendra** as part of a group gambit the AI itself calls "batshit." **Krakaren Prime**, by
contrast, is not something Carl asked for — the AI adds her unilaterally, on top of the requested
summon, as extra punishment: "I wasn't going to bring her here, but I caught wind of what you and
someone else are planning, and I thought it would be appropriate."

The trap Carl engineers to survive the resulting fight uses the newly-sapient **[Penelope](penelope.md)**
as bait, deliberately provoking the gods **Taranis** and **Emberus** into a confrontation with each
other by threatening her at gunpoint on open comms. See Penelope's own page for how that plays out.
