# Zev

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Role:** Borant PR/Outreach Admin assigned to Carl and Donut
    - **Type:** NPC / corporate employee
    - **Race:** Kua-tin ("mudskipper") — small, fish-like, requires a water suit or rebreather
    - **Status:** Alive, still Carl and Donut's assigned contact as of Book 5 — and still theirs at
      the end of Book 7, working out of a Borant headquarters the Syndicate Council has ordered to
      self-destruct and which she is refusing to blow up
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None — a production/administrative liaison, not a combatant
    - **Notable items:** A rebreather (Book 5, replacing her usual full diving suit for the first
      time on screen)

## Overview

Zev has been Carl and Donut's Borant PR/outreach contact since Book 1, reinstated after Book 4's
death of her replacement, Loita. She's grown genuinely fond of Donut over the series, bonding over
shared taste in Earth television, and has repeatedly risked her own position warning the pair about
dangers she isn't supposed to disclose directly.

## Role in the Story — Book 5

Zev spends Book 5 walking an increasingly dangerous line between her employer and her personal
loyalty to Carl and Donut, as the Valtay Corporation's acquisition of 51% of Borant destabilizes
the entire production chain of command around them:

- Urges Carl and Donut to hurry to a saferoom as the newly-conquered village of Alucarda turns out
  to be held by the unstable crawler Lucia Mar — starting to warn them off going at all, then
  cutting herself short ("Or maybe you should… Never mind. I can't say that") because she isn't
  allowed to say more
- Relays Odette's decision to reveal Beatrice's survival to Carl and Donut moments before it
  happens live on air, visibly shaken by the timing
- Confirms Carl's lawsuit against outside intelligence-feeding to hunters succeeded, cutting off a
  major advantage the hunters had been exploiting
- Confides to Carl, in a rare unguarded moment, that her entire family was slaughtered back home
  because *she* refused to wear a pin (its significance unspecified), and that she doesn't know if
  she'll be welcomed home or executed as a traitor once the current corporate upheaval resolves
- Reveals a Valtay-mandated tightening of security around crawlers taken to production facilities,
  and cryptically urges Carl — before his very first CrawlCon event, without explaining why — to
  "linger" at the production ship as long as he can rather than rush back into the dungeon; the
  warning proves prescient during his second event (the hostile "Crawling Through the Ages" panel
  moderated by Circe Took), when his two guards quietly step out and leave him drugged and
  unsupervised

## Role in the Story — Book 6

Zev spends Book 6 visibly drowning. The Valtay corporate changeover has gutted her department and she
is doing the work of five people:

> "I'm sorry. I am so busy. I have so much going on right now that I shouldn't even be here. **We lost
> dozens of staff during the Valtay changeover, plus we weren't prepared for this eighth floor, plus
> the Valtay don't allow certain types of micro AI systems to assist, and now I'm doing the work of
> five.**"

And later, on top of that: "We have literally thousands of ships coming into Earth orbit, all
arriving early because of Prepotente's stunt. It's a bureaucratic nightmare… I have to be in a
meeting in a few minutes when you're at yours." Then, looking at her own tablet: "Gods, I don't even
know what that meeting is for."

The exhaustion makes her careless in ways that consistently benefit Carl. She forgets to tell Carl's
attorney that the hostile liaison **Harbinger** will be at the *Shadow Boxer* taping, and when
Harbinger kills the broadcast she is angrier than Carl has ever seen her — "Liaison, you do not have
the authority to do that… We're going to have to refund them! And you can't just dismiss an attorney
like that!" She also lets slip that Harbinger is the liaison who was bribed by the Skull Empire, and
then refuses to continue the conversation.

### Protecting Carl from Odette

The clearest example is the adjutant conversation. She opens it by locking the channel down —
"I've turned on extra security for this conversation… I am the only person in on this conversation
other than the system AI" — and then tells him what she should not:

> "As you're aware, Odette has history with Mordecai… If you get them physically together, **he
> *will* attack her. He will get kicked out. You'll lose him. His contract will be in jeopardy. Half
> of those kids he's fostering will immediately get ejected because your personal space will change.**
> Odette is a big fan of… drama. Having her involved wouldn't be healthy for anybody."

She follows it with an outright corporate leak — the Valtay are distancing themselves from day-to-day
production after the Prepotente incident, and Borant will be liquidated after the crawl regardless,
so the Valtay can blame "the mudskippers" for the numbers — and then a plea with no corporate angle
at all:

> "Just don't pick Odette, okay? **I like Mordecai, and it would hurt him if you picked her.**"

Carl has to pick Odette anyway, and spends the rest of the scene inventing an excuse.

She is also the one who tells Carl that his entire sponsor organisation has vanished: "The box got
seized, and the entire charity and everyone involved just up and disappeared. Nobody knows where they
went or if something happened to them. They're all gone, even Rosetta."

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Zev spends Floor 9 as a conduit for things nobody wants to say out loud. She delivers an order from the head
of the Syndicate, she confirms the worst piece of worldbuilding in the book, and she tells Carl the one fact
that makes it impossible to kill Lucia Mar. She is never in the room for any of it.

### The Order About Agatha

The residual **Agatha** turns up in Shanty Town, wandering with a shopping cart, level 23, no class. Before
Carl can work out what that means, Zev arrives with an order from higher up than either of them has ever
dealt with:

> Zev: Carl. I know I’ve been quiet, but we are monitoring. I’ve gotten a warning. This is coming directly
> from the Syndicate council. Prime Minister Glory himself messaged me. The prime minister!

> Zev: We’re not quite there yet, Carl. We still have some control. The liaisons are still talking with the
> system AI, negotiating. Listen, this is important. You are not to interact with Agatha. This is a direct
> order. I don’t know why. If and when she appears, you’re not to talk to her. And I know your big thing is
> doing the opposite of what we ask every time, but I think this is something you should pay attention to.

She has no idea why, and says so — “We have the residuals in the dungeon every season, and they are harmless.
I don’t know what’s changed... But he was especially urgent in his message, and I’m just passing it on.” She
also lets slip the one detail that makes the order strange: the AI has withdrawn its protection from every
other residual and they are all dead, “except this one. It seems to have a soft spot for her, kinda like it
has for you.”

Carl reads the escalation correctly: “The fact the leader of the entire Syndicate was sticking his nose into
this was telling. Something had changed since the end of the previous floor, regarding their understanding of
the residuals. Of Agatha.” The order is overtaken within days — the system names Agatha co-warlord of the War
Mage Rebellion — and Prime Minister Glory later tries to have Carl killed outright through a Syndicate override
panel, an attempt the AI blocks and publicly avenges.

### The Aryl System Goes Dark

Zev’s most consequential scene is a piece of news delivery. Princess Formidable’s ship reaches the failsafe
station without her; a handful of orcs go in and trigger the crawl’s failsafe by hand. Nothing happens on
Earth. Something happens somewhere else.

> “No. They tried triggering it, and nothing happened here. But the moment they pressed the button, the Aryl
> system went dark.”

> “Aryl was the location of the previous crawl. It was a battle royale-style season. The planet was free of
> citizens, but there are tens of thousands of people still in the solar system, mostly those on mining ships
> and mineral reclamation finishing the clean-up. Apparently the AI had stopped communicating a few weeks
> back, and there was also a team on the surface investigating why. That’s all I really know. The whole system
> is now dark, and it appears the tunnel node is offline. That’s.... You need to understand that has never
> happened before. Never in history has a tunnel node just switched off like that.”

Pressed on why a dead node is worse than a dead star, she explains the scale of it:

> “No! Even before the Plenty figured out how to open the nodes for communication and travel, we could see
> them. They were a map of the stars, pointing us to all of the seeded worlds. We’ve seen systems go nova, but
> the tunnel nodes remained active. We didn’t even know it was possible for a tunnel strand to just disappear.
> It happened the very moment, like the exact nanosecond, the orcs hit the failsafe, and nobody knows what it
> means.”

She adds two details that matter later: the previous season’s AI had gone silent — not offline, *silent* —
some time around Floor 6, before the current AI went primal; and it will be at least three cycles before
anyone can see whether Aryl’s star is still there, because the nearest neighbour is 2.9 light cycles away and
the node is the only fast route.

The thread pays off at the end of the book, when Baroness Victory reports a second one: “The newest mantis
colony system just went dark... initial reports are a little more holistic than they were in the Aryl system.
The system’s star went nova.”

!!! note "Whose failsafe, and who pressed it"
    Zev’s account is that Princess Formidable teleported away and **orcs from her own ship manually triggered
    the failsafe** at the station — it was the Earth crawl’s failsafe, not Aryl’s, and no crawler pressed it.
    The captured naga **Rishi** later refers to it loosely as “the moment Princess Formidable hit the
    failsafe,” and Carl corrects him on the spot: “Princess Formidable herself didn’t make it to the failsafe.
    The AI pulled her off her ship and dropped her into the ocean on the surface.” See
    [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for the failsafe itself.

### The Dead-Man’s Switch

After [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md) loses her last dog and warns Carl that he can no longer afford to kill her,
Zev is the one who explains what she meant. She should not be telling him any of it and tells him anyway: the
children who tried to enter the dungeon and ended up in one wing of the surface kinder facility are somehow
tied to Lucia, the glitch that should have released them on her death no longer will, and “the Eulogist is
another name for the AI in the center system.”

> Zev: About 120,000. Give or take a few.

Carl’s reply — “I almost killed her, Zev. You almost let me kill her” — gets the closest thing to an apology
Borant can offer: “We learned something new the moment the dog died. There’s so much more, but now is not the
time.”

### Drowning, Again

The rest of her Book 7 appearances are logistics under collapse. She schedules the warlord council the
Syndicate tried to cancel and the AI insisted on (“Looks like we’re doing this warlord council whether we like
it or not”), explains why Juice Box has to attend virtually and Ferdinand in person, warns Carl not to smuggle
anything into the production facility, and drags him and Katia into a mandatory interview mid-battle because
“the AI is demanding we do this.”

Her last message in the book is sent while her own building is being shot at:

> Zev: Carl, that ejection request didn’t come from us. Several warships in orbit have just started firing on
> several other ships. These aren’t just pot shots, but full-on missile barrages and maser blasts. We just got
> a notice from the Syndicate Council telling us to initiate headquarters self-destruct followed by a message
> from the Valtay showrunners telling us to not comply. Obviously we’re not complying. Not sure yet if the
> mercs are going to be on our side or the Syndicate’s. Say what you were going to say. Quick. Get it out
> there.

Carl says it: “Epitome Tagg is a goddamned cyborg!”
