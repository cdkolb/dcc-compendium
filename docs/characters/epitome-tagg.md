# Epitome Tagg

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Off-world player — **Warlord of the Dream**, one of the nine Faction Wars teams on
      Floor 9. A CEO of the Epitome family, “one of the most famous CEOs in the galaxy”
    - **Race:** Dream elf — always described as bald. Secretly a **cyborg**, with most of his brain
      transferred to “the Reaver net” and only a small amount of biological material left
    - **Class:** None stated
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** Not stated anywhere in the book
    - **Role:** Faction Wars warlord; the antagonist of the Louis Santiago rescue arc
      (chapters 80-84)
    - **Family:** Son of **Epitome Noflex**, whose death he ordered (Book 6); in Book 7 he claims
      she is alive on the 18th floor and will bargain for him
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None shown — he never fights Carl directly. His one real
      capability is the cybernetic graft that lets him inhabit and hold hostage another person’s
      vital organs
    - **Notable items:** None named. Boomer’s warning is that “He wears the most ridiculous helmet
      you’ve ever seen”
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — chapter 84. His heart-and-lung assembly is cut out of Louis
      Santiago during a transplant and eaten by [Mongo](mongo.md), who gets the kill credit

## Overview

Epitome Tagg is the warlord of **the Dream**, the galaxy’s dominant food conglomerate and the team
Carl spends most of Book 7 fighting on the southern front. He arrives in Book 7 already established
as a villain: in Book 6 he ordered his own mother’s death and dismissed Carl’s team outright at the
Faction Wars table.

> “There are no other teams,” Epitome Tagg said. “It’s nine teams. Eight, really. Plus the chaff.
> That’s what you are. You’re not even real.”

Tempest’s Floor 8 recap flags him early as one of the rich off-worlders who can now genuinely die:

> “Yeah. All those rich fuckers on the floor, like Prince Stalwart the orc, or that banker Operatic
> guy, or Epitome Tagg from the Dream? They all can really die now. It’s gonna be a shitshow. An
> epic, but-sir-you-haven’t-taken-a-bath shitshow.”

He is reputed to be a front-line commander. Boomer’s briefing in chapter 22 warns that the **Epitome
household guard** is in the dungeon with him — “Their security forces are all veterans of
non-dungeon conflicts and are tough as gristle” — and that “Epitome Tagg likes to fight near the
front.” When he finally appears in person at the warlord summit in chapter 42, the staging matches:

> Then came Epitome Tagg of the Dream. The bald elf looked as if he came straight from the
> battlefield. His utilitarian armor had a splash of blood across it, and I wondered who he’d been
> fighting. By all reports, he hadn’t been seen on the battlefield yet. At least not against any of
> our forces.

The reputation is theatre. Every time the Dream is actually threatened, Tagg runs.

## At the Summit

Tagg is the loudest voice in the chapter 42-44 warlord meeting, and the most openly contemptuous.
He needles King Stalwart of the Skull Empire over the Dream’s counter-measures and Stalwart’s
collapsing position:

> “Oh, please. Tell us, your majesty, what you’re planning on doing? We have already deactivated all
> Skull-empire-manufactured hardware in our fleet. You had your personal monetary reserves depleted
> because of your own lax security, your reputation is destroyed, and you are about to have a civil
> war on your hands that’ll make the Borant system look like a scuffle between toddlers. Tell us how
> we’ll be sorry?”

> “We are opposite sides of the map, and you do not have an air force,” Tagg said. “So, I ask again,
> what are you going to do, you coward?”

Odette’s broadcast then exposes how the teams are beating the communications blackout — by renaming
their own entertainment channels as a signalling system — and names Tagg as the worst offender:

> “But, and this is a big but, the feed directory is still active to all subscribers… They are paying
> the fee to repeatedly change the names of the programs in order to communicate. Just today, Epitome
> Tagg was shown to have a real-time conversation with the news program formerly called Sensation
> Updates, which changed its name seven times in the course of an hour. That program, as of this
> moment, is currently called Yes. We have Deactivated the Infected Hardware.”

When [Juice Box](juice-box.md) shifts into the form of the Nothing and startles the entire room, Tagg
is the one who insists on covering it with scorn:

> “It’s superstitious nonsense,” Tagg said, pretending like he, too, hadn’t been startled by whatever
> that was. “A cheap jump scare. As children, we would use this image to scare each other all the
> time.”

> “Superstitious nonsense,” Tagg repeated.

## Running

Once the shooting starts, Tagg’s actual behaviour is consistent. In chapter 62, with Team Retribution
occupying the Dream throne room and three feral gods loose in Dream territory, Edgar spots him:

> Edgar: Donut, I see Epitome Tagg. He is still near the Dream castle. He did not teleport with his
> army. He’s with a small contingent of his troops, riding zebras. I think he’s fleeing into the
> woods. I marked him. He is not the one in the fancy hat. They are trying to trick you.

Donut’s missile strike misses. Carl’s read:

> Tagg was making the safe choice to flee and hide, effectively abandoning his own troops who he sent
> to attack our base.

He survives the Witness spell that sweeps everyone outdoors into the rolling ball of bodies, because
he was outside when it was cast — Carl confirms it with **Ping** from the top of the Nest in chapter
73, finding both Tagg and Warlord Fang alive inside the ball. Carl’s plan at that point is blunt:
“All we need to do is find and kill both Tagg and Fang, and all their soldiers will drop dead.”

That never happens. Instead the Dream’s own throne room, held by Team Retribution, becomes a
countdown clock: when the six-hour capture timer expires, Tagg dies automatically wherever he is.

## The Cyborg

The reveal comes sideways. In chapter 80 Warlord Fang of the Reavers is killed after surviving
Donut’s War Crime spell as “a tiny amount of biological material surrounded by a robot” and hiding
inside his own pet drake. Donut asks how you hide inside a dragon, and Juice Box answers:

> “He was hiding inside the dragon the same way Epitome Tagg is hiding inside of my husband,” Juice
> Box said.

Juice Box has just wraith-possessed her husband, **[Louis Santiago](louis-santiago.md)**, out of the
Reaver castle, where he had been cut open on an operating table:

> “They were doing surgery on him. He was... cut open. His heart has been replaced. His lungs have
> been replaced. It looked like they were getting ready to replace his brain, but we got to him in
> time. I had to heal him, but when I did it, it didn’t regrow his heart or lungs.”

> “He was hooked up to a machine that was keeping him alive. When I touched him, I sensed the
> presence. It’s there, in the new lungs and heart. I can feel the form I can take, and it’s wrong...
> I don’t understand. The new heart and the new lungs are mechanical, but they both have a little biological
> material as well. Those pieces. It’s not Louis. It’s... It’s pieces of Tagg. It’s elf, but if I
> didn’t already have his kind in my library, I’m not sure if I’d be able to store the image or not.
> It’s confusing.”

Mordecai’s framing is the one that sticks:

> Mordecai: Maybe. But Maggie was inside Chris’s brain. In this case, it sounds like Tagg isn’t in
> Louis’s heart. He is Louis’s heart.

## Carl’s Deduction

[Carl](carl.md) works it out at the end of chapter 81 on a single question to Juice Box, who spent
Book 6 infiltrating the Dream:

> Carl: Before the dungeon started, did you ever actually touch Tagg?
>
> Juice Box: Him? No. We just blew him up a lot. He was always in that flag room, so we could never
> get to him.

Juice Box is a changeling, and changelings can only copy something they have physically touched. She
has never been able to touch Tagg. The answer lands:

> “Holy shit,” I said out loud. Sometimes the most obvious answer was the real one. “Epitome Tagg of
> the Dream is a...”

The sentence is cut off mid-word — Prime Minister Glory picks that exact moment to try to have Carl
ejected from the game, the AI overrides it, and the resulting achievement notice (*Executive Order
11905*) is the AI publicly warning the entire Syndicate off. When time restarts, Carl finishes:

> “Epitome Tagg is a goddamned cyborg! He is the same thing as Fang! It’s the only thing that makes
> sense.”

Mordecai calls it a wild theory — “He’s one of the most famous CEOs in the galaxy, and that’s a big
secret to keep” — and Rosetta confirms it:

> Rosetta: Carl and Mordecai are right. Gondii don’t have the wetware to inhabit organs. The Reavers
> do. They can isolate their processing systems in any part of their bodies. That’s why the real ones
> can keep coming for you after you blow off their heads.

Baroness Victory later fills in the politics: the Dream are not all Reavers, but Tagg personally had
“decided to secretly start moving toward the post physical form path,” and an undisclosed Dream/Reaver
alliance between the galaxy’s largest food producer and its largest manufacturer would have been a
mega-monopoly. Fopsy, the Dream’s own adjutant, says he had “no inkling whatsoever.”

!!! note "Unresolved"
    The book never explains how Tagg grafted himself into Louis’s biomechanical organs in the first
    place, or how he survived losing his own body. Mordecai’s answer to Donut’s direct question is
    “We don’t know. We need to figure it out,” and no later scene resolves it.

## The Hostage Negotiation

Tagg is still in warlord chat, and he opens with the only leverage he has:

> Epitome Tagg: Fuck off. I control his vital organs. You try to remove me, and I will shut him down.

> Epitome Tagg: Keep him alive, and you keep me alive. We can make a deal. My mother is on the 18th
> floor. She will trade his life for mine.

His offer in chapter 83 is a full body swap:

> Epitome Tagg: It’s quite simple, Carl. Get me to the 18th Floor. I will have a new body grown for
> me, and we will grow replacement organs for your friend here, and we will do a swap. He will be out
> of the dungeon, and I will be back in control. We both get what we want.

> Epitome Tagg: We’ll have to finish the procedure at the Reaver castle. Fang was being an ass and
> wasn’t going to allow the brain implant until he, too, was free. We will keep your friend’s brain
> intact, and it can be reimplanted. Once that happens, I will have control, and he will cease to be
> a crawler.

It is worthless. The Reaver castle has already been nuked, Tagg has no outside communications left,
and Victory confirms there is no legal exit: he cannot abdicate, because he is the only member of the
Dream still alive, and he cannot leave the floor, because his host is a crawler. When the Dream throne
room timer expires, Tagg dies — and Mordecai’s assessment is that “He will take Louis out with him.”

The solution is Samantha’s: Carl is carrying the flesh golem body of **Princess Lunette**, a naiad,
which has a usable heart and lungs. Mordecai, Edgar, Imani, Rosetta and a Fleshmancer-shaped Juice Box
run a live transplant in a field hospital tent, with Carl and Tipid holding the ribcage open.

## Death

The plan is to stall Tagg for two minutes. Donut’s contribution is to rename herself
**EPITOME TAGG’S SEXY MOTHER** and start doing an impression, which works considerably better than
stalling:

> Epitome Tagg: Quit saying that! That disgusting human never had sex with my mother! He besmirched
> my mother’s name with a changeling who took her form!

> Epitome Tagg: Change your name back this instant! I will kill this fat loser right now! Right
> fucking now! You puerile, disgusting mongrel. How dare you? How fucking dare you? I will starve out
> all of your kind!

> Epitome Tagg: CHANGE THAT NAME NOW.

Carl: “He said stall, not piss him off.” Donut: “We’re playing good cop, bad cop, Carl.”

With the surgical team thirty seconds short, Tagg gives up:

> Epitome Tagg: You know what? Fuck this. Fuck this existence. Fuck this life. And fuck all of you.
> You don’t deserve singularity. None of you do.
>
> “Heart has stopped,” Edgar shouted.

Mordecai cannot pull the organs himself — as an NPC it would count as an attack — so Bucket Boy yanks
the whole assembly out in one piece and throws it over his shoulder onto the tent floor. Louis gets
Lunette’s naiad heart and lungs, a kua-tin rebreather sewn into his bronchial tube, and a permanent
set of gills. He survives.

Nobody notices where the discarded organs went until Carl asks:

> Mongo popped up and let out an angry screech. He had blood on his beak, and he spit out the
> chewed-to-hell plastic heart and lungs onto the ground of the tent with disgust.

> System Message: Warlord Epitome Tagg of the Dream has fallen. Credit for the kill has been given...
> Well, this one is just as complicated as the last one, but imma give it to pet Mongo of the Princess
> Posse. I almost called it a suicide, and I almost gave it to that Bucket Boy kid, but it’s going to
> Mongo. Consider it a present for the baby shower.
>
> System Message: The Dream has been defeated.
>
> System Message: All assets of the Dream have been awarded to the Princess Posse.
>
> System Message: And then there were two.

The baby shower is literal — Mongo’s mate Kiwi had given birth two chapters earlier. Donut’s verdict
on the whole affair: “Well, that was significantly easier than I thought it would be.”
