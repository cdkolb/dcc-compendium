# Colonel Boomer

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Former crawler — a **tenner** — serving as a **Colonel in the Princess Posse**. On
      Carl's interface he reads as "This is a subordinate."
    - **Race:** Elf. Carl's reaction on meeting him: "I would never have guessed he was an elf."
    - **Class:** **Legate** — "it allowed him to buff a large number of soldiers at once"
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** **62**
    - **Role:** Field commander of the **106th Bloody Leeches** battalion — warriors, trap mechanics
      and thieves. Carl hands him operational command of two of the war's major offensives
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** No named spell is printed. His stated function is mass buffing
      across a large body of troops
    - **Notable items:** "a pair of war gauntlets similar to my Grull gauntlet" — never named or
      statted. Plus the cigar, which he chews all through the planning and lights only on the "go"
      order
    - **Status:** **Alive** — last seen in the epilogue, off the floor and back in orbit aboard the
      Crest behemoth *Homecoming Queen*

## Overview

Boomer is the professional soldier the Princess Posse did not otherwise have. Carl first hears of
him as a name on the order of battle — "the 106th Bloody Leeches, which was currently led by a
former crawler I didn't know named Boomer" (Chapter 21) — and meets him the following chapter in a
fortified bunker on the southern front, leaning over a map.

> “The leeches are in place,” Colonel Boomer said, chomping on his unlit cigar. “Ready to pluck some
> cheese sticks.” I examined the man. He was a grizzled, old elf that had survived one of the
> earliest seasons of Dungeon Crawler World. He looked like someone had taken the world’s fattest
> bulldog, pickled it for about 5,000 years, gave him elf ears, and then shoved a cigar in his
> mouth. I would never have guessed he was an elf. He looked as solid as he was old. He had a voice
> to match.

> **Boomer. Elven Legate. Level 62.**
>
> **Colonel in the Princess Posse.**
>
> **This is a subordinate.**

His class caused confusion at first — "His class of “Legate” led to some confusion, but it allowed
him to buff a large number of soldiers at once." **Tipid** put him in charge of the 106th
immediately, "and the soldiers all loved him." He is the only figure in the army who talks to
[Carl](carl.md) the way an actual subordinate commander would, up to and including telling him on
the record to stay out of his operation.

[Donut](princess-donut.md), who spends the entire briefing staring at him, files the following
report into Carl's private chat:

> **HE LOOKS LIKE HE MIGHT STINK, BUT HE ACTUALLY SMELLS QUITE NICE. LIKE CEDAR MIXED WITH ACORNS.
> YOU KNOW, LIKE HOW A GIFTSHOP IN AN EXPENSIVE MOUNTAIN RETREAT WOULD SMELL. DO YOU THINK HE’D BE
> MAD IF I CALLED HIM ‘POPS?’ HE LOOKS LIKE SOMEONE WHO’D BE CALLED POPS. BOOMER DOESN’T FIT HIM AT
> ALL. BOOMER SOUNDS LIKE THE NAME OF SOMEONE WHO PEAKED IN HIGH SCHOOL AND NOW SELLS ALUMINUM
> SIDING. DO YOU THINK HE EVER LIGHTS THAT DISGUSTING CIGAR? BECAUSE THAT WOULD JUST RUIN IT.**

He does light it — once, at the end of the briefing, in a single long puff, followed by "Fuck yeah."

## What a "Tenner" Is, and Why There Is a Warrant

A tenner is a crawler who took the exit deal rather than continue. The book defines the term twice.
Tipid's version, describing the veterans who joined the Posse: "We may be level 60, but each one of
us is at least a tenner. We’ve all survived Faction Wars at least once. Or Land War." The
narration's version is more precise — "tenners—those who tapped out at the start of the tenth
floor".

Boomer's own history, as Tipid relays it:

> He was a tenner, someone who’d bowed out on the 10th floor, and he’d survived as a merchant on the
> sixth floor for many, many seasons before finally escaping. He’d been banned from Crawl Con years
> ago after threatening to “Fist fuck” an orcish show runner to death during a panel. Porthus had
> publicly distanced himself from the militant, former crawler after that, but apparently, they’d
> been in close contact the whole time.

The warrant is a separate matter from the Crawl Con ban:

> There was an active warrant for the old man’s arrest after a deadly waystation brawl a few cycles
> back. A brawl that had started after an argument about the crawl. He’d killed someone and had
> disappeared into the ether. The man had become a legend after that.

The whole veteran contingent is in a comparable position. When Donut asks "So, are you like
fugitives? Are they going to swoop in and arrest everyone at any moment?",
[Rosetta](rosetta-thagra.md) answers that once they are inside the game there is nothing the
Syndicate can do: "They’ll wait until we get out to try to arrest us."

## The Southern Offensive

The first operation Boomer field-commands is the attack out of the southern trench line into naga
territory, against the dug-in Prism, Dream and Madness forces (Chapters 22–25). He plans it in the
bunker with **Tran** of the **102nd Bulwark Battalion**, working from
[Elle McGibbons](elle-mcgibbons.md)'s reconnaissance. His contribution is threat assessment: the
Dream's bombardiers will run if you get near their trebuchets and will have ringed them with traps;
the real danger is the Epitome household guard, whose "security forces are all veterans of
non-dungeon conflicts and are tough as gristle"; and the invisible Madness infiltrators are called
the **Monkeywrenchers** — "A few seasons back, a group of 20 of them single-handedly took the
Dreadnaught and Naga castles during the first few hours."

Once the crater-and-*Build Trench* breach works and the enemy front line collapses, Carl leaves for
the Blood Sultanate castle and the system hands the battle over (Chapter 25):

> **The command of this assault has been transferred to Colonel Boomer.**
>
> **Colonel Boomer: Quit yer celebrating, all. Nobody fights harder than someone that’s trapped.
> Only a few of them will be dumb enough to jump into that middle trench. I need you to form up.
> Don’t push too hard. We don’t want them surrendering. We want them thinking it’s a stalemate.
> Generals, you guys have maybe a half of an hour before they either escape or we gotta kill all of
> them.**

The 106th converts the surviving mercenaries — "Luckily, the NPC mercenaries were happy to accept
their new colors" — and Boomer immediately proposes exploiting the hole: "We can probably push to
the Blood Sultanate castle while you have our back covered." Carl refuses. Boomer's reply is a
single word: "Understood." Carl concedes in narration that Boomer was right, that taking the naga
castle "would’ve been the safest and smartest play," and that "I didn’t care."

## The Reaver Assault

The second offensive is his own from the start. In the master plan for the general offensive
(Chapter 59) the assignments are "Florin will deal with the orcs, Boomer with the Reavers, Donut
with the Dream, and my task is the Madness." Rosetta is the designated "Theater Commander," meaning
she is in charge of coordinating it all. Boomer opens his part in Chapter 62:

> **Boomer: Starting our raid on the Reaver castle. Bomo and Sledge. Initiate the barrage.**

When Carl tries to divert [Louis Santiago](louis-santiago.md)'s bomber away from a dragon circling
the objective, Boomer sends the sharpest rebuke anyone gives Carl in the book (Chapter 63):

> **Colonel Boomer: Warlord. We need those bombs dropped. We don’t have No Disassemble missiles to
> kill their electronics. If you call General Louis off his run, that puts the rest of us in danger.
> I know he’s your friend. But we can’t do this without air support. He’s a good pilot. He knows
> what he’s doing. And with all due respect, you handed operational command over to Rosetta after
> you put me in charge of the Reaver assault. You need to focus on your own operation, not mine.
> I’ll try to get close enough to mark the dragon for a missile barrage, but you can’t call off his
> run just because it’s dangerous.**

Carl's narration: "Goddamnit. He was right, and I knew it." He countermands himself — "This is
Boomer’s op and listen to him from now on" — and Louis flies the run. The run is what kills *Party
Planner* and strands Louis inside the Reaver castle for most of the rest of the book.

Boomer is still "pushing against the front door of the Reavers" when the goddess Eris casts *Bring
Forth the Witnesses* and freezes most of the army; Rosetta's report is "Boomer, Tran, and Bodi aren’t
answering, either." He survives it, and by the Peeling phase he is back on the work detail with
Florin, Bodi, [Britney](britney.md) and Rosetta, clearing lava damage and repairing the city's
defensive ring.

!!! note "Order of battle"
    The **106th Bloody Leeches** is one of several numbered Princess Posse battalions, alongside the
    **102nd Bulwark**, the **101st**, the **103rd Recon Legionnaires** and the **104th Naughty
    Little Piggies**. The army's structure is out of scope for this page.

## "Time to Bring the War Out of the Dungeon"

Boomer returns in the epilogue, which is told from the point of view of **York**, the tenodera poet
left alone aboard the Crest behemoth *Homecoming Queen* in orbit. York's ship is secretly armed:
"not only was the ship well-armed, but it had a full complement of enhancement zone guns and beams.
Highly regulated, high-energy weapons that would normally only work in the center system. Porthus
had ordered them installed." Those weapons are now "green across the board, making Homecoming Queen
likely the strongest ship in orbit."

Boomer calls in to dock — visibly aged — and needles York about poetry:

> Boomer grinned at him over the display. “Roses are red. Violets are blue. We won Faction Wars. Now
> we get to kill more of them.”
>
> “That is a terrible poem, Boomer.”

Then he asks after the equipment. As he does, a pre-recorded broadcast from **Porthus** goes out
announcing the formation of a new militant group that will move against Syndicate government
property and systems across the known universe unless the crawl is stopped.

> York clicked a button. “Not only are the breaching suits ready, Boomer. But the enhancement zone
> suits appear to be online and working, just like Porthus predicted. And the power armor is all
> online as well.”
>
> “Fuck yeah,” Boomer said, popping a cigar into his mouth. “Set aside four of the Cronus suits and
> a landing skiff. We have a pregnant veteran on the surface who needs backup. And for the rest of
> us, it’s time to bring the war out of the dungeon. Better warm up the lasers.”

**Cronus-class power armor** is established earlier in the book as the heavy gear the Reaver
security cyborgs were specifically *not* allowed to bring into the dungeon: "If I’d allowed them all
to come in here with their heavy duty, Cronus-class power armor, they’d already be putting those
collectible, heart-covered boxers on clearance in all waystation gift shops across the galaxy."

!!! note "Unresolved"
    The "pregnant veteran on the surface who needs backup" is never identified in Book 7, and
    neither is the target of "phase two." Boomer's remark that "we made some friends down there, and
    this next part isn’t going to be nearly as difficult as we originally thought" is likewise left
    hanging.
