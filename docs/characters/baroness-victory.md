# Baroness Victory

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC — **Adjutant for the Princess Posse** in Faction Wars, formally logged by the
      system as a “non-combatant” observer; by the end of Book 7, **acting Prime Minister of the
      Syndicate**
    - **Race:** Orc — Skull Empire nobility. Sister of the late Queen Consort **Ugloo**, aunt to
      Prince Maestro and Crown Prince Stalwart, sister-in-law to **King Rust**
    - **Class:** None stated — no character sheet or class is ever shown for her
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** Not stated anywhere in the book
    - **Role:** Rules referee, legal counsel and procedural weapon. Outside the dungeon she is a
      judge; Quasar calls her “Judge Victory”
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** Adjutant teleportation (she can follow the warlords onto the
      field and “cracks into existence” at will, even during Ramp-Up), adjutant invisibility, a
      protected non-corporeal form that weapons and hands pass straight through, and a direct line
      to the AI which she argues with out loud, eyes flashing
    - **Notable items:** A massive, cruel-looking battle-axe carried in her waistband
    - **Status:** **Alive** at the end of Book 7 — and promoted

## Overview

Victory is the referee Carl picked, and the single most useful non-combatant he has. She was chosen
as adjutant at the end of Book 6 — see her row on the [supporting cast](supporting-cast.md) page for
that selection, where she won the job by mentioning she had recently sentenced her own aunt to
indentureship for unpaid taxes and, drily, “**I’m the only one the others sued to keep out of the
game.**” On Floor 9 she becomes a fixture: teleporting into trenches, standing silently in corners of
the flag room, and appearing at [Carl](carl.md)’s elbow at the exact moment a ruling is about to
matter.

[Quasar](quasar.md) tells Carl how to use her before the floor even starts:

> Just keep your head down and listen to what Judge Victory tells you. She’s a smart one.

Her opening briefing sets out exactly what she is and is not:

> “I know you two prefer a more hands-on approach. I will be present when it’s prudent. If my
> presence causes a problem, let me know, and I will come back here. All adjutants can also go
> invisible if need be. But I will warn you. I can and will call out all fouls as I see them. That
> includes anything perpetrated by your team. If you have any questions about the rules, ask me
> first. I am on your side. I can help with strategy. In addition, I can make suggestions to fit
> something into the existing ruleset, if required.”

[Donut](princess-donut.md), unconvinced, asks her whether her dead sister was nice or a jerk.
Victory answers seriously, at length, about Ugloo losing herself when she married into the royal
family. Donut’s justification: “You can judge someone based on how they talk about the dead.”

## Killing Herman the Fleet

In Chapter 13, Carl and Donut walk into the antechamber of their own flag room and find an Elite
legionnaire of their own army writhing behind a desk.

> Elite Herman the Fleet. Ogre Strongman. Level 70.
>
> Legionnaire.
>
> Princess Posse.

**Herman the Fleet** — third of the three ogre strongmen from Grimaldi’s circus — is a suicide
bomber, packed with explosive satchels under his clothes and fighting the compulsion. “Been waiting.
Brother says you good. Supposed to do it already. Been fighting it. Tell brother... tell sorry.”
Donut fires a Magic Missile; Florin hits the deck; Tipid turns to smoke. And then the flag room door
opens.

> At the same moment, the door to the flag room opened, an armored orc stepped out, and she slammed
> a massive, cruel-looking battle-axe into the head of the struggling ogre.
>
> The elite slumped over, dead.

Carl inventories the destabilising corpse. The orc watches him do it with “a slight hint of distaste
on her boar-like face.” This is his first sight of her in the flesh, and of the tag over her head:

> Baroness Victory.
>
> Adjutant for The Princess Posse.
>
> This is a “non-combatant” observer.

The quotation marks are new. “My eyes focused on the ‘non-combatant’ part of her name, which now had
quotes around it. The AI had done that.”

Her only explanation to Carl is “I can only do so much for you, warlord.” Her justification to the
system is a full legal argument delivered before anyone can charge her:

> “That wasn’t a legal attack,” the orc said, sliding her blood-splattered axe back into her
> waistband. A bit of brain dripped off it and splatched onto her boot. “That was an unlawful
> assassination attempt by Sensation Entertainment, and I will be calling foul. I will be filing a
> grievance against the Dream, who now own the entertainment company. They were exerting undue
> influence on one of their elites to affect Faction Wars. Princess Donut, that was an admirable
> shot, and it may have saved you all. I will file for you to get credit for the kill.”

Then she argues it out with the AI in the room, out loud, alone:

> The orc paused, looking up at the ceiling. “No,” she suddenly said out loud, angrily pointing at
> the ceiling. “Absolutely not. That was clearly a foul by the corporate interests who purchased
> Sensation, and if you don’t like it, you should re-read your own rules. In addition, you are out
> of line by not warning me he was being influenced.”
>
> She paused again.
>
> “I accept your apology,” she finally said.

Florin’s verdict, walking in behind her: “I don’t know if I should be terrified or in love.” Victory
herself later files the incident away in one line — “What you saw just now was an anomaly.”

## The Rules Lawyer

Her value to Carl is almost entirely procedural, and the book keeps finding new uses for it.

At the minor-quorum adjudication in Chapters 18–19 — convened after [Katia](katia.md) and Carl’s
consciousnesses got tangled and Shi Maria slaughtered a ballroom of Lemig goblins — she sits between
Carl and two enemy warlords and simply refutes the accusation that the Princess Posse were also
cheating. “‘I will refute this,’ Victory said. The simple pronouncement was followed by a ping from
the system.” That ping is the whole defence. She also winks at Carl across the room mid-hearing, and
assesses the other warlord frankly:

> Victory shrugged. “Houston is obviously guilty, but unless he admits it, he was smart enough to
> let Stockade do all of his dirty work. I will vote not culpable for both the Madness team and the
> Princess Posse.”

In the field she reads debuffs, spell exploits and enemy tricks for him — identifying the Gurgles
(“A really nasty, contagious, non-curable debuff… The factions had agreed many years ago to never
use it again because it’s so insidious”), and later cracking [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md)’s
inexplicable power source in one snap of her fingers: “It’s Power Siphon! Those jewels used for the
towers leak all sorts of energy. They’re in range. It’s drawing power from the active towers in
Larracos.” She calls it genius and admits she would never have thought of it.

She is equally willing to point the weapon at Carl. “Carl, I am very busy right now, but I’m not so
busy that I won’t call foul. Don’t tempt me.” And she draws a hard line at intelligence:

> Baroness Victory: There are rules. I follow those rules. I am not to be an intelligence source.

In the late-book adjudication that Carl is frantic to end, she reveals the tactic:

> Baroness Victory: Carl, trust me on this. You want this trial to go on as long as possible. We’re
> stalling on purpose. We will stall until the AI gets so pissed, it ends it.

It does not work. Her message when the Madness falls and the Peeling Phase opens is four flat
sentences: “I’m sorry. We tried to delay as long as possible. Our attempts have backfired. We were
out maneuvered.”

One of the few duties she visibly hates is the live viewer update — standing in a privacy bubble
alongside Drick, the adjutant for Team Retribution, doing colour commentary in “a strange,
newscaster-like persona I hadn’t heard from her before,” and being leaned on by unnamed parties to
do more of it.

## The Deal with Porthus

Victory is also running her own operation, and the book only shows it in the
[Rosetta](rosetta-thagra.md) interlude near the end of Part 3.

[Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md) killed **King Rust** on the battlefield — lassoing and
decapitating her own people’s oldest enemy — and immediately scanned his hand, ring and severed head
into a hand-held credit device to drain his accounts. What she needed and did not have was the PIN.
Victory supplied it.

> Baroness Victory: I was able to check the financial feed during the adjudication while we waited
> for that idiot Ferdinand to come down out of the rafters. It appears the money has reached the
> intended destination. Obviously we can’t see what’s really happening out there anymore, but we can
> still see transactions and their amounts. I can’t decipher what the gods is happening in the Naga
> system, but there was a transaction more than big enough to suggest your people were able to
> purchase two ships, despite the apparent chaos in the system. Assuming your Homecoming Queen
> starship follows your orders and shoots down that yacht, our transaction is done.
>
> Rosetta: Thank you, Baroness. I know your sister would be proud.

The price of the PIN was an orc yacht in orbit — “The yacht they’d agreed to kill in exchange for the
pin number contained several children. Orc children, yes, but children nonetheless.” The money buys
two ships for **Porthus** and the Open Intellect Pacifist Network, so that the families of everyone
trapped in the dungeon can run: one generation ship to board, the second handed to the goats in the
Plenty system in exchange for passage through the forbidden gate at the Impetus Point.

Rosetta cannot work out what Victory gets from it. The narration is explicit that she is not playing
for the Skull Empire throne — “There were no circumstances in which Baroness Victory would end up in
charge of the Skull Empire, not unless she first killed about 1,000 other nobles spread across the
galaxy, so her motivations in helping the Pacifist network were a mystery to Rosetta.” The deal was
brokered between Porthus and Victory **before Victory was chosen as an adjutant at all**.

!!! note "Unresolved"
    Her motive for the Porthus deal is never explained in Book 7. Rosetta flags it as a mystery and
    the book leaves it there.

Asked in the same exchange whether the arrangement compromises her, Victory is blunt:

> Baroness Victory: Just because we have dealings together outside the dungeon doesn’t mean I am not
> taking my duties as adjutant seriously. I told you before I will not cheat nor will I help you
> cheat. Any information I give about in-dungeon rulings must go through warlords Carl or Donut.
> That said, everything is fucked. That’s what’s going on. We’re all going to die because the AI is
> insane.

## Prime Minister Victory

Three things happen in sequence in the last quarter of the book.

**First**, in Chapter 81, **Prime Minister Glory** of the Syndicate tries to have Carl killed by
overriding the emergency ejection sequence through a legacy Syndicate control panel. The AI blocks
it, freezes the world, and issues a threat instead of a reward:

> A galactic power—In this case, Prime Minister Glory himself—just attempted to assassinate you
> using a little known, but apparently still active control panel that allows the Syndicate to
> override certain showrunner-controlled aspects of the game.
>
> It didn’t work. And not only did it not work, but now I’m irritated.
>
> You may be relieved that you’re not currently in this system, Prime Minister Glory. But you should
> be very, very worried that your adult children are.

**Second**, in Chapter 83, Victory and Fopsy blink back into the room from a virtual Syndicate
Council session. “They’ve just called a No Confidence vote on Glory, and that’s happening right now.”
She notes that Fopsy — a former Prime Minister, and the **Dream**’s adjutant — might get the job back
if the vote breaks a certain way.

**Third**, in Chapter 86, she is still in Carl’s tent when Donut asks her why she hasn’t left with
the other adjutants:

> “No,” she said. She straightened. “With the unfortunate death of my underage nieces and nephews up
> in orbit, I once again became eligible for political office. Not allowed to be in office if you
> have underage leaders in your family. It’s an old, stupid rule, but a rule nonetheless. Anyway, as
> people weren’t certain of former Prime Minister Fopsy’s relationship to the Dream, he was passed
> over for the newly-vacated position of Syndicate Prime Minister. As a result, I am now acting Prime
> Minister Victory. There will be a real vote in a few months, but in the meantime, I’m it. They
> wanted someone stuck here with the rest of us so I could truly focus on the crisis.”

Those underage nieces and nephews are the children on the yacht she helped arrange to have shot down.
The goddess Huanxin makes the connection out loud and to her face — “And I’m sure it’s just an
unfortunate coincidence your underage nephew was blown out of orbit. Now that idiot the Maestro is
the king, and you’re Prime Minister” — and Victory answers by telling her a mantis colony system has
just gone nova. Minutes later Katia’s choice kills Huanxin at Victory’s feet.

Donut’s immediate reaction is that they now know a prime minister and can be rescued. Victory shuts
it down: “It’s only temporary. And even if it wasn’t, it wouldn’t matter. I have less power now than
I did when I was a judge. The council is just as bad as the Lemig Sortion these days. It’s total
gridlock.”

Carl puts a hand on her shoulder, feels the softness of a protected form that his fist would pass
straight through, and wishes her luck. Then:

> As much as I loathed the idea of killing another few thousand schmucks who’d gotten railroaded into
> coming down here for the crawl, as much as I actually liked Victory, I wouldn’t hesitate. Not even
> for a moment, to kill the goddamn prime minister of the Syndicate.
>
> She snorted. She knew it, too.

Her last words in the book are an apology for leaving early:

> Okay, team. They are calling me away. I’m sure we’ll speak soon. I’m about to get yelled at for
> allowing the murder of another CEO. It was all within the rules, but they won’t care.
> Congratulations on your victory.”
>
> “Yeah,” I said as Baroness Victory, now Prime Minister Victory disappeared for the last time.

## Voice

She is the driest character in the book, and the book gives her the best throwaway lines.

On spotting King Rust’s battle regalia on the horizon: “Well I’ll be fucked raw with a Soother
sausage. That’s him alright. He must’ve come up from the 18th floor at the last minute. I wonder if
he brought all his concubines with him?”

On Carl asking whether her family is as dysfunctional as his: “The bigger the universe, the smaller
it becomes.”

On [Prepotente](prepotente.md) delaying an adjudication: “Your boyfriend is holding up the
adjudication. We’re waiting for Drick to get him out of the rafters. Literally.”

On her own rulings during Donut’s more improvisational moments, after asking what the plan is and
being told there isn’t one: “You’re not going to question her?”

And her stock response to anything genuinely unprecedented — which on this floor is most things:
“Don’t even ask because I have no idea what that was.”
