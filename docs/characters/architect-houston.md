# Architect Houston

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC warlord — leader of **the Madness** in Faction Wars, and **prime of the
      Viceroys**, a title he holds “not because he was elected so, or because of some innate ability
      to lead his people, but because he’s made the most scientific discoveries”
    - **Race:** Viceroy. Publicly a white featureless mask with a small red teardrop at its centre
      and long, flowing, starkly white robes “like a pope.” Underneath: a multi-limbed half-bug,
      half-monster with six arms and mandibles, which Carl compares to “a mix between a Predator
      alien and a goblin with no lips”
    - **Class:** None stated — warlords are not given character sheets
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** Not stated
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** No named spell list. He casts at least twice in the surgical
      theatre — once on the corpse suspended above him — and produces a rifle from under his robes
    - **Notable items:** **The Grease Monkey**, a Viceroy Portable Surgical and Scientific
      Exploration Unit — a chrome chandelier of robotic arms and surgical tools hanging from his
      theatre ceiling; a ring of eight **Containment Breach Trap Mark IV** modules loaded with Sheol
      fire, chained in sequence, Tripper-proof, and wired to a dead man’s switch attached to him
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — Chapter 68, killed by [Carl](carl.md) with **Gloom Wraith Phase**

## Overview

Houston is the book’s most menacing antagonist, and almost all of it is delivered through what he
says — which is not much, and never loudly. Through the whole first adjudication he does not speak or
move at all; he simply watches Carl, his neck making “a slight creaking noise, like from a wooden
ship” when he turns his head.

He is introduced second-hand, by the awakened dwarf NPC Arief, who draws his sword in a corridor to
announce a grudge: “When the fighting starts, I will have my revenge against Architect Houston!”

> “The leader of the Madness,” the dwarf said. “A Viceroy outworlder. He caught my brother as he
> attempted to sabotage their fortifications.” He re-sheathed his blade over his shoulder. “They say
> my brother was tortured before Juice Box infiltrated and ended his suffering.”

> “That was one of his surgeons,” the dwarf said. “Nobody has managed to get into Houston’s operating
> theater. That’s what they call his lair next to the throne room.”

King Rust, in the middle of telling Carl the solar system is about to be destroyed and that he no
longer cares about anything, stops to say this:

> And if not us, that lunatic Architect Houston has been using this season’s rules to... open up his
> full arsenal.” He sighed, almost wistfully, and he met my eyes. “I can admit this now. He’s the only
> living creature in this universe I fear. But that doesn’t matter anymore. Death looms for us all.”

## The Adjudication

Chapters 18 and 19 put Carl in a room with him. The hearing concerns
**Commander Stockade** of the Lemig Sortion’s attempt to kill Carl by timing a Summon Ally request to
land while Shi Maria had control of his unconscious body; the adjutants conclude that Stockade and
Houston conspired together to run real-time outside intelligence through **Nurse Yugoslav of the
Madness** — Houston’s mother — on the 18th floor.

Houston refuses to confess and is found not culpable. [Baroness Victory](baroness-victory.md)’s
assessment is exact:

> Victory shrugged. “Houston is obviously guilty, but unless he admits it, he was smart enough to let
> Stockade do all of his dirty work. I will vote not culpable for both the Madness team and the
> Princess Posse.”

Carl, who has just sat through a screen replay of [Katia](katia.md)’s worst memories being watched
for entertainment, needles him with the one thing available: “Your mom? Your mom is here in the
dungeon? I can’t wait to meet her.” The reply arrives privately in the warlord chat, and it is the
first time Houston says anything at all:

> Architect Houston: If you ever mention my mother out loud ever again, you and that animal companion
> of yours will spend the last thousand hours of your existence upon the amplification table in my
> surgical theater. This is a promise.
>
> Warlord Carl: Oh, fuck off.
>
> Architect Houston: You are in mortal danger at this very moment, and you don’t even realize it. I
> do hope you escape so we can extend our play, but gracious pet owners accept the gifts their animals
> drop at their doorsteps.

Then Stockade, driven insane by the spider’s Insanity spell, begins smashing his own face against the
floor in front of everyone. Victory offers Houston the chance to intervene — “Feel free to help him.
Or call his guards.” He does not move. He continues to stare directly at Carl while the caterpillar
beats his own brains out, and the Madness inherits the entire defeated Lemig Sortion as a result.

The ceasefire ends in the same breath as the ruling, and Houston’s opening move of the war is to
stand up, throw back his robes, and shoot Carl in the chest at a conference table.

## The Beautiful Place

At the Chapter 43 warlord council, [Juice Box](juice-box.md) shapeshifts into a form that unnerves
every leader in the room. Houston’s reaction is the one Carl notices:

> Architect Houston’s reaction was the strangest of all. The Viceroy jumped to his feet, his strange
> robes undulating. He put both hands on the table and leaned forward. He was shaking, and if I didn’t
> know better, he was shaking with excitement.

He is the one who explains what she turned into, over Epitome Tagg’s objections, and the book lets
him talk:

> “Now is not the time or place to rehash that story, but to say that was the personification of the
> Nothing is one way to describe it. Though what that thing truly is goes well beyond that. There is
> some scientific basis that this creature actually exists and is...”

> “There is some scientific basis that this creature, who does not have a single name, is what some
> people see at the moment of death. It is theorized it’s not some supernatural deity, like many
> believe, but a trans-dimensional entity, similar to the Apothecary. There is no proof that merely
> seeing this creature imprints you in some way that damns you.”

> “It has a thousand names,” Houston said. “Before most cultures in this galaxy found the stars,
> they’d already had a name for it. The Hag. The Unwashed. The Stalker. The Last Light. The Thirst.
> The Fell. The Inexorable. But we, the Viceroys who seek all understanding of life and death... We
> call it The Beautiful Place. There’s an interesting, involuntary nerve reaction in some species to
> the very image of the creature, even in those who are otherwise paralyzed. It is studied
> extensively, but we still have more questions than answers.” After a moment, he added, somewhat
> wistfully, “Gaining access to this creature is my life’s work.”

For the rest of the council he paces back and forth, on his feet, unable to sit down.

## The Surgical Theatre

Carl reaches the Castle of the Madness in Chapter 67 with Dong Quixote, Bigs, Jamal and Rend. *Ping*
shows twenty soldiers and, in the room beside the throne room, one purple dot and one white — plus
three crawlers and a crawler’s corpse.

The corpse is **Zeynep**, a Turkish crawler Carl met on Floor 8 and never spoke to on this one. Two
of her surviving squadmates, Selvi and Tuba, are conscripted into the Madness army. The third crawler
in the theatre is **Burcu**, the badger-headed porsuk swashbuckler from the Butcher’s Masquerade, and
she is still alive.

Down the hallway, the screaming keeps starting and stopping. “That... that sounds like it’s him
screaming. Architect Houston.”

The doors are marked **Do Not Enter**, with a lit neon sign beneath reading **Amplification in
process**. Inside is an octagonal white room with a raised viewing gallery so students can watch, four
surgical tables, and the Grease Monkey overhead — “a chandelier covered in robotic arms and surgical
tools, all spinning and waving about, silently whisking as they worked.”

Zeynep is on the first table, cause of death **Died on the Surgical Table**. Carl will not describe
what was done to her: “They had done... something to her. Something unspeakable. Something I won’t
describe.”

Burcu is on the second, bound by magic, tongue and voice box cut out. Her first successful message
is:

> Burcu: There you are! None of my messages were delivered. He cut out my tongue and voice box. I
> can’t speak. He tortured my mother. He killed her right in front of me. Free me so I can kill him.
> These bindings won’t let me cast magic. You owe me, Carl. You killed my father. Free me now.

On the third table is Houston himself — robes gone, mask gone — with the peeled-open corpse of a
changeling named **Namico** suspended upside down ten inches above him, the two of them being
physically spliced together by the machine. “Things were coming out of the changeling and going into
Houston and vice-versa.”

He is being operated on by his own machine, without anaesthetic, and he is screaming. His first words
to Carl are not a threat:

> “Stay back,” Houston called at me. He was clearly in pain as the machine continued to work. Hundreds
> of tiny little arms appeared, and they were connecting what appeared to be nerves from the
> changeling’s neck to Houston. “You take one step in here, and it’ll set off the traps, incinerating
> us all. “I’m almost done. I’m almost there. You’ll have your victory, Carl. I care no longer for
> what happens here. Let me finish. Let me finish, and your fellow crawler doesn’t have to die. This
> is my life’s work. I’m almost there.”

And, as Carl reaches for the switch that will release Burcu’s bindings:

> “Carl. Do not... Do not press that button. I can stop it all,” Houston called. “You don’t
> understand. It is called the Beautiful Place. I can get there. They are the key. The changelings.
> This world. Why do you think we come here, cycle after cycle? You’ve helped make the greatest
> discovery in history, and you don’t even realize it. It was here all along, right under our
> mandibles.”

## Death

Carl cannot simply step into the room and let the traps kill everyone, because Burcu has no
invulnerability and would be turned to ash. So he crosses the theatre by jumping table to table with
*Walk on Air*, which is how the whole thing comes apart: his own invulnerability expires mid-crossing,
he knocks Zeynep’s corpse to pieces landing on it, he puts Burcu to sleep with the wand of
Nighty-Night, and then the table he lands on tips over.

Before that, he does something stranger. Houston’s health bar drops and he begins to die on the table
— and the changeling corpse above him starts to change, growing roots.

> “Yes!” Houston called as he died. He was sobbing. “I found you. I found you. After all this time.
> You were right here all along. It’s really you, isn’t it? Not just an avatar. It’s you. It’s you.”

Carl throws a Fine Healing potion at his head to stop it. “‘Gah!’ he cried as his health rocketed back
up. ‘No! No! What did you do?’”

Then the table tips, Burcu falls, Carl’s bare feet hit the floor, and the dead man’s switch arms the
whole ring of Sheol-fire traps.

> “You don’t know what you’ve done,” was all Houston said in the moments before the traps fired.

> For my part, I activated Gloom Wraith Phase the very moment my feet touched the ground.
>
> I shot forward in the direction I was facing, which was toward Architect Houston, still alive, still
> on the table, still under the creature surgically attached to him.
>
> I pushed through them, everything moving in slow motion as the fire descended upon the room from all
> directions at once. I passed through the naked Houston, unraveling him.

Carl exits the room non-corporeal, hits something magical on the way out, dislocates a shoulder and is
briefly knocked unconscious. Burcu dies in the fire. In the spin, the thing that used to be the
changeling turns to face him and briefly becomes [Li Jun](li-jun.md), who thanks him and is whisked
away by roots.

> System Message: Architect Houston of the Madness has fallen. Credit for the kill has been given to
> Warlord Carl of the Princess Posse.
>
> System Message: The Madness has been defeated.
>
> System Message: All assets of the Madness have been awarded to the Princess Posse.

!!! note "Unresolved"
    What Houston found, whether he reached the Beautiful Place, what the changeling became, and what
    Carl actually saw and passed through on his way out are all left open. Carl’s own account is
    deliberately uncertain: “I’d seen something, but I wasn’t sure if it was real. It felt like a
    dream.”

## The Peeling Phase

His death is the trigger for the last act of Faction Wars. The system messages run straight on from
the kill credit:

> System Message: Battlefield conditions have been met. Larracos is no longer safe.
>
> Warning. Warning. Warning.
>
> System Message: The Peeling Phase is upon us. This city is open. First to take the castle, wins.
>
> And remember, folks. Only one team can win. Alliances don’t matter in the end.

Within seconds, the War Mage Rebellion is granted standing, full access to the Conscription spell, and
a reversion to the original Faction Wars rules — under which “every affiliated soldier and warlord on
a team who is still on the battlefield will now drop dead where they stand the moment the team is
defeated.” Victory’s message arrives in the same barrage: “I’m sorry. We tried to delay as long as
possible. Our attempts have backfired. We were out maneuvered.”

Eliminating the Madness removes the faction Carl most feared and hands him its entire asset pool. It
also ends the phase of the game in which anyone was safe.
