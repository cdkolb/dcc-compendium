# Rosetta Thagra

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Former crawler — broadcast host and, secretly, an operative of the
      **Open Intellect Pacifist Network**; from Book 7 a re-entered combatant fighting on Floor 9 as a
      mercenary sworn to the Princess Posse
    - **Race:** **Crest** — a human-like race distinguished by having no eyebrows; Crests were a
      common hunter type on Floor 6
    - **Class / Subclass:** Books 1-6 never state what class she ran; in her own era she was, per
      Zev, "a big fan of blowing things up." Book 7 gives two answers and reconciles neither — the
      name over her head on Floor 9 reads "Rosetta Thag. **Crest Barn Burner**. Level 60," while her
      own status card lists her original crawl as **Class: Gazetteer, Top Level: 91**. No subclass is
      ever named
    - **Role:** Host of *Shadow Boxer*; maker of the documentary *The Other Side of the Glass*;
      former crawler and former game guide, released from her indentureship only recently
    - **Seniority:** A former crawler **two seasons older than [Mordecai](mordecai.md)**
    - **Cookbook:** Contributor/owner of the **9th edition** of
      [The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md) — her notes are
      mostly explosives recipes
    - **Level:** **60** (Book 7, as a re-entered former crawler); 91 at the top of her own crawl
    - **Rank (Book 7):** **Colonel** in the Princess Posse; named **"Theater Commander"** of
      **Operation Ruin**
    - **Status:** Alive. Went to ground with the Open Intellect Pacifist Network mid-Book 6;
      resurfaces in the Book 6 epilogue crewing the freighter *Homecoming Queen*; re-enters the
      dungeon on Floor 9 and is confirmed alive at the end of Book 7
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None described as a spell list. In Book 7 she fights with
      **drop shields**, a tech-based **invisibility cloak**, and tunnelling/ground-collapse effects
      she never explains
    - **Notable items:** A neck-worn **scrambler** that hides her from security sweepers (Book 6
      epilogue); a **whip** (Book 7); a hand-held banking **datapad** and a **credit chit**

## Book 6: *Shadow Boxer*

Rosetta is introduced when Zev books Carl and Donut onto her interview program, *Shadow Boxer* — a
non-profit show owned by the Open Intellect Pacifist Network that interviews crawlers about their
pre-crawl lives in conversations "designed to look like regular conversations." Zev's summary is
blunt: "Rosetta Thagra. An Odette wannabe. She's a former crawler and game guide. She's a Crest…
Actually, she was more like you, Carl, than Odette." Carl recognizes the name from the cookbook
immediately — the 9th edition's dozens of explosives recipes are hers.

She presents as a pale, pretty woman in her early twenties in a professional pantsuit, bald but for
a blob of brightly-dyed hair perched at an angle on her head, with a flowery tattoo circling her
right leg. Her tell is rubbing the side of her right thigh when she's nervous, which is often.

Her first act out of the dungeon was to make a documentary about her own home planet, *The Other
Side of the Glass*. Its success is what got the Open Intellect Pacifist Network to fund *Shadow
Boxer*. The title appears to echo her own cookbook note about watching Faction Wars victors murder
a crawler through a portal: "We watched as the orc and his friends laughed, just there on the other
side of the glass."

## Milk and Potatoes

Rosetta's interview is the episode in which Carl's entire backstory finally goes on the public
record — his mother's suicide, his father's abandonment, the Anacortes Boys' Ranch. See
[Carl](carl.md#book-6-the-eye-of-the-bedlam-bride) for that material.

Underneath the interview she is running a coded message. She puts a photograph on the display of a
teenaged Carl chopping a potato in the boys' home kitchen, next to a jug labelled "Milk." Carl
clocks the photo as a fake instantly — he was never allowed near that kitchen, the milk jug is
shaped like a two-liter soda bottle, and the camera angle is physically impossible, taken from
where a wall stood. She then lays in the line: "Apparently Carl needs a proper recipe to do his best
work," and repeats the ingredients. Carl plays along:

> "Potatoes are very versatile," I said.
>
> "Very," Rosetta agreed. "Most…"

Harbinger cuts the feed mid-word.

The decode: **"potatoes" is the toraline root vegetable** his mystery sponsor sent him, and
**"milk" is Crawler Milk's 6th-edition cookbook notes on special inks**. The two together make
magic ink. Carl recognizes the phrasing because the exchange mirrors a note pair in the cookbook
itself — Milk writing about the stew she used to make for her people's children ("I wish to cook a
feast for the whole galaxy. I wish for them all to choke on our pain"), answered by Rosetta:
"Comrade, I agree. One day, we will make certain they all know. Our words will not be lost. I swear
it."

She plants a second message in the same session that Carl only decodes later: every television show
they screen for Donut is set in Miami, steering the party's Floor 8 region pick.

## Banned

The Valtay liaison **Harbinger** — the same caprid previously bribed by the Skull Empire — kills the
broadcast and recalls Carl's attorney mid-sentence: "She and Carl were obviously talking in code…
I have banned Rosetta from participating in any future crawler interviews." Zev, furious in a way
Carl has never seen from her, says they will be sued and will win. Donut's summary: "now she's not
going to be able to do her show any more because she was talking about mashed potatoes!"

Carl's read is simpler: "She'd risked everything to get that message to me."

## Going to Ground

After the Open Intellect Pacifist Network's Miami scheme fails — a plan that involved getting Carl
within earshot of a Residual's recitation, and was willing to spend Carl and the planet to force the
Syndicate Council's hand — the whole organization vanishes. Zev: "The box got seized, and the entire
charity and everyone involved just up and disappeared. Nobody knows where they went or if something
happened to them. They're all gone, even Rosetta."

!!! note "Not stated in the text"
    The book never uses the word "fugitive" and never narrates where Rosetta goes between the
    Network's disappearance and the epilogue. She simply reappears aboard the *Homecoming Queen*
    wearing a scrambler to defeat security sweepers.

## Epilogue: The *Homecoming Queen*

Rosetta is one of two people in the cockpit of a converted garbage freighter parked in Earth orbit —
the largest ship up there, twice the size of a Valtay warship, belonging to **Long Haul Biological
Waste Management Solutions** (the same company that has just become Donut's sponsor). The other is
**Tipid**, a fellow Crest and the 4th edition's cookbook author. Both were recruited by **Doctor Hu**
with a single promise: "I can only promise two things. Eventual death. And eventual justice."

She spends the scene nervous, chewing her nail, watching the news coverage of the Faction Wars rule
change. Her verdict on Carl is unsentimental: "I still can't tell if he's smart or if he's been
outrageously lucky… It took an NPC to show him how to use the ink to share powers, and I'm still not
sure if he gets it." She also confirms it was the two of them who sent Donut her first celestial box.

The ship is renamed *Homecoming Queen*, and it is full: **fifty thousand former crawlers**, smuggled
into the system as troopers for the Princess Posse. Tipid's intercom announcement — "We are
officially all troopers now for the Princess Posse" — is met with cheers through the hull. Her last
line before the book ends:

> "Comrade," Rosetta said. She reached over and placed a warm hand on his arm. He clasped his hand
> in hers. Both of them trembled.
>
> They were coming home.

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Rosetta comes back into the dungeon. She arrives on Floor 9 in chapter 11 alongside **Tipid**,
already sworn in as a trooper for the Princess Posse — her dot purple with a pink outline, the name
over her head reading:

> Rosetta Thag. Crest Barn Burner. Level 60.
>
> Colonel in the Princess Posse.
>
> This is a subordinate.

She looks nothing like the woman in the pantsuit. Black shorts, boots, a plain unenchanted leather
top, no weapons visible, and the floral tattoo on her right leg now *glowing*, its tendrils running
up her arm. Her tell is unchanged: she still rubs the side of her leg when she is thinking hard.

!!! note "Text inconsistency: which edition is hers?"
    Carl's narration in chapter 11 calls her the seventh-edition author:

    > Rosetta. Rosetta from the Shadow Boxer television show. Rosetta from the seventh edition of the
    > cookbook.

    This is an authorial error. Her own status card at the end of her interlude says:

    > Author of the ninth Edition of the Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook.

    And the seventh edition belongs to **Volteeg**, whose status card at the end of his interlude
    says:

    > Author of the seventh edition of the Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook.

    Book 6 also has her as the ninth ("*<**Note added by Crawler Rosetta. Ninth Edition**>*"). This
    wiki keeps **9th edition** as the established fact and treats the chapter 11 line as a slip.

### Killing King Rust

Chapter 34 is a parley. **King Rust**, leader of the Skull Empire, comes out to the neutral ground
between the armies not to fight but to tell Carl that his own daughter, **Princess Formidable**, has
seized a cruiser, is five hours from the monitor station orbiting Venus, and intends to manually
trigger the **system failsafe** — which would ignite the star and kill everyone in the system. He
asks Carl to plead with her on behalf of Earth. A deal is struck: the orc armies will withdraw, the
Posse will not harass the retreat, and everyone will spend their last hours writing goodbyes.

Rosetta was not party to any of that. She was underground.

> ...And that’s when Rosetta appeared, rising up out of the dirt like some fiery goddess of the
> underworld. She’d been wearing one of those tech-based invisibility cloaks as she launched from the
> ground. She had a whip of her own in her hand. In a movement so fast, she’d clearly been practicing
> it, she flipped in the air, landing on the back of the yak directly behind the king. She tossed the
> whip around the neck of the king, and she yanked.
>
> The head flew from his body like a popped champagne cork.

Five seconds of silence across the field, and then tens of thousands of enemy troops charge. The
ceasefire Carl had just bought is gone.

Her own reason surfaces later, in her interlude. Rust killed a crawler named **Brooks** with that
same whip, after Faction Wars was over, for sport, through a portal — the scene that gave her
documentary its title.

> Rust, still laughing, had stepped back through the portal, lassoed Brooks, and pulled him into the
> glass, which had cut him right in half. It had been so casual, so fast. Brooks had died
> immediately.
>
> But Rosetta remembered. She would never forget. She relished that moment when she used the whip to
> decapitate the old orc.

She had nearly missed the chance: Tipid, who did not know about the bank plan, had tried to hide
Rust's presence from her. The original target had been **Stalwart**.

### The robbery

The killing was a means to an end. Chapter 35 opens with Carl sprinting the wrong way, into the
charge, because Rosetta needs the corpse.

> “Grab his head!” Rosetta called as we rushed up… She was on her knees, and she had some sort of
> weird data pad object in her hand. She had King Rust’s fat hand atop the pad, and one of the
> rings—not the Ring of Divine Suffering—was placed inside the pad. “Quick, I need a retina scan!”

Carl fetches the severed head out of the grass. Rosetta presses it to the pad, something beeps, and
she tells him to inventory the head and the body both. The three factors were the **family signet
ring**, the **eyeball**, and a **password** — the PIN came from Baroness Victory as her half of the
bargain, and the **credit chit** Carl had looted off an orc hunter on the sixth floor, which Rosetta
had quietly asked him about back in chapter 11, is what she loads into the machine.

A Valtay official, overheard later, lays it out exactly:

> “…from what I understand, it required the family ring, his eyeball, and a password. We know how she
> got the first two… Sounds like she used Colloid… that’s the only bank the orcs use… No, sir. It’s
> not on the chit anymore. She transferred it out…”

The confirmation comes as a cartoon slime hologram over the datapad — *“Transfer approved! Thank you
for using Colloid Bank.”* — and Rosetta drops the pad in the dirt and sobs on the ground, with Carl
standing there having no idea what he has just helped do. The same official puts the number at
**"the theft of a few billion credits."** War Chief Stalwart's demand afterward is for "my father’s
signet ring, his body, and the credits you illegally stole."

### Where the money went

Her interlude, at the head of *The Peeling of Larracos*, is the payoff. **Baroness Victory** —
Rust's own estranged daughter, and the Princess Posse's adjutant — confirms the transfer landed:

> Baroness Victory: …there was a transaction more than big enough to suggest your people were able to
> purchase two ships, despite the apparent chaos in the system. Assuming your Homecoming Queen
> starship follows your orders and shoots down that yacht, our transaction is done.

The price of the PIN was a killing. The orc yacht the *Homecoming Queen* was to shoot down carried
several orc children, one of whom, with Stalwart dead, had just become king of the Skull Empire.
Rosetta hates the orcs without reservation and still "felt ill about the transaction."

The deal had been brokered between **Porthus** and Victory long before Victory was ever appointed
adjutant, and Victory's motives stay opaque to Rosetta. The money buys **two ships**: Porthus and the
families of everyone who went into the dungeon board one, run for the Plenty system, and trade the
second ship plus the remaining funds to the goats for passage through the "forbidden" gate at the
**Impetus Point**, the tunnel node reaching the furthest known point in the galaxy — to get as far
from the center system as possible and come back only when the wars settle.

Victory keeps the two halves of her life strictly apart:

> Baroness Victory: Just because we have dealings together outside the dungeon doesn’t mean I am not
> taking my duties as adjutant seriously. I told you before I will not cheat nor will I help you
> cheat.

See [Baroness Victory](baroness-victory.md) for her side of it.

### Theater Commander, Operation Ruin

Carl's own summary of the battle plan for the Open Hostilities phase gives Rosetta the top
coordinating job:

> Florin will deal with the orcs, Boomer with the Reavers, Donut with the Dream, and my task is the
> Madness. Tipid will remain at the FUPA to defend, and Rosetta is the designated “Theater
> Commander,” meaning she is in charge of coordinating it all.

**Operation Ruin** is the Posse's simultaneous multi-front offensive, timed to kick off the moment
the Open Hostilities phase begins. Most of the floor's tactical planning is hers and Tipid's — the
assault on the Dream artillery, the naga raid, the tunnel routes under the map. She plans Donut's
**War Crime** finisher in chapter 73-74 down to the spell selection, talking Carl out of *Laundry
Day* and into a *Dinner Bell* variant called **Bijanbi** extracted from a dead cleric, because
*Laundry Day* would strip the fleshers off the enemy and turn them loose on the Posse's own soldiers.
She also confides the rule Carl had not worked out on his own: spells taken from an unwilling owner
are stronger, and the owner loses them for good.

### Doubt

The interlude is the only place in the series where Rosetta's certainty cracks. Her job is done — the
money is away, her mother is avenged, Rust is dead — and she finds it has bought her nothing:

> Yet, yet... No amount of revenge would ever bring her mother back. It would never bring Brooks
> back. No matter how many orcs and orc children she killed, her people would still be dead.

Her mother came into the dungeon with her and died on the first day, killed by goblins. *Avenge us*
was the last thing she said.

She is briefly willing to let the war mages win — "It will be quick for us, and all the remaining
mercenaries and leaders will be dead, which is beyond what we even imagined" — and **Justice Light**
talks her out of it. She distrusts **the Apothecary**, the collective mind steering Porthus, and
distrusts the residual **Agatha** more. She is in love with Porthus, "despite his unhealthy obsession
with finding a missing crawler from his own crawl. Menerva."

She watched Carl's speech to Donut about masks and wept at how hopeful it was, and pitied him for
believing Donut could ever take hers off. *Was she wrong? Maybe so.*

She chooses the fight anyway:

> Maybe it was a mistake, but nobody could ever fault her for fighting for her friends. There was
> poetry in that. too.
>
> Colonel Rosetta: Comrades, sound off. The fight has moved to the city. The enemy has changed, and
> they have taken the castle of Larracos. We don’t know how many there are, but we fight. We fight
> with all we have.

### Elsewhere on the floor

She assists at **Louis Santiago**'s heart-and-lung transplant in chapter 84, holding the extracted
naiad organs with Imani and Elle. She hauls **Edgar the tortoise** up a siege tower strapped to her
back like a rucksack, wheezing, to tattoo Donut in time for the finisher. She is one of the handful
of NPCs and former crawlers who declines the **Zerzura** transfer and stays on Floor 9 with Carl's
party at the end of the book.

Her status card closes it:

> Worked as a game guide and in the Desperado Club as a merchant renting mercenaries and occasionally
> was in charge of distribution of the dungeon newsletter. Upon dungeon exit became a journalist and
> filmmaker of some renown and eventually became host of the documentary series Shadow Boxer.
> Eventually returned to the dungeon to fight for the Princess Posse and to assist the mysterious
> Open Intellect Pacifist Network finance their great plan.
>
> Current status: Alive.

## Book 8: A Parade of Horribles

### The Scavenger Storyline

In Heat 5, Carl finally asks Rosetta directly to lay out everything she knows about "the Scavenger
storyline" — the mythology behind his own **Scavenger's Daughter** patch. Her answer is the fullest
lore-dump on Scavenger/Apito/Scolopendra mythology given anywhere in the series to that point,
delivered in her usual register (comrade, plainspoken, treating the myth as contested oral history
rather than settled fact):

> "The all-tree is an actual tree in the dungeon, but it's also a metaphor for existence itself. The
> tree gave fruit to the old gods, including Nekhebit, who is now also called the Scavenger Mother of
> Mothers. In the old times, she was a life and protector deity, though she was depicted as a
> vulture, a scavenger... She supposedly wasn't allowed to have children, but she did anyway. She had
> three kids, including Apito, who in turn got impregnated via either the all-tree or she made
> herself pregnant. Take your pick. Nekhebit was the last of the old gods to fall, and she was
> defeated by her daughter, Apito, absorbing the last of the vulture goddess's worshippers."

Carl's follow-up question — is the Scavenger's Daughter Apito, or something to do with Scolopendra —
gets a correction rather than a confirmation:

> "No. Nekhebit is *a* scavenger, but she's not *the* Scavenger. Nekhebit had three children. A son,
> a daughter, and a centipede."

Rosetta lays out the competing, contradictory versions of the myth without picking one: sometimes
Scolopendra herself is "the Scavenger" and the Scavenger's Daughter is her offspring with someone
else; sometimes it's an unnamed son who cleans up escaped souls in "the empty plane"; sometimes
Apito, Scolopendra and the son are all considered "a divine trinity... just one of them, but they've
been split." What she's certain of is what the Scavenger's Daughter actually *is*, myth aside: "this
child... is considered one with the same power that Apito had. An entity that has the power to erase
the current pantheon and start again. But this one is even more powerful. She can take the powers of
gods and use them for herself."

This is the scene the wiki's eventual Mechanics coverage of the Primal/Eulogist cosmology will build
on; Rosetta's version here is the crawler-side, half-mythologized account, distinct from the fuller,
AI-narrated cosmology reveal delivered later through [Grigori the Placid](grigori.md) at the Floor 11
finale.
