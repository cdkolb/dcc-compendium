# Volteeg

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC / former crawler — “Weeper Commander Volteeg,” a mercenary tank commander for
      the **Operatic Collective** (Team Two) during Faction Wars
    - **Race:** **Gargoyle**, chosen on the third floor of his crawl. Birth race: **Sturnus** — the
      book never glosses the name, but shows him as a small caged pet bird who sang
    - **Class:** **Tank Buttress**
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** Top level **71** during his crawl. No current level is shown
    - **Role:** Author of the **7th edition** of
      [The Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md) — a single
      entry, written at the very end of his own ninth floor
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** Gargoyle traits — he can turn his skin to stone and disappear,
      and sleep for very long stretches unnoticed. Both stop working outside the dungeon, where heavy
      gravity makes him collapse in on himself and he needs a brace to stand
    - **Notable items:** None personally owned. He dies using Operatic Collective equipment — a
      **Weeper**-class tank and a **blitz stick**
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — Chapter 35, by his own hand, inside his own tank

## Overview

Volteeg is the strangest entry on the cookbook’s author list, and the text says so in the first
line of his interlude: “Volteeg did not consider himself worthy of love.” He is also the only author
who came into the dungeon as a literal pet — a caged bird belonging to **Mistress Henspar**, who took
a pet biscuit the way [Prepotente](prepotente.md) did, and whose entire crawl was framed by a debt he
believed he could never repay.

His single cookbook entry was written at the very end of the ninth floor, as the orc armies roared
in victory. He never used a recipe from the book himself — which is why **Porthus** did not recognise
him as an author when they first met.

## Mistress Henspar

The pre-dungeon memories are small and domestic: a clean cage, a shoulder to perch on at the market,
the hearth turned up how he liked it. He once bit a shopkeeper’s finger going for a berry, and she
forgave him for it that same night. The next time they went to the shop she hid him in her jacket.

> She whispered to him, “Quiet now. It’ll be our little secret. They won’t see you, but I’ll know
> you’re here.”

He sang for her afterwards, and it made her cry.

She died on the seventh floor. The text shows him standing over her, transformed and screaming,
“trying to protect her from the scags,” and later names the thing he could not stop as a crossbow
bolt.

> You’re supposed to protect those you love.

Two changes made him who he is, and he regrets both. The pet biscuit gave him intelligence and
speech. Choosing gargoyle on the third floor gave him strategy, memory and a self — and took away
the one thing that mattered.

> He’d chosen a race that could not sing. Not like before, and that was such a terrible, terrible
> mistake.

> You can’t unknow the universe any more than you can unbite the shopkeeper or you can turn back
> time and stop a crossbow bolt from shattering everything you’ve ever held dear.

He spent Faction Wars — his own Faction Wars — hiding, reading the hidden words in the cookbook’s
prayer cast projection, and feeling his rage and his shame grow together. His shame is specifically
about inaction: he had the book, and he did nothing with it.

## The One-Offs

He took the first deal offered on the tenth floor: unprotected NPC guard in a combat-based external
guild, 26 seasons, low odds. He survived it without ever fighting or killing a single crawler,
largely because “nobody actually wanted to fight” a gargoyle. He calls these “the numb times.”

Outside, his adopted race’s magic simply stopped working, and nobody had warned him it would. He
found the **Outreach guild**, and a job that never required him to go down a gravity well: security
on the Auditor General’s compliance barge, “watching the Operatics fart and gurgle with glee as they
bombed settlements back to oblivion if they couldn’t pay their mortgages.”

His co-workers called themselves the **One-Offs** — unique former crawlers with no home and physical
problems outside the dungeon. His only two real friends were a wheelchair-bound djinn and a
former-human “cave fairy” who could no longer fly and burned under UV light. Neither was dragged to
the Earth system, and he is glad of it.

## Smuggled In

Every other former-crawler mercenary sent back into the dungeon on the wrong side was segregated and
hauled off to the Bone Clan; Volteeg was not, and got his racial abilities back. That was **Porthus**’s
doing. Because Volteeg had entered the dungeon as a pet rather than a crawler, his paperwork carried
a slightly different designation, and someone from the **Open Intellect Pacifist Network** buried deep
in Syndicate bureaucracy altered the code on it so he read as a former indentured — “like the bopcas
and so many others. Not a crawler. Just another refugee.”

The plan Porthus gave him was to help without ever being traceable:

> You have to help, but you have to do it in a way where nobody will ever know it was you. Any
> research into your past, and they’ll know. If they suspect a former crawler is involved, they’ll
> purge the mercenaries of the few former crawlers amongst them.

Volteeg’s private verdict on that plan is bitter, because the purge had already happened: “You were
wrong, Porthus… The former crawlers were dead before Volteeg was ever discovered. It was too late for
them.” He reads **King Rust**’s sudden jump in levels — impossible from the **Ring of Divine
Suffering**, which raises stats — as the evidence of what was done with them.

It was [Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md) who recognised his odd name and told Porthus her
suspicions. Porthus asked him to his face, and he answered honestly.

> Why didn’t you tell me? Porthus had asked.
>
> Because I am ashamed at my inaction, Volteeg had replied. And it was true.
>
> Porthus had put his hand on Volteeg’s shoulder, looked him right in the eye, and said, It’s not too
> late to do something about it.

## Killing the Operatic Collective

His moment comes seconds after Rosetta beheads King Rust in front of the Bloc’s advance army —
Chapter 35 in Carl’s POV, the interlude immediately before it in Volteeg’s. The Bloc has just
fractured: **Warlord Hortense Leadership Unit** of the Operatics declares himself the new leader, the
title is disputed by **Warlord Stalwart**, and Hortense — present in person, carried on a flat litter
with a dozen shields around him because the Operatics ran a cost-benefit analysis showing a 0.01%
profit — gives the order.

> Warlord Hortense Leadership Unit: I guess we attack.

The exploit Volteeg uses is a known flaw in the Weeper tanks’ fire control. To keep the tanks
immune to enemy mages, each Weeper charges an energy ball just before loading it into the turret, so
the volatile round exists only for moments. The safety depends entirely on the driver:

> ...As long as the driver of the Weeper didn’t deliberately remove the fire control chamber which
> stored the round energy balls just before they were shot out.

So he removes it, and lets the tank fill with dozens of charged rounds, each the size of his head,
humming “like that of a vespa hive.” He puts one on his lap. He turns up the Mozart — classical
piano from Earth, which he has recently become obsessed with, and which makes him cry — and drives
for the middle of the formation, toward Hortense’s caravan.

He cannot whistle any more, and his voice “was like rocks being dragged against one another,” but he
sings along.

> AJ Pavrimax: Volteeg, did you blow a tread? You’re off course!
>
> Warlord Hortense Leadership Unit: Weeper Commander! What are you doing? You’re getting too close to
> my caravan!
>
> Weeper Commander Volteeg: I know. That’s the point.

> Volteeg reached down and picked up a blitz stick. He popped it into his mouth, lit it, and said to
> himself, “Show me Mistress Henspar.”

> It would be a long time before someone figured out what had happened. Odds were good nobody would
> even remember Volteeg’s name. That was okay. He didn’t want to be remembered. He didn’t deserve
> love, or admiration.
>
> But, vengeance? Yeah. He was due a little bit of vengeance.
>
> He took a long drag, and then he shoved the lit stick directly into the energy ball sitting on his
> lap.

Carl, a dozen feet underground in a dwarven tunnel at the time, gets only the shockwave and the
notifications:

> System Message: Warlord Hortense Leadership Unit of the Operatic Collective has fallen. Credit for
> the kill has been ruled as either accidental or a fragging incident, which would be funny as shit.
> Investigation is pending.
>
> System Message: The Operatic Collective has been defeated. As this defeat was, well, self-contained,
> the closest ally to their headquarters will receive all their assets.
>
> System Message: All assets of the Operatic Collective have been awarded to the Bone Clan.

Nobody on the Posse side ever learns who did it. Imani reports the enemy retreating with heavy
casualties and asks whether Carl or Louis was responsible; Carl explains that “fragging” means one of
their own. Donut’s verdict stands as the only eulogy Volteeg gets from the protagonists:

> Donut: WELL GOOD FOR THEM, I SAY. IF I HAD TO WORK FOR A TALKING PILE OF DESSERT, I WOULD BLOW THEM
> UP, TOO.

The casualty figure comes from his own status card, not from the battlefield chatter:

> Current status: Died after deliberately filling a Weeper-class tank with explosives and detonating
> it in the center of the Bloc force, killing over 10,000 of his fellow soldiers with a single blast,
> including Warlord Hortense Leadership Unit, thus eliminating Team Two, the Operatic Collective, from
> Faction Wars.

One faction destroyed, one warlord killed, 10,000-plus dead, by a former pet bird nobody was
watching. His name appears nowhere in the book outside his own interlude.

## The Edition-Number Question

Volteeg’s status card is the confirming evidence for the cookbook’s edition numbering:

> Author of the seventh edition of the Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook.

[Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md)’s card, at the end of her own interlude before Chapter 69, reads:

> Author of the ninth Edition of the Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook.

!!! note "A contradiction in the text"
    In Chapter 11, Carl’s narration on first seeing Rosetta on Floor 9 calls her “Rosetta from the
    seventh edition of the cookbook.” That conflicts with both status cards, which are the more
    specific and later evidence: Volteeg is the seventh edition author and Rosetta the ninth.

## Music

He never gets to explain the Mozart to anyone, but he does wonder about it, in the last minutes of
his life:

> He wondered if it was similar to the music Prepotente’s owner, [Miriam Dom](miriam-dom.md), would’ve
> played for the goat before he’d taken his pet biscuit.
