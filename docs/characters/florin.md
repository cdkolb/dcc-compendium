# Florin

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Crocodilian Crawler
    - **Race:** Crocodilian
    - **Weapon:** A modified, magical Mossberg shotgun with unlimited ammo — never shown empty
    - **Rank (Book 7):** **General** in the Princess Posse — the first officer Donut promotes, and the
      man who designs the army's whole rank structure. Commands the **101st Crocodiles** on the
      northern front
    - **Status:** Alive; carries a **golden player-killer mark**, one of only two in the dungeon
      (the other belongs to [Katia](katia.md)). Declines to take a deal at the end of Book 7
    - **Party:** Solo through Books 2–3; joins Katia's new splinter team at the end of Book 4
    - **Notable items (Book 7):** Nine **flame spider tanks** — defensive war machines that eat most
      of the Posse's ten Armor slots; a T'Ghee card depicting a 25-foot **giant caiman**; a
      bullet-holed dictionary from the 1800s

## Books 2–3: The Recurring Survivor

Florin first appears as a recurring recap-show presence in Book 2, teamed with an unnamed human
crawler and ranked 4th on the Floor 3 leaderboard. He remains active and visible on broadcasts
through Book 3, a reliable, capable fighter who never quite breaks into the top tier of attention
Carl and Donut receive.

## Book 4: Losing Ifechi, Finding Hope

Florin's partner through Floor 4 and into Floor 5 is **Ifechi**, a fellow crawler he describes to
Carl as "the bravest, kindest person I'd ever met" — a source of hope through the worst of the
crawl. Ifechi dies before Book 4's endgame (her death referenced only in past tense; Florin never
fully explains the circumstances), and Florin is left refusing to choose a bubble quadrant,
seemingly having given up.

He resurfaces alive during Floor 5's endgame, having independently cleared three of his bubble's
four castles alone before Carl, Elle, and Katia's rescue-gate operation frees him from the
impossible fourth (an air quadrant he couldn't solo). His golden player-killer mark — identical in
color to Katia's — comes from having "killed" Ifechi, implying her death was some kind of
mercy-kill or unavoidable PvP circumstance the text never spells out directly.

Florin tells Carl that hearing, secondhand at the Desperado Club, a story about "this man and his
cat" who stopped everything to help a group of stranded elderly crawlers (the Meadow Lark
evacuation, Book 1) — and who was now planning to use a stolen artifact to save total strangers
trapped in their bubbles — gave him a reason to keep fighting rather than give up entirely. He
thanks Carl directly, in one of the book's more openly emotional scenes, before descending to
Floor 6 alone.

### Joining Katia

At the very end of Book 4, Florin agrees to join **Katia**'s newly formed team — alongside Gwen,
Tran, Louis, Firas, and Bautista — dedicated to protecting Eva Sigrid's scattered former
"daughters" before Eva can pick them off.

## Book 5: Imogen's Face

While recapping who's who for the reader, Carl recalls "the haunted look on Ifechi's face just
before she died on the recap episode, killed by Lucia Mar at the end of the fourth floor" —
Book 5's clearest statement yet of how Ifechi actually died.

Floor 6's country boss, **Queen Imogen** of the High Elves, turns out to be modeled on Ifechi's
twin sister. It's Katia who first identifies this — recalling something Florin had mentioned in
passing when the group met the Popov brothers, that Ifechi had a twin — and warns Florin before he
sees her; Florin confirms it himself once he actually lays eyes on Imogen en route to the
masquerade ("Katia was right. It looks like her, but it ain't. It's her twin."), visibly shaken but
composed. He nearly breaks the party's peace seal once, marching on Lucia Mar (whom he'd earlier
claimed as his own kill-target over her role in Ifechi's death) before Carl talks him down —
though Florin backs off on his own once he senses "something ain't right" with her. During the
climactic battle he fights on the front pressing Queen Imogen, firing his shotgun in coordinated
volleys with the rest of the crawlers, and openly sobs when Prepotente lands the killing blow —
the narration explicitly compares it to how he cried when Ifechi died — then heads down the
stairs alone shortly after.

## Book 6: Uzi Jesus, and a Favor Called In

Florin spends Floor 8 a long way from Carl — **Ecuador** in phase one, **eastern Nigeria** in phase
two — but he turns up in person at the Desperado Club to hand over the single strangest card in the
book.

### HeyZoos

He caught it on a quest in Ecuador. His own account of how:

> "I had a quest to stop him… Turned an entire river into wine because he was thirsty. I walked right
> up to him when he was falling down pissed and gave him a supreme healing potion and said his dad
> wanted him to drink it. He thought I was an angel and drank it right down. He's undead, so it
> dropped him onto his ass, and I stuck him."

He slides it across the dining-room table to Donut:

> **T'Ghee Card. Unique. Totem Card.**
>
> **HeyZoos. Uzi Jesus.**
>
> **"I am the way, motherfucker."**
>
> **Level: 140. Origin: Ecuador.**

A 75-second, 200-constitution undead ranged healer whose notable attacks include Blood into Lead,
Resurrect Totem and Bullet Hell. Florin's warning, and his reason for giving it away: "He really
thinks he's the real deal. He's strong and can cast a lot of great spells. **The trick is getting him
to do any of it.**" And: "Might not be a bad idea with Donut's charisma. Maybe you guys can keep him
in line. If it doesn't work out, you can always rip the card in two… Good luck, mate."

Uzi Jesus goes on to behave like a toddler for Donut and, at one point, to shoot Florin.

### Lucia

His Book 5 friendship with [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md) is still the thing he can't let go of. His
goodbye to Carl before the phases split:

> "Do me a favor. If you get word of Lucia, send me a message. **She has good days and bad days, and
> it's been more bad than good lately.**"

It pays off in a way nobody expects. After the Battle of Beijing, Lucia drops **five Tier-3 Doggie
Door personal-space upgrades** on the ground for Carl's guild before going down the stairs, and tells
Li Jun why: "She said **Florin had messaged her before he'd gone down the stairs and told her to help
us.**" Elle's reaction on hearing about it: "I also wonder if Florin knew about them. I already chewed
him out just in case."

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Floor 9 is Faction Wars, and Faction Wars is an army game. Florin — who has spent five books being
competent and peripheral — turns out to be the one person in the guild who already knows how to run
one, and he spends the whole book as Carl's senior field commander.

### General

Donut makes everyone in the room an officer at once and then hands Florin the org chart. He works the
base menus until he understands them, upgrades his own rank twice, and builds the hierarchy from
scratch:

> Donut had allowed Florin to set it up after giving him the rank War Leader III, which had changed to
> Colonel after he'd spent some time examining the base system. After some more tinkering, and Donut
> bitching at him to upgrade himself, the Colonel changed to simply General.

He also names the sergeant rank "Adjutant" and has to be made to change it, because the game already
has Adjutants. He is the first to grasp that magical airships and vehicles count as limited "Armor"
slots, the first to say the guild needs to know what the other teams have — "It's like a half-sized,
half-crew B-17 had a baby with one of those next-gen stealth bombers" — and the first out the door to
the headquarters. Donut's verdict: "Carl, I feel as if Florin is letting the general thing get to his
head." Carl's: "I was especially glad for Florin, who'd fallen right into the position of general."

It is Florin who calls Carl to the base for the book's biggest reveal — "Mate, I am about to blow your
fucking mind" — where Rosetta, Tipid and the rest of the former crawlers are waiting. Florin is the
one who puts a number on it: "If just over 50,000 former crawlers count as a few, then yeah, mate.
It's just a few." He also leaks the castle's acronym into the group chat within seconds of Carl
working it out, permanently christening the Fortress of Unyielding Power and Authority the **FUPA**
over Donut's furious objections.

His T'Ghee card from Floor 8 turns out to be a 25-foot **giant caiman**, which he summons once a day
and mostly uses as construction equipment — lifting a buried wall section out of the dirt with its
snout and then doing "a happy little hop, reminding me of Mongo."

### The Northern Front

Florin commands the **101st Crocodiles** on the northern border for the entire war. Nine of the
Posse's ten Armor slots are his **flame spider tanks** — "wholly defensive machines," five north and
four south.

Almost everything Carl knows about the northern half of the map comes through Florin's war-chat
reports, which are their own voice: clipped, profane, Australian, full of "mate" and "10-4" and
klicks, and constantly reaching for a real-world comparison.

> Florin: The 103rd and 105th are three klicks into former Operatic territory, but it's slow going.
> Not much infantry resistance, but they've left a lot of non-magical traps we gotta sweep. Most are
> janky, anti-personnel shrapnel mines like the ones we'd see in Borno.

He fights the war competently and grimly. He holds a stalemate against a much larger force and then
falls back — "Bad news from the northern front, mate. I don't think we can sustain this" — personally
leads the charge that kills an enemy tank and jams their armour column into a minefield, and captures
two Operatic **Weepers**:

> Florin: Good news, bad news, Mate. We stopped the armor in its tracks. As you just saw, we even
> managed to capture two of their rolling boom boxes. As long as they got infantry support, they're
> bloody ripper machines. Look like those British Mark V's from the first world war, but with a top
> turret and a single anti-air tube, which was surprising to me. They're called Weepers.

The same message ends with him detaching Katia and Bautista with the remnants of the 105th to keep
Carl's western retreat open, and a warning about the enemy standard-bearers: "You be careful of those
cunts, especially the ones with the banners. They are beasts. Keep your head down, and I'll see you
soon." Carl signs off with a running joke:

> Carl: You too. Oh, and Florin?
>
> Florin: Yeah, mate?
>
> Carl: Are we having fun yet?
>
> Florin: You just keep that castle safe, and I'll give you my answer when we're face-to-face.

During the four-front offensive he draws the orcs and Prince Stalwart of the Skull Empire, fights a
five-metre mechanised scorpion tank that leaps a river into his flank, and is the closest friendly
unit to the mountain when the Semeru dwarves detonate the volcano. Told in advance that the guild
could blow the mountaintop off directly above his position, his entire response was: "Wicked, mate."
He is also caught, along with the whole of the 101st, in the war mages' mass **Witness** spell — Li Na
reports "Florin and the entirety of the 101st are affected" — and ends up in Donut's ball with
everyone else. He signs off the northern campaign with "101st is at mission accomplished."

### The Alligator Mother

The book's running Florin joke is Samantha's, and Florin loses it on a technicality:

> "By the way, do you know what G.I. stands for? Louis says it stands for ground infantry, but Florin
> said it actually means galvanized iron, so of course I had to threaten Florin's alligator mother,
> and he said his mother wasn't even an alligator which got us all talking about Kiwi again."

He also, in passing, turns out to be the man carrying a dictionary from the 1800s in his inventory,
with a bullet hole most of the way through the middle of it. Carl uses it to look up the word
"Atrocity" and never gets around to asking him about the hole.

### Kiwi

The single most unexpected Florin scene in the book is his delivery-room debut. He is the one who
messages the group that **Kiwi**'s water has broken, then stays for the whole thing:

> "Breathe!" Florin cried, holding the hand of the panting Kiwi, who was on her back. Bucket Boy stood
> between her legs, looking ill. Britney was in the room also, rubbing the bear's head with a wet
> cloth.

He congratulates her — "Congratulations, mama." Florin coughed. "They're, uh, amazing." — and then,
once he is safely back in the text chat, says what everyone in the tent was thinking:

> Florin: If we were back home, we'd be drowning them both in the bath right about now.
>
> Imani: Not a word, either of you. Not a word.

### Lucia, and Not Taking a Deal

His Book 6 thread with [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md) closes here. He fights her, survives her nets
("You're lucky Lucia only cast two nets, mate. She's the queen of the triple tap"), and then spends
the aftermath trying to get her out. He is one of three people sitting with her on the ground at the
end while she sobs, trying to talk her into taking a deal, and when that fails he parties with her
himself.

He is very clear that he is not leaving:

> Florin scoffed. "I suspect most will take a deal if they're actually offered. They're tired, mate.
> But you and I both know everything is fucked, and it's possible it's all going to come crashing
> down. If that happens, I want to go down fighting. Not locked up in stasis or whatever. I think
> there's a lot of us who feel that way."

In the same conversation he explains **Jurgen**, the barbarian colonel of the 105th, who will also
never take a deal — his pregnant wife Heidi disappeared into the facility where the dungeon keeps the
children — and lands on the thing that is actually driving him:

> "It's the most important thing one can do, don't you think? Protecting the kids?"

Carl's last word on him: "Florin had talked Lucia Mar into partying with him. He said she'd agreed to
take a deal, but she'd been sketchy. None of us believed she actually would. And as a result, he
would not take one, either."

He also, at some point nobody narrates, steals an aeroplane. Carl mentions it to a grieving Louis in
the last chapter as a consolation prize: "Besides, we have another plane now. Florin stole one, and
it's in the hangar."

## Book 8 Notes

Florin's role continues at a smaller scale on Floor 10 and into Floor 11 — leading one of the five
Parade of Horribles float groups (**Resolution**, which he openly admits nobody put much effort
into: "I still have no clue how we could've made a float based on that"), calling out enemy movements
in real time during the arena fight, and reporting on the fate of others (including Chaco's on-air
mutilation by the possessed Grigori). He's knocked unconscious in the finale's crash and is tended by
[Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md) afterward.
