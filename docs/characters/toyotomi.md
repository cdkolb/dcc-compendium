# Toyotomi

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC mercenary — *“Toyotomi. Level 70 Sai Outpost Guard Mercenary Leader.”* /
      *“Sergeant in the Princess Posse.”* Inherited, contract and all, when Carl’s team defeated the
      [Prism Kingdom](../factions/prism-kingdom.md)
    - **Race:** [Sai](../species/sai.md) — bipedal rhinoceros, seven feet and change, samurai-styled,
      complete with a samurai helmet
    - **Class:** None separately printed. His examine line gives his profession as **Outpost Guard
      Mercenary Leader**, a fortification class
    - **Subclass:** None printed
    - **Level:** 70
    - **Role:** Fortification engineer for the Princess Posse; leads Li Na’s builder squad and
      spearheads construction of the **Nest** tower
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** ***Edifice Shield*** — doubles a fortification’s strength; it only
      works on outposts, not on primary palaces, which is the whole of his tragedy
    - **Notable items:** None named beyond his samurai helmet and his boots, one of which Rend eats
      the first time they meet
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — chapter 70, cleaved in half by a spell while holding the ground-floor
      entrance of the Nest against [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md). “Toyotomi was dead-dead.”

## Overview

Carl acquires Toyotomi by accident. Having stormed the former Prism castle and killed Empress
D’Nadia, the Posse inherits her hired help — and the first Carl sees of his new sergeant is Rend
trying to swallow him:

> “Get this thing off of me,” came the shout, interrupting us. We stepped out to see Rend attempting
> to swallow the foot of the struggling, still duct-taped Sai mercenary.

> “He ate my boot,” Toyotomi said as he ripped the duct tape off his ankles.

The examine text is one of the more pointed the AI writes in Book 7, because it lands the
responsibility on Carl personally — twice:

> Toyotomi was once an honorable, experienced leader of Sai. He has since lost his way. His entire
> squad was killed during an unfortunate incident at the Desperado Club that you may or may not have
> been partially responsible for. During the club’s reorganization, the few Sai survivors were
> relegated to the mercenary markets, where he was quickly snatched up.
>
> What stands before you is a broken creature. His utter failure to protect his friends against the
> demon haunts him. He refuses to lead men now, despite his great talent. He was just starting to gain
> his confidence back when a guy in his underwear and a talking cat walked in, trounced him, tied him
> up, and slaughtered the person he was sworn and honor bound to protect. I’m sure that’ll do great
> things for his self-esteem.

The demon is Minge, loosed during the destruction of the Desperado Club in Book 6 — the operation
Carl ran to kill Astrid. The sai guards died by the dozens in that fight, and Toyotomi is what is
left of them.

## “I Will Accept My Exile”

Offered a choice between fighting on the northern border and defending the Posse castle, Toyotomi
chooses neither, because his magic will not reach:

> “I was hired to help protect this castle, but my magic is for outposts, not primary palaces. If
> your castle is bigger than this place, I won’t be able to cast my Edifice Shield spell. Therefore,
> I am useless to you. Leave me here, and I will defend this building until I die. It is what I want.”

Pressed on whether he had a shield over the Prism castle at all:

> He hung his head. “This castle is too big for me, but Warlord D’Nadia insisted. She had me keep a
> shield going anyway, and it tired me, so I could only do it in spurts. I was resting when you
> arrived, which is how you got the jump on me, I am shamed.”

Carl declines to leave him to die and reassigns him to **Shanty Town** instead. Toyotomi takes it in
the spirit he takes everything: “I will accept my exile.”

The exile is the making of him. It is the first time in two books that anyone puts a sai fortification
specialist in front of buildings the right size for his magic.

## Building the Nest

By chapter 47 he is directing the erection of a clocktower in Shanty Town and is unrecognisable from
the broken creature in the examine text:

> The bipedal rhinoceros Sergeant Toyotomi was also there, pointing up at the top of the tower. The
> large, sai mercenary was talking animatedly. The former Prism NPC looked to be in his element as he
> directed the last-minute construction.

His real job comes next: **the Nest**, the tall, absurdly expensive synergy tower built at the lip of
the hole down into **Larracos**, from which [Princess Donut](princess-donut.md) is meant to see and
shoot the entire battlefield. Toyotomi leads Li Na’s builder squad on it, and it goes up in under two
days — a project Carl reckons would have taken months and a hundred workers and a lot of cranes in the
real world.

Chapter 56 is Toyotomi at the peak of his competence, being badgered from two directions at once.
Donut wants the tower repainted:

> “It is already built,” Toyotomi said. “It is the color of granite because it is made of granite. If
> we paint it now, we won’t have time for the mages to coat it with their protection spells. Plus,
> assuming the build as a whole is considered master quality, it will glow gold the moment it is done.”

Carl wants reassurance about the numbers and keeps getting the same answer:

> “I said there will be at least 20 on each block, which is true. Fifteen is the maximum for the
> individual blocks, but several of the protections we’re using bleed to nearby blocks, and we will be
> creating a net pattern,” Toyotomi said. “And as soon as the men up there place the last block and the
> tower is complete, the bonus will activate and the strength will increase to 200%. And after that, I
> will cast Edifice Shield which doubles that. With Princess Donut’s Sentry and Flak spells, most
> incoming fire should get deflected.”

And on Carl’s third repetition of the tunnelling question:

> The large rhino appeared as if he wanted to strangle me. This was the third time I’d asked him a
> version of this question, and his answer hadn’t changed. “As far as we’re aware, the Larracos army
> owns the subterranean passages. But this tower will be so encased in protections, it will float if
> the ground underneath it disappears. It is more protected than the FUPA itself.”

He is right about all of it. The Nest holds under sustained bombardment from the Skull Empire’s war
mages, and its integrity is still at 74% when Carl reaches it in the final assault.

## Death

Toyotomi’s last act is the one he asked for in chapter 28. During the Peeling of Larracos he is on the
ground floor of the Nest with Bomo, Sergeant Bret S and a group of mages, guarding Donut, who is
trapped at the top by a net thrown over the tower. Lucia Mar — hunting Donut, with her dog Gus — walks
up to the door.

The exchange comes to Carl as three messages in a chat scroll he almost misses, each one followed by
the system’s own epitaph:

> Bomo: Someone trying to come into the tower.
>
> Toyotomi: I am securing the front entrance now.

> Toyotomi: No. It is a crawler. A woman with a dog. She’s coming in.
>
> This message is from a deceased mercenary.
>
> Sergeant Bret S: We need backup! Backup!
>
> This message is from a deceased crawler.
>
> Bomo: Will stop her now.
>
> This message is from a deceased mercenary.

Those messages are what send Carl over the railing of Larracos on a levitation potion, dumping an
entire inventory folder of bombs behind him. When he finally gets inside the tower a chapter later:

> I spared a quick, sad glance for Toyotomi, Bomo, and the defending mages, all of whom were cleaved
> right in half with a spell.
>
> Toyotomi was dead-dead. Bomo would supposedly regenerate on the 10th floor, but we no longer had his
> contract, and I didn’t know if I’d ever see him again.

He held the door of a building he had built himself, against a crawler far above his level, for
however long it took her to cast once. “Dead-dead” is Carl’s shorthand for an NPC who does not come
back.
