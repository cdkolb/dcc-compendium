# Ferdinand

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Race:** Cat
    - **Type:** Boss-tier familiar → Donut's bonded pet/party member
    - **Class/Subclass:** Not applicable — a pet-class mob, not a crawler
    - **Level:** 100 — a **Province Boss** as "Sir Ferdinand," familiar of Queen Imogen of the
      High Elves
    - **Role (Book 7):** Co-warlord of **Team Retribution**, the NPC faction, alongside
      [Juice Box](juice-box.md) — added because the system would not give a dungeon-born changeling
      access to the warlord menu. Also the former **Mayor of Shanty Town**, until he trades the office
      away for a hat
    - **Status:** Alive; joins [Princess Donut](princess-donut.md)'s party at the book's climax.
      Still alive at the end of Book 7 — he leaves Floor 9 for the **12th floor** with Juice Box
      rather than continuing the crawl
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None stated in Book 7 — he holds a warlord's menu and a lightning
      bolt he once fired at Mongo, and otherwise fights not at all
    - **Notable items:** A **magic turban** (drained of magic by Book 7) and a **black, weathered
      bowler hat** taken from Quan's inventory — thrown, it decapitates a target and boomerangs back
      to the owner (Book 7)
    - **Notable relationship:** Donut's pre-apocalypse "first love" and long-running Book 1-5 mystery

## Overview

Ferdinand is a running mystery since Book 1 — Donut repeatedly mentions "Ferdinand" as someone
she loves, with Carl (and the reader) never getting an explanation, until Book 2's on-air reveal
that "Ferdinand" was actually Gravy Boat, an ordinary neighborhood tomcat back on Earth. Book 5
resolves the mystery a second time: Gravy Boat was apparently swept up in the same harvest that
took every other Earth creature and has been trapped in the dungeon this whole time, transformed
into **Sir Ferdinand**, a Level 100 Province Boss serving as the personal familiar of **Queen
Imogen** of the High Elves — his memory altered, personality twisted into a cruel,
self-aggrandizing caricature of a pampered royal pet. (Empress D'Nadia, who's followed the "Gravy
Boat" saga since Book 2, confirms the identity in passing at the masquerade, mockingly addressing
Ferdinand as "Gravy Boat" when he jumps on the judges' table.)

## The Reveal

Donut first spots him mid-book and is devastated: "It was Ferdinand. I saw him. It was him. They
brought Ferdinand down here and changed his memory and turned him into a jerk. He tried to kill
Mongo." She initially insists she's moved on ("I am a mother now... if anything, I see it as a
good thing... it'll make for a good song"), but the emotional stakes clearly aren't resolved.

His in-game monster card reveals the High Elves' pattern of exploiting non-elf pets before
eventually turning on them, and notes he survives specifically because Queen Imogen is fond of
him — "in the same way a surgeon is fond of their favorite scalpel." He's shown to have quietly
carved out small pockets of unauthorized magical influence over the castle itself, consistent
with an intelligent creature the elves haven't yet decided to purge.

## The Rescue

During the climactic infiltration of the High Elf castle (staged as a televised talent-show
event), Carl's group's plan hinges on a **Charm Animal potion** — brewed by Mordecai from a full
liter of Donut's own blood, since Ferdinand is the same species as her — combined with an
artificially boosted Charisma (a base 138 doubled to 276 by a benefactor-box tiara, over 300 with
further buffs) to out-charm Ferdinand's own stats (constitution + charisma of 175) by the 100-point
margin required to charm a province boss, flipping his loyalty back to her mid-performance. Donut
drinks the potion on stage; it works:

> "Sir Ferdinand, minion of Princess Donut, has joined the party."

Ferdinand actively fights alongside the group in the final battle against Queen Imogen,
slamming into her head mid-fight and taking a brutal backhand hit that sends him flying before he
disappears — possibly via his own "phase jump" ability, though the text doesn't confirm the
mechanism. He survives the battle and is confirmed alive via the party status list, though
unreachable by message — having already been swept down to Floor 9 along with the rest of the
collapsing castle the moment the Sledge casts *Zerzura* on it. Per Mordecai, Ferdinand will simply
drop out of the party roster once Carl and Donut themselves progress past Floor 6 — they're headed
to Floor 7 next, several floors behind him.

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Ferdinand arrives on Floor 9 with the rest of the swept-down elf castle and turns out to be a head of
state. Team Retribution — the NPC faction defending the city of **Larracos** — is run by
[Juice Box](juice-box.md), but the system will not let a dungeon-born changeling into the warlord
interface, so it installed the cat next to her. Baroness Victory, explaining a later case: “Agatha is
co-warlord, similar to what happened with Juice Box and Ferdinand. Ferdinand was added because the system
didn’t allow Juice Box access to the warlord menu.”

He is a coward, a lech and almost entirely useless, and the book keeps giving him things to do anyway.

### Trading a Town for a Hat

Chapter 10. Ferdinand wants two things back: his rhinoceros mount **Simoom**, confiscated by the high elf
stablemaster Kibben, and his magic turban, which Donut has been quietly holding since the Butcher’s
Masquerade. Carl offers him a substitute — a black, weathered bowler hat looted from Quan, which decapitates
whatever you throw it at and boomerangs home.

> I pulled out the black, weathered bowler hat. It’d been in Quan’s inventory. One could throw it, have it
> decapitate an enemy, and it’d boomerang back to the owner. Both Donut and Ferdinand gasped at the same
> time when they saw it.

Carl names his price:

> Ferdinand reached a paw toward the hat, but I pulled it back. “I want to be mayor of that town. Shanty
> Town. You’ll have to transfer your mayorship to us, which means that jewel on your necklace will go away.
> You’ll get this hat, and we’ll trade it back for your turban in a day or so. We’ll try to get your rhino
> back if we still have it. If not, we’ll figure something out. We’re allies.”

The trade goes through while the cat is still admiring himself in Britney’s hand mirror and not listening.

> “It won’t let me transfer the town without you giving us some money,” Ferdinand said.
>
> “That’s okay,” I said. “We have gold.”
>
> But the cat wasn’t even listening to me. He adjusted the hat. “I’m a very handsome boy.”

> Trade Request has been accepted.
>
> Your Truce with Team Retribution has been renamed to “The Good Guys.”

Donut is not impressed: “Still, I can’t believe that stupid cat gave it all away just for a temporary hat.
He’s not even charmed anymore, and it still worked.” He never gives the hat back. At the end of the book he
has his drained turban in inventory and is still wearing the bowler, because “Donut had allowed him to keep
it.”

### The Live-Feed Blackout

With the off-world teams caught feeding real-time intelligence to their warlords, Ferdinand burns one of Team
Retribution’s three Emergency Action Items — reading out, more or less, what Juice Box told him to say:

> Warlord Message. Sir Ferdinand of Team Retribution has proposed an emergency action item. “Because them
> alien cunts on other floors are being little bitches and cheating, we propose that nobody in the dungeon,
> including those pricks partying it up in that centipede’s asshole, be allowed to watch any outside feeds
> for the remainder of this little game.”
>
> This action item has been approved. Effective immediately, everyone within the dungeon is subjected to an
> immediate, live-feed blackout.

It becomes the single most consequential rule change of the ceasefire. Carl, who had not wanted to spend one
of his own items on it: “That was fast. I hadn’t wanted to waste any of our own action items on this, but I
was glad it had worked.” The teams immediately route around it by renaming their own news programmes in the
feed directory, which forces Carl to spend an action item of his own at the warlord council to close the
loophole.

He burns a second item later, at Juice Box’s prompting, asking that summoned gods not have to leave the floor
right away. That request is split: the first half is denied, and the half that passes is the one that makes
Ascendency players pilot their own deities in person — the rule that eventually gets Huanxin Jinx killed. His
delivery is characteristic: “Uh, Juice Box told me to say a bunch of other stuff too but now I can’t remember
it all.”

### At the Warlord Council

He spends Chapter 42 on the table, sniffing the trail mix and complaining about the snacks. He and Donut bet
a gold piece on whether King Stalwart would show up actually wearing his crown, and both collapse laughing
when he does — “He really does look stupid in that thing.” He backs Carl’s outburst about outside information
without understanding a word of it: “Here, here! I don’t know what the hell anyone is talking about, but I
agree with Carl!” His verdict on his own co-warlord’s apocalyptic prophecy, delivered from Carl’s shoulder to
a silent room: “Wow. That chick is intense.”

Chaco greets him as “Gravy Boat.” He does not take it well.

### The Tower

His one moment of genuine courage comes when [Lucia Mar](lucia-mar.md) traps Donut at the top of the Nest
tower. Carl is pinned in a ditch under a gale-strike tower that has locked onto him; Ferdinand jumps in beside
him, tail scorched, shaking:

> “Why are you lying down on the job?” Ferdinand demanded. The orange cat was shaking with fear, and the end
> of his tail was scorched, like he’d barely missed getting skewered by a fire lance. His bowler hat sat
> cockeyed on his head. “Kitten is in trouble!”

> “We gotta save her,” Ferdinand said, now having to shout.

Carl sends him back — “You go into that tower with me, you’re toast” — and admits the cat steadied him:
“Somehow Ferdinand’s bravery, despite his overwhelming fear, eased my own.” The exchange also gets the closest
thing to a straight answer out of him about where he fits:

> “Trust me on this, dude. You’re on her team because you got a raw deal, but you’re like me and Donut.
> You’re from Earth. I want you alive. And so does Donut.”
>
> “Really? You really think so? Because Kitten is still a little saucy about that time I shot a lightning bolt
> at Mungo.”

### Leaving for the 12th

Team Retribution’s negotiated exit is that its leadership transfers off the floor rather than dies. “Ferdinand
would transfer to the 12th. Juice Box would, too. And with their transfer would come the defeat of Team
Retribution and the victory for the Princess Posse.”

His goodbye, delivered from the adjutant Drick’s head with four minutes left on the clock:

> “Just wanted to say ‘Yo’ before I moseyed on down to the 12th floor to bang some hot goddess chicks.”

> “Oh, and Ferdinand?” Donut said.
>
> The orange cat turned around. He gave a muffled response.
>
> “Thanks for trying to come save me in the tower.”
>
> He spit the hat back out. “Anytime babe.” He picked it up and strolled away.

> System Message: Both Warlords Ferdinand and Juice Box have left the realm. Under the current ruleset, they
> have abandoned their posts and have forfeited their position.
>
> System Message: Team Retribution has been defeated.
>
> System Message: The Princess Posse has won Faction Wars.
