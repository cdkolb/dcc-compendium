## Background

Before the collapse, Donut was a champion show cat owned by Carl's ex-girlfriend Beatrice, with a room in their apartment devoted to her competition trophies and ribbons. She was with Carl (not Beatrice) when the dungeon opened because Beatrice was traveling and Carl had been asked to cat-sit. Donut initially enters the dungeon classified as a **pet**, not a full crawler — a status that changes when Carl force-feeds her an Enhanced Pet Biscuit from a Legendary Pet Box.

## The Transformation

The enhanced biscuit dissolves Donut into a puddle of goo for several minutes before she reforms — physically almost unchanged, but now able to speak and reason at a human level. Her stats before the transformation were all 1 (except Dexterity at 8, Charisma at 5); the biscuit boosted four random stats and applied the permanent Enhanced Growth buff. Post-transformation stats climb fast — by the tuskling "Ball of Swine" fight she's already at Strength 18, Intelligence in the 20s, and a Charisma so high (37–43 through most of Book 1) that she can casually charm sapient mobs (goblins, fairies) into compliance, provided their aggro is already neutral.

Once she can speak, Donut immediately renames the party **The Royal Court of Princess Donut**, appoints herself **Grand Champion Best in Dungeon**, and demotes Carl from "manservant" to **Royal Bodyguard**.

## Personality

Vain, status-obsessed, and initially dismissive of Carl, Donut nonetheless refuses to abandon him when he tries to split up early on, admitting she doesn't want to be alone and that she'd noticed him quietly looking at apartments that allowed cats (i.e., planning to keep her) before the world ended. Over Book 1 she becomes a genuinely loyal partner — dragging Carl to safety twice after near-fatal injuries — while never losing the diva affect, especially once cameras get involved.

She develops a visible addiction to audience attention after her first talk-show appearance (on *Dungeon Crawler After Hours with Odette*), obsessively tracking her Views/Followers/Favorites counts, to the point Carl has to repeatedly talk her down from playing for the camera during actual combat.

## Combat Role

Donut is the party's ranged/magic damage dealer, though she's reluctant to use her claws (calling
melee "inelegant") until circumstances force her to (see Quick Facts above for her spell/ability
list). Her physical stats eventually let her one-shot low-level mobs with her claws, but her
Constitution stays crippingly low (as low as 2, raised to 4 with gear) because Enhanced Growth
never lets her allocate points there — making her fast and hard-hitting but fragile.

The **Crown of the Sepsis Whore** (see Quick Facts) is worth calling out specifically: a
Legendary, cursed tiara she puts on without reading the full warning. It's a *Fleeting* + *Unique*
item that permanently enrolls her in the royal line of succession for the **Blood Sultanate** on
Floor 9, meaning she (and by extension Carl) will eventually be required to kill the Sultan and
the entire royal family to descend past that floor.

## Book 2: Race and Class

On Floor 3, Donut refuses every race option and stays **Cat** — but confirming the choice (rather
than leaving it a non-choice) unlocks new racial benefits: **Night Vision**, **Slash Attack**,
**Cat-like Reflexes**, and **9 Lives** (halves damage from her first nine physical hits per day).

For class, she's shown only 34 options (the fewest Mordecai has ever seen, due to her Desperado
Pass and her Crown) and picks **Former Child Actor** — a rare Charisma/Chance Earth Class built
around the **Character Actor** skill: each floor, she re-rolls a temporary "specialty" class for
that floor only, with the AI randomly determining how many of that specialty's skills/spells she
actually receives. Her hidden **Manager** benefit (see [Carl](carl.md) for how it was obtained)
makes Mordecai personally accompany the party instead of being guildhall-bound.

## Book 6 Notable Events

Floor 8 is Donut's most competent book. She starts it at **level 56/57** and ends it at **63**, and
she spends the whole of it as squad leader — which on this floor means **deckmaster**, the only
person who can hold, draw, play or flee with T'Ghee cards. Carl is legally a minion for the duration
of every card battle.

### New class: Master Telephone Psychic

Her Character Actor floor specialty. Mordecai offers her a choice between **Agony Urchin** (safer)
and **Master Telephone Psychic** (better mapping, offensive charm spells); she refuses the Urchin on
sight because it would change how she looks — "I HAVE AN IMPORTANT IMAGE TO MAINTAIN."

What the psionic kit gives her:

- Enormously improved mapping — "IT'S ALMOST LIKE WE ALREADY HAVE THE MAP FROM A NEIGHBORHOOD BOSS…
  IT'S EVEN BETTER THAN THAT PATHFINDER SKILL"
- **+5 Intelligence**
- [Brain Freeze](../spells/brain-freeze.md) — single-target, drains health and lowers resistances,
  and blinds at level 10 or above. It starts at level 8.
- [Bad Attitude](../spells/bad-attitude.md) — per Mordecai, "similar to Carl's *Fear* spell, but it'll
  be a lot more potent in your hands… *Fear* tends to affect dexterity while *Bad Attitude* drains
  intelligence and lowers resistances"
- [Why Are You Hitting Yourself?](../spells/why-are-you-hitting-yourself.md) — "IT MAKES PEOPLE STAB
  THEMSELVES IN THE FACE!" She eventually lands it on a level-250 demon prince: **I CAST *WHY ARE YOU
  HITTING YOURSELF* ON SATAN!**

The class grants **no fire spells** — her fire magic (Fireball, Magic Missile, Wall of Fire) is all
gear-granted and is separately amplified by her new nipple ring. She also keeps her Viper Queen venom
spit (level 3) and her Bard songs, minus a couple she's forgotten.

!!! note "No system text"
    Master Telephone Psychic has no printed class-description block, and none of the three spells has
    a printed spell block. Everything known about them comes from dialogue.

### Deckmaster

Donut builds an eighteen-card deck off crowdsourced chat math (roughly an 80% chance of pulling a
totem in the opening spread) and learns to run it well. Her final active deck is six totems — **Shi
Maria**, **Jola** the Yule Cat (a gift from Katia), **Lazarus A-Bang-Bang**, **Skylar Spinach**,
**Geraldo** the monk seal and **Raul** the crab — plus utility, snare and mystic cards.

The real skill turns out to be personnel management: refereeing Raul against Geraldo, shouting the
orisha Asojano into line, coaching Raul out of grovelling, and negotiating with Shi Maria, who demands
to see her hand and offers tactical advice. Carl deliberately stays out of it — "I was trying not to
be a backseat deckmaster. When the real fights started, I wouldn't be able to sit there and help
her."

Her best plays include stealing a summon out of an enemy deckmaster's hand mid-cast with a **Thief**
card, and permanently fusing Lazarus with Skylar Spinach using a **Golden Combo** followed by the
**Glow-Up** consumable Tserendolgor gave them — building the best possible single card, since only
one may be carried down to Floor 9.

She does not enjoy it. "I don't like being in charge of the deck. You're usually in charge of the
important stuff."

### The Golden Skull

Carl brings **Quan Ch** down, but Quan dies a beat before the finishing blow lands and the system
credits the kill — plus the bounty and both exit keys — to Donut. She gets her first **golden
player-killer skull** for it.

> Donut sported a new, golden player killer skull. She hadn't commented on it, and neither did I.
> That felt important, the nonchalance of it all. **We were losing more of ourselves every day.**

### Losing D'Nadia, Gaining the Garbage Truck People

Mid-card-battle, Donut gets a notification that **Empress D'Nadia** — now warlord of the Prism
Kingdom Clan, and therefore an opposing Faction Wars faction — has sold her sponsorship contract to
someone else.

> "I'm not a trading card to be collected and discarded, Carl." … "Carl, I thought she liked me."

Carl's read is that it was partly conflict of interest and mostly money: "Selling Donut's sponsorship
had to have made her a lot of money. I'd actually been expecting this for a while now." D'Nadia
subsequently files emergency action items to delete the Princess Posse from Faction Wars, and to
delete Carl and Donut.

The replacement sponsor is **Long Haul Biological Waste Management Solutions**.

> **MY NEW SPONSOR IS NAMED LONG HAUL BIOLOGICAL WASTE MANAGEMENT SOLUTIONS, AND I JUST CAN'T WITH
> THAT. I KNOW I'M SUPPOSED TO APPEAR GRATEFUL, BUT THAT SOUNDS ABSOLUTELY DISGUSTING. CARL, ARE
> THEY GOING TO SHOW COMMERCIALS FOR GARBAGE TRUCKS ON MY FEED? THIS IS NOT ACCEPTABLE.**

Long Haul are the galaxy's corpse-disposal contractors — they normally don't arrive until after the
last crawler has died. The epilogue reveals what the sponsorship actually is: their freighter
*Homecoming Queen*, parked in Earth orbit, is crewed by [Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md) and Tipid
and loaded with **fifty thousand former crawlers** who have just been cleared to enter the dungeon as
troopers for the Princess Posse.

In the last minute before the floor collapses, she gets her **first celestial box** from them. It is
still unopened when the book ends.

> "It's a celestial box! My first celestial box, and it's from the garbage truck company? The posse
> is going to be scandalized."

### Ripping Shi Maria's Card

After Shi Maria hijacks Carl's body, burns Mongo's wing off and tattoos herself into Carl's chest,
Donut ends the argument the moment the battle does:

> "I ripped her card," Donut said after a moment. "Shi Maria. As soon as the battle ended, I ripped
> it. **She's not going to hurt Mongo ever again.**"

It doesn't work the way she hoped. The escort quest to deliver Shi Maria to Larracos survives the
rip, and so does Shi Maria — in Carl. Donut also holds, and at Carl's insistence rips,
**[Paz Lo](paz-lo.md)**'s card. She does not get a skull for that one.

- **New tiara:** the **Enchanted Tiara of the Inebriated Dragonfly** — a blank silver tiara plus a
  25,000-gold flawless citrine — granting a 50% potion cooldown reduction, +10 Intelligence, poison
  protection, negation of the inebriation dexterity penalty, and a level-10 ***Hover*** skill. "I can
  fly now. Carl, I can fly! I'm like Wonder Woman!" / "Wonder Woman can't fly." / "She has an
  invisible jet, Carl. Don't ruin this for me." She never gets to use *Hover* in a real fight.
- **New nipple ring:** the **Enchanted Nipple Ring of the Superior Fire Demon's Hand Maiden** —
  cursed-item removal, immunity to natural fire and lava, no health loss inside Sheol, fire spells
  scaling with Intelligence, and a once-daily berserking spell, [*Bitch, What?*](../spells/bitch-what.md), which Mordecai
  tells her to only ever cast alone.
- **New spells:** *Legionnaires of the Damned* and *Mute*, in addition to the psychic kit.
- **Mongo** reaches level 40, is tripled by *Clockwork Triplicate* in almost every fight, and has his
  right wing burned off by Carl's body under Shi Maria's control. A potion Carl upgraded will
  regrow it.
- She hears Carl's whole childhood for the first time on *Shadow Boxer*, and the most striking thing
  she does with it is decide not to push.

## Book 7 Notable Events

Floor 9 — **Faction Wars**, fought over the funnel city of **Larracos** — is Donut's book. She enters
it as Co-Warlord of the Princess Posse and the party's ranged damage dealer, and leaves it as the
highest-levelled crawler in the dungeon, holding two classes, a goddess's title, and the only spell in
the game the system files under **Atrocity**. It costs her more than any previous floor, and the book
is unusually direct about the cost.

!!! note "Level endpoints"
    She finished Book 6 at 63. The text's first Book 7 figure is **65**, the level at which she climbs
    into the Nest tower around Chapter 70. Hours of sniping take her to **74** by Chapter 74. Then her
    third Atrocity, in Chapter 76, jumps her straight to **135** — "Her level had grown to 135." That
    is the last level stated for her in the book, and Mordecai's read is blunt: "I think you're now
    the most powerful crawler in the history of this game."

### New class: Deathbed Hellcat

Her Character Actor floor specialty for Floor 9, picked off-page while Carl is distracted. It turns
her from an artillery piece into a sniper:

> "Great, isn't it? It's a long-range sniper and assassin class. It doesn't come with many new spells,
> but the range on all my existing spells is quadrupled, and my dexterity is much higher. The odds of
> one of my spells inflicting an instakill is higher, too! Oh, oh, I can go invisible at will, and to
> top it off, I kept most of my spells from the last floor! But best of all, it comes with a hat! And
> a ghoulie suit! I don't know what that means, but I see a saferoom around the corner, and I must
> insist we get there straight away so I can collect my accessories. But isn't the name of the class
> absolute perfection? It's downright terrifying, and I love it."

Carl has to break the news that it is a *ghillie* suit, which she refuses to wear on sight — "It's
like one of Signet's tattoo people fell through a paper shredder. If I put that on, I'll look like a
tassel. You know how I feel about tassels, Carl." The kit is a cat-sized colour-changing ghillie suit
(jacket slot; **Blend into the Background**, **Cone of Silence**) and a boonie hat (**Targeting**, plus
further range). Worn together the bonuses compound, and Carl notes she could "snipe line-of-sight
enemies from well over a mile away."

!!! note "No system class block"
    Like Master Telephone Psychic before it, Deathbed Hellcat never gets a printed class description.
    Everything above is Donut's own dialogue and Carl's examination of the gear.

### The Tiara of a Thousand Lights

Her first celestial benefactor box from **Long Haul Biological Waste Management Solutions** — opened
in Chapter 4, carried over unopened from the end of Book 6 — delivers a spaceship that folds into a
mechanical hand holding a pillow and a tiara.

> The Tiara of a Thousand Lights and Accompanying Charge Pillow.
>
> This particular tiara is imbued with 99 Absorption Jewels.
>
> The tiara itself has no inherent magical properties, and in its base state it gives no additional
> benefits or buffs except plus 10 to Charisma. However, it has the ability to be permanently charged
> with up to 99 different buff items.

Any enchanted hat left on the charge pillow for ten hours is permanently drained into the tiara — the
hat survives, stripped, for her collection. The system's own assessment: "With some careful planning,
one can turn this tiara into one of the most powerful items in the history of Dungeon Crawler World."
The first thing Mordecai has her drain into it is the Deathbed Hellcat boonie hat, which is what makes
the rest of the floor possible: with the tiara's targeting she no longer needs a sniper tower at all.

### The Nest, and sniping a city

Because the tiara covers the targeting, the Posse's great tower — **the Nest**, built on the lip of
Shanty Town — is instead tuned to synergise with **Atrocity**-class spells, amplifying their area of
effect across the whole battlefield. Donut works out of the top of it for the back half of the book,
invisible in the ghillie suit, dropping war mages and defence towers with *Magic Missile* from across
the funnel.

It is also where the book stops treating her competence as a joke. Carl finds her mid-shift:

> Only then did Donut look up, and I could see her face. I tried not to react. I could see the
> hollowness to her eyes, as if they were trying to focus on something so far away, she couldn't quite
> see it. It'd only been a few hours, but the change was stark.
>
> She'd been level 65 when she'd gone up into this tower. She was now level 74.
>
> We gain things, and we lose them.

### Three Atrocities

Over the course of Faction Wars Donut casts three spells the system classifies as Atrocities. None of
them are hers by choice; each one is the least-bad option on the table at the time. The mechanics
belong on the individual spell pages — what follows is only when and why.

**Rolling Battle Formation** (Chapter 65) — with Louis shot down, a feral god loose and thousands of
allied and enemy bodies fused into a single writhing pile outside the Madness castle, Donut casts it
to roll the entire mass away from the god fight and onto a field of corpses. It sweeps up more than she
intended, including the feral god **Meatus** and the goddess **Eris**, and holds every creature inside
immobile and invulnerable until it breaks. Carl later tells her flatly that it was the right call:
"You saved them by casting the Battle Formation spell. If you hadn't done it, half of everyone would be
dead by now."

> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has committed an atrocity.
>
> Donut: WELL, I NEVER. THAT WAS VERY RUDE.

**You're Not Done Yet** (Chapter 69) — a Legendary quest-box spellbook from early in the floor, which
Mordecai recognises with genuine fear: "It's similar to Donut's Second Chance, but it's a lot more
insidious." Tower-enhanced and cast over a field of thousands of donated corpses, it flays them into
**fleshers** that sail across the battlefield at the Madness army. The AI's commentary elsewhere notes
that war mages exist at all as "a result of too many combatants having access to You're Not Done Yet."

> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has committed another Atrocity. Wow. What a naughty kitty. I
> sure hope she isn't forced to cast a third.

**War Crime** (Chapter 76) — from the Legendary 1914 box awarded for killing Empress D'Nadia. It can
only be cast three times ever, only on Scolopendra levels, and it combines two of the caster's own
spells into something "most governments would consider unconscionable"; it also drops the caster to 1%
health and cannot itself kill, so a finisher is mandatory. Carl reads the full description early and is
horrified, mostly at what casting it might do to *her*. The plan is to use it to separate out the
Reaver and Dream armies as the battle-formation ball breaks. Rosetta's case is that without it "we will
lose hundreds, if not thousands," and Carl agrees. The combination she fires is **Bijanbi** and
**Astral Paw** — a kilometre-wide ethereal cat's claw that catches thousands of soldiers and a god and
crushes them into a flesher swarm over Shanty Town, to a world-wide soundtrack of Limp Bizkit's "Break
Stuff."

She casts it under protest and mid-panic, with Carl shouting revised instructions at her:

> "Okay, okay," I said. "Cast it, but don't pick fire! Pick something else! We need the Nest to stay
> intact!"
>
> "What? What should I pick!?"

Afterwards she goes rigid, her health draining, and asks: "Did it work... Carl, I don't feel good. I
have a new class. I have a bunch of new spells. What's happening?"

### The Oak Fell, and Champion of Nekhebit

The third Atrocity triggers the rest of it, all at once:

> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has committed a third Atrocity.
>
> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has received a new class.
>
> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut will now be referred to as The Oak Fell.
>
> System Message: The Oak Fell has been named Champion of Nekhebit.

**The Oak Fell** stacks on top of Former Child Actor rather than replacing it, and Mordecai and Rosetta
both say they have never seen a class awarded that way. Its spells are deity spells and read as druidic
— **Raise Forest** (trees and plants over a square kilometre per spell level), one that turns water to
salt water, and **Gather Ye, Vermin**, which draws every small mammal within ten kilometres to her.
Donut is scandalised by the last one and blames the AI.

**Champion of Nekhebit** is the goddess **Odette** is currently driving. It is presented as a reward and
functions as a leash. Donut's own summary:

> "This new Oak Fell class and this champion thing are in addition to my original class. I don't even
> worship Nekhebit, and it says I don't have to if I don't want to, but I'm not allowed to worship
> anyone else who's not in her 'court.' Both the class and the title come with a bunch of weird
> spells. ..."

Rosetta gives Carl the version she takes care Donut does not hear:

> "It's similar to worshiping the deity. Enemies of Nekhebit will receive a bounty if they kill her."
> She'd lowered her voice, making certain Donut wasn't in earshot. "Honestly, it's not too unlike
> [Conscription](../spells/conscript.md), but with a lot more autonomy for Donut. Should the goddess
> want her to do something, she can order her to do it or face a smite."

**Milk**'s theory is that the class is "a side effect of some posturing that's currently happening on
the 12th," and that the Champion title in particular "was something that was set in motion by
Nekhebit." Rosetta guesses at the mechanism: "Probably something along the lines, 'the first crawler
to cast three atrocities on the next floor gets the title and class.'" Carl's reaction is two words:
*Fucking Odette*. Donut's objection is administrative: she spends the next
several chapters in warlord chat as **The Champion of Nekhebit**, unable to change her display name
back — "ALSO HOW CAN I CHANGE MY NAME BACK TO WARLORD DONUT? IT DOESN'T DO THIS IN THE REGULAR CHAT."

### Breaking the stat ceiling

The same moment hands her four benefit choices at once, and she takes them all in a single burst of
chat while Mordecai tries to slow her down:

> Donut: GOODNESS, I'M NOT GOING TO PICK THE SWOLE BENEFIT. DO YOU REMEMBER WHAT IT DID TO CARL? CAN
> YOU IMAGINE? I'M GOING TO PICK MUSCLE MOMMY FOR STRENGTH SO MONGO IS STRONGER, AND FOR INTELLIGENCE I
> PICK BRAIN TRUST SO I CAN MAKE MAGIC MISSILE EVEN MORE POWERFUL. I ALSO HAVE ONE TO PICK FOR MY
> CHARISMA TOPPING 200. I'M GOING TO PICK ONE CALLED WHOLESALE DISCOUNT.
>
> Mordecai: Uh, are you sure about that Muscle Mommy benefit?
>
> Donut: TOO LATE. I PICKED IT. WOW! HAVE YOU SEEN MY CHARISMA?

- Base **Intelligence** and **Strength** both cross **100** — **Brain Trust** and **Muscle Mommy**, the
  latter adding 25% of Donut's strength to [Mongo](mongo.md)'s.
- Base **Charisma** crosses **200** — **Wholesale Discount**, everything at cost in the store.
- Mordecai on the result: "I think the Bopcas will be worshiping you again. Your charm and
  [Love Vampire](../abilities/love-vampire.md) skills are stronger than that of most gods."

A fourth natural benefit, **Monologue**, arrives separately and on different terms — Carl describes it
as "one of several benefits crawlers received when their boosted stats topped 300." It is a
charisma-based skill that terrifies the enemy and pays her experience in proportion to how frightened
they are. She debuts it while circling Huanxin Jinx in Chapter 86.

Her own verdict on the whole package, delivered while insisting Mongo and Rend be kept caged away from
the gore: "As the strongest crawler in the dungeon I have to maintain a sense of decorum."

### The dog on her flank

To make *War Crime* work, Rosetta needs Donut to carry a second spell at level 8 or higher within
minutes, which means a tattoo. Donut refuses, loudly and repeatedly — "DONUT: ROSETTA AND EDGAR ARE
TRYING TO DEFILE MY ASS" — and Carl calls it off rather than force her. Then she tells him the real
objection:

> "Carl," Donut said, looking up at me. "It's okay... You're right. It'll work. It's just... I hoped
> none of them would be on the outside."
>
> "What do you mean?"
>
> "The scars."

Carl's answer is that a chosen mark is not a scar, "it will be a badge of honor," and that she can still
say no. She agrees on one condition — "I'll only do it if you also get a matching one. A matching ass
tattoo" — and Carl accepts.

**Edgar** the tortoise inks it on her shaved right flank, holding the poker in his mouth, using an ink
he has never tried before, refusing to say what he is drawing until the last second ("A dog."). **Milk**
then writes **Words of Power** over it in two colours of calligraphic ink to force the spell temporarily
up to level 15. The result:

> It was a simple, rather adorable line art tattoo of a dog. As I watched, the tattoo did, indeed,
> move. It lifted its leg and started to lick itself.

The spell is **Bijanbi** — a wide-area variant of Dinner Bell, extracted from a fallen cleric, which
drags enemies toward a designated point. It comes from a god called **Wepwawet**, which delights her for
entirely the wrong reason: "Carl, Carl, it says he's in the 'Court of Nekhebit!' and I can worship
Odette now, too!" She also picks up two achievements for it, one calling her a traitor and one awarding
her a stolen catalytic converter, and immediately announces she is getting it lasered off on the next
floor.

### Killing Empress D'Nadia

Her former sponsor, now warlord of the Prism, dies in Chapter 26 at Donut's direction. Carl has marked
her and is on the edge of doing it himself in a state he has never been in before; Donut talks him back
over chat ("No, Carl. Please, no. I need you to step back."), then takes the kill herself, in character,
out loud:

> "Empress, Darling," Donut said. "Mongo would like to express his utmost disappointment at how you
> judged him during the pet show."

The system credits it to the raptor:

> System Message: Warlord D'Nadia of the Prism has fallen. Credit for the kill has been given to pet
> Mongo of the Princess Posse.
>
> System Message: The Prism has been defeated.

It also earns Carl the **Gavrilo Princip** achievement and the Legendary 1914 box that *War Crime* comes
out of — so D'Nadia's death is, indirectly, the origin of the spell that reshapes the rest of the floor.

### Killing Huanxin Jinx

Chapter 86, with eight minutes left before Emberus destroys the floor. **Huanxin Jinx**, CEO of Icon
Industries, has arrived driving the goddess [Eileithyia](../bosses/eileithyia.md) to collect
[Katia](katia.md). Donut has arranged for her to be standing on a floor inscribed with words of power,
and the moment Huanxin is distracted she is ripped out of the goddess's soul armour and dumped, shielded
and furious, into the circle. Eileithyia herself is frozen until Katia makes her choice.

Donut circles her, weaving *Monologue*, strips her shields with a **Claw** skill she has trained from
level 4 toward 15 ("All cats have it, naturally"), and then introduces the last guest.

The chain of custody on the kill is precise, and every link matters:

1. The mount is a **Quadruped weapon mount — "The scorpion's bite,"** a non-magical bandolier from a
   gold benefactor box sent by **the Apothecary**. It allows ranged weapons to be mounted on her back
   and fired and loaded by inventory action.
2. The weapon is the
   **[Enchanted Repeating Crossbow of the Scavenger Mother of Mothers](../items/enchanted-repeating-crossbow-of-the-scavenger-mother-of-mothers.md)**
   — **Katia's** crossbow, formed onto the mount, "bigger than Donut" and unloaded. Donut notes it is
   underpowered for her because "I don't have any women in my party."
3. The bolt is **Mordecai's**. Plain, unenchanted, and tipped with his mother's ashes:

> "No, I can't. Not directly," Mordecai said. He held something up. It was a simple crossbow bolt.
> There was nothing magical about it. "You see this?" He flicked the tip. "My brother died because of
> you and because of Odette and because of Chaco. But mostly because of you. Some of my mother's ashes
> are built into the tip of this bolt. It's a funeral rite in my culture. To make a weapon with the
> ashes of those you lost. My mother died before the crawl came to my world, and I'm grateful for the
> opportunity to allow her to avenge her son."

Mordecai — a non-combatant NPC who cannot touch her — loads the bolt into the crossbow on Donut's back
and walks out of the tent. Donut finishes her sentence and fires:

> "We don't really need to go through all this. But we want to. We want to show those of you on the
> 12th floor what's coming. You're strong, yes. But we are getting stronger every day. And you know
> what? We don't need to kill the gods and goddesses to win. No, honey..."
>
> The crossbow on her back fired, and the bolt slammed right between the multiple sets of eyes of the
> alien.
>
> Huaxin Jinx, CEO of Icon industries rocketed backwards, slid across the floor, and came to a rest at
> Victory's feet, dead.
>
> "...We just need to kill you."

She undercuts the whole thing herself a chapter later, which is the most Donut thing in the book:

> "I feel really bad about pooping in Mordecai's mother's ashes now. Do you think any of it got into the
> tip thing? I'm never going to not think of that now when I think of Huanxin. It was supposed to be
> this big, grand gesture, but she was really killed by a cat poop arrow."

### "They are not going to break me."

Donut ends Book 7 having lost Katia to the twelfth floor, lost the sponsor who sold her, and spent
hours doing things she will not be able to un-know. Carl watches her cover it — "I could tell she was
struggling, but the fact she was actively covering it was such a relief" — and at the very last moment,
walking to the stairwell as Emberus burns Larracos off the floor, asks her directly:

> "You doing okay?" I asked Donut as we moved to the stairwell.
>
> "I'm going to miss her," she said. "But you know what, Carl?"
>
> "What's that?"
>
> "They are not going to break me. No matter how hard they try."
>
> I reached up, and I gave her a pat.
>
> Together, we walked into the stairwell, leaving the now-empty battlefield of Larracos behind.

It is deliberately the same sentence Carl has been saying to himself since Floor 1, turned outward and
made plural.

### Other Book 7 notes

- **Party leader:** Carl takes the role from her in Chapter 27 on base stats and she pretends to be
  furious about it; she takes it straight back in Chapter 77 — "At least I'm back in my rightful spot as
  leader of the party."
- **New gear:** the **Tiara of a Thousand Lights** and its charge pillow; the Deathbed Hellcat ghillie
  suit and boonie hat; a cloak she insists on calling her "champion shawl," which has been quietly
  training her **Claw** skill; celestial **knee pads** passed to her by Katia, carrying an Eileithyia
  500% damage bonus against enemies; and the Apothecary's quadruped weapon mount.
- **New spells:** **You're Not Done Yet**, **War Crime**, **Flak**, **Shield** and **Sentry** from
  floor and quest boxes; **Bijanbi** by tattoo; and the Oak Fell deity spells.
- She collects two more **Celestial boxes** and a **Lorena Bobbitt** achievement in the same minute she
  becomes The Oak Fell.
- Mongo's burned wing is regrown, and **Muscle Mommy** leaves him "decidedly more buff." **Rend**,
  Carl's inherited meatball, joins the household.
- Her charisma finishes the book "way above 300" with gear, though not yet at base — the distinction
  annoys her, since **Li Na** gets system-wide announcements for maxing base stats and she does not.

## Book 8 Notable Events

### Claw Breaks the Level-15 Cap

Early on Floor 10, Rosetta fits Donut for claw caps to keep her from accidentally shredding
furniture, and casually suggests she check her **Claw** skill again. It has passed the previous
level-15 ceiling entirely:

> "My goodness," Donut said a moment later. "It's at level 17! 17! I thought it topped out at 15!"

Mordecai's read is that a divine-tier item is behind it: "It's from the gods, and when it comes to
gods, all bets are off... you've gone from a ranged powerhouse to one of the most powerful melee
fighters in the game's history. Or you can use your *Astral Paw* spell to cut through almost
anything." (The full mechanics of the upgrade belong on an Abilities-pass page once one exists for
Claw; this page notes only that the jump happens and when.)

### Astral Paw, Twice More

The upgraded claw gets two standout demonstrations later in the book. In Heat 7, three Russian
crawlers (the "Tracksuit Troika") try to double-cross an escape pact and make a run for a portal in
a shielded Cadillac; once the shield drops, Donut ends it in one line:

> Donut cast *Astral Paw*. She smashed the car where it stood, crushing it like a tin can. The
> Cadillac simply ceased to exist.

Moments later in the same heat, the drunk, grieving unicorn **Dwight** — armed with a missile
launcher and screaming threats — loses his head "in a single swipe of Donut's claw" the instant the
race begins, before he can fire a shot. See [Supporting cast](supporting-cast.md) for Dwight and
Lucienne.

### Killing Mitch

During the Heat 7 Desperado Club ambush, the shadow mimic posing as the casino's croupier ("Mitch")
is frozen solid by Carl's Eye of the Bedlam Bride while Carl is transformed into a dog; Donut lands
the actual kill with repeated *Magic Missile* casts while he's immobile. See [Carl](carl.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles)
for the full scene.

### Facing Down Chandra

When **[Princess Chandra, Esq.](chandra.md)** — Donut's newly assigned attorney — presents her with
a conservatorship claim arguing Donut's species "was not yet recognized as a legally competent
racial entity," Donut dismantles the argument without raising her voice, turns a matching set of
Carl's father's own court documents back on her, and drives her out of the guild's safe room in a
panic:

> "They paint a very distinct picture of who Carl's father was as a person. Much the same way these
> papers paint a picture of who you are, *Princess* Chandra... What a sad little snake you are. I
> pity you."

See Chandra's own page for the full confrontation and its aftermath.

### The Wrong Biscuit

At the Floor 11 finale, mid-battle, an Eris-triggered inventory scramble scatters every party
member's items into everyone else's hotlists. Carl needs Donut to find and combine a specific banger
sphere with an enriched pet biscuit meant to turn the boss **Scolopendra** into a harmless,
mindless pet the party can carry in inventory as a fail-safe. In the chaos, she hands him the wrong
one — the same kind of "Nothing Special" sapience-granting biscuit already used on **Penelope**
(see [Penelope](penelope.md)) — and only admits it after the fact:

> "So, with the inventory. It was happening so fast, I made a mistake. Like, a really, really bad
> one."

Instead of a mindless pet, the transformed Scolopendra emerges as a fully sapient, flirtatious
crawler — **"Sexy Scolopendra," Level 1, Crawler #12,953,456** — who promptly wraps herself around
Carl: "Hi, Carl." Carl's own reaction makes the stakes plain: "Donut had given me the wrong pet
biscuit." The intended fail-safe — carrying a mindless Scolopendra as insurance against the party's
own deaths — is gone; what they get instead is a new, willing ally with no obligation to obey them.
