# Justice Light

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPC / former crawler — a returned cookbook author fighting for the Princess Posse on
      Floor 9
    - **Race:** **Skyfowl** — birth race also Skyfowl. A one-winged eagle, “shorter and stockier
      than most,” black feathers tinged with gray, with a twin tuft on his head that makes him look
      horned
    - **Class:** **Trap Master**, per his own status card. In Book 7 the system shows his class
      title as **Origamist** — Carl notes he has “no idea what the hell that even meant”
    - **Subclass:** None stated
    - **Level:** **65** during Faction Wars (Chapter 32), earned mostly from partial credit on his
      own traps. Top level during his original crawl: **86**
    - **Role:** Trap master for the Princess Posse; author of the **8th edition** of
      [The Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md)
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** A trap-concealment spell he worked out long ago (previously
      believed to work only on alarm traps); a presence-hiding spell used in the final chapter;
      built-in **neural implants** including **seismic sensors** sensitive enough to track a dwarven
      digger idling underground and to read a body’s temperature, weight and hormonal emissions
    - **Notable items:** None personally worn or wielded. His final trap is assembled from a **bijou
      fairy trap**, a **demon trap**, and the broken winding box of the
      [Gate of the Feral Gods](../items/gate-of-the-feral-gods.md) used as a permanent portal
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — stayed behind alone on the burning Floor 9 in Chapter 87, triggered
      his trans-dimensional trap, and died in the fire as **Emberus** melted the floor

!!! note "Status card vs. the ending"
    The status card at the end of his interlude — which opens Part 3, between Chapters 55 and 56 —
    reads “Current status: Alive.” That card is written as of the interlude, thirty-odd chapters
    before he dies.

## Overview

Justice Light comes to Floor 9 with the mercenary waves **Porthus** arranged — Carl calls him “the
third, and the last I believed, of the cookbook authors who had come with the mercenaries.” **Tipid**
introduces him to [Carl](carl.md) in Chapter 11 with a straight promise:

> “And wait until you see what my friend Justice Light can do. That eagle knows more about traps
> than anyone else who ever set foot in the dungeon.”

In person he is unnerving. He stands in the corner of the guildhall muttering, head jerking
back and forth “like he was hearing things that weren’t really there,” sewing folded scraps of
coloured paper into three-dimensional sculptures — which Tipid says is how he calms himself. When
Carl worries aloud that losing a saferoom entrance will cost him his workbenches, Justice answers
without looking up: “It’s safer and faster to use your table, but there’s nothing left I can’t do
on my own.” Pressed on whether captured **gondii** brain worms might have learned the party’s
secrets, he pulls a thread and all the scattered paper pieces snap together into a spiral:

> “It doesn’t matter,” Justice Light said… “I have contingencies heaped upon contingencies. Have
> faith in the process. Danger lives in what they can’t see.”

Donut is not reassured.

> Donut: ARE WE SURE WE TRUST THIS GUY? HE’S OBVIOUSLY UNSTABLE.
>
> Carl: I trust him with my life.
>
> Donut: OKAY. BUT YOU’RE TRUSTING HIM WITH MORE THAN THAT. IT’S WITH ALL OF OUR LIVES.

Both Tipid and [Rosetta Thagra](rosetta-thagra.md) are described as fiercely protective of him.
Rosetta vetoes his request to be deliberately infected with slugpox so he can breed more sluggalo
assistants, warning that the skyfowl “had a tendency to hyperfixate on his projects, and he’d end up
killing himself in the process.”

## The Shopkeeper

His entry in the 8th edition, reprinted at the head of Chapter 20, is about one killing out of
thousands:

> I think about him all the time. I have killed many NPCs since I arrived in this horror, but I have
> only killed one out of anger. He did not deserve that, and it haunts me. I keep going over it.
> It’s the manifestation of how I’ve changed, all boiled down to a single incident. The old me
> wouldn’t have done that. Snapped and just killed someone.
>
> The memory is abhorrent to me. I have become what I hate.

The victim was a lizard-like NPC who ran the only trap store on the level; with the store gone,
“dozens of my fellow crawlers were unable to find the supplies they needed to complete the level,
and they died.” On taking his exit deal, Justice asked for and was granted an indentureship as the
shopkeeper he had killed, accepting extra time on his contract for the privilege — something
**Dante** notes was not normally allowed.

Crawler **Drakea**, 22nd edition, answers him directly in the book:

> Listen to me, brother. We all make mistakes… But in this case... it was not your fault. No. They
> put you here. I remember your passage about how you would hunt and give food to the flightless
> elders of your world. You were once a good, gentle soul. They turned you into a warrior. They are
> the engineers of this all.

## Dante, the Ice Elves, and the Wing

The **Dante** interlude (between Chapters 4 and 5) shows the two of them as neighbours in
indentureship — Dante a crocodilian thief, author of the 3rd edition, who had transferred to the
trap master guild specifically to be near Justice without ever telling him they were brothers in
the book. Both are reassigned off their posts to guard an ice castle on the eighth floor. Dante’s
card records the outcome: “Was killed by administrative action for refusing to follow orders.”

Justice finally tells the rest of it himself, to a changeling, in his own interlude. The castle was
staffed by **ice elves** — “More fairy than anything… They are gentle, simple creatures. Innocent…
They would play in the snow, like children. This was all off camera.” He knew all their names. They
loved puzzles, as he does.

> “All this time later, and I still don’t understand why they ordered us to kill them all.”

Dante refused twice. There was no third warning.

> “He’d already passed the point of no return, or so I thought. But in the end, I was wrong. He
> stood his ground, and he died. And by the gods, he died free.”

Justice refused once, was given a warning, and was ordered again. A beam took his wing, and then he
did as he was told.

> Justice held out the stump of his wing. “I, too, refused. But I only did so once, and unlike
> Dante, I was given a warning. They ordered me a second time.” He lowered his head… “I am still
> not free.”

He adds the part he has never told anyone, not even Porthus: a recurring dream in which the elves,
having overheard, kill themselves to spare him the act. “Even that would’ve been the coward’s
solution, so why do I yearn for it?… what difference is there between anger and cowardice?”

> “They didn’t even fight back. They loved puzzles and the snow. I was already done. It was all off
> camera. I still don’t understand why.”

His personal proverb, repeated twice in the interlude, is his answer to all of it: “Wishing for
favorable air currents does not manifest them. You ride the currents you have, or you don’t fly.”

## The Changeling in the Tunnel

Justice never trusted the NPC team, and said so to Tipid and Rosetta. He picked sluggalos as his
work crew partly because he assumed procedurally generated mobs could not be copied by
**changelings**. He was wrong — and his implants caught the substitution the moment it happened,
from a change in body temperature, weight, and the absence of hormonal emissions.

He sends the rest of the crew up, then quietly arms eight hidden traps and addresses the last slug
in line by her real employer’s name.

> “Your real boss,” Justice repeated. “Juice Box. Tell her we need to talk.”
>
> …”Damn, man,” the slug finally said, sighing. “I’m supposed to kill you if you figure it out.”

The changeling — an old, powerful one named **Jessica** — counts six of his traps, and takes a
humanoid form with [Juice Box](juice-box.md)’s head so Juice Box can speak through her over chat. A
dwarven digger idles beneath them the whole conversation, ready to surge up and kill him.

The moment that lands hardest is not his: when he describes the ice elves, Jessica switches to one.

> “Juice Box does not have these creatures in her library, but I do. I lived amongst them many
> lifetimes ago. They are indeed simple, gentle creatures.”

## Reading Juice Box

Justice lays out the case that Team Retribution’s alliance with the crawlers is real but secondary.
His evidence is the **Blood Sultanate**: their security was worse than the Madness’s, yet Juice Box
claimed she could not reach them, “an obvious lie,” after having decapitated multiple Viceroys
before the ceasefire. He concludes she could kill every faction leader on the floor at any moment
and has chosen not to.

> “The gods. It’s all about the gods. It has been about them the whole time. You’re risking it all
> so you and your kind can physically touch as many of them as possible.”

He is right. Changelings can only take the form of something they have personally touched — Carl
works through the same logic separately in Chapter 44 while thinking about Juice Box’s dead brother,
whose quest was “to touch as many rare creatures as he could.” Juice Box’s reply gives away the
tally:

> “We lost three. We did not cause the gods to appear, but we were in position just in case. One of
> us managed to touch Bast—the panther god—but she died before she could escape. At this point, only
> one of us has touched a deity, and that one is... wrong.”

His offer is a trade: he shows them how to touch a god and survive; they stop the subterfuge and
back **Operation Ruin** in full. She points out that if Operation Ruin works, the gods stop
mattering.

> Justice Light finally did chuckle. “Oh, my dear. You are thinking much too small. That’s not the
> war I’m talking about.”

He then explains what he has actually been building under the castle — a trap he says he started
designing “the very moment he watched Carl discover the Gate of the Feral Gods.” The digger engine
comes fully on, and then moves away.

## Building It in the Open

Everything Justice does for the Posse during Faction Wars doubles as cover. He wires the throne
room, teaches Carl the alarm-trap upgrades reprinted in the 25th edition (recordings, playlists, a
wider music database — still no volume control), hides the minefields with his concealment spell,
supplies the teleport-trap trick that lets a mount and all its riders through at once, and holds the
tunnel network under the castle as the last line of defence. His attitude to the last of those is
not defensive:

> Justice Light: That’s too many. Chop ‘em up a little first for me, Rosie. Get ‘em down to under
> 3,000, then we can let them in. I will welcome any and all guests into my lair. Would prefer it be
> Reaver, but I’ll take what I can get.

The one thing he cannot hide is how badly he needs the **Gate of the Feral Gods** back after the war
mages steal it. He will not say why.

> Justice Light: GODSDAMNIT, WHY ISN’T ANYONE LISTENING TO ME? GET THE GATE BACK, AND IT WON’T
> MATTER.

Carl tells him where it is — in the stomach of a merchant named **Paca** in the Midnight Market —
against Tipid and Rosetta’s hesitation, on the grounds that “Who was I to stand between a brother and
his revenge, whatever it may be?” Justice retrieves it in Chapter 81: “We have retrieved the Gate of
the Feral Gods, and we are back in our location. Juice Box, it is ready.”

By the time Emberus is announced, it is already live.

> Carl: In 65 minutes, Emberus will melt this entire floor.
>
> Justice Light: Oh. Is that all?
>
> Carl: Set your trap or whatever the hell you’re doing and get to Larracos.
>
> Justice Light: The trap is already turned on. It has been turned on for days now.

**Baroness Victory** is the one who finally names the thing, after Rosetta reports him missing:

> “Perhaps you should have watched him better,” Victory said. “He built a trans-dimensional trap
> right underneath your castle, and you didn’t even notice because he decorated it with regular
> traps.”

Carl’s working definition, when Donut asks: “It’s a trap that affects more than one place at a time.
At least here in the dungeon.”

## Breaking the Nothing

He told Juice Box he was jumping into **the Nothing** with her. He lied; he needed to be on the
crawler side of the veil to fire the trap. Everyone else has transferred. Only he and Emberus remain
on the floor.

> Justice Light: I am not sorry. Peace to you all, brothers and sisters.
>
> He took his talon, and he snapped a piece of twine.
>
> System Message: A Legendary Trap has been triggered.

The mechanism is deliberately small. Snapping the twine topples one tiny trap, which drops two items
into the permanent portal below it — a portal made from the broken, blood-soaked winding box of the
Gate of the Feral Gods, the same one Juice Box had just used to flee. The two items are a **bijou
fairy trap**, carrying the thousands of threads tying the bijou to the 12th floor, and a **demon
trap**, carrying thousands of evicted demons “with the power to open a portal back to Sheol, if only
they could find something to latch onto.”

> System Message: The Nothing has been broken.
>
> System Message: The Nothing is currently draining into The Halls of the Ascendency.
>
> System Message: The Nothing is currently draining into Sheol.
>
> System Message: The Nothing is currently draining into Scolopendra’s Lair.
>
> System Message: Scolopendra stirs.
>
> …
>
> System Message: Scolopendra has awakened.

He does not see any of it. “Still, his last moments were spent envisioning what was happening. Four
separate planes—all jumbled and confused—folded and moved together as the unbreakable lines of the
bijou fairies tightened, creating a flower pattern before the darkness in the center of it all broke
apart.”

> As the fire engulfed him, finally giving rest to a soul that had been so tired for such a very long
> time, Justice Light cawed into the air.

The narration, not a character, supplies the framing for how much this matters: he turns his
attention to “the single, small trap that was about to change the course of all history.” On the
text’s own terms it is the most consequential act in the book — it wakes the dungeon’s final boss
years ahead of schedule, at the end of the ninth floor rather than the eighteenth.

!!! note "On 'self-immolation'"
    The book never uses that word for him. What it shows is a deliberate choice to stay behind on a
    floor he knew Emberus was about to melt, in order to trigger the trap by hand; the fire that
    kills him is Emberus’s, not the trap’s. He also knowingly accepts the cost to everyone else —
    “All his friends left in the dungeon could possibly suffer because of what he was about to do.”

!!! note "Unresolved"
    Justice told Juice Box that if she survived her passage through the Nothing “she would be reborn
    as the ultimate weapon to end this madness. This war.” What that means, and what he actually
    promised her about touching gods safely, is not explained before the book ends.
