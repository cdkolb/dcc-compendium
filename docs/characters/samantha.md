# Samantha (Psamathe)

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **True identity:** **Psamathe**, a minor deity, banished to "the Nothing" by her father after
      an affair with a mortal king
    - **Current form:** A decapitated half-naiad sex-doll head, modeled on a fictional character
      named Lika from the stage play *Troubadour's Bounty*. By the end of Book 7 she is burned black
      — "nothing but a scorched, round head" with a single surviving white curl, and after a second
      trip into a volcano, worse than that
    - **Status:** Hired staff — trainer in Carl and Donut's personal space, as of Book 4. Alive and
      still with the party at the end of Book 7; she gives up the promised flesh-golem body to save
      [Louis Santiago](louis-santiago.md) and goes down to Floor 10 with the rest of them
    - **Type/Class:** Withering Spirit — a soul split between two vessels during a botched escape
      from the Nothing; not fully alive, extremely difficult to kill, effectively powerless outside
      her vessel

## What She Is

Psamathe tricked the Mad Dune Mage **Ghazi** into opening a portal to the Nothing on his behalf
(claiming to be Yarilo, the banished god of Lust, whom Ghazi wanted to trade with) as her own
escape attempt. The escape went wrong: her essence split in two. One half fled into the necropolis
and possessed the ghost of the emperor's wife, Quetzalcoatlus; the other half — unable to find any
better vessel in time — took up residence in a sex-doll head modeled after Ghazi's obsession,
Lika, moments before Ghazi's own botched containment spell turned the whole castle (doll included)
to glass. She spends years frozen and aware, unable to move or speak, while Ghazi unknowingly
keeps her within arm's reach.

Mordecai identifies her condition as a **withering spirit** — a soul-split creature that isn't
alive or dead, nearly impossible to kill, but with very little independent power. He compares her
(unfavorably, in terms of danger) to Remex, the deliberately-weaponized soul leech capacitor from
Floor 4.

## Recovery and Personality

Freed when Mongo accidentally knocks over and shatters her glass-encased doll body, Samantha
(named by Carl, since he refuses to use "Psamathe") is foul-mouthed, unfiltered, prone to
non-stop declarations that she's "going to kill your mother," and entirely without a body below
the neck for most of the book. Despite this, she proves genuinely useful — walking Carl through
monster weaknesses she's picked up over her long, strange unlife, and helping Mordecai finally
identify a use for a mysterious sponsor-gifted root vegetable ("the yam"), which becomes essential
to the Phase Lava Rock potion that frees Chris Andrews.

## Role in the Story — Book 4

- Refuses Carl's offer to send her back into the Nothing, and instead is hired as a hidden
  personal-space **trainer** (on Donut's insistence) so she can be kept both useful and contained
- Is chased by her aunt/cousin — a feral, banished demon named **Slit**, hunting for her on
  another demon's behalf — after being thrown fifty-plus kilometers as a distraction/weapon via a
  custom [xistera attachment](../items/xistera-extension-head-tosser.md); Slit is killed moments
  later by an unrelated god before she can catch up
- Reveals that the demon that killed Slit was her own **mother** — one of several harem-demons who
  hate her — deepening the sense that Samantha's family history is as dysfunctional and dangerous
  as the gods and demons hunting Carl's own party
- Helps talk Chris Andrews's captor, Maggie My, into a final confrontation stance alongside Carl at
  the book's climax (indirectly, via her earlier knowledge-sharing with Mordecai)

## Book 5: A God's Cousin

Samantha travels with Carl and Donut through Floor 6 as an expendable, indestructible scout and
distraction weapon — thrown, duct-taped to missiles, and used repeatedly to lure or infiltrate
hostile positions since a killing blow simply can't land on her. She reveals she's a blood
relation (calling herself Signet's "third cousin thrice removed," "by marriage if you're being
super technical" — the connection runs through Signet's own great-grandfather, "Tsar Guggenheim,"
though Carl cuts her off before she can finish explaining exactly how) of the elite Tsarina Signet,
and briefly tries to
convince Signet to sacrifice her own body so Samantha can finally have a physical form again —
Signet deflects with a vague future promise the narration explicitly calls a lie. Well before
Signet's death, Donut already notices Samantha is growing stronger and rolling faster than
Mordecai's original "withering spirit" assessment predicted, comparing her to the creatures from
the movie *Critters* and worrying about what she's turning into. Her demand for a body is never
fulfilled by Signet; after Signet's death, one of Signet's tattoo companions, Miss Nadine, instead
hands Carl the inert body of Signet's own late mother — an "Incomplete Flesh Golem" — suggesting
Samantha might use it with the help of "a Pulpmancer," but this thread is left hanging at the end
of the book.

Samantha's decisive contribution comes at the Butcher's Masquerade: infiltrating the castle's
hidden control room through a network of cat-sized tunnels (built, unbeknownst to her, by Queen
Imogen's own familiar, Ferdinand), finding the elf guards already dead there — decapitated by
mantises sent ahead of Circe Took/Diwata. The room holds two pairs of soul crystals: one pair
frees the crawlers' own ballroom from its magic/inventory block when Samantha pulls it, while the
other pair (controlling the hunters' ballroom) turns out to have already been pulled by the same
mantis infiltrators — so, on Carl's order, Samantha instead pushes that second pair back into
place, keeping the hunters locked out of their own magic and gear. When the goddess Diwata (Circe Took, mid-mantis-birth) breaks into the room to
retake it, Samantha, unable to hold the room, instead grabs multiple soul crystals in her mouth and
bites down on them right next to Diwata, deliberately triggering a massive overload explosion to
try to force the goddess to stop giving birth and leave. It works — the blast blows a hole through
the floor and ceiling between the two ballrooms and knocks Diwata out cold — though it does not
kill her; Circe Took's actual death comes later, on the battlefield, when her own mantis children
turn on her once Donut's *Laundry Day* strips away her divine "armor." After the seal breaks and the "Cage Fight Extreme" battle erupts, Samantha reappears in the
crawlers' ballroom (surfing in on a wave of bug gore from above), helps finish off Vrah, and hands
off Signet's mother's inert "flesh golem" body to Carl — then, rather than agree to reunite with
him on the ninth floor, declares "Fuck that!" and is last seen firing off toward parts unknown,
rocketing down through a hole in the floor. Carl
tries and fails to summon any further help from her afterward ("We weren't able to coax any sort
of spells or buffs out of Samantha. She was gone anyway to god knew where") — her fate at the end
of Book 5 is left unresolved.

## Book 6: Spell Enhancement, Acid, and the Nothing

### She Can Enhance Spells After All

Book 5 ended with an unexplained note from Carl's missing sponsor suggesting he use Samantha to boost
Donut's *Laundry Day*. Samantha claimed to have no idea what they were talking about and acted like
they were crazy, and they had to burn a Pawna's Tears potion instead. Book 6 confirms the note was
right:

> As it turned out, **Samantha actually *did* have the power to enhance spells. In fact, she could
> enhance them quite a bit.**

Her own account of it:

> "I can help spells. I can make them more powerful. **But it doesn't always do what I want.** Like,
> sometimes it's a lot stronger than I thought it would be. And sometimes it doesn't work too good…
> **And sometimes my magic does stuff without me asking.**"

The proof event is Carl's xistera recall of Sister Ines, amplified by Samantha, which drags back not
just the nun but a level-125 sea snake, roughly a thousand zombie baby seals, five thousand zombie
crabs, five hundred adult zombie seals and most of the region's fish. She also enhances Donut's
*Minion Army*, and is at least partly responsible for [Elle](elle-mcgibbons.md)'s level-5 *Graupel*
casting at level 16.

### She Keeps Getting Stronger

She was barely able to roll at the start of the floor, and by the end she is a serious combatant —
noticeably heavier, with a faint vibration to her that wasn't there before. Nobody knows why. Carl's
hypothesis, formed right after her ex-boyfriend the badger bartender dies:

> "I'm starting to think maybe **Samantha gets more powerful every time somebody dies**… If that's
> the case, we really need to be careful of her on the next floor. If we let her inhabit the body of
> Signet's mom, I'm afraid she's going to end up stronger than we can handle."

!!! warning "Unresolved"
    The death hypothesis is Carl's speculation. Neither the system nor Samantha ever confirms it, and
    Book 6 offers no other explanation. Carl's earlier worry stands: "Nobody knows why or how you're
    getting all these new powers, and it kinda freaks me out because we don't know where it's going
    to stop."

### The Neck Hole

Samantha's ragged neck hole turns out not to be a hole at all but a deep, pliable, acid-filled
storage cavity. It is discovered the hard way, during the Desperado Club disaster, when the bartender
Charles ends up shoulder-deep in it — "there was a deepness there that hadn't been present before."
(Samantha's explanation: "Oh, him? We're on a date. His arm got stuck. I'm practicing my Kegels.")
What comes out of her later has had the flesh burned off it.

Carl immediately weaponises it. Teleport-trap anchors can't be pre-placed by anyone else, so he loads
**spider automatons** carrying anchors into her and has her fly them wherever he needs to jump to.
Samantha's offer: "**I can take anything you got.**" Carl's condition: "just make sure you pop them
out the moment you land."

In Chapter 69 he uses it on himself. He needs to be under 5% health to trigger his Emberus boon
**The Martyr's Path** — which would set fire to every footprint he's left outdoors, and since his
fire is currently Sheol fire, would bathe the demon prince Amayon in it — and he needs to be back at
the battle at the same moment. So he teleports into the trap he left inside her.

> I landed headfirst inside of Samantha's neck hole.

Head-first, suffocating, wedged, being slammed repeatedly against something, his hair burning off and
"the acid literally melting my face off," while Samantha shrieks and tries to push him out and he
holds her down onto himself. The timer runs out at 7%, which he thinks is failure; he keeps dropping,
hits the button anyway, and the Path fires. He bottoms out at **2% health** with a blinking red line
of debuffs.

Her verdict afterwards, hovering next to him in the demon's palm:

> "Carl, that was the second weirdest sex I ever had. You gotta warn a girl if you're going to just
> pop in like that." She looked me up and down. "We are going to have to do something about your hair
> before we announce that we're dating now."

She breaks up with him at the end of the book anyway: "Carl. We need to break up. I've been thinking
about it, and I've decided to go back to my king."

### Sheol and the Nothing

Samantha supplies most of the book's cosmology, in her usual register.

- The **Nothing** is a mixing prison, and she was in it: "Before I was banished to the Nothing when I
  had my original body, I would get more powerful when someone prayed to me or bought a charm with my
  likeness." Her fellow inmates include a crocodile-headed goddess who "makes sushi rolls out of her
  own innards. They keep growing back, and she keeps making more and selling it. She has a little
  stand and everything. She's quite the entrepreneur."
- **Sheol** is separate, and its royalty don't get sent to the Nothing: "You know the four crown
  princes of Sheol? The oldest brother calls himself the King even though his mommy is in charge?…
  Amayon is that guy's brother. He's the youngest one I think… We all mixed together in the Nothing,
  but he wasn't in there. He likes to set things on fire. Kinda like Emberus, actually. **They don't
  like each other.**"
- On Amayon's escape spell: "He is casting a spell to get home. It's a portal. They try casting it a
  lot in the Nothing, but it doesn't work there because **he needs to suck a lot of souls in for it
  to work.** It'll probably work here, though." And on scale: "**he's a big boy and big boys need big
  doors and lots of souls.**"
- She is also personally responsible for the Desperado Club catastrophe: a knife-game loser was pulled
  into the Nothing, "and when it opened, it tore," letting out **Minge**, a level-225 minor feral
  demon and one of a group of sisters who hate Samantha for insulting their king.

### The Elle Rant

Immediately after Elle's *Graupel* fires six times stronger than it should, Samantha loses coherence
completely — the only time in the book she looks frightened:

> "**Elle is part earth, part water mage,**" she said, floating past, talking to herself as she
> continued to rotate. "**She's on the four seasons path. I… but how did that happen? I am stronger
> too. I didn't do that. It's the tree, I think. Or maybe. It's all there. He's opening a path
> through the river, the water, and the tree, the earth. It's good she's not fire yet, but maybe the
> prince's spell enhanced her. No. No. Gotta kill her. No, not Elle. Her mother. No, not *her*
> mother.**" She shook her head like a dog. She turned to look at me. "What just happened? Carl, did
> you roofie me again?"

Carl asks her directly what she just said and gets "You need to stop gaslighting me, Carl."

!!! warning "Unresolved"
    "The four seasons path" appears exactly once in the book. Nothing in this rant — the tree, the
    river, "the prince's spell," the mother — is ever picked up or explained. It rhymes with Carl's
    own recurring "river" motif, but the text never connects them.

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Samantha spends most of Floor 9 in the register she has always been in — a foul-mouthed, indestructible
nuisance who is also, repeatedly, the only asset that fits the job. Then, in Chapter 83, she says one
sentence out loud and destroys the floor.

### Battlefield Asset and God Bait

Carl’s opening achievement haul includes a *Stretching!* achievement “for shoving all those traps in
Samantha’s neck hole,” which is a fair summary of how she gets used. She is thrown, she is loaded with
gear, she scouts, and she is deliberately parked next to danger.

The most deliberate use is as bait at the [Gate of the Feral Gods](../items/gate-of-the-feral-gods.md).
Both previous feral demons to come
out of the Nothing — **Slit** on the bubble floor and **Minge** at the Desperado Club — locked onto her
instantly, so the plan is to stand her next to the gate and drag the resulting monster across the
battlefield “like a kite.” What comes out instead is a god:

> Samantha: YOU DON’T HAVE TO TELL ME TWICE. THIS IS A GOD, NOT A DEMON. IT’S YARILO, AND HE DOESN’T
> LIKE ME! HE’S MAD BECAUSE HE THINKS I HELPED GET HIM TOSSED IN THE NOTHING IN THE FIRST PLACE. IT’S
> NOT MY FAULT HE’S SEXUALLY ATTRACTED TO ANIMALS. NO OFFENSE, KATIA.

Yarilo is the god she impersonated to trick Ghazi into opening her escape route in the first place, so
the hatred is earned. He spends the rest of the sequence bellowing “PSAMATHE! Are you here? We need to
talk! Quick! Wait, don’t run!” across the realm.

She also picks a fight of her own. Ghazi’s letters to the Larracos college revealed that his ex-girlfriend
**Tish** still works there, and Samantha breaks out of the guild early in the floor to go kill her —
“I went out the door, Carl. I went looking for my child, but then Louis told me he was at the college,
and I went to kill her before she could steal him from me like she tried to do to Ghazi.” Donut’s ruling:
“Samantha, you shouldn’t hold Tish accountable for what happened. That was 100% Ghazi’s fault.”

Later, alone inside the Reaver castle hunting for the captured [Louis](louis-santiago.md), she befriends
the castle’s robot guard dogs, names one **Buster**, removes its “explodey part,” and keeps the resulting
nuke. She gets Louis out of the castle blast personally, arriving back at the party as a falling comet:

> A flaming, screaming comet fell from the heavens and embedded itself into the ground nearby. Samantha.
> She was nothing but a scorched, round head. The word “Fuck” remained emblazoned on her forehead. A
> single, white curl of hair had somehow survived and remained on the top of her head.
>
> She floated upward and shook herself off. She floated up to us standing over Louis.
>
> “I saved him,” she said. “You’re welcome. Now where’s my new doggie?”

She stays burned for the rest of the book — Donut: “Samantha, you look like a piece of popcorn shrimp that
has been stuck at the bottom of the oven for two years straight.”

### The Daughter

Samantha comes onto the floor looking for her child, the sand-ooze daughter she used to trick Ghazi back
on the bubble floor, and early on she is certain she has found her: “She’s here! I can feel her, but she’s
in the castle of the stinky dwarves! She’s still in the water.” By Chapter 83 she has concluded the child
is dead — killed, she believes, in the acid of the flooded Reaver castle, and she blames Tish for it:

> “Carl,” she said, sounding serious and morose. “I think my child is dead. I think she died in the acid.
> I felt her, and now I don’t feel her anymore, and it is my fault. I should have gone to save her sooner,
> but I did not. I was too caught up in my own lust and anger, and now I have lost my only child. I have
> failed as a mother.”

Carl, trying to console her, has a thought he never gets to chase down. The war-mage warlord **Akuma** had
just announced that his team’s entire reason for being in the Reaver castle was to retrieve “the Scavenger’s
daughter, who was hidden within the castle, and we found her. She is with us now.”

> I had a sudden thought. What was it that War Mage had said? They’d found and taken the Scavenger’s
> Daughter. Could that possibly be Samantha’s daughter? And if so, did that mean Samantha was really the
> scavenger? I still didn’t know who that was.

!!! note "Unresolved"
    The text never answers this. Carl asks Mordecai directly — “who is the Scavenger? And who is the
    Scavenger’s Daughter? I know it’s the name of my patch, but I didn’t know there was more to it than
    that” — and Mordecai deflects with “Are you talking about the in-dungeon one or the fairy tale? Either
    way, that’s a conversation that’s not going to matter if you don’t figure out how to find and kill
    those two warlords.” The connection between Samantha, the item **the Scavenger’s Daughter**
    ([see the item page](../items/the-scavengers-daughter.md)), and whatever Akuma’s team carried out of
    the castle is raised and then dropped. Nothing confirms Samantha is the Scavenger.

### Giving Up the Body

With [Louis](louis-santiago.md) dying — his heart and lungs replaced by the machinery of the possessing
warlord Epitome Tagg — Samantha is the one who finds the answer, and the answer costs her everything she
has been chasing since Book 5:

> Samantha sniffed. “The flesh golem body you have in your inventory. Of Princess Lunette. She has a good
> heart and lungs. If you swap them out fast enough, you can save Louis.”

**Princess Lunette** is Signet’s mother, the “Incomplete Flesh Golem” handed to Carl at the end of the
Butcher’s Masquerade and earmarked for Samantha ever since. Carl spells out the price — “If we do this,
that means you won’t get your body back. At least not for a while” — and she takes it anyway:

> She nodded solemnly. “I know. It’s okay. Louis deserves it more than I do.”

Carl’s reply: “That’s... very brave and strong of you. That makes you a hero, Samantha. And I’m very proud
of you.” Her answer is the closest the series has come to her explaining herself — “I don’t always mess
things up, right? I’d like to think maybe I can be a good influence sometimes and not someone who ruins
everything, which is what King Blaine said to me and what my father said to me and what my mother said to
me before they all cast me out... I used her, my daughter, to get what I wanted. I ruined everything.”

The transplant works. Louis lives, with naiad gills and a kua-tin rebreather sewn into his bronchial tube.
Samantha’s verdict: “We’re cousins now,” followed by kissing him on the lips.

### “You Did Not Just Fucking Say That Out Loud”

In the same conversation, minutes earlier, she adds a footnote to her own apology. **Geyrun** is the murdered
son of the god **Emberus** — Carl’s own patron deity, and the subject of Carl’s long-running personal quest
**Find Out Who Killed My Son**:

> She nodded. “I may be a neglectful mother. I may have had an indirect, possibly direct hand in the death
> of Geyrun, but a girl can find forgiveness, right?”
>
> I sighed.
>
> “You did not just fucking say that out loud.”
>
> “What?”
>
> System Message: Emberus has entered the realm.

Chapter 84 opens on a clock:

> 66 Minutes Until the destruction of the 9th Floor.

And on Carl’s quest updating in the worst possible direction:

> Quest Update: Find Out Who Killed My Son.
>
> This so-called Psamathe claims she was part of my son’s death. Capture her for me, and I will personally
> handle the interrogation. Once I have finished grilling her, I will cleanse this world in fire to remove
> the stink of this murderer’s presence.
>
> If she is not within my presence in 66 minutes, this world will be destroyed in fire and lava and wrath.
>
> Fly, martyr. Bring my enemy to me.

The system then issues a floor-wide, no-opt-out team quest, **This is How the Gristle Grinds**:

> Here’s the problem. A very angry god that many of you are familiar with has just entered the realm. His
> name is Emberus. His son, Geyrun, was murdered. It’s a big mystery that probably isn’t going to be solved
> today, even if any of you bastards do survive this. But a demigod named “Samantha” has just admitted to
> having taken part in the crime. Emberus, being the loving father he is, has come to the realm to question
> this demigod. Regardless of what Samantha says to him, Emberus is going to turn the entirety of the 9th
> floor into a cremation chamber.
>
> This will happen in at least 66 minutes, but your cremation may be delayed slightly if Samantha actually
> shows up to the interrogation.
>
> Regardless, everything and everybody on this floor is going to die. This is not something that can be
> stopped.
>
> That is, unless you manage to kill Emberus, which as we all know is possible, but unlikely.

Samantha’s reaction is to be offended: “Wow. I wouldn’t have opened my heart if I’d known you were going to
be all dramatic about this.” When Elle suggests “a solid positives and negatives analysis of letting you keep
that pet sex doll of yours,” Samantha mutters, “Like you never accidentally triggered an apocalyptic event,
Elle.”

Everything that follows — Katia eating the Orchid early and losing her goodbyes, the rushed end of Faction
Wars, the evacuation — runs on that 66-minute clock. Carl’s assessment to Katia, who had joked he was about
to “Carl it up”: “Yeah, this one is on Samantha.”

In the final minutes he fires her out of the city with the head-thrower xistera attachment, streamers tied
round her, to stall the god in person. Her reports from inside Emberus’s hand are in character throughout:

> Samantha: HE’S JUST HOLDING ME AND CRYING. YOUR GOD IS A LITTLE BITCH, CARL. MAYBE IT’S GOOD I HELPED
> KILL OFF HIS BLOODLINE.

> Samantha: MAN, THIS GUY DOES NOT HANDLE BAD NEWS VERY WELL.

Carl recalls her at the last moment, “heavy and smoking” and barely recognisable. Her summary: “Okay, that
was pretty wild. He was going to eat me, but I had him throw me in a volcano like you said. He’s blowing up
the world now, so, yeah we should probably go.”

### Kimaris

On the stairwell at the very end, [Bautista](bautista.md) — who received an electronic guidebook to his
stuffed animals as a Book 7 achievement reward — finally looks up a plush figure Carl has been carrying:
“I looked up the Kimaris figure you have. He’s one of the brothers of Sheol. He has a summoning time of
‘the remainder of the floor.’ Be careful with him.”

Samantha abandons Louis mid-flirt:

> “Kimaris!” Samantha asked, zipping away from Louis, who took the opportunity to also dive into the
> stairwell. “You have Kimmy as a stuffed animal? Give him to me. Give him now!”

Asked who he is to her, she produces the book’s last piece of her own backstory, and hits a hole in her
memory doing it:

> “He’s one of my best friends. I met him when...” She paused. “Huh,” she said. “That’s weird. Why couldn’t
> I remember that? He’s... He’s the one who gave me the potion that would put my baby in me when I couldn’t
> get pregnant. We were friends. He’d give me advice on who to date, who to see and who to...” She floated
> off, muttering. She gasped. “His mother! I will kill his mother!”

Earlier in the book she had tried to name Carl’s new pet “Kimmy” — “Kimmy the second. I don’t care what you
call him, he will always be Kimmy to me” — which nobody understood at the time. The pet was named
[Sir Rendlegore](sir-rendlegore.md) instead.

!!! note "Correction on the plush's owner"
    The Kimaris figure belongs to **[Carl](carl.md)**, not to Bautista. Bautista’s Book 7 prize was the
    scanning guidebook for plush toys, which he had promised to lend Carl so Carl could identify the figure
    he already had. Bautista’s own stuffed animals are the Eclipse Pyxies he throws in combat.

## Book 8: A Parade of Horribles

Samantha continues in her established register — indestructible, foul-mouthed, useful in spite of
herself — but ends the book on the single most consequential line she's ever delivered about her own
past.

### Chachi

In Chapter 52, unprompted and mostly to gossip, Samantha identifies the mysterious presence Lucia
Mar's alter **Azra** had mentioned hiding among the trapped children inside Lucia's mind: a goddess
named **Chalchiuhtlicue**, whom Samantha calls "Chachi." Her introduction of the goddess is
characteristically callous about the actual horror underneath it:

> "She's always so high and mighty, always talking about how she's protecting them, but the second
> something bad or inconvenient happens, she's like 'We need to sacrifice a seven-year-old to make
> things right.' She even did it once at a Yarilo party because the meatballs had cilantro in
> them... She sent out an emergency prayer request and had her worshippers kill a whole village day
> care in the hopes the next waiter would bring her something that didn't taste like soap."

Rosetta confirms Chachi is a minor, unsponsored goddess of childbirth, water, fire and harvests —
never powerful enough to matter in the Ascendency game — and Prepotente supplies the Earth-myth
parallel, the Aztec goddess of the same name, "considered benevolent" despite a culture that
practiced human sacrifice. Carl's private takeaway is the one that matters: whatever Chachi actually
is, she is real, dangerous to children, and already inside Lucia Mar's head.

### Examined by Grigori

Samantha spends much of the floor trying to get the fleshmancer **[Grigori the Placid](grigori.md)**
to examine her own withering-spirit condition, at one point catching him trying to avoid her
entirely: "I knew it! You've been hiding him from me!" She succeeds in dragging him off for a
private examination, but the book never shows what he finds or says.

### Saying Goodbye to Louis

Samantha spends most of Floor 10 loudly courting [Louis Santiago](louis-santiago.md), and takes his
departure through the Desperado Club's casino portal harder than anyone — sobbing on his shoulder,
proposing they simply shove Florin and Lucia Mar through instead, and cataloguing, at length, why
nobody left in the party is an acceptable replacement affair ("Elle is too much woman for me to
handle... Donut is too star-crossed with that orange kitty"). She offers to go with him and he tells
her no.

She turns up in his story once more, in the book's closing interlude, but only as a voice: with
Louis disoriented in **Sheol** after his jump is secretly redirected, Samantha messages him over
chat. She is not physically present with him, and the text never says where she actually is — only
that Taranis and Krakaren are "at the temple" and that she "slipped away."

### Leaving With Taranis

At the Floor 11 climax, after the god **Taranis** is lured into the open by Carl's use of
[Penelope](penelope.md) as bait, Samantha slips away with him and the newly-departing
**Krakaren Prime** rather than staying with the party. Her explanation, delivered in the book's
final pages via chat to Louis, doubles as its last and biggest open thread:

> "I JUST HITCHED A RIDE WITH TARANIS AND KRAKAREN. THEY'RE AT THE TEMPLE, TRYING TO COMBINE A BUNCH
> OF THINGS TO MAKE WHOLE HIS MOM WIFE, BUT IT'S NOT GOING TO WORK BECAUSE THEY'RE MISSING A PIECE.
> I SLIPPED AWAY. IT'S ALL PART OF MY PLAN. I'M REALLY HERE TO KILL THE DEMON MOTHER LADY AND HER
