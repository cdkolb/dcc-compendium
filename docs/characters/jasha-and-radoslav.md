# Jasha and Radoslav

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** NPCs — bugbear musicians, the recurring heart of **Team Free Love** on Floor 10
    - **Race:** Bugbear
    - **Class/Subclass:** Not applicable — non-combatant racers, not crawlers
    - **Level:** Not stated
    - **Team:** **Team Free Love**, a "hippie" van team (tie-dye aesthetic, Pan flute, bongos,
      tambourine, violin) that races alongside Carl and Donut across every heat of Floor 10; their
      third original member, **Alevtina**, dies in a crash during Heat 1
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None described — comic-relief racers, not fighters
    - **Notable items:** A modified van with a floating/hover upgrade
    - **Status:** **Deceased** — killed together near the end of Heat 6, by [Carl](carl.md), via a
      hidden bomb planted in their own beer cooler

## Overview

Jasha and Radoslav are Floor 10's longest-running running joke — a pair of tie-dyed bugbear
musicians Carl and Donut rescue in the very first heat and keep bumping into, garage to garage, race
to race, all the way through Heat 6. Then Carl kills them both on purpose, in cold blood, to buy
time for a rescue that has nothing to do with them, and the book asks the reader to sit with exactly
how ugly that decision is.

## Team Free Love

They're introduced in Heat 1 (Chapters 3–4), rescued by Carl and Donut after their minivan crashes;
their bandmate and third member, **Alevtina**, dies in the wreck. Donut's read on them at first
glance is "undercover cops" dressed as hippies — bugbears playing Pan flute, bongos, tambourine, and
violin, entirely earnest about it. They're heavily implied to be reincarnated or "awakened" NPCs
carrying fragmented memories from past lives across other floors, racing purely for the shot at
freedom that winning would represent.

From Heat 2 on they're a fixture of the cul-de-sac — beer on the garage lawn chairs, comic
sideline commentary on every other team's disasters, a running presence through the alliance that
forms around the Heat 2 boss fight. They pitch in without being asked: at the end of Heat 5 they're
the ones who find the unconscious, "cheating" unicorn **Dwight** spinning in circles outside the
exit and physically drag him back in, over their own objection ("You really think we should save
him? He's the worst one!").

## The Bomb

The kill happens in Chapter 76, near the end of Heat 6 — a race through a shrunk-down human
apartment building, collecting seven hidden gates floor by floor. Carl's team has just cleared its
final gate with minutes left on the clock when an ally radios that **Prepotente** has gone
near-suicidal, immobile inside an AI-generated "what-if" vision of his own lonely mother, and someone
has to physically go get him (see [Prepotente](prepotente.md)). Carl is the only one who can. Doing
it costs time his team does not have — and coming in last, on this heat, is fatal.

This is not a fight, and it is not an accident. It's premeditated triage, planned before the race
even started:

> "How many NPCs had we killed at this point? What were two more?"

Carl had already infiltrated Team Free Love's cooler on the cul-de-sac *before the heat started*,
using his *Oozy Form* spell for the first time while Samantha distracted the two bugbears as cover,
and swapped one of their peach-flavoured Busch Lights — the flavour nobody ever drinks — for a
drilled-out, identical can built in his own "bomber's studio," undetectable without a level-12+
Find Traps skill. An enchanted cooler that automatically returns to their van after every race guarantees the
bomb is always in position as a fail-safe. He carries the detonator in his own interface the whole
heat, dreading it:

> They did not wave, and I did not wave back. We all knew what had to happen next. I felt a chill,
> looking at them. I had been afraid that the dungeon wouldn't allow me to use my Oozy Form spell
> in the cul-de-sac, but it had let me. I almost wished it hadn't. Fuck everything about this place.

He detonates it from a distance, mid-crisis, with a single line of narration standing in for
everything he doesn't let himself feel in the moment:

> I pulled up my explosives menu, scrolled to the Primed subtab, and found the detonator I was
> looking for. Fuck everything about this place. "Forgive me, friends," I whispered. I clicked
> Detonate.

The system confirms the kill flatly, folding two names into a bracket update:

> **Team Free Love has been eliminated due to the death of both racers. Three teams remain in the
> current heat.**

Neither Jasha nor Radoslav gets any final words on the page. There's no cutaway to their side of it
— Carl narrates the act retrospectively, analytically, immediately followed by the one line that
gives away how little the tactical logic actually helps him:

> That was the logic, but it wasn't reality. They were our friends, and I felt like an utter piece
> of shit. That was the point, of course. But it still hurt. At least it's not another crawler, I
> thought.

## Afterward

The reaction is muted rather than confrontational — no one in the party calls Carl out over it
on-page. Passing the wreckage of the van on the way out, the only response shown is wordless:

> On the way there, in the hallway, we passed the wreckage of team Free Love's van. We'd killed
> them just before they'd hit the finish line. Donut jumped from the seat and into my lap upon
> seeing that, and I held her tight as we approached the exit.

Heat 6's final standings list the consequence in the same clinical register as the kill itself:
**Eliminated: Team Free Love.**

!!! note "A joint page, by design"
    Jasha and Radoslav are treated here as a single entry rather than two, matching how the text
    itself handles them — as an inseparable pair, killed in the same instant, credited and mourned
    as one unit ("both racers"). No distinguishing personality traits or individual dialogue are
    given to either bugbear separately anywhere in Book 8.
