# Odette

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Role:** Host of *Dungeon Crawler After Hours with Odette*
    - **Type:** NPC / former crawler, now corporate figure
    - **Race:** Human ex-crawler; broadcasts using a bug-helmet, crab-body holographic persona
    - **Status:** Alive, married to the president of the corporation that bought out her prior
      criminal warrant (confirmed by Odette herself in Book 6). As of Book 6 she has put her show on
      hiatus and entered the dungeon personally, sponsoring the resurrected goddess **Nekhebit** in
      the Celestial Ascendency. In Book 7 she is on the 12th floor **piloting Nekhebit in person**,
      and names [Princess Donut](princess-donut.md) her **Champion**
    - **Notable spells/abilities:** None described — hosts remotely via hologram/avatar. From Book 7
      she wields Nekhebit's divine powers by piloting the goddess directly, and Nekhebit's "Court"
      hands out spells to worshippers (Book 7)
    - **Notable items:** None described

## Overview

Odette is a human ex-crawler who made it to the stairs of Floor 13 before striking an unexplained
"deal" and stepping away from the crawl. She's hosted *Dungeon Crawler After Hours* for
generations, trained Mordecai during his own crawl, and has run a secret off-the-books rescue
operation since at least Book 4 — extracting surviving Earth humans from the "Walk-On List" bounty
market before rival hunters can capture and sell them.

## Role in the Story — Book 5

Odette reveals to Carl and Donut, just before airtime, that Beatrice — Carl's ex and Donut's
original owner — is alive, and that Odette personally arranged her rescue in order to protect her
own investment in the pair (and to pre-empt a rival production planning to ambush Carl with the
same reveal). She stages the reunion as a serious, non-comedic segment, framing Bea's survival
through a false "Borant heroically intervened" cover story in exchange for a higher revenue share
on the remainder of the crawl's broadcast rights — a deal Odette insists was "an afterthought" next
to protecting her investment in Carl and Donut, though Carl remains openly skeptical of that claim.

Odette also warns Carl and Donut, through her assistant Lexis, about the sixth floor's Butcher's
Masquerade and its coming corporate upheaval — feeding them dangerous advance information
(including that Queen Imogen is a repurposed human and that the party is being engineered to end
in a mass battle) indirectly through Lexis rather than risk saying it herself. She confirms she
personally caused something significant enough to Mordecai in the past that he still refuses to
name it, telling Carl to "get the story from him." Separately, Mordecai reveals he once testified
*against* Odette at her own criminal trial, from back when Odette was serving as his manager (not
during her own crawl) — though the underlying incident is left unstated. Odette does not attend the masquerade itself;
during the party it is a different sponsor-aligned celebrity, Empress D'Nadia, who taunts the elite
Signet about the crawlers' odds of survival.

## Role in the Story — Book 6

Odette stops being a talk-show host in Book 6 and becomes a player. The book opens with her worst
act, told from Mordecai's point of view, and Chapters 35–37 are her explaining it.

### The Prologue

Book 6's prologue is Odette as Mordecai's manager, generations ago, on the tenth floor. See
[Mordecai](mordecai.md#book-6-the-prologue-how-uzzi-died) for the full account. In short: she bribed
the grixist heiress **Huanxin Jinx** through her friend **Armita** to have the storm goddess Dodola
open a stairwell puzzle; Huanxin kept escalating her price; Odette broke the deal at the last minute
and instead had the crawler **Chaco** put a Dart of Ophiotaurus into Mordecai's brother **Uzzi**,
force-summoning Dodola into the corpse inside a containment circle so she could be killed there. It
worked. Mordecai reached the eleventh floor. Uzzi did not.

### On Air

Her final show before hiatus is not a confession — it is a press conference. Asked why she can't be
the Princess Posse's Faction Wars adjutant:

> "Oh, believe me, I *was* going to ask to do it, but I received an opportunity I can't pass up…
> **That's right folks. The rumors are true. My show will soon be put on hiatus as I step into the
> dungeon. I have sponsored a deity, and I will be attempting my hand at the Celestial Ascendency.**"

The deity is **Nekhebit**, a massive Egyptian vulture goddess just resurrected on-floor by four
unrelated crawlers hatching a speckled egg — slender human body, red dress, yellow vulture head, oak
bow, and covered in healed wounds "almost like she'd been a corpse that had been exhumed."

> "**The origin story of Nekhebit is one of betrayal and revenge. She has earned every one of those
> scars. Whether she is considered a villain or a hero depends on one's perspective.**"
>
> "**Unlike all the other resurrected gods and goddesses on this floor, Nekhebit is not a retainer to
> any existing gods or goddesses. And yes, she will have a retainer of her own.**"

Carl reads it correctly: Huanxin was planning something, and Odette is refusing to let her have the
advantage. She is going into the dungeon on equal footing.

The adjutant refusal has a second reason, which she gives him privately: adjutants stand physically
on the battlefield, and Huanxin wanted her there "so she could legally exact her revenge." Her line
to Carl: "**Oh, I'm sorry. I didn't allow you to set me up for an assassination?**" Carl picks
**Baroness Victory** instead.

### The Confession

Chapter 37 is a private, off-the-record conversation engineered by having a Syndicate probe shot out
of orbit to trigger an all-hands and pull Zev away. Fifteen minutes, her assistant Lexis the only
witness.

Her stated motive for everything: "You think my motivations are money. Or fame. They're not. **It's
about Mordecai. It always has been.**"

- **Pieter and Armita.** Her cousin Pieter, about thirteen, and her lifelong friend Armita, about
  seventeen, were taken into the dungeon with her. "Eventually, they forced me to choose. Pieter or
  Armita. I picked my friend. Simple as that. It was the right choice. Still, I think sometimes, I
  wonder… I had pushed them all. *One more floor*, I'd said… They'd all died because of it."
- **Why she became a game guide** rather than a Celestial Attendant, which is what Armita took: "I
  didn't want to have anything to do with them. Those rich assholes running it all." She became
  Mordecai's manager and promised to get him out. "**He reminded me so much of Pieter, the way he
  looked up to his brother. Once, he'd transformed into a human, and he even looked like him.**"
- **The bribe, in her own words:** "I made an illegal deal with Huanxin to rig the game in exchange
  for credits. It was facilitated by Armita… But it went bad. She was driving a goddess named
  Dodola, and she'd asked for more money, and I was so tired of it. I felt just like you feel right
  now, Carl. I broke the deal at the last minute. I tricked her. We trapped the goddess in a
  containment, and Chaco killed her, causing her to lose the Ascendency game before it even started.
  Mordecai made it to the next floor, but **Uzzi, Mordecai's brother, died**."
- **Armita's death, the arrest, the warrant.** Out of guilt, she filled out a voluntary
  crime-admission form after her release — "I was sure Armita would've turned me in, but she hadn't…
  she remained my friend while I betrayed her." Nobody read it in time. "**The very first thing
  Huanxin did when she returned to the dungeon the following season was kill Armita.** It was like it
  was nothing. After all, it was the dungeon. Perfectly legal. But then some bureaucrat finally read
  my statement, and **I was arrested.** Huanxin was given nothing more than a token punishment…
  **my warrant was purchased by the man who'd eventually become my husband.**" She has also never had
  her legs fixed: "It felt wrong."
- **Watching Mordecai.** "I watch him every day… drinking himself into oblivion. Crying over his
  brother… *I did that. I killed Pieter. I killed Armita. And this one. This one I broke.*"
- **The title of the book comes from her.** "We had a saying, my people. ***Never stare into the
  blinding eye of the Bedlam Bride.*** What it means is don't become obsessed with something, lest
  you're blinded to everything else. When it shows itself, it's all you can see. And that's exactly
  what I'd done."

She has set aside a large trust for Mordecai and for Chaco. Her plan against Huanxin, she says, is
"going to kill me, most likely. But that's okay. It'll kill her, too."

Carl is not moved: "*You're a part of it*… You are the highest paid cheerleader for all of this. We
die, and you applaud." Her exit line: "**See you on the inside, Carl.**"

### What the Crawls Are For

Also in Chapter 37, almost in passing, Odette gives away the largest piece of worldbuilding in the
book. See [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for the mechanic itself; this is her delivery of it:

> "This whole machine exists for a reason beyond what you see. **The mantises mine and resurrect the
> AIs and then graft them into the planetary engines for a reason. The elements they mine, what do
> you think they're for?**"
>
> "**All planets used for a crawl or any other game, such as Battle Royale or Land War are used to
> collect fuel. The elements. The byproduct of the biological overgrowth of the seeded systems. The
> entertainment aspect of it all came along later, but it's necessary in its own way. It's
> expensive, what they do. But without it, it all gets shut off. All of it. We need to shut the
> crawl down, but we have to do it in a responsible way. Lest we kill half the known universe. If we
> do this right, future collections will be humane. It will be done in a way that removes all the
> suffering. What I did to Mordecai. To Armita. To Pieter. What is happening to you. It will never
> happen again. And it's all coming together this season. This is our chance. Finally, after all
> these centuries. It's our chance.**"

Carl's entire response is "What the hell are you talking about?" Lexis cuts the conversation off
seconds later, and the reveal is never elaborated on again in Book 6.

## Book 7: This Inevitable Ruin

Odette is off-screen for most of Floor 9 and her fingerprints are on a great deal of it. She is on the
12th floor, inside the Celestial Ascendency, and everything she does reaches Larracos secondhand — through
a goddess, through a title dumped onto Donut without warning, and through a trail of dead gods.

### Piloting Nekhebit

Book 6 had her “sponsoring” Nekhebit. Book 7 makes clear what that means in practice: an Ascendency player
does not stand behind their deity, they get inside it and drive it. The text uses “piloting” and “driving”
interchangeably. Carl’s running tally of who is inside whom: “Eileithyia, who was being driven by Huanxin
Jinx, wasn’t a surprise... Odette was driving Nekhebit, and the Open Intellect’s agent, Rectrix, was driving
Theia.”

The mechanic has a physical layer called **soul armor**. When Donut’s *Laundry Day* strips the divine armor
off a summoned goddess, the pilot is “violently ripped from the soul armor of the goddess” and dumped on the
floor as their ordinary, mortal self — which is exactly how Huanxin Jinx dies at the end of the book. The
same thing had already happened to the influencer Nami, whose ejection left her a helpless human in the
middle of a battlefield.

Piloting was optional until Ferdinand, prompted by Juice Box, burned a Team Retribution emergency action item
that made it mandatory:

> The second action item has been approved. Effective immediately, the following ascendency rules are
> applied. Ascendency players may no longer choose whether to pilot their sponsored deities should they be
> summoned off the 12th floor. Attendance is now mandatory. In addition, during gameplay summons, ascendency
> players may no longer choose to appear as themselves. All deities will appear as designed, and all
> sponsored deities will be piloted by their sponsors. Any summons currently in progress where the pilot
> stayed back because she was being a cowardly little chicken have been corrected.

### Hosting the Warlord Council in Person

The Faction Wars warlord council (Chapter 42) collapses before it starts: King Stalwart punches the
correspondent, the former crawler **Chaco**, hard enough to knock him out of the room. Roughly two minutes
later, Odette appears in his place — and not as the crab-bodied, bug-masked persona, but as herself.

> There was a flash, and to everybody’s surprise, a new figure appeared in the room. It was Odette, but she
> wasn’t in her usual costume. She was without her mask, her breastplate, and her crab body. It was just her
> floating in her wheelchair. She wore a simple, yellow shirt, and she had her normally bigger-than-life hair
> back in a bun. Her long fingernails were gone. She, too, was holding a microphone.

> “Hello, everyone,” Odette said, talking into the microphone. “I’m Odette. Yes, this is me out of gear if
> you haven’t seen me like this before. I just onboarded into the Ascendency where I am playing Nekhebit.
> They asked me if I would fill in for my very old friend Chaco, who is feeling a little...” she paused and
> gave Stalwart a look, “punched in the face right now.”

She runs the meeting flawlessly — an ad read for the Ultima Corporation, a read on the unprecedented
*Scolopendra Stirs* warning, and a brutal interview of the drunk, disintegrating showrunner Cascadia. Her one
genuine slip is a twitch of the eye when Epitome Tagg mentions the Skull Empire’s finances: “Odette, I
realized, wasn’t used to having to hide her real reactions because she was usually behind a bug mask.”

The council’s substantive item is hers: she is the one who explains how the off-world teams are still talking
to their home systems despite Team Retribution’s live-feed blackout, by paying to rename their own news
programmes in the feed directory over and over. Carl burns an emergency action item to close the loophole,
and notes that Odette looked “kind of irritated” about it.

His read on her presence:

> The fact Odette was even here right now was a sign she was a master at this game. Was it possible she’d
> colluded with Chaco to get to this meeting? And how did she know so much about the activities outside the
> 12th floor? She was hosting this meeting like she’d never entered the dungeon. Was she getting prompted in
> real time?

### Champion of Nekhebit

Donut casts her third **Atrocity** in Chapter 76 and is immediately handed a new class and a title she did
not ask for:

> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut has received a new class.
>
> System Message: Warlord Princess Donut will now be referred to as The Oak Fell.
>
> System Message: The Oak Fell has been named Champion of Nekhebit.

Milk’s read: “it’s likely a side effect of some posturing that’s currently happening on the 12th. The fact
she was also named Champion means it was something that was set in motion by Nekhebit.” Rosetta’s read on
what the title actually costs:

> “It’s similar to worshiping the deity. Enemies of Nekhebit will receive a bounty if they kill her.” She’d
> lowered her voice, making certain Donut wasn’t in earshot. “Honestly, it’s not too unlike Conscription, but
> with a lot more autonomy for Donut. Should the goddess want her to do something, she can order her to do it
> or face a smite.”

Carl’s reaction, in full: *Fucking Odette*. Rosetta guesses the mechanism was a standing spell — “something
along the lines, ‘the first crawler to cast three atrocities on the next floor gets the title and class.’”

Donut is delighted rather than alarmed. Earlier, having picked up a spell from a god called **Wepwawet**, she
had already worked out the connection: “Carl, Carl, it says he’s in the ‘Court of Nekhebit!’ and I can worship
Odette now, too!” She keeps the title to the end of the book, and the goddess Eileithyia signs off on it:
“That is wisdom, champion of Nekhebit, harbinger of doom.”

### Clearing Out a Sub-Pantheon

The thread runs under the whole book. Carl kills the bug-headed god **Khepri** on the ninth floor, and Khepri’s
own description had already flagged the pattern — he “was a retainer of the god Khnum, but like several of the
other deities in this particular sub-pantheon, Khnum mysteriously dropped dead a few days back,” leaving Khepri
scrambling for a new patron.

Carl puts it together afterwards:

> Khepri’s original description had given me a pretty big hint about some things that were currently going down
> on the 12th floor. He and Odette’s Nekhebit were in the same sub-pantheon. Someone was going through and
> killing off all the gods in that group. I figured it was either Odette for some reason or possibly Huanxin
> Jinx. I didn’t know enough to form a proper guess.

Two other pieces point the same way. The dying god **Hellik**, passing a message to his brother Emberus through
Carl, names her directly: “Tell him the All Tree is beginning to rot. Tell him Nekhebit has returned, and I
believe she is behind it all.” And Huanxin Jinx, arriving in the last chapter, confirms at least one kill in
passing while complaining about the mess Larracos has made of the 12th floor:

> “The whole court is in a tizzy about that city you fools just teleported in. It splattered an entire convent
> of Resheph’s nuns, and he is losing his mind. And now all those zombies are wandering all over the place and
> fucking Odette has taken control of them because she’s already taken out Inpewt, and it’s a big mess and the
> games haven’t even started yet.”

!!! note "Unresolved"
    The campaign is established but never narrated. No scene shows Odette killing a god, and no count is given.
    Her Book 6 stated aim was Huanxin Jinx — “It’s going to kill me, most likely. But that’s okay. It’ll kill
    her, too.” — and Book 7 kills Huanxin without Odette present, by Donut’s *Laundry Day* and a crossbow bolt
    packed with Mordecai’s mother’s ashes. Whether the sub-pantheon purge was aimed at Huanxin, at the Ascendent
    throne, or at something else is not answered on the page.

### Still Unforgiven

Nothing has thawed between her and Mordecai. Standing over Huanxin Jinx in the last chapter, he lays out who he
holds responsible: “My brother died because of you and because of Odette and because of Chaco. But mostly
because of you.”
