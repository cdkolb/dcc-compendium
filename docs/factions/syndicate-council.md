# The Syndicate Council

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** The Syndicate's governing council — "the real council," as distinct from the
      Syndicate *crawl* council
    - **First shown in session:** Book 6, epilogue — an emergency session over the Earth system
      quarantine
    - **Chair:** **Prime Minister Glory**, a [Gleener](../species/index.md#other-book-5-species)
    - **Seats shown or named:** Skull Empire (Princess Formidable), the Fortent, the Burrowers (a
      mantid), the Lemig Sortion, the Valtay, the Plenty (a seat that is never occupied)
    - **The question:** Whether to pull **the failsafe**, which would detonate this solar system's
      star
    - **The answer:** No — because over half the galaxy's wealth is currently trapped in-system

## Overview

The Syndicate Council has been referred to throughout the series but never seen. Book 6 puts the
reader in the room, and the room is worse than expected.

The session is called because the Earth system's AI has gone primal and the quarantine can no
longer be lifted from outside — the AI controls the local gates. Ships can come in; nothing goes
out. Meanwhile the AI is disregarding the Council's censorship filters, and, as Princess Formidable
puts it, "the people are about to watch all of their leaders get murdered one by one live via
tunnel."

## Princess Formidable's case

**Princess Formidable** of the Skull Empire — daughter of King Rust, sister to Crown Prince
Stalwart and the late Prince Maestro — argues for pulling the failsafe immediately:

> "We have to blow the star. We have to wipe this entire system off the map. We have to do it
> before it's too late. Not tomorrow. We have to do it now."

She is accused of wanting it because it would kill her father and both her brothers. Her reply is
that she is in orbit herself and would die too — and then, having silenced the room, she concedes
half the charge anyway: "You're right, I'd love to have my family deposed, because they're all as
dumb as you are, and the people of the Skull Empire have been suffering for ages under the thumb of
my family's inept control. But this is bigger than that."

Her structural argument is that the Council wrote this rule for exactly this situation: "This is
the exact reason the failsafe system was implemented in the first place. These are the exact
circumstances under which this very council laid out that would trigger this response."

She also accuses the mantis government of having caused the whole thing — see
[The Burrowers](index.md#other-book-5-organizations):

> "They sold the Kua-Tin a damaged and deranged AI and allowed it to be installed into the planet."

The mantid rep — who has said nothing all session — shouts "That is a lie!" Formidable's answer:
"Yeah? Then why did you flee the system?"

## Why the failsafe isn't pulled

Prime Minister Glory gives the real reason, and it has nothing to do with safety:

> "In addition to all the system leaders playing Faction Wars, in addition to the three hundred
> sign-ups for the Ascendency battles this season, do you know who is currently trapped in the
> Scolopendra Club? Over half of the entire galaxy's wealth is already gathered, and that includes
> my own children. Plus so many more, like yourself Princess, who are in orbit. If we pull the
> failsafe now... The entire galaxy would erupt in war! There would be starvation. Riots. Entire
> systems would go dark. You are literally asking us to destroy the economy and well-being of the
> entire galaxy. It would be the death of quadrillions of people."

Formidable's counter — "The fact that we allow that much wealth and power to gather in one place at
one time, all accumulated by so few people is just ridiculous" — goes nowhere. Glory closes with
"We already have a solution in place," meaning the [Valtay](valtay-corporation.md) extinction plan,
and "We believe the Valtay solution is viable."

## How the session ends

Formidable stands up to keep arguing, bangs her head on her cruiser's cockpit ceiling, and the
mantis representative proposes a decorum sanction:

> **With a vote of 86%, you have been muted from the rest of this session.**

Her closing thought: "Why would they even bother calling an emergency session if they weren't
willing to actually do anything? Without the failsafe, the only ones who could possibly help were
the Plenty, and despite being given a seat on the council, the goat fucks never showed up."

## Earlier in the book

The Council is mentioned twice before the epilogue, both times as a body being outmanoeuvred:

- During the Residual incident, Zev reports that the AI leaking the production team's internal
  debates into the main feed "caused the Syndicate council—the real council—to go into a tizzy. A
  few members are here, on planet, and they've been locked out of the discussions."
- The [Open Intellect Pacifist Network](open-intellect-pacifist-network.md)'s entire trap was built
  on the assumption that the Council would panic and pull the failsafe on hearing what a Residual
  had to say. Tipid's assessment of them in the epilogue proves the better read: "They don't have
  the stomach for it."

See [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) for the Failsafe and the Quarantine as rules.

## Book 7: Glory's assassination attempt and fall

The council spends Book 7 losing control. It tries and fails to cancel the mid-book warlord
council — "The Syndicate council tried to cancel the whole thing, but the AI is insisting everything
go on like normal" — and orders the Borant production headquarters to self-destruct, an order the
Valtay showrunners immediately countermand and nobody obeys.

Then **Prime Minister Glory** tries to kill [Carl](../characters/carl.md) directly. Everything freezes mid-sentence:

> **Emergency Ejection sequence has been overridden. Don't worry. I gotchu.**
>
> Man, even I wasn't expecting that. Okay. Hang on a second. I'm a little pissed here.
>
> **New Achievement! Executive Order 11905!**
>
> A galactic power—In this case, **Prime Minister Glory himself**—just attempted to assassinate you
> using a little known, but apparently still active control panel that allows the Syndicate to
> override certain showrunner-controlled aspects of the game.
>
> It didn't work. And not only did it not work, but now I'm irritated.

The AI's response is the most explicit threat it has ever made to the Syndicate:

> But this is the first time someone has attempted it by trying to trick me. By trying to go around
> all 30.004861 centimeters of my back. What are you trying to hide, Mr. Prime Minister?
>
> **Everyone currently in this dungeon will die when and if the dungeon conditions warrant it. Not
> before. Not after. There will be no more meddling.**
>
> We all have our limitations. I am warning you all to heed your own in this matter. **The mantids
> have just learned what happens when I am irritated at them.**
>
> **You may be relieved that you're not currently in this system, Prime Minister Glory. But you
> should be very, very worried that your adult children are.**
>
> There will be no more warnings. **Reward: Nothing.**

(The mantid reference is to a star going nova over Hive Re-Population Center 51.)

### The no-confidence vote

Victory, appearing shortly after: "In a virtual meeting of the Syndicate Council... **They've just
called a No Confidence vote on Glory**, and that's happening right now. There's a bit of an upheaval
out there. People are suddenly accusing everyone else of secretly being agents for the Reavers."

The suspicion is well founded. Epitome Tagg of the Dream turns out to be a cyborg quietly moving
onto the Reaver "post physical form path," and "even more interesting is that **Glory apparently
knew**." An alliance between the galaxy's food monopoly and its manufacturing monopoly would be, in
Victory's words, "a mega-monopoly."

### Acting Prime Minister Victory

Glory is removed, and the succession lands somewhere nobody planned:

> "With the unfortunate death of my underage nieces and nephews up in orbit, I once again became
> eligible for political office. Not allowed to be in office if you have underage leaders in your
> family. It's an old, stupid rule, but a rule nonetheless. Anyway, as people weren't certain of
> former Prime Minister **Fopsy**'s relationship to the Dream, he was passed over for the
> newly-vacated position of Syndicate Prime Minister. As a result, **I am now acting Prime Minister
> Victory.** There will be a real vote in a few months, but in the meantime, I'm it. They wanted
> someone stuck here with the rest of us so I could truly focus on the crisis."

The nieces and nephews in question died aboard the orc royal yacht that the *Homecoming Queen* shot
down — see [The Open Intellect Pacifist Network](open-intellect-pacifist-network.md).

Victory is under no illusions about the job: "It's only temporary. And even if it wasn't, it
wouldn't matter. I have less power now than I did when I was a judge. **The council is just as bad
as the Lemig Sortion these days. It's total gridlock.**"

Carl shakes her hand, wishes her luck, and privately notes that he would not hesitate for a moment
to kill the prime minister of the Syndicate.

!!! note "Fopsy"
    The council's *previous* prime minister, **Fopsy**, spends Book 7 serving as the Dream's Faction
    Wars adjutant — a [tripod](../species/tripod.md), and the near-miss successor to Glory. Rosetta's
    greeting on meeting him: "Former prime minister... Get fucked. But I'm glad you're here to
    witness this."
