# The Faction Wars Campaign (Floor 9)

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Book:** 7, *This Inevitable Ruin*
    - **Setting:** The [plains](../locations/plains-of-larracos.md) and [city of Larracos](../locations/larracos.md), the ninth floor
    - **Format:** Ten teams — nine attacking, one defending — arranged "like a pie with Larracos
      in the center"
    - **Opening alignment:** eight sponsored teams form **The Bloc** and declare war on both the
      Princess Posse and the NPCs' **Team Retribution**; those two form **The Good Guys**
    - **Winner:** **The Princess Posse**

This page covers how Faction Wars actually *played out*. For the roster as it stood at the end of
Book 6 — the warlords, the preproduction meeting, the mass abdication — see
[The Faction Wars Teams](faction-wars-teams.md).

## The rules Cascadia announces

Cascadia's opening broadcast (audibly drunk or stoned, per Carl) sets the terms:

- Ten teams total: nine attacking, one defending. The defenders are the NPCs, **Team
  Retribution**, headquartered in Larracos. Everyone else holds a numbered wedge of the
  surrounding **Plains of Larracos**.
- A **60-hour ceasefire** opens the floor. When it ends, only Team Retribution, unaffiliated
  NPCs, and Team Retribution's *allies* may remain inside the city.
- Crawlers may freely enlist with any team during the ceasefire. Crawlers who don't enlist can be
  **conscripted** by force once the fighting starts.
- The city stays sealed **until four teams remain**, at which point the final phase — **"The
  Peeling of Larracos"** — begins. The defenders win by holding a full week; an attacker wins by
  holding the throne room for six hours.
- **Only individual teams, not alliances, may take the throne room.**
- An attacking team is eliminated when its warlord(s) die **or** its own primary throne room is
  held by an enemy for six hours.
- 293 stairwell chambers are seeded across the floor, but none open until Faction Wars is won or
  30 hours remain to floor collapse.

Two other rules changes matter enormously. The AI altered the **15K rule** to let outside
mercenaries in: each of the eight sponsored teams receives roughly **20,000 hired mercenaries**
(≈150,000 total), while the Princess Posse receives **just over 50,000 former crawlers** shipped
in aboard the *Homecoming Queen*. [Baroness Victory](../characters/baroness-victory.md)'s read on the opposition is that most of them
are security contractors, not soldiers: "These 'warlords' are called that in name only... They'll
be looking for a single punch knock-out."

## Opening alignment: the Bloc versus the Good Guys

Within minutes of Carl and Donut's arrival, the eight other sponsored teams line up against them:

> Warning: The Bone Clan has announced a formal truce with The Operatic Collective.
>
> The Blood Sultanate has joined the truce.
> The Prism Kingdom has joined the truce.
> The Dream has joined the truce.
> The Lemig Sortion has joined the truce.
> The Madness has joined the truce.
> The Reavers have joined the truce.
>
> **The eight teams within this truce have been named "The Bloc."**
>
> **The Bloc has declared war against Team Retribution.**
>
> **The Bloc has declared war against the Princess Posse. That's you!**

Donut, still sitting in the mud reading the warlord help files, accepts Team Retribution's
counter-offer unilaterally before Carl can finish his sentence — "But before we accept the
truce..." — and the counter-alliance is formed on the spot. She names it, and Ferdinand
immediately renames it:

> **Warlord Donut of the Princess Posse has named the truce "The Good Guys."**
>
> **Warlord Ferdinand of Team Retribution has renamed the truce "Ferdinand and his Bird."**

Donut: "Hey! Carl, it won't let me change it back!" The name reverts to **The Good Guys** later in
the book.

The practical effect of Donut's snap decision is enormous: as Team Retribution's ally, the
Princess Posse is **not** ejected from Larracos when the ceasefire expires.

## The Skull Empire's name change

The orc team competes in Book 7 as **the Bone Clan** — the Skull Empire's Faction Wars banner —
and its warlord is **War Chief / King Stalwart**. Their wedge, spot 1, holds **The King's Point**,
the only one of the nine wedges that comes with a pre-built castle. Likewise, the Lemig team is
listed throughout Book 7 as **the Democratic Sortion**, and the Prism Kingdom's team is called
simply **the Prism**.

The Princess Posse's own stronghold is the **Fortress of Unyielding Power and Authority** —
which the AI refuses to let Donut rename once Carl works out the acronym. Everyone, including the
system, calls it **the FUPA**.

## Mid-book: the Bloc fractures

Three shocks land almost at once.

**King Rust dies.** The orc king marches his horde to the Posse's line in person to deliver a
warning — his daughter **Princess Formidable** has seized a Skull Empire cruiser and is hours from
reaching the monitor station where she intends to **manually trigger the system failsafe**, which
would kill everyone in the system. [Rosetta](../characters/rosetta-thagra.md) kills him on the spot. (Formidable never reaches it;
the AI pulls her off her own ship and drops her in the ocean.)

**The orcs' backdoors are exposed.** Rust confirms it himself before he dies:

> "The Skull Empire manufactures the satellites. We build backdoors into everything we manufacture
> so they can't be used against us. This was a secret until my daughter announced it to the whole
> galaxy."

Volteeg's POV chapter gives the Bloc's-eye view of what Formidable's broadcast actually did to the
alliance: "Not only are you all going to die, but guess what? My family has been spying on you this
whole time. Also, you know all that military hardware you've been buying from us for thousands of
years? We have a button that can turn it all off with a flick of a switch. We can even make it turn
against you." Every remaining team begins **deactivating all Skull-Empire-manufactured hardware** —
Epitome Tagg says so on camera at the warlord council, and one of the Dream's own news channels is
caught renaming itself *Yes. We have Deactivated the Infected Hardware* as a covert signal.

**The naga plot leaks.** Rishi later explains that "just before the meeting of the warlords, news
broke that laid out our plans regarding our intention to renege on our shipyard contracts, which
caused immediate chaos in our system" — see [The Great Consensus](the-great-consensus.md).

The result:

> **Warlord Message. The Bone Clan is no longer a member of the Bloc.**
>
> **Warlord Message. The Dream is no longer a member of the Bloc.**
>
> Alliance Update:
>
> The Bloc Alliance: The Blood Sultanate / The Madness / The Reavers
>
> The Good Guys Alliance: Team Retribution / The Princess Posse
>
> Unaffiliated: The Bone Clan. The Dream.

Donut's reaction: "Wow. What happened? I was expecting the orc guys to break up because of that
whole 'We've been spying on you' thing. But what happened with the bald Dream guys?" Carl's read:
"The fact everyone is making these moves now suggests they already know the failsafe isn't going
off."

## Notable betrayals and self-inflicted wounds

**Commander Stockade cheats and destroys his own team.** Stockade and **[Architect Houston](../characters/architect-houston.md)** of the
Madness conspire to use the secured messaging system to reach **Nurse Yugoslav of the Madness** on
the 18th floor, who relayed live feed intelligence through a third party in the Celestial
Ascendency. Their plan was to extract Shi Maria from Carl and turn him into a vegetable while the
spider killed everyone in the Posse's base. It goes catastrophically wrong: the spider manifests in
the *Lemig* throne room instead and slaughters fifty-odd Lemig, including Stockade's beloved Luke.
Adjutant Panford's verdict:

> "What happened is exactly what always happens when you attempt to improperly extract an entity
> that powerful. This is your fault... Unless you can prove you have a mole in the Princess Posse
> camp, I am going to assume you have an external influence assisting you, which is clearly
> cheating."

**Houston sacrifices Stockade.** Houston refuses to confess, and the quorum clears him — Victory:
"Houston is obviously guilty, but unless he admits it, he was smart enough to let Stockade do all
of his dirty work." When Stockade, hit with a delayed dose of the spider's Insanity spell, begins
beating his own face to pulp on the floor, Victory offers Houston the chance to intervene: "Feel
free to help him. Or call his guards." Houston does not move; he stares at Carl the whole time. The
payoff is procedural — Stockade's death is a **voluntary withdrawal**, which triggers acceleration
scenario two, **shortening the ceasefire by 23.33 hours** and ending it instantly, and hands every
Lemig asset to the Madness. Houston's private message to Carl beforehand: "You are in mortal danger
at this very moment, and you don't even realize it."

**The Dream and the Madness squabble over the vacated Prism castle — exactly as Carl predicted.**
After killing D'Nadia, Carl can't hold non-contiguous land, so he parks a captured Madness
mercenary named **Lockjaw** in the Prism throne room with a chair and snacks:

> "By making the Madness mercenary sit in there, it would at least force the other leaders to make
> a decision. If Epitome Tagg was as much of an ass as I suspected, he was going to make a move and
> usurp poor Lockjaw out of that room. That would, hopefully, piss off Architect Houston of the
> Madness. I had the impression none of these leaders liked each other very much. So hopefully this
> would sow a little discord."

It works. The castle "changed hands for the third time, which reset the six-hour clock... as if the
Madness and the Dream were fighting over who had the rights to the sliver of land. But as long as
they kept going back and forth, the castle remained Posse territory." Volteeg's chapter confirms the
knock-on: "The Dream and the Madness were now bickering." The castle is only finally ceded to the
Dream after the Bloc has already fractured.

**The War Mage Rebellion breaks its own team's Conscription enchantment to kill its own king.**
Victory's post-mortem:

> "It appears the War Mages broke their own allegiance against the Skull Empire, which they
> shouldn't be able to do. They were under strict Conscription rules, but they used a clever
> combination of Pull spells to kill Stalwart anyway. I think Stalwart might've ejected them from
> his army on purpose in an attempt to get them to stop hurting him. It didn't work."

Stalwart's last words, as three war mages tear his flesh apart: *"My sister was right."*

**Agatha hijacks the War Mage Rebellion, and its actual leader asks Carl to kill her.** See
[The War Mage Rebellion](war-mage-rebellion.md).
