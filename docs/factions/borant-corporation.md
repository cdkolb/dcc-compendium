# Borant Corporation

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Media/mining corporation, current season operator of *Dungeon Crawler World*
    - **Owned by:** The kua-tin (an aquatic species)
    - **Role in Book 1:** Runs the dungeon Carl and Donut are trapped in; assigns Carl's PR agent (Zev)
    - **Rival:** The Squim Conglomerate (runs a battle-royale-format season instead)

## What They Are

Per the opening Syndicate announcement, Earth was seized under "the Mined Material Reclamation
act along with subsection 35 of the Indigenous Planetary Species Protection Act," and Borant —
"having been assigned regency over this solar system" — chose "option 3, also known as the
18-Level World Dungeon" as the method of resource reclamation. Borant retains "all rights to
broadcast, exploit, and otherwise control all aspects of the World Dungeon" as long as it follows
Syndicate regulations.

## Business Model

Per Mordecai, Borant's revenue comes from three sources: the mining operation itself, advertising
revenue from the broadcast, and "a commission on every credit spent by patrons" who sponsor
crawlers with loot boxes. The real money, per Mordecai, is in the show, not the mining: "It's a
big universe out there, and there are plenty of places to mine. That's not why they're really
here."

Not every season is run by Borant — different corporations rotate control of a given world's
season, and formats vary wildly by operator. The rival **Squim Conglomerate**, for example, runs
a "battle royale-style" season instead of a structured dungeon crawl. Borant is specifically
known, per Mordecai, for running "the most elaborate, most entertaining dungeons."

## Political Pressure

Later in Book 1, Zev reveals Borant is under real strain: the kua-tin system government (led by
the ultra-nationalist party **the Bloom**) forced an early release of this season for a cash grab,
even though Borant doesn't see any of the patron/advertising money until the season concludes.
Borant has already received a formal warning over "bugs" (design failures like the bathroom
punishment mechanic and overpowered mobs) — one more violation risks losing the season entirely.
Mordecai is careful to distinguish the corporation from its owners: "Some of those folks are
okay. All they want is to put on a good show," in contrast to "nobody likes the kua-tin, at least
not their system of government."

## Rules & Enforcement

The in-dungeon "system AI" monitors crawler speech about Borant and the Syndicate; disparaging
either on-camera risks having a crawler's "experience... 'accelerated'" — an ominous, undefined
threat.

## Book 2: Financial Crisis

Borant's finances become an explicit plot point once Carl and Donut reach Floor 3. Loot boxes are
taxed by the Syndicate per unit given out — Celestial-tier boxes especially, since a single one
can make an underperforming crawler "almost immortal" through Floor 9. Each season is allotted
only a handful of free Celestial boxes before the tax becomes ruinous; historically, only 18 have
ever been awarded in a single season (a Naga-run season, which lost money and effectively ended
that faction's ability to run its own crawls afterward).

A quest exploit engineered by Carl, Donut, and Katia — using stacked "Hadji" and "Bandit"
achievement upgrades — nearly forces Borant to pay out 83 Celestial Quest Boxes in one event.
Borant appeals to a Syndicate court and wins the right to void all quest rewards from the
incident using its single free veto for the season (normally saved for high-stakes rulings on
Floor 10+) — a decision made *before* the tenth floor for the first time in the season's history,
underscoring how close to the financial edge the company is running. One crawler who'd already
opened his box before the ruling keeps his; Borant is stuck paying for that single Celestial box
regardless.

Separately, an assassination attempt against Carl and Donut (ordered by the Skull Empire, see
[Skull Empire](index.md#other-book-2-organizations)) accidentally kills a popular Valtay-sponsored
singer instead, escalating tensions between two of Borant's most powerful rivals/creditors right
as the company can least afford instability.
