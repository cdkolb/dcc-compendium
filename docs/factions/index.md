# Factions & Organizations

Interstellar governing and broadcast powers — the entities running the show, as distinct from
groups encountered *inside* the dungeon itself (see [Dungeon Factions](../dungeon-factions/index.md))
and from the peoples/species themselves (see [Species](../species/index.md)).
Full pages exist for factions with real plot weight in Book 1; everything else is listed below.

## Full pages

- [Borant Corporation](borant-corporation.md) — current season operator; runs the dungeon as a broadcast
- [The Dark Hive](dark-hive.md) — Circe Took's private mantis hunting corporation, distinct from the mantis government
- [Nebular Sin Patrol](nebular-sin-patrol.md) — nomadic religious cult that fields hunter-priests every season

## The Big Six

The system AI's own explicit taxonomy of every power group vying for control of or escape from the
dungeon, delivered mid-fight during the Floor 10 Shadow Mimic ambush (see
[Mitch](../bosses/mitch.md)) after [Akuma](../characters/akuma.md) had already been circling the
same framework earlier in the book. This is a **worldbuilding/mechanics concept** (the AI's own
analytical framework for the endgame) rather than a faction in its own right, so the full quoted
breakdown of all six groups — including the still-unidentified "Group Six" — lives on the
[Game Mechanics page](../mechanics/index.md#the-big-six-book-8) rather than here, to avoid
duplicating the same quotes in two places.

See [Carl: Book 8](../characters/carl.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) and
[Mitch](../bosses/mitch.md#the-reveal) for the scene itself, and
[Species: Shadow Mimics](../species/shadow-mimics.md#as-one-of-the-big-six) for how one member
of Group Four is treated on its own dedicated page.

## Other Book 1 organizations

| Name | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The Syndicate | Interstellar government | Sets the overarching legal framework (resource-reclamation law, the World Dungeon format itself); dispatches the "neutral observer AI" narrator |
| Squim Conglomerate | Rival season operator | Runs a "battle royale-style" season instead of a structured dungeon; mentioned by Mordecai as less profitable than Borant's format |
| Valtay Corporation | Corporate system government | Referenced via found tech in Book 1; in Book 2, destroys a Skull Empire royal yacht in retaliation for the death of their sponsored singer Manasa, escalating a bankruptcy-collection dispute with the kua-tin into open hostility. Becomes Carl's own sponsor in Book 3, after his Floor 4 fight with [Gore-Gore the Mantaur](../bosses/gore-gore-the-mantaur.md); their slogan, "Keeping the Best of You Alive," ties to Book 1's revelation they're brain-parasite Intellect Hunters. Gifts Carl a permanent Neural Enhancer implant that lets him see through subspace portals — see [The Iron Tangle Railway System](../mechanics/the-iron-tangle-railway.md#subspace-portal-vision). **Book 5:** wins a Syndicate ruling acquiring 51% ownership of the Borant Corporation mid-Floor-6, formally severing Borant from the kua-tin home-system government; the crawl itself keeps running under the same kua-tin production staff, but the sixth floor's own AI begins quietly disregarding Syndicate court rulings it disagrees with in the fallout — see [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md#syndicate-warrants-corporate-franchising-book-5). **Book 6:** steps back from day-to-day production after the Floor 7 disaster, and then proposes an engineered extinction event as its "solution" to the quarantine — see [The Valtay Corporation](valtay-corporation.md) |
| The Plenty | Interstellar communications-technology company | A real caprid (goat-like) species who invented the modern real-time subspace tunnel network, replacing Borant's older, slower communications relays — Borant's in-dungeon "Pooka," goat-like drug-den distributors, are a deliberately unflattering caricature of them. **Book 5:** sponsors Carl, [Prepotente](../characters/prepotente.md), and [Miriam Dom](../characters/miriam-dom.md) directly; their children are shown at a CrawlCon art contest answering in creepy unison ("Judgment received"), and their own mythology includes a boogeyman figure, **the Midnight Epicure** ("the Caprid Who Devours the Sky"), whose ward-symbol doubles as a real anti-vampire/anti-lycanthrope charm |
| The Krakaren (real species) | Telepathic collective-mind species, sometimes called "the Apothecary" | A real, galaxy-spanning hive-mind species capable of synthesizing substances from whatever they consume; per Mordecai, Borant's in-dungeon "Krakaren" bosses (drug dealers, moonshiners) are a hostile caricature of them, part of the same anti-Plenty propaganda campaign — see [Dungeon Factions](../dungeon-factions/index.md) for the in-dungeon caricature itself. **Book 5:** sponsors Carl, Donut, and most of the rest of the top 10 as "the Apothecary"; a masked human-presenting spokeswoman appears in person at both a Floor 6 battlefield and Carl's CrawlCon panel, publicly egging him on against [Circe Took](../characters/circe-took.md) — heavily implied to be coordinating with the Plenty toward Carl and Donut's survival. **Book 6:** the epilogue reveals the Apothecary runs **many thousands of [Residual](../species/gondii-residuals.md) agents** seeded across Earth, all ordered to kill themselves if they fail to make it inside the dungeon — and that they are one side of a **shadow war** against the rival Residual faction that [Agatha](../characters/index.md) belongs to. Agatha obtained the Apothecary's informational feed and equipment by hunting down and killing one of those agents and taking over its body. Her own side numbers a handful against the Apothecary's thousands, and she concedes her competitors "were the only ones who ever had any sort of success so far" |

## Other Book 2 organizations

| Name | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sensation Entertainment, Incorporated | Independent production studio | Produces the serialized drama *Vengeance of the Daughter* (Tsarina Signet's storyline); not Kua-Tin owned — the Kua-Tin are noted as generally bad at scripted drama |
| Skull Empire | Orc royal dynasty | Ruled by King Rust; Prince Maestro is stripped of his title after the "Pork Boy" incident. Presumed killed alongside Queen Consort Ugloo in the Valtay yacht strike, but Book 3 reveals he survived — secretly smuggled into the dungeon by his brother beforehand — and resurfaces as Grull's sponsor during the Floor 4 [Mimic Rex Death Challenge](../bosses/mimic-rex.md), humiliated by Carl a third time. **Book 5:** fields Floor 6 hunters (including the ill-fated "The Talent" and "Future Huntress"); Prince Maestro's ongoing Faction Wars franchise ownership is revealed to run through a Skull-Empire-linked subsidiary — see [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md#syndicate-warrants-corporate-franchising-book-5) — and the Empire is the only other faction besides Carl's own Princess Posse willing to vote in favor of removing Faction Wars' sponsor safety protocols |
| The Prism Kingdom | Small but influential trade-focused kingdom | Ruled by Princess D'nadia (a Saccathian); formally condemns the Skull Empire's attack after it kills her friend Manasa. **Book 5:** D'Nadia — now **Empress** after her father's retirement — sponsors Donut, judges the Butcher's Masquerade's pet/talent contests in person, and claims one of two Faction Wars spots vacated when the Dreadnought Clan and the Burrowers both withdraw. **Book 6:** a full heel turn — sells Donut's sponsorship and burns all three Emergency Action Items trying to delete the Larracos NPCs, bar crawlers from Faction Wars, and delete Carl and Donut; all three rejected by the system. Now has its own page: [The Prism Kingdom](prism-kingdom.md) |

## Other Book 7 organizations

Book 7's corporate subplots share a theme: every one of them turns out to be lethal.

| Name | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dictum Waystation Controls, Limited | Waystation infrastructure corporation; crawler sponsor | **Murdered its own sponsored crawler to run an ad.** The mantis crawler **Everly** received a benefactor box from Dictum containing *Gordon's Compass of Vertical Labyrinth Management*, 49 uses, which guided her and ten mercenaries up a fifty-storey tower. At the top, instead of a fan box, was a folded paper note the system read aloud: *"Never trust your life to **Gordon Waystation Outfitters**. There's no safe or cheap path to safety. **This dead end was sponsored by Dictum Waystation Controls, Limited.** 'Don't get caught with nowhere to go.'"* The entire tower was an advertisement attacking a competitor, built by her own sponsor, and the ceiling begins crushing downward the moment she reads it. Her chapter supplies the book's title: *"The Inevitable Ruin."* The AI notes sponsored traps were "a pilot program this season" |
| Gordon Waystation Outfitters | Rival waystation corporation | Exists in Book 7 only as the target of Dictum's attack ad; the compass is named after it. Also rendered in the same passage as "Gordon Waystation Controls" |
| Colloid Bank | The orc faction's bank | "The only bank the orcs use." The instrument of **Rosetta's heist**: after killing King Rust she uses his signet ring, his eyeball and a password to move the Skull Empire's liquid reserves out — "'Transfer approved! Thank you for using Colloid Bank.'" She drops the datapad and sobs. A Valtay representative, fielding the furious call afterward: "They're trying to claw it back, but she's already transferred it to a credit chit, and it looks like she then sent it off... **Monetary transactions aren't covered in the communications blackout**... the only one with the authority to possibly have stopped it, maybe, just got splattered all over the playing field by one of his own." The money funds Porthus's ship purchases |
| Sensation Entertainment, Incorporated | Production studio | **Bought by a subsidiary of the Dream** between books, and immediately weaponized. Quasar: with Tsarina Signet dead, "that guarantee of a third season we negotiated is about to haunt the fuck out of us. Sensation Entertainment just got purchased by a subsidiary of the Dream, and things are moving. They're demanding their third season... the new showrunners won't be caring about that anymore. They're going to try to use your contractual obligation to ass fuck you." An elite subsequently tries to kill Donut under that cover; Baroness Victory calls it "an unlawful assassination attempt by Sensation Entertainment" and files a grievance against the Dream for "exerting undue influence on one of their elites to affect Faction Wars" |
| Blood Hunter Holdings | Naga-owned entertainment studio | Odette reveals on live television that the Blood Sultanate "fielded several dramas this season through their **Blood Hunter Holdings** studio" — including *Blood Hunter*. She winks at Carl as she says it; the revelation lands "like a meteor" |
| Icon Industries | Habitat-structures megacorporation | **Huanxin Jinx** continues to play the Celestial Ascendency through a sponsored deity (Eileithyia). She is killed on-page late in the book: "Huaxin Jinx, CEO of Icon industries rocketed backwards, slid across the floor, and came to a rest at Victory's feet, dead" |
| Hereford War Machines, Inc. | Arms manufacturer | Not mentioned in Book 7 |
| The Operatic Banker's Federation | Interstellar banking body | The Operatic Collective's financial arm; War Chief Stalwart invokes it when demanding his father's stolen credits back — "The banker's federation will not stand for this!" |
| Dictum (leaseholder) | Waystation landlord | In the epilogue, a ruined waystation operator named Zander owes "a 25,000 credit a month lease to Dictum I can't pay" after the embargo kills his traffic — the same corporation, seen from the bottom |
