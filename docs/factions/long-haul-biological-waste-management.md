# Long Haul Biological Waste Management Solutions

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Galactic corpse-disposal company, owned and crewed by the **Crest**
    - **Business model:** They show up after a crawl is over. "It wasn't until the last crawler drew
      their final breath before they started their grim task"
    - **Book 6 role:** Becomes [Princess Donut](../characters/princess-donut.md)'s new sponsor after
      Empress D'Nadia sells her sponsorship
    - **Their freighter:** the ***Homecoming Queen***, the largest ship in Earth orbit — twice the
      size of a Valtay warship
    - **Epilogue reveal:** The *Homecoming Queen* is a converted troop transport carrying **50,000
      former crawlers**, cleared to board Faction Wars as troopers for the **Princess Posse**

## Donut's new sponsor

Mid-battle on Floor 8, Donut gets a notification that
[Empress D'Nadia](prism-kingdom.md) has sold her sponsorship to someone else. Carl isn't surprised
— D'Nadia had overextended herself buying a Faction Wars spot, and she's about to be fighting
Donut. Donut is devastated, and then finds out who bought her:

> **Donut:** "MY NEW SPONSOR IS NAMED LONG HAUL BIOLOGICAL WASTE MANAGEMENT SOLUTIONS, AND I JUST
> CAN'T WITH THAT. I KNOW I'M SUPPOSED TO APPEAR GRATEFUL, BUT THAT SOUNDS ABSOLUTELY DISGUSTING.
> CARL, ARE THEY GOING TO SHOW COMMERCIALS FOR GARBAGE TRUCKS ON MY FEED? THIS IS NOT ACCEPTABLE."

Carl's reaction is quieter and more accurate: "*Biological* waste? I didn't like the sound of
that."

The joke lands twice. The first time, it's a status humiliation — a princess sponsored by a garbage
company. The second time is the epilogue.

## The *Homecoming Queen*

Princess Formidable, stewing in her cruiser after being muted by the Syndicate Council, notices a
ship she can't place:

> "Her eyes focused on a single, out-of-place garbage freighter floating not too far away. It was
> easily the largest ship in orbit. The thing was twice the size of one of those Valtay warships.
> It was one of the freighters from Long Haul Biological Waste Management Solutions.
>
> This particular ship's name was *Homecoming Queen*. She grunted. Those Crest always named their
> vessels the strangest things."

She writes it off as a corpse hauler that turned up early and got caught by the quarantine like
everyone else — "They usually didn't bother showing up until after the crawl was done." Then she
reconsiders:

> *But then again*, she thought, *they're probably right on time*.

## What's really aboard

The last scene of the book is inside that freighter's cockpit. **Tipid** and **Rosetta** — both
Crest, both former crawlers, both of the
[Open Intellect Pacifist Network](open-intellect-pacifist-network.md) — are watching Carl and
Donut's escape from Floor 8 on replay. Tipid converted the ship at enormous expense. The name is
newly given.

> **"You have been cleared for on-boarding."**
>
> "Okay, folks. We have been cleared for onboarding. The AI has approved our request. We are
> officially all troopers now for the Princess Posse."
>
> Cheers rose up through the hull. Fifty-thousand former crawlers, all hidden inside the
> newly-renamed *Homecoming Queen*. Once they made it onboard into the system, there was nothing
> anybody could do to stop it.

Their stated motive is not rescue. Rosetta, asked if she wants to back out: "And miss the
opportunity to really kill some of those bastards?" Tipid is under no illusion about the odds —
"I always knew I would die in the dungeon. Even after I got out, I always knew."

The corpse-disposal cover story is the point. A company whose entire business is arriving to
collect crawler bodies is the one organization in the galaxy that can park the largest ship in
orbit without anyone asking what's inside it.

!!! note "Sponsorship and the troop ship"
    Book 6 does not spell out whether Long Haul's purchase of Donut's sponsorship was itself part
    of the Open Intellect plan or an unrelated transaction the network later exploited. Tipid does
    mention paying for "that Celestial box we just sent Princess Donut," which strongly implies the
    two are the same operation, but the book never states it outright.
