# Character Stats & Progression

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Taught to the reader:** Book 1, chapters 3–4 — Mordecai's tutorial to Carl, the first
      thing any crawler learns
    - **Six stats total, five visible:** Strength, Intelligence, Constitution, Dexterity,
      Charisma — plus a hidden sixth, **Wisdom**, that never appears on the sheet
    - **Starting values:** Based on the crawler's real, pre-dungeon physical/mental profile; a
      typical adult human averages 3–5 in each of the first five stats
    - **Stat points:** +3 per level, but **undistributable** until a crawler reaches Floor 3 and
      picks a race and class (see [Race & Class Selection](race-and-class-selection.md))
    - **Base stat cap:** 300 (confirmed Book 7) — a threshold nobody in the story reaches until
      deep into Faction Wars

This page covers the core character system as Mordecai first lays it out for Carl, plus every
later book's additions to it. For the separate one-time choice of race/class on Floor 3, see
[Race & Class Selection](race-and-class-selection.md). For the minimap dot-color system, see
[Mob Tagging](index.md#mob-tagging) on the main mechanics page.

## The Crawler Menu

Access to a crawler's own interface — stats, inventory, party, magic, ratings — isn't automatic.
It's granted by a guide (Mordecai, for Carl and Donut) inside a Tutorial Guild:

> **You have been granted access to the Crawler Menu.**

From that point on, the HUD is always present: a health bar (top right), a notifications folder
(top left), and a minimap (bottom right) that can be mentally resized and repositioned. Everything
else — stats, skills, magic, party, ratings — lives in a mental menu system a crawler opens just by
thinking about it. Mordecai's summary of the learning curve: "*Once you got used to the weirdness
of it being in my head, the system was quite intuitive.*"

Two notification types exist from the start: **crawler notifications** (seen only by that
individual) and **system messages** (broadcast to every crawler on the floor, sometimes in
different in-character voices). Floor-specific notifications are added later as the dungeon's
structure gets more elaborate.

## The Five Visible Stats

Carl's starting sheet, read cold, with no context yet attached:

> **Strength: 6**
> **Intelligence: 3**
> **Constitution: 5**
> **Dexterity: 5**
> **Charisma: 4**

Mordecai's baseline: an average adult human runs **3–5** in each. These numbers are **not
manually adjustable** at this stage — they float up and down on their own, reflecting the
crawler's actual physical and mental condition, until a race and class are chosen on Floor 3.
Every level-up grants **+3 stat points**, but those points sit unspent until that same milestone.
Items and potions can nudge the numbers temporarily or permanently in the meantime (the game's
loot is soaked in small stat buffs), but a crawler otherwise has no say over their own sheet for
the first two floors.

What each stat is understood to govern, per Mordecai and later confirmation across the books:

- **Strength** — physical power; melee damage, carrying/lifting capacity.
- **Intelligence** — the size of a crawler's mana pool and (per Book 2) the effectiveness/mana
  cost of their spells. Carl's starting Intelligence of 3 gives him exactly 3 mana points at the
  very start of the tutorial.
- **Constitution** — health pool and general physical resilience.
- **Dexterity** — accuracy, evasion, reflexes.
- **Charisma** — social/persuasion stats, and (as later books make explicit) the size and
  strength of a crawler's effect on NPCs, audiences, and — at very high values — even other
  species and deities.

## The Hidden Sixth Stat: Wisdom

Carl gripes to Mordecai about his 3 Intelligence, using his friend Billy — who once urinated on a
police motorcycle in front of the officer — as his measuring stick for stupidity. Mordecai's
answer is the series' first mention of Wisdom:

> "Intelligence told you that bike belonged to a police officer. *Wisdom* told you not to urinate
> upon it. We all have a wisdom stat, but it doesn't appear on that list. It used to, but they
> discovered changing one's wisdom greatly changed their personality, so it's no longer
> adjustable. I do not know what this Billy's intelligence is, but I guarantee his wisdom is not a
> 5."

So: every crawler has a Wisdom score, it exists in the system, and it is **permanently locked
from display and adjustment** because early seasons found that changing it altered a crawler's
actual personality — an ethical line the Syndicate apparently won't cross (or won't let players
exploit). Book 2 adds one clarification without lifting the veil any further: visible spell costs
and effectiveness scale off a caster's *displayed* stats (chiefly Intelligence), a separate
mechanism from whatever Wisdom is quietly doing underneath. It has never been revisited since.

## Leveling & Experience

Killing mobs, completing objectives, and even passive participation all grant experience. The
mechanics, worked out empirically by Carl over the opening chapters:

- **Party members share experience.** Anyone who enters the dungeon at the same moment as someone
  else is auto-partied; being in a party pools XP among its members.
- **Sharing isn't equal, and it isn't pure damage-dealt either.** After a fight, Donut receives a
  fraction of a shared reward (in one case *"a 9.8 EXP"* to her, *"the other .2"* to Carl) —
  support roles like tanking and healing appear to count, and simply being present and partied for
  a battle grants a nominal amount of experience even with zero direct contribution.
- **Leveling up grants +3 stat points** (unspendable pre-Floor-3, see above) and, from Floor 3
  onward, flows into whatever a crawler's class dictates instead (see the level-up table on
  [Race & Class Selection](race-and-class-selection.md) for an example: Katia's Level 1 sheet
  auto-grants a boost to four random stats, then +1 Strength/+1 Intelligence per level with +2
  Charisma, and no further free points at all).
- **Returns diminish sharply at higher levels.** By around level 8, Carl and Donut find the
  experience curve has become "*absurdly high*" — they describe themselves as "*plateauing*," with
  their raw combat skill (see below) still climbing even while their character level stalls.
- **Achievements are themselves a major XP/reward channel** — see
  [Achievements](index.md#achievements) on the main mechanics page.

## Health & Mana

A health bar only becomes visible once a target has taken damage — undamaged mobs and crawlers
show no bar at all until the first hit lands. There's no partial recovery outside of magic,
potions, and rest: health that reaches zero means death, and per the dungeon's basic rules (see
[The World Dungeon's Basic Rules](index.md#the-world-dungeons-basic-rules)), death is permanent.

Mana works differently: it's a small, separate pool sized by Intelligence (Carl's starting 3
Intelligence gives him exactly 3 magic points), spent per spell cast, and it **regenerates
naturally** at roughly one point per hour without any action needed.

## Skills

Distinct from the five stats, and covered on its own in brief on the main mechanics page — see
[Skills](index.md#skills) for the base mechanic (every real-world habit and practiced skill is
tracked automatically, filterable by level, and combat-relevant skills level up with use and stack
multiplicatively with gear).

One addition worth flagging here because it's a *stats-and-progression* story, not a combat one:
skills were understood throughout the series to cap at **level 15** — until Book 7, when Carl's
**[Mind Balance](../abilities/mind-balance.md)** skill breaks straight through that ceiling to
level 16 purely from sustained battlefield pressure, with the system calling it out as a first:
*"You've trained a skill past 15 using battlefield conditions, as opposed to just taking a bunch
of potions to do it."* The same book separately shows level-16 spell effects reached the "easy"
way — through a flat item/natural-benefit bonus rather than training — which the wiki treats as a
mechanically distinct shortcut; see [Mind Balance](../abilities/mind-balance.md) for the full
breakdown of the difference.

## The 20% Buff Display Bug (Book 3)

A long-standing, apparently unfixed bug: the "Good Rest" (post-nap) buff and the shower buff each
add roughly 20% to a crawler's *effective* stats, but the boost is calculated **before** gear
bonuses are applied and never actually shows up on the displayed stat sheet. A caster only
notices it indirectly — Donut's spell points read higher than her listed Intelligence should
allow. Mordecai says he's filed a support ticket about it for several seasons running, with no
fix.

## Base Stat Cap and Natural Benefits (Book 7)

Base stats cap at **300**. A crawler can keep climbing past that number with equipment, buffs, and
spells stacked on top, but the underlying *base* stat itself stops there.

Crossing each hundred-point threshold in a base stat unlocks a free, mandatory choice of **natural
benefit** — a permanent passive keyed to that stat, picked from a short menu the system won't let
the crawler dismiss unanswered. Donut collects four in a single evening after casting several
Atrocity-class spells in a row; Mordecai's line is the clearest statement of the rule: "*You'll
have a few choices you need to make for both your intelligence and strength now that they're both
over 100.*" Her picks — **Muscle Mommy** (Strength, 100), **Brain Trust** (Intelligence, 100),
**Wholesale Discount** (Charisma, 200), and later **Monologue** (Charisma, 300) — are documented
on the [Abilities & Skills](../abilities/index.md) side rather than here, since each is a distinct
trained effect.

**Li Na maxes everything.** During Floor 9's Peeling phase, Li Na becomes the first crawler in the
game's recorded history to hit exactly 300 in every base stat simultaneously, at level 87. The
system announces it stat by stat, then openly gives up trying to have a plan for it:

> System message... yeah, I can keep doing this, but she maxed them all. Even Charisma, which is a
> hard one.
>
> System Message. So, this has never happened before, but a crawler has reached max base stats.
> She can still raise her stats beyond that with equipment and bonuses and spells, but we don't
> actually have a plan to deal with this, so I'm gonna have to come up with something.

Carl's reaction is a chill rather than celebration — the AI openly admitting it has **no
contingency** for a maxed-out crawler is one of the clearest signals in the series that the
dungeon's designers never modeled what their own crawlers were capable of becoming.
