# Boss Tier Ladder

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Standard tiers (ascending):** Neighborhood → Borough → City → Province → Country → Floor
    - **Separate category:** Elites (not part of the standard ladder)
    - **Reward scaling:** Bronze Boss Box for Neighborhood, Silver for Borough, and so on up the ladder

## The Ladder

Per Mordecai, woken up specifically to explain it: "There are six main types of bosses...
Neighborhood, Borough, City, Province, Country, and Floor bosses. There are also others, but
those are called elites. You won't need to worry about them just yet." Each tier scales up in
danger, the group size typically needed to survive it, and reward quality.

## Rewards

All bosses drop persistent loot that stays on their corpse for any crawler to claim. A
Neighborhood boss drops a neighborhood map; a Borough boss drops a field guide (confirmed Book
3) — each tier up the ladder is implied to drop a progressively more useful public map/reference
item. The crawler who lands the killing blow receives an achievement and a Boss Box scaled to
the tier — Bronze for Neighborhood, Silver for Borough, etc. — plus a permanent star appended to
their name in all future notifications.

## Elites

Elites sit outside the standard ladder entirely, and Book 2 substantially expands on what that
means. The [Rage Elemental](../mobs/rage-elemental.md) from Book 1 is a punishment-mechanic
example — a Level 93 monster summoned by the System AI in direct response to crawler misbehavior,
with no scripted room or reward structure behind it.

Book 2 introduces a second, more common category of Elite: NPCs and mobs tied to ongoing,
producer-driven **serial storylines** that exist independently of the crawl itself, active on
every urban ("Over City") floor from Floor 3 onward. Per Mordecai:

- Elites are never "natural" NPCs — they're designed, printed, and imprinted with the memories
  needed to play out their assigned drama, and as far as they're concerned, that drama is their
  real life.
- Unlike regular autonomous mobs, elites can travel freely *between* urban floors, carrying their
  storyline forward to the next one even while crawlers are still working through the levels in
  between.
- A quest involving an elite is, functionally, a television show with its own writers,
  producers, and audience — one that will "play itself out whether or not you get involved," and
  whose writers will protect it (including by killing an interfering crawler) if a confrontation
  threatens to end the storyline early.
- Elites carry heavy plot armor as a result. Mordecai's standing advice is to avoid engaging one
  directly at all — a crawler who "goes full murder hobo" on an elite is treated as the
  disposable extra in someone else's story, not the protagonist of their own.
- An elite **can** simultaneously hold a standard boss-tier rank — [Ringmaster
  Grimaldi](../bosses/ringmaster-grimaldi.md) is both an Elite and a Level 85 City Boss, an
  unusual double classification that still followed normal boss-tier reward rules where it applied.
- It's possible to negotiate directly with an elite's production team mid-encounter (through a
  PR contact) for narrative concessions — Carl secures a non-lethal resolution to the Grimaldi
  confrontation this way, in exchange for future exclusive quest participation.
- Showrunners can also nudge an elite's behavior mid-scene via suggestion rather than direct
  control — Book 5 confirms this explicitly through [Tsarina Signet](../characters/tsarina-signet.md),
  whose *Vengeance of the Daughter* storyline is a licensed secondary production that visibly
  steers both her and [Queen Imogen](../characters/queen-imogen.md)'s actions around the main
  broadcast's own climax (the [Butcher's Masquerade](index.md#the-butchers-masquerade-book-5)),
  including forcing a scheduling standoff neither elite is allowed to break first.
- **Areson the Wise** — a Level 45 ogre elite allied with Signet on Floor 6, one of three "nodling
  yearling" siblings (with Apollon the Mighty, met in Book 3's circus storyline, and Herman the
  Fleet, whose fate on Floor 9 is unconfirmed) split from a single dead progenitor, the
  three-headed nodling Di-we — see [Species](../species/index.md#other-book-5-species) for the
  nodling race mechanic.

## Death Challenges (Book 3)

A distinct broadcast format, explicitly *not* a boss battle:

> "A Death Challenge is not a boss battle. It's a pop-up gambling event where viewers, in systems
> where gambling is legal, are given the option to bet on a multiple set of outcomes, with each
> possible outcome being given different odds."

A Death Challenge frames an existing encounter — Carl's confrontation with the [Mimic
Rex](../bosses/mimic-rex.md) City Boss, ambushed mid-fight by an involuntarily-summoned deity —
as a live wagering event with full announcer pageantry, introducing both "combatants" with
portraits and dramatic voiceover before play resumes. It sits alongside deity summoning (see the
[War Gauntlet](../items/war-gauntlet-of-the-exalted-grull.md) item page for the underlying
summoning mechanics) as one of several ways the show layers extra broadcast spectacle on top of
an otherwise standard encounter.

## Player-vs-Player: Skulls, Coupons, and Bounties (Book 3)

Killing a fellow crawler ("player killer") permanently appends a skull icon to the killer's name
in every future notification — one small skull per kill, plus one large skull once a party's
*first* player-killer skull is earned. That first-kill milestone also awards a **Savage Box**
containing a **PVP coupon book**: one named coupon per member of the killer's own party, each
redeemable — by killing the crawler named on it — for a Gold Savage Box, Gold Weapons Box, Gold
Apparel Box, up to three Platinum Adventurer Boxes, and up to three bonus character levels. The
coupons are explicitly designed to sow paranoia within a party; both Imani's and Donut's parties
independently chose to discard theirs unused rather than risk the temptation.

Separately, the season's first crawler to kill a fellow crawler ranked in the top 10 earns a
**bounty** — [Katia](../characters/katia.md) claims a 500,000-gold bounty and a Legendary Bounty
Box for (accidentally) killing [Hekla](../timeline/index.md#floor-4-the-iron-tangle-book-3) this
way, jumping two levels in bounty rewards alone. The **[Enchanted Night Wyrm's Ring of Divine
Suffering](../items/enchanted-night-wyrms-ring-of-divine-suffering.md)** grants a related skill,
**Marked for Death**, that converts a crawler into a dedicated PvP hunter at severe personal risk —
Mordecai calls it a magnet for faction-war recruiters and insists it be sold rather than worn.

## Live Balance Patching

The tier ladder isn't static — the System AI actively rebalances mid-season. A broadcast
announcement in Book 1 confirms the removal of an overtuned "Blender Fiend" mob line after it
wiped through 15,000 crawlers in a single day, alongside an unrelated tier promotion: "The Street
Preacher neighborhood boss has been upgraded to a borough boss."
