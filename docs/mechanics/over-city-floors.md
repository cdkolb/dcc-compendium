# Over City Floors

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Which floors:** 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 — every third level, per Mordecai
    - **Size:** Much smaller than Floors 1–2 (Floor 3 is roughly the size of Arizona, ~300,000
      square kilometers), except Floor 9, which is large again
    - **Structure:** Fortified villages connected by dangerous "ruins" wilderness
    - **First appearance:** Floor 3 in Book 2, themed as a destroyed volcano-kingdom ("the Over City")

## The Pattern

Every third floor of the World Dungeon is built around a persistent, semi-independent
"urban level" setting — the Over City is Book 2's example, spanning six narrative layers built
inside a dormant volcano's crater, of which crawlers only ever experience the top layer at a
time. Per Mordecai, this is a *Dungeon Crawler World* tradition: floors 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18 will
each present a new, self-contained urban setting with its own recurring cast of Elites, ongoing
storylines, and species.

Deeper Over City-style layers escalate in danger and exclusivity:

- **Floor 6 ("the Hunting Grounds")** — jungle-like ruins, bigger and wealthier villages, and the
  first floor open to paying tourists who hunt crawlers for sport
- **Floor 9** — hosts the "Faction Wars" segment; unusually large again, unlike the floors around it
- **Floor 18** — described as a luxury retreat for the galaxy's wealthy elite, where otherwise
  illegal activities (like the operation of genetically-engineered brothel staff) are permitted
- **Floors 15 and 18** — nobody has ever recorded a crawler reaching either; even Borant and the
  system AI reportedly don't know for certain what a crawler would find there

## Villages & the Ruins

Each Over City layer consists of scattered fortified villages (sized tiny → small → medium →
large → extra-large) surrounded by "ruins" — abandoned, monster-infested wilderness making up
most of the floor's area. Villages are unnamed by default; a crawler can rename one by killing
its mayor, though this rarely comes with meaningful benefits on a short-timer floor. Every medium
(or larger) village includes a Tutorial Guild.

City guards patrol village streets only while an in-world "sun" is up; the same streets (and
especially alleyways) become lawless and dangerous after dark, when the guards vanish entirely.
Ruins-dwelling monsters exist at all hours but are markedly more dangerous at night.

## Navigation Without a Pathfinder Skill

Concealed holographic signposts — a **Dungeon Locator** — stand at major ruins intersections,
giving general directional information (nearest settlement, stairwell, restroom) even without a
Pathfinder-type skill. They're less precise than a true Pathfinder skill (which reveals a wide
radius of the map outright) but still make blind wandering unnecessary.

## NPCs & Ethics

Over City villagers are living, biological beings engineered and owned by the season's operator
(here, Borant), reused season after season with wiped and rewritten memories and backstories —
last season's meth-addicted goblins might return next season worshipping a different god, with no
awareness of the change. Using such NPCs for anything outside a sanctioned production is treated
as a serious crime, comparable to a war crime, under Syndicate law — a protection that does not
extend to how the season's writers are allowed to use them within the show itself.
