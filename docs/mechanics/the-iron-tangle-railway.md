# The Iron Tangle Railway System

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Floor:** 4 (Book 3), themed as a sprawling subway/rail network
    - **Scale:** 150,000+ stations, only 9,375 stairwells, spread across dozens of parallel trainyards
    - **Central hub:** The Abyss — a mile-wide pit where every colored-line train ultimately terminates
    - **Corporate operator:** see [Iron Tangle Management](../dungeon-factions/iron-tangle-management.md) for the org running it

Floor 4 replaces the usual walkable cityscape with a transit maze. Getting anywhere requires
understanding the rail system itself — this page covers the mechanics; the company running it is
covered separately under [Dungeon Factions](../dungeon-factions/iron-tangle-management.md).

## Colored Lines vs. Named Trains

- **Colored lines** (Puce, Vermillion, Mauve, Zomp, Fulvous, Camel, and dozens more, up to ~48 per
  quadrant) are ordinary subway trains that only travel *up* the numbered stations, never back
  down, and never loop.
- **Named trains** (the *Nightmare Express*, *Escape Velocity*, the *Eviscerator*, the *Raven
  Blade Unlimited*, the *Dismemberment Limited*) are old-style steam/diesel engines that loop
  continuously through a fixed set of stations, including some far lower-numbered ones — making
  them the only way to travel backward down the line. Each pulls dozens of freight cars, some
  carrying stat-boosted monsters, and each is staffed by a two-person crew (an engineer and,
  reserved for the harder lines, a **war mage** — see [Levi the Seventh](../mobs/index.md)). Only
  a caboose (entry) and a forward passenger car (exit-only) allow boarding; hijacking one requires
  killing its engineer for a **Steam Engineer's Key**, which also grants access to all 400 of the
  Iron Tangle's steam-type trains.
- Only station numbers ending in a prime are true **transfer stations** with a full settlement
  (restaurant, general store, and often a Desperado Club entrance); non-prime stops are small and
  often monster-free.

## Portals, Trainyards, and the Abyss

Every colored-line train eventually reaches its terminus at a **DungeonWerx subspace portal**,
one-way and passable only by a train (or a crawler wearing the free souvenir conductor's hat given
out at the floor's start). Passing through separates the train: only the engine car survives,
teleported back to a **trainyard** to be reassembled into a new train from scratch; the passenger
and freight cars are simply hurled into **the Abyss**, a mile-wide, mile-deep pit at the map's
center where every line's wreckage and monsters ultimately fall. Dozens of trainyards (lettered
E, H, M, Q, and more) ring the Abyss's edge, each a walled compound patrolled by lizard-like
**Wall Monitors** and, once the ghoul-disposal pipeline overloads, flooded with escaped ghouls.

**Interdiction carts**, small hobgoblin-and-gnoll-crewed maintenance vehicles kept at transfer
stations, carry a portable version of the same portal technology on a wide front-mounted "scoop" —
anything the moving scoop touches, including other trains, gets teleported to wherever the
portal is dialed (a specific trainyard, or the Abyss). A **Rapid-Response cart** requires two
gnolls' left hands on separate control plates to activate — a security measure the crawlers
subvert by farming an indestructible gnoll NPC's severed hand.

## Station Mimics

Six enormous **Station Mimic** City Bosses exist floor-wide, each one large enough to occupy the
mass of five or six ordinary stations at once — [Mimic Rex](../bosses/mimic-rex.md) is one of the
six, encountered at station 433/Terminus. A Station Mimic can't be killed conventionally: severed
pieces regrow legs and crawl back to the main body, and only stripping over 50% of its mass at
once (with no way to reabsorb it) leaves it briefly vulnerable — the removed pieces keep fighting
independently until the main body dies. Mordecai identifies the design as a callback to the
**H'lene**, an extinct species that ran identical ambush "waystations" near the galactic core
before being wiped out.

## The Ghoul-Disposal Pipeline

See [Iron Tangle Management](../dungeon-factions/iron-tangle-management.md#the-ghoul-disposal-pipeline)
for the corporate cause. Mechanically, non-undead mob deaths spawn Blister Ghouls (station 72) or
Jikininki (station 12); those that congregate in large enough numbers at a stairwell station fuse
into a **Province Boss** — a single conglomerated, many-mouthed monster that fills the entire
chamber. Every ghoul generator runs on a **soul crystal**; killing enough monsters near one at
once (or blowing one deliberately) causes it to detonate, destroying everything nearby but
permanently ending that generator's output — and Mordecai suspects killing a Province Boss
directly would blow every soul crystal on that stretch of line at once.

## The Mirrored "Noodle" and the Syndicate Logo

Katia eventually works out that the entire railway, viewed from above, traces the Syndicate's own
overlapping-rings logo — each named-train loop is one ring, tracing back to the wormhole ship
*Escape Velocity* that founded the Syndicate. Each individual colored line, she further deduces,
is actually a single "noodle": tracks run along the *outside* of a tube-shaped tunnel, with an
identical, inverted station on the tube's opposite face — meaning digging straight down from any
station 59 leads not to open ground but to an upside-down mirror of station 59 on a different
line, separated only by a thin **escape-tunnel** buffer ("the noodle") that the ghoul-disposal
conveyor and hidden robot-tended tunnels run through. This is why province bosses often appear in
pairs on directly opposite lines, and why plugging a station's central floor with a downward-
facing portal (as Carl does with an interdiction cart, suspended by an improvised crane) can
redirect an entire incoming horde into the Abyss instead of the room.

## Subspace Portal Vision

A **Valtay Corporation Neural Enhancer**, delivered as an unsolicited Bronze Benefactor Box gift,
permanently grants the ability to identify and see through subspace portals (but not ordinary
doors) — including taking a still-photo "visual analysis" of what's on the other side before
stepping through. It only functions on true portals, not disguised or magically-locked doorways.

## Deity Summoning Rules

The cookbook and Mordecai both describe three categories of deity summoning: a **celestial boon**
(a worshipper prays and the god comes willingly), an **indentured summoning** (a powerful mage
compels the god against its will), and an **involuntary summoning** (an accident, as with Carl's
[War Gauntlet](../items/war-gauntlet-of-the-exalted-grull.md)). Gods are invulnerable below the
12th floor. An involuntary summon lasts (vessel's level + god's level) seconds — a Level-40
Mantaur vessel summoning the Level-250 Grull lasts 290 seconds — and once unsummoned, the same god
can be re-summoned immediately with no cooldown; summoning him a second time via a different
vessel while already bound will forcibly transfer him to the new vessel. Per one cookbook note,
the gods themselves are "Soul Armor" — bodies worn by an alien pilot exactly like the Intellect
Hunters and Valtay — meaning in theory a biological-armor-removal spell could strip the alien
driver out of a god's body, though no crawler has ever been positioned to try it.
