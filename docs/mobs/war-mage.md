# War Mage

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Level:** 100 (Akuma, the Rebellion's leader, reads at level 100)
    - **Floors:** Encountered as early as Floor 4 (Book 3, "Dismember the War Mage"); central to
      Floor 9 ("Faction Wars")
    - **Type:** A **true dungeon-born entity** — nobody designed them, and nobody can un-design them
    - **Made from:** A [flesher](flesher.md)-symbiote re-sleeved twelve or more times until the
      accumulated magic becomes spontaneously sapient
    - **Signature hazard:** Their heads explode roughly **ten minutes after death** — a "Sapient
      Moment of Destruction"
    - **Spell list:** "War mages come with knowledge of hundreds of spells"

## The Worldbuilding Reveal

Book 7 is where the dungeon explains what a war mage actually is, and the answer is that it is an
accident that the showrunners have been exploiting for thousands of years.

!!! quote "Monster description (Book 7, ch. 62)"
    "Want to know some interesting trivia about war mages? These guys have been around since the very
    early days of Dungeon Crawler World, but they are one of the few, **true dungeon-born entities**.
    They weren't dreamed up by a sweaty, caffeine-addled writer, nor were they developed by one of my
    kind. **They just sort of happened**, a result of too many combatants having access to *You're
    Not Done Yet*.
    The final form a war mage takes is entirely dependent on the combination of its first two
    forms... It's rare for a symbiote to be resleeved at least a dozen times, but when it happens,
    the magic itself can become sapient. When this happens, the flesh finally fuses to the reanimated
    skeleton, turning the three creatures—the skeleton, the flesher, and the sapient well of
    magic—into a single being. A war mage.
    This process usually takes at least 12 iterations, but there's no hard rule about it. Sometimes
    it takes up to 15 or 16 iterations. It depends on the latent magic in the skin of the fleshers."

Two consequences follow from being born rather than built.

**They all look different.** The final form depends entirely on what the first two components were —
Akuma, built from "a human crawler from a very long time ago and a high elf," reads as a long-haired,
old, wizened elf with a sagging, sneering face. Standing in a group, "even though they were all the
same type of creature, they all looked like different monsters," each with a crackling, multi-hued
glow.

**They remember.** Because they are made rather than spawned, they are not routinely recycled between
crawl seasons the way ordinary mobs are:

!!! quote "Book 7, ch. 62"
    "Each war mage one may find in the dungeon was created naturally, meaning they could have been
    made several seasons previous, and because the showrunners like to use war mages, especially for
    their precious Faction Wars, **they are rarely recycled**."

Most war mages nonetheless find their minds wiped and themselves parked in quests or mercenary guilds
each time a new dungeon opens — "just because nobody engineered their existence doesn't mean that
existence hasn't been exploited." Their existence has been the subject of multiple academic studies.

## Sapient Moment of Destruction

!!! quote "Book 7, ch. 62"
    "War mage heads explode and create something called a **Sapient Moment of Destruction**
    approximately ten minutes after they die. So, heads up. Pun intended."

Carl collects war mage bodies as loot regardless, and at one point is carrying a war mage's head in
his inventory.

## The War Mage Rebellion

The Skull Empire assembles "the largest collection of war mages ever assembled" for this season, held
under strict Conscription. The war mages break it anyway — using a clever combination of *Pull* spells
to kill their own War Chief **Stalwart** despite being unable to attack him directly — and the system
responds by inventing a category on the spot: a **non-team entity** called the **[War Mage Rebellion](../factions/war-mage-rebellion.md)**,
a thing [Baroness Victory](../characters/baroness-victory.md) flatly states was not a thing until that moment.

The Rebellion is the book's wild card:

- It is awarded **[Akuma](../characters/akuma.md)** as warlord, with the Residual **Agatha** forced on it as unwanted
  co-warlord — Akuma does not like her and did not consult on her emergency action items
- It wins full warlord-system access and unrestricted use of the Conscription spell by quorum vote,
  which is what lets any faction start enslaving gods
- It floods Larracos with generated [zarogoths](zarogoth.md) and seizes Team Retribution's throne
  room, putting a six-hour clock on the entire season
- It takes the [Gate of the Feral Gods](../items/gate-of-the-feral-gods.md) out of
  [Elle McGibbons](../characters/elle-mcgibbons.md)'s hands and later trades it away to a Midnight
  Market merchant named **Paca**

Two war mages fly at the trapped gods during the Rolling Battle Formation sequence and one digs its
way into the ball to free [Eris](../bosses/eris.md) by stripping her soul armor — an attempt to
conscript a level-250 deity. A third is stopped by Li Na before it can reach
[Harpocrates](../bosses/harpocrates.md).

Akuma's exit is the strangest defeat in the book: he simply leaves.

!!! quote "Warlord Akuma, Book 7, ch. 78"
    "You won't be seeing me again on this floor. We never wished to keep that falling-down castle. We
    sought to test the potions we gathered. We wished to find the Scavenger's daughter, who was
    hidden within the castle, and we found her. She is with us now. Once we had her in our
    possession, we left. That was all we ever wanted with this place, and we are no enemies to you.
    But Agatha's actions continue to place everybody and everything in jeopardy, and she must be
    dealt with... **She seeks to kill us all. Not just you and not just me. Everybody and
    everything.**"

!!! quote "Warlord Message (Book 7, ch. 78)"
    "Warlord Akuma has left the realm. As per the current season's ruleset, the War Mage rebellion
    has been defeated. **They have no assets to award.**"
