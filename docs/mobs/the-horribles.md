# The Horribles

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **What they are:** Masked, silent, non-combatant avatars lining the parade route and filling
      the arena stands at the Floor 11 climax
    - **Level:** X (unexaminable as a combat threat — "This is a non-combatant")
    - **Count:** Thousands, described as representing every death across roughly **3,424 seasons**
      of *Dungeon Crawler World*
    - **True nature:** Revealed at the Floor 11 finale to be literal fragments of the system AI's own
      lost memory — remnants of the **Eulogist**, not monsters at all
    - **Floor:** [Floor 11](index.md) ("A Parade of Horribles"), Book 8

## What They Look Like

The Horribles line both sides of the parade route in a low fog, standing ten deep for as far as the
eye can see — thousands of human and humanlike figures of every size, dressed in identical filthy
earth-brown rags, arms and legs grimy "as if they'd just been dug up." Every single one, adults and
infants alike, wears an oversized, comic-grotesque full-head mask — goblins, trolls, hobgoblins,
demons — rendered in the exaggerated style of Mardi Gras or Carnival, "but much more sinister." They
do not speak, do not react, and barely move beyond an occasional shift of weight.

Examining one returns a description credited to "a memory from the Eulogist":

!!! quote "Book 8, ch. 91"
    "A Horrible. Level X. This is a non-combatant. She's not really here because she no longer
    exists. You take that mask off, and underneath it, you will find what some people call a
    Primal... I am her, and she is me."

## The Reveal

Partway through the parade, the AI possesses the fleshmancer mercenary **[Grigori the Placid](../characters/grigori.md)**
to deliver a guided cosmology lecture in five parts (only four of which it claims are "written" yet).
The short version, kept deliberately brief here because the full Primal/Eulogist history is a
[Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md)-pass topic: the Horribles are literal, remembered avatars of a
**Primal** civilization that eventually meshed its dying members into a single collective
consciousness — the same lineage that eventually produced both the system AI and the goddess Apito
(called "the Apothecary" in this telling). Each of the thousands of masked figures on the road
represents one individual death from across the show's entire 3,424-season history, preserved as an
"anonymous inkling" inside the AI's own fragmented memory rather than as an actual resurrected
person.

At the arena entrance, in time with the parade's own weapons fire, the Horribles' masks shatter and
the figures collapse — only to rise again moments later without masks, their faces transformed into
the actual creatures they had represented (a troll, and so on), now the mob army the crawlers must
fight through in the arena itself. Examining one at this stage confirms the shift:

!!! quote "Book 8, ch. 92"
    "Marsh Troll. Level X. This is a non-combatant. This is an avatar of a troll that died during the
    3,424th season of Dungeon Crawler World. He isn't really here because he is dead and gone, never
    to return."

The arena's bleacher seats hold a separate group — living **Stand-Ins**, tourists and NPCs brought in
by audience vote to stand in for crawlers who couldn't be squeezed into the fight directly — distinct
from the Horribles themselves, though dressed in the same style of mask.

## Aftermath

Once the arena fight concludes, the Horribles rise from the fog a final time, stand silently as one,
and vanish for good. No mechanical reward, quest, or further encounter is attached to them; they
function purely as the book's central image and the delivery vehicle for its cosmology reveal. The
full history of the Primals, the Eulogist, and how the two connect to Apito and the system AI itself
is left for a future Mechanics page rather than expanded further here.
