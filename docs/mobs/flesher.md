# Flesher

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Level:** 1
    - **Floors:** First seen on Floor 4 (Book 3, the *Dismemberment Limited*); mass-deployed on
      Floor 9 ("Faction Wars")
    - **Origin:** The skin of a corpse, stripped off by the spell [**You’re Not Done Yet**](../spells/youre-not-done-yet.md) and
      animated
    - **Behaviour:** Autonomously hunts anything flagged as an enemy; dissolves the victim's own
      flesh and re-sleeves them
    - **Resistances:** Immune to fire. (Symbiotes, their next stage, are **not**)
    - **Matters because:** Twelve or more re-sleevings of a symbiote produce a
      [War Mage](war-mage.md)

## The Flesher Cycle

The flesher is stage one of a three-stage escalation that the dungeon never designed and cannot fully
control.

!!! quote "Monster description (Book 7, ch. 62)"
    "The skin of a corpse is ripped off of a body and turned to a creature named a flesher. The
    flesher then consumes a living being, melting the original skin off and re-sleeving the creature.
    The flesher casts *Boned*, reanimating the skeleton, and the two become a **symbiote**.
    Symbiotes are extremely attractive targets to other fleshers. And because young symbiotes are
    slow and awkward, they are oftentimes quickly re-sleeved by fresh fleshers. Each iteration
    results in a more powerful being."

Twelve or more iterations and the accumulated magic becomes spontaneously sapient — see
[War Mage](war-mage.md).

Physically they read as dark, flat shapes that skitter "like manta rays across the ocean floor,"
flapping onto a target, wrapping around the legs and moving upward while the victim's flesh dissolves.
They burn through cybernetic armor. Carl's reaction to watching a field of them move is "a deep,
primal revulsion."

## Floor 9: Weaponised at Scale

The Princess Posse turns the flesher cycle into a deliberate tactic. Every allied graveyard's corpses
are collected by **Bodi and the 103rd** and dumped on the battlefield, then
[Princess Donut](../characters/princess-donut.md) casts a wide-range, tower-amplified
[**You’re Not Done Yet**](../spells/youre-not-done-yet.md) over them — stripping thousands of skins at once and sending the resulting
fleshers sailing across the field at enemy formations. The Madness abandoning that front wastes the
first batch; the second batch is the one that matters.

When Donut's [Rolling Battle Formation](../spells/rolling-battle-formation.md) ball forms, the fleshers crawl over and *into* it, worming
through the cracks to find the Dream and Reaver soldiers inside. Because the prisoners are
invulnerable while the spell holds, the fleshers simply wait, wrapped around them.

That is the whole design. [Rosetta Thagra](../characters/rosetta-thagra.md) explicitly vetoes casting
[*Laundry Day*](../spells/laundry-day.md) to strip them off, because the fleshers **are** the
finisher:

!!! quote "Rosetta, Book 7, ch. 74"
    "With them both amplified by [War Crime](../spells/war-crime.md), it should separate the enemies all out and drop them into
    a ring of fire. Fleshers are fire resistant, but symbiotes aren't, so if any enemies resist, they
    should be pounced upon by the fleshers, turned to symbiotes, and then killed. That's the finisher
    since War Crime keeps them alive. It'll kill the enemy and any potential baby war mages."

Carl's other standing concern all floor is preventing new war mages from being born in the middle of
his own battlefield. When **Nami** — the influencer piloting the goddess [Eris](../bosses/eris.md) —
is ejected from the ball and immediately skinned into a flesher wearing a dromedarian hide "much too
big for her frame," two more fleshers attach within seconds. Carl blows the whole cluster up rather
than let a twelfth iteration start.
