# Rage Elemental

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Level:** 93 (vastly above Floor 2's normal range)
    - **Classification:** Elite — not a standard boss-tier encounter
    - **Trigger:** Summoned by the System AI as a punishment for public urination/defecation outside designated bathrooms
    - **Outcome (Book 1):** Kills Yolanda Martinez; ultimately defeated by Carl and Donut via a collapsing-stairwell trap

!!! quote "AI Commentary (in-voice, written for this wiki — not a book excerpt)"
    "You had one job, humans. One. And instead of finding a toilet, you got Grandpa Jack killed
    by a screaming, teleporting nightmare with anger issues. Let this be a lesson: hold it, or
    we will send something that cannot be reasoned with. This has been a public service
    announcement from Sanitation Compliance."

## Official Description

Per the in-universe monster card:

> "The first recorded summoning of a Rage Elemental, blah, blah, blah. If you are reading this,
> you likely don't give a shit about the monster's (rather interesting and tragic) history.
> You're probably running. It's not going to matter. The almost-indestructible Rage Elemental is
> said to only dissipate after it has claimed 666 souls."

## Encounter

Announced via a System AI broadcast as a deterrent after repeated bathroom-etiquette violations
by human crawlers, the Rage Elemental is dropped directly into Team Meadow Lark's location after
elderly resident Jack urinates in a hallway and is shot dead by Yolanda moments before the
elemental arrives. A badger-headed, multi-limbed horror that teleports short distances and
appears to have near-limitless stamina, it kills Yolanda during the group's escape. Carl and
Donut ultimately deny it a real victory by luring it into a collapsing stairwell alongside an
explosive, destroying it (or at least driving it off) without it claiming the achievement/XP a
normal kill would grant — a deliberate choice to spite the system that sent it.

The event becomes a broadcast segment, and the show frames it as a consequence of "disrespect...
regarding the bathrooms" rather than acknowledging it as a design failure.
