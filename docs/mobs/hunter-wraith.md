# Hunter Wraith

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Ethereal, non-corporeal possession monster
    - **Native floors:** 10 and 11 — "Those are floor 10 and 11 monsters," per Tipid
    - **Core ability:** Possession. A wraith-possessed body can be puppeted **even if it is dead**,
      and the wraith can use items from the host's inventory
    - **The cost:** A curse called **the Inevitable Ruin**, which forces the possessor to see an
      unwanted truth about whoever they possess
    - **Seen in Book 7:** Taken on as a form by the changeling
      [Juice Box](../characters/juice-box.md) in ch. 79, to rescue
      [Louis Santiago](../characters/louis-santiago.md) from the Reaver castle

## The Form

A hunter wraith is a ghost-like thing — Carl's first sight of one is Juice Box shifting into "an
ethereal, ghost-like creature" so fast he misses the change. Tipid recognises it immediately and flags
both its usefulness and its price in the same breath: "It's, uh, not going to be a clean getaway.
There's something that happens when that particular wraith touches people, but that's more an issue
for Juice Box than Louis."

Its combat value is the possession mechanic, which is unusually total:

!!! quote "Book 7, ch. 80"
    "People wraith-possessed could be moved like puppets even if they were dead, which was
    terrifying, especially since the wraiths could—and did—utilize items in the crawler's inventory.
    It was a common, terrifying mob from higher floors."

That is precisely how Warlord Fang of the Reavers dies: Juice Box possesses Louis inside the castle,
puppets him into dropping a **No Disassemble** from his own inventory, and teleports out. The system
splits the credit accordingly — 51% to Carl for detonating the bomb, 1% to Louis, 40% to Juice Box,
and the remainder to Fang himself "for hiding in the worst possible place, which was inside the
dragon."

## The Inevitable Ruin

The curse is what gives the book its title.

!!! quote "Juice Box, Book 7, ch. 82"
    "It's also a spell. Or rather, it's a curse, inflicted upon the mind of the Hunter Wraith, which
    is why they are so bitter. When they possess someone, **it ruins something pure. It doesn't
    change whatever it is. It just tells you the truth.** It's difficult to explain, and it manifests
    itself in different ways. But for me, when I had Louis possessed in order to save him, the curse
    showed me the truth. And the truth is that he's terrified of me. The truth is he's not in love
    with me..."

Her verdict afterward: "I wish I hadn't taken on the form of that hunter wraith. It was worth it to
save him, but I wish I hadn't."

!!! tip "Thematically central"
    **The Inevitable Ruin** is the title of Book 7, and the phrase is used three separate ways across
    the book: as the High Elf queen's name for the moment you accept you are going to die
    (Everly's chapter); as Carl's private label for the sense of universal doom that follows Juice
    Box's warning; and finally as the literal name of this wraith curse. The hunter wraith is where
    the title lands as a game mechanic.
