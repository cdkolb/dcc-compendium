# The Pineapple Cabaret

!!! info "Quick facts"
    - **Type:** Hidden pocket dimension built as a permanent sanctuary for NPCs and mobs fleeing
      the dungeon — the book's central escape-hatch MacGuffin
    - **Built by:** Two former crawlers, **Herot** (16th-edition cookbook author — see
      [The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md) and
      [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md#naturals-and-npc-personhood-book-6)) and **Menerva**
      (no prior appearance in the series)
    - **Cover story:** The never-visited seventeenth floor — an officially "ticked box" the
      showrunners believe is a normal, if unused, dungeon level
    - **Known access routes:** The Desperado Club casino's Wheel of Fortune "Nothing" spot, and a
      cleaner-bot/personal-shop-interface exploit discovered mid-book
    - **Currently infested by:** A **Shadow Mimic** presence (see [Species](../species/shadow-mimics.md))
      and a remnant of the **Ogre Imperium**, contested alongside the **War Mage Rebellion** and a
      third faction Mitch calls "Cock-A-Doodle-Do"
    - **Confirmed evacuees:** [Li Na](../characters/li-na.md) (first, as the test case), then 227
      additional volunteers (228 total, not including support staff)
    - **Status:** Open thread — the party never confirms the Cabaret is fully secured, nor whether
      leaving the dungeon cures anything

## Overview

The Pineapple Cabaret is Book 8's answer to a question the series has been circling since the
Open Intellect Pacifist Network's reveal in Book 6: is there any way *out* of the crawl that
doesn't require winning or dying? For NPCs and mobs — who, unlike crawlers, have no indenture
countdown and no Syndicate citizenship path — the answer the book offers is this pocket dimension:
a permanent, hidden sanctuary built generations ago and kept secret from the AI and the showrunners
alike.

The name is first heard in passing, offered by the AI (wearing Growler Gary's body) to the orc
princess Formidable as a banishment option — "Backstage at the Pineapple Cabaret. Not the cabaret
itself." Its real nature isn't unpacked until Akuma, tortured for information by Elle McGibbons and
Donut, gives it up under duress in the book's first half. See
[Akuma](../characters/akuma.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) and
[Elle McGibbons](../characters/elle-mcgibbons.md) for that interrogation scene in full rather than
duplicated here.

## Origins: Herot, Menerva, and the Seventeenth Floor

Per Akuma, two former crawlers — **Herot**, already known to this wiki as the skink 16th-edition
cookbook author who campaigned for waking NPCs up via his **Worn Path Method**, and **Menerva**, not
previously named in the series — discovered that mobs and NPCs were supposed to be periodically
"recycled" between seasons for their own sake, but were instead being reused and exploited
indefinitely because proper recycling was expensive and poorly audited:

!!! quote "Akuma, Book 8"
    "The dungeon is full of mobs who know their own nature. Everything is broken. It's all
    accelerating... but these two former crawlers, Herot and Menerva, their exploit is a way for us,
    the NPCs, to survive. But you crawlers can use it, too."

Their solution was to build a fake dungeon floor — the seventeenth, never opened to any crawler —
as bureaucratic cover, then quietly attach to it something the AI never noticed: an entire separate
pocket dimension.

!!! quote "Akuma, under interrogation, Book 8"
    "They are, or were, trapped by design. They didn't want to be seen. The seventeenth floor is a
    front. They weren't just building the seventeenth floor. Attached to the floor is the only
    entrance to a pocket dimension they built. They were building an oasis, a place for mobs and
    NPCs to escape to. A world within a world. The two crawlers sacrificed the possibility of ever
    getting out to make a place where NPCs and mobs could permanently escape the horrors of the
    dungeon. *That* is the Pineapple Cabaret."

The seventeenth floor itself ("the Backstage Death Maze") is not the Cabaret proper but its staging
ground — a training area where recovered NPCs and mobs, including the elevated **Growler Gary** the
AI once wore as an avatar, grow stronger in preparation for the day crawlers might actually reach
that far. **Menerva** runs operations there. **Herot** himself is on the far side, trapped inside
the Cabaret dimension itself, where he retains a limited shop interface but no other real control.

!!! warning "Cross-reference to Book 6–7 cookbook lore"
    Herot's cookbook contributions (the Worn Path Method, his essay on self-aware NPCs) are already
    documented under [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/index.md) and
    [The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook](../items/the-dungeon-anarchists-cookbook.md) — not repeated
    here in full. This page covers only what Book 8 adds: that he is still alive and secretly
    running the Cabaret.

## Access

Akuma describes exactly two known ways in, both discovered or exploited within Book 8 itself:

### The Wheel of Fortune "Nothing" Spot

The Desperado Club casino's Wheel of Fortune game has a two-space result called "the Nothing." Per
Akuma, it had previously let their ally Pontiff jump safely into a stasis holding area connected to
the Cabaret. This is the route Mitch (see [Species: Shadow Mimics](../species/shadow-mimics.md) and
[Mitch](../bosses/mitch.md)) uses to send **Chris Andrews, Britney Proskurina, and Louis Santiago**
through — a jump whose actual destination is never confirmed on-page; the book's final interlude
instead finds Louis and Chris in **Sheol**, met by Britney, Pontiff, and a demon calling himself
**Forkith** (see [Supporting Cast](../characters/supporting-cast.md)), with no explanation
for the diversion. With the Nothing itself broken (a Book 7 development), Akuma warns this route no
longer opens directly onto the Cabaret but into "some sort of catchall holding area" that Herot can
pull people out of manually — the same mechanism used to originally recruit NPCs for the
seventeenth floor.

### The Cleaner-Bot / Shop-Interface Exploit

The route the party actually uses is stranger. **Herot** has a standing purchase order buying up
*every* Cleaner Bot upgrade sold anywhere in the dungeon's personal-space shop system — which is why
crawlers everywhere had been complaining the upgrade was suddenly unavailable due to "overwhelming
demand." Using his limited shop-interface access from inside the Cabaret, Herot can empty a
purchased Cleaner Bot's contents directly into the Cabaret's space:

!!! quote "Carl, working out the mechanism"
    "Basically, Li Na gets sucked into the cleaner bot, the cleaner bot gets put up for sale, Herot
    buys it, and he empties her out inside of the other place. And now Li Na has left the game
    because this Cabaret place is outside the playing field. She'll be considered to have been
    'lost.'"

Capacity is limited and expensive: the first bot used holds only about fifty people, and Herot
cannot re-list a used bot for thirty days due to a standard dungeon-upgrade cooldown. A second,
larger bot — borrowed from another crawler, Bobby Montague — fits 105 more. The system itself
frames anyone who goes through this way as having been "lost," not killed, which is what makes the
whole plan survivable in the first place.

## Current Occupants and the Three-Way Struggle

By the time the party reaches out to Herot, the Cabaret is not the peaceful refuge it was built to
be. It has been overrun by two hostile forces:

- **The Ogre Imperium** — per Akuma, "a long-forgotten plotline that had gotten binned because the
  ogres were made too intelligent and they had immediately figured out that they were trapped in a
  dungeon." A remnant of this abandoned storyline has apparently persisted inside the Cabaret for
  "several seasons."
- **Shadow Mimics** — see [Species: Shadow Mimics](../species/shadow-mimics.md). Mitch, dying,
  claims his kind wanted captured crawlers "for the Cabaret" specifically.

Mitch's dying testimony frames the occupation as a genuine three-way contest for control of "the
dungeon back door" — the Cabaret's access point — between the **War Mage Rebellion**, the Ogre
Imperium, and a faction he names only as **"Cock-A-Doodle-Do,"** a name never explained or repeated
elsewhere in the text:

!!! quote "Mitch, Book 8"
    "We needed some of you. We needed you for the Cabaret. The war mages, the ogres, and
    Cock-A-Doodle-Do. They all vie for control of the dungeon back door. With some of you, we could
    control it all."

The **War Mage Rebellion** (led by [Akuma](../characters/akuma.md)) wants the party's help clearing
the Cabaret out specifically so it can be used as an exit — see
[The War Mage Rebellion](../factions/war-mage-rebellion.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) for how
that alliance plays out on Akuma's side.

## Li Na and the 227

**[Li Na](../characters/li-na.md)** is sent through first, alone, as the test case for the
cleaner-bot method — both to escape her permanent no-healing condition (a Book 7 consequence of the
Ring of Divine Suffering) and to prove the exploit works before risking anyone else. Mordecai rigs a
signal system using a potted cactus tied to her life and location; a single bloomed flower means she
made it safely.

Once confirmed safe, the guild sends a much larger wave:

!!! quote "Imani, Book 8"
    "It went smoothly. Your cleaner bot could only fit forty people, but the one from Bobby
    Montague, the Illusionist guy with the tall hair, managed to fit 105. The total number sent to
    the Pineapple Cabaret is 227, not including Na. Zhang, Tran, Daniel, Archie, Randall 3. All
    gone."

That puts the confirmed total at **228**, including Li Na. Mordecai builds a "Garden of Deliverance"
— over two hundred individually-tied potted plants, one per volunteer — as the only way the guild
can track who lives, dies, or gets out, since "even the showrunners can't see what's going on in
there." Eligibility is strictly limited: no god-worshippers (entering would allegedly trigger an
automatic smite, per Akuma — though Carl is never able to confirm whether that's a real mechanic or
a lie meant to keep certain crawlers out), no fairies or flying classes, nobody whose survival
depends on dungeon magic (Louis's gill surgery, Chris's rock-elemental physiology), and no one whose
race's behavior outside an enhancement zone is unknown (Donut, Florin). See [Li
Na](../characters/li-na.md#book-8-a-parade-of-horribles) for her own arc in full.

!!! warning "Confirmed: safe arrival, not a cure"
    Book 8 confirms only that Li Na and the 227 volunteers after her reached the Cabaret alive.
    Nothing in the text confirms the permanent no-healing condition, or any other dungeon-inflicted
    condition, is actually cured, reduced, or otherwise changed by leaving the dungeon this way —
    that remains an open, unconfirmed hope.

## Official Position (The Showrunners' Denial)

The exploit becomes public enough within the season that Borant is forced to address it on the
record, disclaiming any responsibility:

!!! quote "Book 8, in-universe broadcast notice"
    "Also, I have been asked to directly address this so-called Pineapple Cabaret exploit many of
    you are using to shirk your duties as a crawler. We, the showrunners, do not endorse any such
    methods and we are not responsible for anything that may happen to crawlers who venture into
    unauthorized areas of the World Dungeon infrastructure."

The system AI itself volunteers its own warning rather than blocking the attempt outright. It is
tucked into the completion text for an entirely unrelated quest, delivered in the same stretch of
pages in which the party learns Li Na arrived safely: "Beware exits that seem too good to be true."
By the epilogue, that line reads less like taunting and more like a warning nobody heeded — see
**The diverted jump** under Open Threads, below.

## Open Threads

- **Whether the Cabaret can ever open to Earth's surface.** Herot's stated theory — that a second
  door might eventually be built from the Cabaret to the planet's surface — is never confirmed or
  attempted on-page. The AI's own caveat: "We *might* be able to create an exit from the Pineapple
  Cabaret to the surface of this planet. We *might*."
  Not attempted by the end of Book 8.
- **What happens to anyone still inside if the seventeenth floor collapses.** Per the AI, anyone
  inside "when the floor collapses will be deemed 'lost.' But they won't be killed" — an explicit,
  unresolved risk hanging over all 228 evacuees.
  See [Species: Shadow Mimics](../species/shadow-mimics.md) and
  [Krakaren Prime](../bosses/krakaren-prime.md) for related open threads about the dungeon's other
  contested "back door" spaces.
- **The diverted jump.** Chris, Britney, and Louis Santiago were sent through the casino's Nothing
  spot believing they were headed for the Cabaret; the epilogue instead shows Louis and Chris
  surfacing in Sheol. Whether this means the Cabaret route failed, was intercepted, or was never
  actually connected to their jump is left open — see [Mitch](../bosses/mitch.md#aftermath).
