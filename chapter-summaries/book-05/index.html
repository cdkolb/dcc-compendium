    Chapter 1 — Collateral for the Gate

    Floor 6, "the Hunting Grounds," opens with the Gate of the Feral Gods already a liability: hauled before the hooded, worm-headed Valtay liaison Orren, Carl learns the Syndicate can't legally confiscate the artifact but won't let him keep it either, given the danger it poses on a floor about to be flooded with hunters. Orren lays out three options — lock down every portal on the floor, "accelerate" (kill) Carl outright, or have him voluntarily surrender the gate for a receipt, redeemable on the ninth floor. Carl demands a lawyer, halting the meeting cold. Shunted into a stadium-sized staging arena for mandatory class specialization, he learns via chat that Donut has picked a new class, Legendary Diva (a singing bard subclass), over Mordecai's objections, and that Katia got a HAZMAT endorsement for her Monster Truck Driver class. Waiting in line, he meets an amputee crawler named Ajib and hears the floor's opening announcement: hunters, bumped to level 50, will be loosed in under 28 hours. A Bush Elf attendant, Ian, warns him in passing about a mantis hunter named Vrah who killed his brother and wears the heads of her kills — the first mention of the antagonist who will define the floor. The chapter ends with Carl specializing into Agent Provocateur, the bomb-focused class upgrade.

    Chapter 2 — The Legendary Diva

    Carl arrives at a dryad settlement outside Zockau and is immediately ambushed by an invisible pack of night weasels that paralyze two level-95 Funeral Bell guards in seconds. His new Ping spell and a thrown sack of gunpowder reveal the creatures just in time for Donut, Mongo, and Chris to drive them off before Carl himself is paralyzed and dragged into the personal space's saferoom. There, Mordecai's Floor 6 NPC form is revealed as "Pocket Kuma," a comically small and cuddly bear completely at odds with his usual gruffness, reducing Carl to helpless laughter. The chapter ends with an unexpected knock at the personal space door — the first delivery from the Gate negotiation.

    Chapter 3 — Quasar's Condom Speech

    Quasar, a foul-mouthed Nullian, introduces himself as Carl's court-appointed attorney, cutting Orren's meeting off cold with a memorable metaphor: he can't stop Carl from "getting fucked" by the Syndicate, but he's the condom. He warns Carl off "naked" (bodiless) Valtay like Orren and explains liaisons exist to manufacture leverage for the system. Together they settle on surrendering the gate as collateral rather than giving it up outright — Carl gets it back on the ninth floor. Back in the personal space, the mercenary package itself arrives: four cretin bodyguards (Bomo, the Sledge, Clay-ton, and Very Sullen) split across the party, plus two bonus spells taught to the mercenaries rather than to Carl directly — a Teleport to Stairwell spell for Bomo and a mysterious spell called Zerzura for the Sledge, Quasar's own suggestion. Katia returns from the previous floor's climax at level 52 with a new Steely Skin passive.

    Chapter 4 — The Advanced Bomber's Studio

    Carl buys the Advanced Bomber's Studio, a room that lets him simulate explosive yields on virtual targets and dissect ordnance he doesn't own. Donut's five new Legendary Diva spells are cataloged: Standing Ovation, Entourage, Encore, an offensive psionic song, an ice song, and a bonus spell, Laundry Day, that strips armor off a target — none of them useful yet, since her singing can't hold pitch. With the timer on the Hunting Grounds' 30-hour hunter grace period running down, Carl executes Predator, an invisibility-and-infrared recon move, and has Bomo cast the stairwell-teleport spell to jump himself and Donut directly into the hunter capital of Zockau before the hunters are even released.

    Chapter 5 — The Zockau Raid

    Carl's first strike against the hunters is a bloodbath. He mines the alleys around Zockau's Desperado Club entrance with barrel bombs, then uses the Ring of Divine Suffering — looted earlier, worn for the first time — to mark a drunk hunter, Chin'Dua, as living bomb-bait before beating him to death by hand. Spotting Vrah's flying sister Xindy overhead, he activates his toe ring's Super Spreader ability to copy his own Left to Fester mark onto her from a distance, then finishes her with a five-missile homing salvo that blows her out of the sky. The barrel bombs detonate, leveling a city block and trapping half the Desperado Club's occupants inside. Fleeing on foot, Carl and Donut are hit by a pursuing archer-hunter's crossbow bolts — one buries itself in his leg, another in his shoulder, both carrying a bleeding Blood Trail debuff. Carl collapses at the city border from blood loss and a Woozy-induced coma, saved only when Miriam Dom, Prepotente, and Bianca happen across them on the road outside.

    Chapter 6 — Miriam and Bianca

    Carl wakes in the back of Miriam Dom's cart, badly wounded but alive, having racked up 34 confirmed hunter kills from the raid. He meets Bianca Del Ciao, Miriam Dom's Hellspawn familiar — a formerly ordinary, formerly-asshole pet goat reborn as a horned, flame-wreathed demon after her soul was corrupted despite a protection spell — and learns only that Miriam herself is now a vampire, without yet learning how. Carl accidentally insults Prepotente (calling him by his hated nickname, "Pony," then telling him to "suck my dick"), who nearly attacks him in a rage before Miriam scolds them both into an awkward, forced hug.

    Chapter 7 — The Belching Termite

    Carl and Donut reach a bombed-out bugbear settlement and take refuge in a saferoom disguised as a Whataburger. Mordecai, furious over the reckless Zockau raid, tries to have "the talk" with Donut, who preempts him with a rehearsed speech: yes, Carl was reckless, no, she's not leaving him, and no, they are not doing a will-they-won't-they storyline on top of Katia's. Katia, Louis, Firas, and Britney are all gathered in the space; Mordecai instructs the group to open their boxes and settle in for the recap before the chapter ends.

    Chapter 8 — The Butcher's Masquerade, Announced

    Opening a Gold Junkie's Box from his first ring-kill lands Carl a second, much larger tattoo on his elbow — a sigil granting access to something called the Guild of Suffering, which Mordecai identifies as an obscure cult built around a "memorial crystal" and a demigod called the Night Wyrm, tied to anyone who uses a divine suffering artifact. Carl's 34 Hunter-Killer boxes turn out to contain severed hunter hands and escalating piles of gold (595,000 total) — revolting to Donut, who gets a single hand and a description explaining these are tickets to a prize counter at an end-of-floor event called the Butcher's Masquerade. That night's system message announces private crawler chats are no longer broadcast live (a lawsuit win Quasar had warned Carl not to get his hopes up about, since the hunters'-outside-intel problem is a separate, much shakier case), formally launches the guild system (parties capped at 30, guild charters starting at 500,000 gold), and reveals the Masquerade itself: an invite-only, top-50 party with a pet beauty contest, talent show, and hunter-trade prize booth. Donut immediately begins plotting Mongo's beauty-pageant campaign. The floor's top-10 list shows Carl at #1 with a bounty of 1,000,000 gold.

    Chapter 9 — Bombs for the Bridges

    With the hunters about to be released, Carl and Katia study the map: the Hunting Grounds spans roughly 100,000 square kilometers, bisected by a single winding river. Carl orders every bridge but one destroyed to funnel hunters into predictable chokepoints. Back at the sapper's table, he builds his largest bomb yet — a five-foot, egg-shaped thermobaric device nicknamed "Just Wait Until Your Daddy Gets Home" — designed for Bomo to wheel out as a suicide-and-respawn attack, over Donut's objections. Mordecai supplies Nitro Sludge, an oxygen-amplifying byproduct of his shield-potion research, as the payload.

    Chapter 10 — Point Mongo

    Carl and Donut cross into a large Ursine settlement and are turned away at the gate by a "no dinosaurs" town rule expressly written to keep Mongo out (along with bans on magic, dancing, singing, and naiads). Carl bribes the elderly mayor, Elmer, into stepping close enough to take payment, and Donut shoves him down the riverbank slope with Astral Paw; he dies from the fall itself, and a passing naiad drags his corpse into the water before they can loot it. The town falls to Donut, who renames it Point Mongo. Just before the hunter release deadline hits, a note arrives pinned to the personal-space door: Vrah, having somehow learned Carl's exact location despite the communication bans, promises a slow, ritual death in retaliation for her sister Xindy. Carl writes back "Come and get it, bitch" as the system announces the hunters are loose.

    Chapter 11 — Mongo's People

    Donut's new mayoral collar charm grants her the Snitch skill (summons town guards) alongside stat buffs, and she uses her new authority to strip Point Mongo of its more puritanical ordinances over the town cleric's objections — though Carl talks her out of also scrapping the "bugbears must bathe in the river" rule once he realizes it's a naiad death trap. A trap Carl left on the road nets a hunter kill and a Gold Trapmaster box (a pack of alarm-triggering Fail Safe mods). While arguing with the cleric about reinstating anti-dinosaur ordinances, a new quest, The Recital, appears — something is drawing escalating dinosaur attacks to Point Mongo — and Mongo suddenly bolts from town, drawn by instinct, straight into a semicircle of forty wild velociraptors led by the pack matriarch Kiwi. Carl and Donut nearly trigger a massacre trying to pull him free, until it becomes clear the standoff is a courtship ritual, not an attack — Mongo and Kiwi mate in front of the horrified party.

    Chapter 12 — No Matter What

    Back in town, Donut suffers a genuine panic attack after nearly losing Mongo, blurting out that she's terrified of being abandoned again (as Katia left, and as Carl keeps risking his life) before shutting down mid-confession about "the ninth floor." Zev interrupts with pointed small talk — an unsubtle warning to drop the subject before their scheduled visit to Odette later that day. Carl finally tells Donut directly, for the first time, that he will never leave her behind, no matter what, sidestepping for now the real problem underneath: the Crown of the Sepsis Whore curse still binding her to the Blood Sultanate's royal line on the ninth floor. The moment is interrupted by Mongo's triumphant, exhausted return from the jungle.

    Chapter 13 — Bravvo in the Tree

    Elite Signet reappears at Point Mongo's bridge with a new entourage: the ogre Areson the Wise and six were-creature warriors riding a giant caterpillar. They've found a hunter, the yenk Bravvo, immobilized in a tree after Point Mongo's guards shot him out of the sky, and offer him to Carl to finish off as a professional courtesy — the first hint of the "hunting rights" turf war developing between rival factions over Carl and Donut.

    Chapter 14 — The Were-Castors

    Carl marks and kills Bravvo with the Ring of Divine Suffering, leveling it up. Signet introduces her six warriors — were-castors (transformed beaver-people), survivors of a genocide by then-Princess Imogen decades before Scolopendra's attack — and their caterpillar guardian, Nadine, a transfigured schoolteacher. Signet's own backstory unfolds: a naiad princess's bastard daughter by the high elf king, exiled and cursed so water refuses to touch her, forced into circus life after her mother's murder in a naiad coup.

    Chapter 15 — Vengeance of the Daughter

    Areson delivers another hunter kill and a hand-drawn map, scrawled on the back of a kids' diner menu, revealing the hunter factions have divided the Hunting Grounds into claimed territories and drawn up a target list — Carl and Donut are double-claimed by both the Dark Hive and the Skull Empire, while the Dream has fixated obsessively on Louis for stealing a photo of their leader's mother to have Juice Box impersonate. Signet formally recruits Carl and Donut into her storyline, The Vengeance of the Daughter: help her retake her mother's naiad throne from the usurping Confederacy at their stronghold, Fort Freedom.

    Chapter 16 — Clint and Holger

    Two were-castors, bald Clint and mulleted Holger, escort Carl and Donut back toward Point Mongo, bickering and brawling constantly despite genuine affection, then fighting off a night-weasel pack with terrifying, teeth-and-claw efficiency once real danger arrives. Carl receives his third and final sponsor, the Apothecary, shared suspiciously with most of the current top-10 crawlers — a company Carl suspects is a front for Krakaren's collective-mind faction.

    Chapter 17 — Samantha's Cousin

    Back at base, Samantha throws a screaming fit on learning Donut and Carl are working with Signet, whom she claims (via a tangled royal lineage) is her third cousin and thus a possible source of a new physical body. Carl and Donut are then yanked to an orbital security checkpoint en route to Odette's show, tumbling in near-zero gravity as Earth spins beneath them, before a pair of shade-gnoll mercenaries pat them down and clear them for transfer.

    Chapter 18 — Beatrice Is Alive

    In Odette's production trailer, Odette drops the floor's first real bombshell: Beatrice survived the initial collection, was rescued by Odette from black-market bounty hunters intent on selling her to Borant as a repurposed country boss, and has been alive and hidden off-world this whole time. Odette wants to bring her onto the show as a ratings coup before a rival program can leak the story first. Carl is furious, suspecting Odette engineered the whole situation; Donut, shaking, insists on going through with the reveal on her own terms rather than let Carl shoulder it alone.

    Katia quickly radios that Imogen isn't Ifechi at all but her previously-unmentioned twin sister. Imogen lingers in the wagon to recount the mythic origin of the Masquerade's non-violence spell and warn Carl explicitly: break the peace, and Apito's wrath, not hers, will be the one to punish him — confirming the group's entire plan to force a fight inside the ballroom is dead on arrival. At the castle, stablemaster Kibben separates the animal attendants, and Carl finalizes the goodwill-ballroom logistics with the guild by chat before the group is patted down and led into Ballroom A. Inside, Empress D'Nadia reveals she's judging both the pet and talent shows, and Vrah — crotch fire finally extinguished — charges straight at Carl the moment she spots him.

    Chapter 65 — Britney's Butterfly

    While Vrah threatens Carl in front of D'Nadia, Britney quietly disables the room's alarm-light system using a stuffed slate butterfly from her jacket, buying the group's covert plan crucial cover. Elsewhere, Katia has to be physically restrained from attacking the traitorous Eva, Florin nearly confronts a fragile, dissociative Lucia Mar, and Prepotente demands (and is refused) a spot in Carl's party for the remainder of the floor. As Theobold formally opens the party, Signet suddenly walks into the ballroom — not smuggled in by the party at all, but personally delivered by Apito herself as part of the goddess-curated guest list.

    Chapter 66 — Signet Crashes the Party

    Signet explains to a rattled Carl and Imani that Apito herself appeared and invited her directly, confirming the entire guest list — Signet included — was hand-picked by the goddess specifically to test Queen Imogen, who (per Signet) has never once actually won the boon a completed Masquerade is supposed to grant. Amused that her sister can't touch her without breaking the peace, Signet drifts off to chat up the three off-world celebrity judges instead, unnervingly aware that they aren't really "here" at all.

    Chapter 67 — The Prize Counter

    Carl visits the Masquerade's prize counter, run by Chaco, a wolf-headed bard with a painful shared history with Mordecai, to spend his stockpile of hunter hands, trading barbs along the way with a resigned alien hunter, Zabit, about the economics of the hunt. Among the catalog — potions, upgrade scrolls, a sold-out "Seventh Floor Pole Position" boon Prepotente already snapped up — sits a memorial crystal at the top tier: a storehouse of god-tier knowledge, revealed to belong to Queen Imogen's own necklace and rumored, impossibly, to have come from a dead god. Carl falls one hand short of affording it as the pet show begins.

    Chapter 68 — Mongo's Moonwalk

    Donut's pet-show entry — Mongo, hyped by Louis's over-the-top emcee routine and a blasted Nicki Minaj track — turns out to include a fully-trained moonwalk, stealing the room from every other entrant, including Tserendolgor's giggling, tank-like "tummy acher" pet. Backstage, Elle learns from a grieving hunter that Vrah's people tricked his own brother into the temple as Diwata's unwitting birthing vessel, and the group realizes the goddess is still trapped on the floor, indefinitely occupied giving birth to a swarm of mantis nymphs — Vrah's own children and half-siblings at once. Word arrives that mantis reinforcements are converging on the stairwells outside.

    Chapter 69 — Ferdinand's Turban

    Florin's attempt to intervene between Lucia Mar and the room takes a stranger turn: the crawler he catches comforting a sobbing, clinging Lucia briefly switches to a distinct personality — a ten-year-old Dutch girl named Jill, apparently one of several real children trapped inside Lucia's fractured mind and periodically able to surface, seemingly controlled from afar by "the dog." Before Carl can process it, Lucia snaps back to her usual murderous self, and Zev shuts down further questions. Ferdinand saunters onto Carl's shoulder wearing a ridiculous feathered turban to gloat, and Carl signals Samantha to make her move while he's distracted.

    Chapter 70 — The Room Seals Shut

    Donut, boosted to a god-tier 276 charisma by her benefactor tiara, works to charm Ferdinand mid-conversation while Katia confirms the stat math checks out even against a Province Boss. Outside, the plan unravels fast: Gideon reports elven cavalry pouring out to fight the changelings' airship, and the Twister is swarmed and overrun by mantis nymphs, forcing the Sledge to crash it into two of the castle's sentinel towers rather than lose it outright. Samantha, sent through the castle's cat-sized tunnels toward the security room, kills the five guards there without her sleep-gas potion (having stashed it with Clay-ton so it wouldn't clink) just as the talent show opens — and just as Queen Imogen materializes in the ballroom without warning, sealing the room shut for the rest of the party.

    Chapter 71 — No Way Out

    A bloodied Queen Imogen materializes in the ballroom — she'd been out fighting personally — and confirms the room's peace seal has locked everyone inside for good, herself included, at exactly the moment neither side can safely make a first move. Word arrives that Gideon's whole distraction team has been killed and the Twister destroyed, while Samantha discovers Vrah's own inside agent has already sabotaged the hunters' ballroom and is trying to do the same to theirs — the enemy's real plan, Carl realizes, is Circe Took herself, disguised among the mantis nymphs and coordinating with her daughter to collapse the goodwill system entirely. With every backup plan failing, Carl privately resolves to sacrifice himself and Eva to break the seal, only for Signet to insist on a better option: casting her tattoo-summoning spell using herself as caster, sacrifice, and executioner all at once.

    Chapter 72 — Signet's Sacrifice

    Donut's charm song All Eyes on Me and Big Tina's recital wand hold the entire ballroom transfixed as Sledge begins the five-minute Zerzura cast inside the besieged castle. Signet slits her own throat in front of her horrified sister, shattering Apito's protection and ending the Masquerade's non-violence spell for good — her final act framed explicitly as a message to the watching galaxy that her family, real or not, mattered. Carl marks Vrah with the ring, tosses smoke bombs, and gives the signal.

    Chapter 73 — Sic 'Em

    The seal breaks and everything erupts at once: Kiwi's disguised velociraptors leap off the stage into the hunters, Mongo and Samantha bring down Vrah for good, and Signet's animated tattoo-warriors materialize at full, terrifying scale to hold off Queen Imogen while mantis nymphs pour up through the shattered floor. Imogen retaliates with a room-wide lightning blast and nearly kills Carl with a bone-snapping telekinetic grab before Ferdinand — now genuinely loyal — tackles her and is savagely backhanded for his trouble. Donut's I Take It All Back potion cures Kiwi of her transformation mid-battle, but Tina, seeing her mother restored, can't follow — she remains locked in allosaurus form. Sledge finishes casting Zerzura in the nick of time, teleporting the surviving changelings and the still-half-destroyed castle straight to the ninth floor as everyone left behind freezes in midair.

    Chapter 74 — Cage Fight Extreme

    With the castle gone, everyone remaining — crawlers, hunters, Queen Imogen, and the newly-exposed, still-mid-birth Diwata — is rebranded by the AI as a four-way boss battle royale, plummeting together into the castle's exposed, mantis-choked basement pit as "Thunderstruck" blares. Donut's maxed Laundry Day spell strips Diwata's divine armor away, letting Circe Took's own newborn mantis children tear their mother apart in a berserk feeding frenzy. Imogen falls to a coordinated, beat-synced barrage of blunt-force attacks, finished off by Prepotente smashing a potion directly into her face.

    Chapter 75 — We Are Goddamned Alive

    The cost of the battle is brutal: Firas, Gwen, Horton, and both Popov brothers are killed outright (though the Popovs' nodling deaths split them into two rescued, ineligible infant crawlers who are immediately pulled from the dungeon), Tran loses both legs, and Britney's face is permanently scarred saving the group with her butterfly jacket. Katia finds and finishes off a buried, barely-alive Eva off-page, earning a second player-killer skull. In the shaky aftermath, Carl talks Donut through her guilt over not grieving hard enough, the group claims two open Faction Wars slots for The Princess Posse (Carl and Donut's own newly-sponsored team) and Princess D'Nadia's Prism Kingdom, and Carl uses his last moments on air to publicly shame the other faction-wars sponsors into a vote on removing the "safety protections" that keep non-crawler teams from real danger.

    Epilogue — The Great Race

    Floor 6 collapses into Floor 7, "The Great Race," dropping every crawler into an individual glass starting pod arranged in a spiraling stack of stairwell portals. Prepotente, having pieced the floor's true nature together from his late mother's item-lore boots and an overheard aside on a CrawlCon panel, deliberately shatters his own pod's reinforced glass with a hammer-and-rod combo — collapsing the entire level's Prince Rupert's Drop-style maze structure before it can properly begin, over the AI's own panicked, in-voice objections. Squads are capped at five and locked for the floor. Given ninety seconds to choose a starting location on a spinning globe of Earth, Donut — squad leader by default — picks the Caribbean instead of the intended Bahamas, to Carl's exasperation. Elsewhere, a shaken Katia hunts down and personally kills Eva, only for Eva's dying hand to trigger a Crown of the Sepsis Whore hidden on her own armor — the same crown Donut lost back on Floor 3 — which binds itself to Katia's bare head instead (the protective hat that would have stopped it is the one she'd handed off to Carl mid-battle), meaning only one of them will now be allowed to leave the ninth floor.
