    Chapter 1 — Welcome to the Bubbles

    Carl, Donut, Katia, and Mongo step onto Floor 5 — "the Bubbles" — into a sandstorm on the air quadrant of Bubble #543, a domed desert world sitting somewhere above the Necropolis of Anser. Donut's Character Actor benefit locks in the Glass Cannon subclass, trading further Constitution training for a flat +15 base and cheaper, stronger spells. The party fights through the storm to Hump Town, a brothel settlement of shapeshifting Dromedarian camels, and gets fleeced by a toll-collecting guard named Clay before Mordecai — reincarnated as a clipped, flightless skyfowl — meets them inside. The floor's rules drop: each of 1,172 sealed bubbles hides four quadrant castles, and a stairwell won't open until all four throne rooms fall. Mordecai points out their target: a barely-visible speck near the bubble's ceiling — the gnomish dreadnaught Wasteland, floating miles overhead.

    Chapter 2 — Sticky Feet and Stat Points

    Back in the personal space, the party opens loot boxes held over from killing Grull. Carl receives Pawna's Tears (a legendary potion adding +5 to any skill of his choosing), an Extinction Sigil tattoo that both pacifies and endangers him around lizard-class mobs, and the two-benefit Enchanted Toe Ring of the Leprous Bandit (Sticky Feet plus Super Spreader). New personal-space upgrades — food synthesizers and a marketplace interface — come online, the latter bought under Donut's name so her Charisma bonus applies to prices; Mordecai warns Carl to keep the still-hidden Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook out of his inventory whenever he uses it, since past owners who didn't have vanished. Over chat, land-quadrant crawler Gwendolyn Duet introduces herself and her castle target, the Sandcastle of the Mad Dune Mage.

    Chapter 3 — Hump Town Recruits

    Carl recruits the bubble's other crawlers: drunk, hapless Louis Santiago 2 and Firas M at the Spit and Swallow, and a party of six Finnish car salesmen turned identical Archers led by Langley. A dromedarian explains the local day cycle — two suns, a two-hour night, and an approaching Red Equinox that will lengthen the daily sandstorms. Scouting toward the Bactrian town, the party kills its first Thorny Devil and loots a dead Feral Goose from a dud gnome "egg" bomb — a scripted-feeling clue about the Wasteland's ordnance. A gnomish Drop Bear biplane strafes them with unopened bomb-eggs, and a second wave of eight aircraft forces a retreat back to Hump Town, the trip to the Bactrians abandoned for the day.

    Chapter 4 — The Royal Chariot

    Carl and Katia build the Royal Chariot, a tracked, portable ATV assembled at the engineering and metalworking tables, while Katia marvels at how her Earth Hobby potion feeds her mechanical knowledge without her truly understanding it. Field-testing it outside Hump Town, they watch in horror as the Wasteland, off its expected schedule, bombs the Bactrian town to rubble instead of returning to open water. That night's recap episode reveals the fate of crawler Ifechi: cornered at a stairwell station by the deranged Lucia Mar, her partner Florin unknowingly shoots himself and both her dogs after Lucia reflects his shotgun blasts with a hidden Rubber-type spell, killing Ifechi by accident and earning Florin an unwanted player-kill skull. The new top-10 list puts Carl at #2 behind Lucia Mar; Katia has fallen off entirely.

    Chapter 5 — The Rapid Detox Cure

    As the second, hotter sun crosses the sky, Katia scouts Town Hall's back entrance disguised as a coolant pipe, spotting armed guards and a changeling named Henrik. With direct infiltration too risky, Mordecai forces sobriety on Louis and Firas with a nasty Rapid Detox potion so they can help with the break-in — revealing along the way that Louis's throwaway Cloud of Exhaust spell is actually a legendary-tier mob-stunning enchantment he's been squandering by fleeing every fight instead of following up on it, and that he drove into the dungeon in his mother's roofless "Tiddy Twister" minivan. Firas's Puddle Jumper teleport, at level 10, becomes the team's ticket onto Town Hall's second-floor balcony.

    Chapter 6 — The Changeling Principals

    Puddle-jumping onto the balcony, Carl's team breaks into Town Hall using Donut's Hole spell to punch through locked doors, discovering the "camel" guards are actually Changeling Principals — a cult obsessed with collecting rare forms — who revert to a faceless, glowing-headed shape when knocked out. Carl and the recruits kill two of them, the first real kills for Louis and Firas. A trapdoor leads to a hidden catacomb chamber where Carl loots a full map of the Necropolis of Anser and finds the true hostage: Wynne, an elderly Dirigible Gnome Flesh Mechanic chained up as the dromedarians' bombing-truce collateral. Before Carl can free him, five rival crawlers from the subterranean quadrant stumble in hunting a lost map of their own — and one of them, Low Thi, impulsively spears Wynne to death, instantly failing the rescue quest.

    Chapter 7 — The Grulke Decoy

    A camel rocket blows the barricaded door apart, setting Town Hall ablaze around Carl, Louis, and Firas. Carl blasts the hallway with a hob-lobber, Donut punches an escape hole through the exterior wall, and a summoned Grulke toad decoy — one of Bautista's beanbag-toy tokens — draws the pursuing camels off as a fake culprit before puddle-jumping the group to safety. Katia, waiting outside in disguise, helps sell the cover story to a passing dromedarian as the building collapses behind them. A camel named Emerald reveals the town's gnome-truce depends on daily "proof of collateral," now gone. At the Desperado Club, Carl meets subterranean survivors Morris Sp and Bobby D.J., learns of the ghost Quetzalcoatlus and the tomb's brutal trap-maze, and has Katia begin drawing them a map. All three crawlers receive a second sponsor, including Donut's new toy-company benefactor, Veriluxx RealPet Companions.

    Chapter 8 — Gifts from a Pacifist Network

    Back at base, Carl opens two new benefactor boxes: the Valtay Corporation's second neural-enhancer pill (a UI upgrade showing exact elevation and airspeed) and a nearly worthless Toraline Root Vegetable from a brand-new, mysterious sponsor, the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network. Using the new altitude readout, Carl calculates the Necropolis of Anser towers roughly nine kilometers above the water quadrant — taller than Everest. Hump Town locks down under full guard once word spreads its own hostage may be dead. Juice Box, for a gold coin, explains the borough boss Ruckus: a half-organic, half-mechanical "chicken hawk sentinel" left behind by the long-dead bugbear treasure hunter Shamus Chaindrive, who once sought a legendary artifact called the Gate of the Feral Gods inside the tomb. Carl and Katia head out to field-test the Chariot's new missile launcher against her.

    Chapter 9 — Ruckus

    Carl steals Skarn's gnomish farseer telescope while distracting the boy, and Donut's new benefactor upgrade turns her sunglasses into a heat-vision tool for spotting disguised changelings. Scouting Ruckus through the scope, Carl learns her true master's obsession with the Gate of the Feral Gods — then a glitchy System Message: Please Wait freezes the world, a sign the AI is quietly reshuffling loot around the newly-revealed artifact. The first test missile burns Carl on launch and detonates short of target; the second volley instead wakes Ruckus, triggering a full boss battle as she and her signature spinning multi-buzzsaw weapon bear down on the Chariot at a dead sprint. Ruckus dies in a triple-missile salvo, Katia saving the Chariot from flipping with a last-second counterweight trick, and a bubble notification reveals the water quadrant's throne room has already fallen — to fellow crawler Chris Andrews 2, Brandon's estranged brother.

    Chapter 10 — Squeeze the Juice Box

    Carl relays Chris's dark turn to Katia: banned from the Desperado Club after killing Frank Q in cold blood. Back in Hump Town, Carl bargains with Henrik, the changeling posing as the dromedarian elder holding Wynne's collateral seat, learning the daily "proof of life" code the gnomes use, and improvises a wild rescue plan once he realizes he can fake it. Mordecai can't brew Feather Fall but concocts something worse, Dolores Doesn't Splat, for the coming fall. Carl shelters town children — including the deformed changeling girl Ruby, suffering "Compression Sickness" — inside the personal space as the countdown to bombing ticks toward zero.

    Chapter 11 — The Vahana

    Loot boxes yield 25 more Surefire guidance chips, a Jellyfish Salve, and an Enchanted Roll of Never-ending Duct Tape — which Carl immediately puts to grim use. The team marches Wynne's duct-taped corpse out to meet the Wasteland's black-and-gold Vahana, a ceremonial "Legate Balloon" once used for peace envoys before the dirigible gnomes turned to war. Aboard it are three gnomes: sniper Crixus, pilot Hicks, and negotiator Leon, sent to verify the collateral and retrieve Kane's uncle. Carl's plan hinges on convincing them Wynne survived a resurrection spell gone wrong, using a stolen watch replica, a body-double Henrik, and Donut playing a traveling healer princess — with Katia, in full triple-shapeshift as chariot, seat, and corpse, providing the disguise for everyone at once.

    Chapter 12 — Dolores Doesn't Splat

    Donut, in character as "Princess Donut the famed healer," cons ambassador Leon into believing zombified Wynne is merely suffering post-resurrection sickness while Firas stages Henrik's murder using a hidden changeling corpse Carl had been saving for Mordecai. Leon casts a changeling-detection spell on everyone present — Katia, disguised as the cart's chair, passes undetected — and Carl produces a hand-carved fake pocket watch as proof of good faith. The deal is struck: Wynne and Donut go up with the gnomes, and balloons will return for the rest of the crew. Carl privately administers a bottle of Mountain Dew as the "healing potion," and the team boards the Vahana for its climb toward the Wasteland. At altitude, Donut's Hole spell drops Leon, Crixus, and zombie-Wynne (really Katia) through the basket floor while Langley's archers missile-barrage the escort fleet below. Carl and Katia fly the hijacked Vahana straight at the dreadnaught, tanking anti-air fire behind a Protective Shell and launching Carl's remaining guided missiles at its Knock-Knock bombs before leaping clear and drinking Mordecai's horrific Dolores Doesn't Splat potion, which slams all three into the ground repeatedly rather than let them die from the fall. Quest Squeeze the Juice Box completes as the Wasteland breaks apart over Hump Town — and through his stolen farseer, Carl spots the one piece that survives intact: a crashed suburban house dangling from a single balloon, with Commandant Kane and his young daughter still aboard.

    Chapter 13 — Frenzied Gerbils

    Trudging back to Hump Town through drifting wreckage, Carl loots fallen gnome corpses — earning a "Cannonball" achievement for surviving his own fall — and realizes the round Live Ammo Balls scattered everywhere all contain the same thing: caged Frenzied Gerbils, a not-so-subtle hint that the system AI is still furious he hasn't fought one bare-handed since Ralph on Floor 2. When Carl refuses to take the bait, thousands of the balls reactivate at once and swarm the town, forcing an all-out melee that nearly overruns Hump Town's defenders before Carl finally stomps one underfoot and the assault stops as abruptly as it started. That night's loot brings a Platinum Sharpening Stone, a magical scroll-writing kit, and the first exploding Robot Donut merchandise toy, destroyed by Mongo within minutes. As the dromedarian children finish their movie and filter out of the personal space, Chris Andrews 2 arrives at the gate.

    Chapter 14 — An Old Friend, Infiltrated

    Towering over Carl, Chris Andrews 2 is now an Igneous Zulu Warrior who can't use chat or inventory without taking damage. His answers grow steadily more wrong — he claims two daughters when only one exists — until Carl realizes he's not talking to Chris at all: it's Maggie My, Frank Q's widow, who has infested him as a Scree Infiltrator parasite. A crossbow bolt from a returning Katia petrifies "Chris" mid-attack; Mordecai explains Infiltrators seize a living host completely, leaving the real person trapped and conscious inside. With no cure on hand and the countdown ticking, Donut drops the paralyzed body through a Hole-spell trapdoor into the sealed catacombs below rather than let Carl carry out a mercy kill — buying time, but leaving Chris and Maggie both alive and trapped together underground.

    Chapter 15 — The Gate's Three Pieces

    Juice Box confirms she's Henrik's sister and lays out the full stakes: the Changeling Principals need Quetzalcoatlus's ancient spell to fix Compression Sickness, the birth-defect plague slowly killing their people, and the legendary Gate of the Feral Gods — a three-piece artifact split between Henrik, Commandant Kane, and a "Mad Dune Mage" on the surface — can open a portal home, but only at the cost of releasing an ancient, insane god into the world. Carl receives the floor's true endgame quest: collect all three pieces. As the day's sandstorm hits, the party repurposes the captured gnomish Drop Bear biplane, freshly repainted as the Nightmare II, to launch a direct assault on the crashed remains of the Wasteland's last intact structure — a suburban house still hanging from a massive magic balloon near the top of the bubble.

    Chapter 16 — Denise the Feral Goose Mother

    Landing on the crashed flying house, Carl and Donut meet Bonnie, Commandant Kane's ten-year-old daughter, calmly setting up a lemonade stand beside her father's mauled corpse. The house triggers a boss battle against Denise, the Feral Goose Mother — an environmental boss immune to both magic and physical damage, who nearly kills Mongo and blinds Carl with a shotgun-like beak attack. Realizing Denise can only be hurt by ordinary household objects, Carl clobbers her with dishware, then shoves her head into the kitchen sink and flips on the garbage disposal with one second left on the clock. The Air Quadrant falls, but not before the AI reveals the whole "drink the lemonade" clue was a deliberate trap — the potion was never anything but lemonade.

    Chapter 17 — Bonnie

    With the goose dead, Carl claims the house's Soul Crystal power source and spends hours looting the wrecked home — family photos, a dead couple's bedroom, a stash of tools and electronics — while Bonnie, still under her father's calming potion, cleans up around his corpse. Katia opens up about wanting children before her transformation and being disqualified from Icelandic adoption, admitting she's relieved now, given what became of Maggie My. A new Robot Donut merchandise toy — heavier and slightly sturdier — survives one Mongo attack before dying anyway, and Loita drops Zev entirely from Carl and Donut's account ahead of the upcoming Veriluxx infomercial. The floor's new top-ten list puts Carl at #2 behind Lucia Mar, with three quadrants down and only the land-based Sandcastle of the Mad Dune Mage left standing.

    Chapter 18 — The Disco Ball Incident

    Experimenting at his sapper's table, Carl infuses a smoke curtain with an invisibility potion, accidentally inventing a "Hobgoblin Disco Ball" that fills the crafting room with blinding, multicolored, cotton-candy-scented smoke when Robot Donut knocks it to the floor. Freshly dusted in residue, Carl and Donut are teleported to an underwater production trailer for the Veriluxx toy infomercial, where admin Loita — seated on a couch, soaked by her rebreather — unleashes a venomous rant about human "filth" polluting kua-tin culture, revealing just how deep Borant's internal factional hatred of crawlers runs, and mentioning offhand what was done to coerce Zev back into loyalty. During the taping, Carl needles the toy company's hosts about Veriluxx's ties to a pro-slavery lending fund, buying time for the disco-ball residue and Loita's rebreather moisture to corrode Robot Donut's cheap self-destruct panel — which Carl has secretly rigged to fail on cue with a filed-down fingernail. The toy jumps off the couch to follow Loita and detonates, killing her and nearly killing Carl and Donut too.

    Chapter 19 — Orren

    Summoned before Orren, a Syndicate Liaison investigating admin deaths, Carl bluffs his way through an interrogation that correctly reconstructs almost the entire assassination — Orren reveals fifteen admins have died this season already, all engineered so cleverly the killers were never provably at fault — and lets Carl go with an ominous warning that higher powers intervened on his behalf.

    Chapter 20 — Gwendolyn Duet

    Five days pass in an eyeblink for Carl and Donut, but Katia and the others didn't stand still: they moved the entire flying house to the land quadrant (severing its top floor to free the stairwell closet), fought off feral pazuzu and giant scorpion-men, and Katia earned her own top-ten slot. Reunited, Carl finally meets Gwendolyn Duet face to face — a blunt, battle-scarred fighter who's been holding Katia's team together — and learns the sandcastle's outer walls are breached but the interior remains a maze. Carl also catches up on the wider dungeon: Miriam Dom has been forcibly turned into a vampire, Lucia Mar's rottweiler Gustavo "accidentally" massacred a group of crawlers, and Florin has emerged from his grief to keep fighting.

    Chapter 21 — The Glass Castle

    A lightning strike on rigged tower cables briefly opens a magical doorway into the Sandcastle of the Mad Dune Mage, transforming the entire structure into a hollow, glass-and-crystal ruin frozen mid-collapse. Inside, Carl, Katia, and Donut find a crystallized fountain statue of a half-naiad woman with an inscription from "the glass wizard" swearing to move heaven and earth for his love, before Mongo accidentally knocks it over and shatters it. Carl loots the intact glass head and a stash of decayed potions before the group finds the boss chamber: spying on a slovenly, TV-obsessed mage through a peephole, Carl learns Ghazi summoned a sand-elemental wife out of a botched attempt to conjure a lust god and bring his fictional crush to life, accidentally freezing the whole castle mid-spell instead. When the ooze surges through the door, Carl steals Ghazi's winding-box artifact piece and a damning letter from his own college, then flees upstairs as the sandstorm's two-hour window ticks toward its close.

    Chapter 22 — Mrs. Ghazi

    As the ooze — officially revealed as the borough boss Mrs. Ghazi — slowly climbs the stairs after them, Carl executes a ceiling-crawl burn attack using his Sticky Feet ring and a jar of homemade incendiary gel to melt her core from above. The plan works, but the heat also collapses the castle's floor beneath them; Carl and Donut plunge through the necropolis and out into open ocean, right as Gwen's team floods the ruins to finish the job.

    Chapter 23 — Into the Water Quadrant

    Carl and Donut nearly drown before Donut's water-breathing scroll and a panicked kayak paddle get them to the pazuzu town of Pandinus, fending off a feeding frenzy of Concierge Sharks along the way with resurrected, exploding zombie-beluga decoys. Looting Ghazi's abandoned research reveals the true rules of the Gate of the Feral Gods: the two watches sync a destination, the winding box opens a one-way portal, and the opening side is left permanently vulnerable to whatever comes through from the Nothing on the other end — including, potentially, a way to open a gate straight to the ninth floor's city of Larracos. Loot boxes bring water-breathing rings and Donut's long-awaited Deflection buff, while Mordecai firmly vetoes any early floor-skipping schemes.

    Chapter 24 — Samantha

    Back at Hump Town, Gwen's crew finally drains the necropolis stairwell chamber — and the crystallization spell on Carl's looted glass head breaks with it, restoring the decapitated Lika love-doll head to its unfrozen, screaming, fully conscious state. Mordecai identifies her as a withering spirit: a soul split during a botched escape from the Nothing, now permanently trapped and nearly powerless. She's revealed to be Psamathe, "Samantha", a banished minor deity who tricked Ghazi into opening the portal in the first place, and who is, unnervingly, delighted to be free enough to threaten Carl's dead mother. Rather than destroy her, and risk drawing attention outside a saferoom, the party hires her as personal-space training staff.

    Chapter 25 — Diving the Akula

    With four and a half days left, Carl turns to the last piece of the puzzle: shutting off the flooding pump inside the sunken submarine Akula so the necropolis can be drained and Henrik's second watch retrieved. Water-quadrant survivors Vadim and Britney — the only ones left from Chris's original team — describe how Chris single-handedly cleared the sub's defenses before splitting off with the artifact-code note that led him to Hump Town. Carl, Katia (shaped into a diving-bell "elevator"), and swashbuckler Tran descend five hundred meters to the wrecked hull, past drifting jellyfish and an unseen, sail-shearing monster Katia glimpses circling just out of sight.

    Chapter 26 — Quetzalcoatlus

    Inside the flooded Akula, Carl finds the bodies of Henrik and six other Changeling Principals slaughtered on the bridge — Quetzalcoatlus's ghost got to them first — and loots the second pocket watch along with a torn journal page describing the "Wailing Shrieker" ghost type: born of grieving lovers, defeated only by destroying the corpse of their beloved, exorcism, or electric shock. Before Carl can process it, the glowing pterodactyl ghost herself attacks, and something colossal seizes the submarine and drags it, with Carl, Katia, Tran, and Vadim still aboard, straight down to the ocean floor, a full kilometer below the surface — where the culprit is revealed as Lusca, the Octo-Shark Brood Mother Queen, a Level 82 city boss whose live-broadcast fight is reframed as a comedic "Special Event" complete with cartoon diagrams and two obnoxious announcers. The submarine is swallowed whole, trapping all four of them inside her mouth alongside thousands of ravenous shark-pups.

    Chapter 27 — Lusca

    Carl spikes the water with a Potion of Bloodlust bomb to trigger a cannibalistic feeding frenzy among the babies, then fires a Protective Shell through Lusca's skull before Katia deploys the salvaged buzzsaw weapon as a makeshift battering ram, cleaving the boss's brain in two. Vadim escapes alone through the last hatch and is killed instantly by a pain-amplifier jellyfish that followed him up; Carl, Katia, and Tran claw their way back to the surface through a field of Concierge Sharks.

    Chapter 28 — Summary Judgment

    Back in the saferoom, Carl's haul includes a Ring of Water Breathing, the Enchanted Anarchist's Battle Rattle patch jacket, and Drakea's Enchanted Kerchief of Disorder — named for a cookbook contributor Mordecai reveals died helping bankrupt an entire naga faction through legal sabotage. Seven "Summary Judgment" boxes, awarded for surviving a lawsuit filed against Carl by rival factions, contain unnervingly personal loot: a single share of stock and a photograph of the mother of each of the seven plaintiffs who sued him, evidence the System AI is beginning to actively troll the sponsors on Carl's behalf. As the crew settles in to rest, Elle's panicked message arrives: a flaming giant "taller than the bubble" has just smashed through her already-popped world, screaming a single name — Orthrus.

    Chapter 29 — Where the Red God Glows

    A gate-crashed feral demon turns out to be nothing next to what's actually loose: Orthrus, a two-headed, celestial-tier puppy the size of the bubble itself, accidentally freed from the Nothing and drowning in the flooded necropolis while his true owner's grieving, blinded father — the fire god Emberus — rampages outside searching for him. Reusing the lightning-tower trick from the sandcastle raid, Carl's team finally kills the ghost Quetzalcoatlus for good, but only at the cost of every remaining tomb-raider crawler, including Morris and Low Thi, who die completing the connection underwater. Chris, briefly freed from Maggie's control long enough to warn that she has "outside help," is muted by the system before he can finish. With all four castles down, bubble #543 finally pops, just as Orthrus's health bottoms out.

    Chapter 30 — The Nightmare II

    With no working escape balloon on the flying house, Carl and Donut launch the damaged Nightmare II biplane off its garage ramp to heal the dying Orthrus mid-flight, buzzing dangerously close to top him off with two point-blank Heal Critter casts. Reading Donut's old Meat Hooks scroll — the same spell once used to lure Mongo into an ambush — the pair trail an unbearable stench behind the plane to draw the delighted, oblivious puppy out of the popped bubble entirely and into the dark, foggy Lacuna between worlds, dodging his massive attempts to swat their tiny aircraft along the way. Mid-chase, an unseen crawler snipes Orthrus from a distance, dropping his health bar — and Carl guesses the culprit before anyone confirms it: Quan Ch, the dungeon's most notorious glory-stealer, circling back to finish off the bounty.

    Chapter 31 — Worshipping Emberus

    Tran confirms it's Quan Ch, sniping Orthrus for the massive bounty on his head despite every crawler's threats; Carl leaps onto the biplane's wing mid-flight to land a Hobgoblin Disco Ball and hob-lobber combo that knocks Quan out of the sky. When Quan returns and nearly kills the unconscious puppy, Carl throws a Celestial Grenade — a Skull Empire assassination gift meant for him — while impulsively worshipping the fire god Emberus for the first time, summoning him directly to the scene and earning temporary invulnerability. Orthrus is reunited with his god-father and vanishes; Quan, thinking Carl still vulnerable, attacks again and gets his entire arm torn off at the shoulder for his trouble.

    Chapter 32 — Church Quests

    Carl's accidental Emberus worship comes with real obligations — daily blood offerings, a 5% gold tithe, a ban on Hellik-blessed gear — and two binding quests: identify who murdered Emberus's son Geyrun, and kill the god Hellik before reaching Floor 12. Looting Quan Ch's severed arm nets Carl the glory-stealing Rockard's Ring of Sniping and a new patch-jacket spell tome, Ping. With the survivor count falling under 100,000, Carl launches Phase One of an ambitious plan: using the recovered Gate of the Feral Gods to open one-way portals from other trapped, sealed bubbles straight to Hump Town's Desperado Club, evacuating hundreds of crawlers at the cost of releasing a fresh feral god into each newly-emptied bubble behind them.

    Chapter 33 — Larracos, Previewed

    Phase One's gate-rescues succeed beyond expectation, though most of the freed crawlers never learn what feral horror got left behind in their place; a couple of summoned gods clash and cancel each other out, one bubble is annihilated in a single grotesque explosion, and a giant feral turkey briefly terrorizes the Lacuna before being killed by Prepotente. Borant's official broadcast grudgingly approves of Carl's gate-escape method — while quietly patching the odds of it summoning an active, sponsored deity — and previews Floor 6's guild system and its first wave of paying "hunter" tourists, arriving to farm gear and bounty crawlers alike ahead of the ninth-floor faction wars. The floor's death toll keeps climbing past the halfway mark of the dungeon's surviving population.

    Chapter 34 — The Gate of the Feral Gods

    Katia announces she's splitting off next floor to protect Eva Sigrid's scattered "former daughters" before Eva can pick them off one by one, forming her own team with Gwen, Florin, Bautista, Louis, and Firas. In the final phase of the gate plan, Carl throws the possessed Samantha head fifty kilometers via a sponsor-gifted xistera attachment to lure away her demon rival Slit, who turns out to be killed by Samantha's own estranged, guilt-wracked mother. With Maggie finally cornered, Carl uses a Phase-Lava-Rock potion to reach through Chris's molten skull and rip out the Scree parasite by hand, ending her reign of terror for good. Then comes the true plan: using a decoy fake artifact to trick the system into flooding the ninth floor's Larracos marketplace with shark-infested seawater instead of summoning a god on the open battlefield, crippling seven of the nine warring factions' ability to resupply. Carl closes the floor as the number-one crawler in the dungeon, publicly vowing to kill every hunter who comes for him on Floor 6.

    Epilogue — Gravy Boat

    Aboard an unseen ship, Dr. Hu and a colleague from the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network debate whether their enormous, secretive investment in Carl is worth the risk, hinting his usefulness to their unnamed cause outweighs his instability. On Earth, illegal "Walk-On" bounty hunters Sadir and Gennrik stalk a refugee camp in the ruins of Seattle hunting a specific cat for a corporate collection bounty — and discover it belongs to a woman named Beatrice, Carl's ex, still grieving her real cat Princess and clinging to a feral stray she calls Ferdinand. A Borant-employed operative named Lexis, acting for Odette, kills one hunter, frames the other, and extracts Bea by force — confirming that Miss Beatrice, and the cat she rescued, are about to become part of the show.
