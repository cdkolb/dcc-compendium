    Species¶

    Sapient races and peoples encountered in the series — distinct from corporations/governments (see Factions & Organizations) and from in-dungeon businesses/clubs (see Dungeon Factions). Named monster-type creatures with their own dedicated stat blocks live under Mobs instead.

    Full pages¶

    • The Kua-Tin & The Bloom — aquatic species that owns the Borant Corporation, and its ruling political party
    • Primal — ancient, vanished precursor species; also a selectable crawler race (Carl's choice)
    • City Elves — banished High Elf offshoot; worship the skyfowl as angels
    • Changeling — shapeshifting species stranded on Floor 5; see also the Changeling Principals cult
    • High Elves — near-immortal ruling elf caste, first named in Book 2; shown in full on Floor 6
    • Naiad — river-dwelling species central to Tsarina Signet's Book 5 storyline
    • Were-castor — Chee genocide survivors turned were-beavers, allied with Tsarina Signet
    • Gondii / "Residuals" — homeless hyperspatial collective-mind race that infiltrates any world with a Primal Engine; Book 6's biggest species reveal
    • Hobbledehoy — proportionally small humans; Pater Coal's race
    • Ifrit — smoking, white-eyed demons drawn up from Sheol; Damascus Steel's race
    • Zebani — briefly-seen crawler race; Emir Akbas's race
    • Porsuk — badger-headed race; the Desperado Club's bartenders, plus the crawlers Burcu and Charles Ellis
    • Grixist — six-eyed, hairy race of the Grixist Swarm; Huanxin Jinx's race
    • Sai — bipedal rhinoceros samurai, ex-firefighters, guards of the Desperado Club's Larracos level
    • Ghommid — catch-all term for other-realm spirit creatures from the land of the dead, not a single race
    • Semeru Dwarves — builders of the castle of Larracos along the roots of the All Tree; the only worshippers of Ysalte; shown in full, with the Jatismara, in Book 7
    • Nullian — "the Null," the galaxy's grey aliens; Quasar's race, with his niece Tempest as Book 7's second on-page nullian
    • Bune — small winged dragon-people, "the elves of the lizard world"; a very common former-crawler race, and the Madness's adjutant Opiee
    • Tenodera — smaller, flightless mantis cousins bred for emotionlessness; York's race
    • Xenopus — winged, clawed frog-people; Milk's chosen crawler race
    • Vesper — Milk's bat-like birth race, named only in contrast to Xenopus
    • Sturnus — Volteeg's songbird birth race
    • Gargoyle — Volteeg's crawler race; stone-skinned, long-sleeping, and unable to sing
    • Tripod — basketball-bodied, three-legged, eye-covered and famously emotionless; Fopsy's race
    • Shadow Mimics — Book 8: near-undetectable sapient mimics that grow without limit in intelligence and power; the counter-species to the Changeling Principals, revealed via the boss Mitch

    Other Book 1 species¶

    Name Notes
    Goblins Antagonists of Floor 1; the Goblin Pass tattoo grants free passage through their territory at the cost of +20% damage from fairy-class enemies
    Orcish Supremacy (Tusklings) Ruling class are the Tusklings; Mordecai confirms them as a "real" alien species, not a human-actor caricature

    Other Book 2 species¶

    Name Notes
    Skyfowl Eagle-headed, flight-capable people; former rulers of the Over City; xenophobic toward "flightless" races; Mordecai's true form
    Crocodilian Thick-skinned, semi-aquatic; commonly work as muscle/enforcers (e.g. Clarabelle, the Desperado Club's door guard)
    Fathom Dwarves Mining-culture dwarves; traditionally strap trained salamanders to their hats when working underground
    Tigran Tiger-headed humanoid race; a rare, exclusive race choice (Daniel Bautista 2)
    Lajabless Extreme dual-form race — beautiful human by day (double spell power, half strength), skull-faced goat-legged by night (reverse); Lucia Mar's race
    Amazonian Bodybuilder-physique warrior race; Hekla's race choice
    Doppelganger Shapeshifters who must consciously "sculpt" their own appearance rather than transforming instantly; Katia Grim's race
    Half-Djinn Common hybrid race found on forested dungeon levels; Quint the Desperado Club pharmacist is one
    Forsoothed ("Soothers") Pale, large-eyed alien race; likely the historical basis for Earth's "Gray alien" abduction imagery, per one of their own (Evo)
    Saccathians ("Sacs") Squid/cephalopod-headed species; a common race across the galaxy (Princess D'nadia)
    Setonix ("Quokkas") Small, round, disarmingly cute alien race (talk-show host Ripper Wonton)
    Naga Serpent-headed species; the Blood Sultanate's ruling race (see Crown of the Sepsis Whore); the singer Manasa is Valtay-driven rather than a "real" living Naga
    Intellect Hunters Finger-sized parasitic worm creatures that can take over the bodies of creatures they kill; suspected (not confirmed) basis of the Valtay Corporation

    Other Book 3 species¶

    Name Notes
    Mantaur Centaur-bull warrior race devoted to the war god Grull; staff the Iron Tangle's trains as "engineers" — see Gore-Gore and the Mimic Rex Death Challenge
    Grulke A once-militaristic toad-warrior race, "hunted ruthlessly" by tunnel trolls; now mostly mercenaries and vagrants; Mordecai's Floor 4 form is a Grulke Infantry
    Mole Man Short, squat, mole-like humanoids who "actually just call themselves 'Men' and 'Women'"; Limp Richard, a Floor 4 shopkeeper, is one
    Grapple Humanoid Iron Tangle rank-and-file workers, alongside dwarves; conductors/porters on the colored lines
    Pterolykos Winged, wolf-like people encountered on Floor 4
    Bloodlust Sprite Small fae race; Astrid, the Desperado Club's Station-131 assistant manager, is one
    Cretin Rock-monster people; Bomo and The Sledge (Desperado Club bodyguards) are cretins — capable of casting purchased protection spells
    Grease Gremlin Small, black-furred, goblin-proportioned gnome-like race; staff the Iron Tangle's train-repair towers (e.g. Widget)
    Kravyad Six-armed, blue-skinned, naga-like Iron Tangle "human resources" staff who hypnotize and detain workers between shifts — see Iron Tangle Management
    Changbi Demonic-featured race with long, tool-like chain-arms; Li Na's race, class Slave Driver
    Wisteria Fairy Plump, elderly-presenting healer-type fae; Silfa of Brynhild's Daughters is one

    Other Book 4 species¶

    Name Notes
    Dromedarian Camel-people nomadic warrior race running Hump Town on Floor 5; can survive two months without water; in a long-standing stalemate with the Bactrians and the Dirigible Gnomes
    Bactrian Rival two-humped-camel race/town on Floor 5; wiped out by the Dirigible Gnomes early in the book after their gnome hostage is killed
    Dirigible Gnome Small, red-hatted airship-dwelling race; the "sky garden" builders of the dreadnaught fleet — see The Wasteland for the militarized faction they've become
    Igneous Rock-elemental humanoid race with a molten core; very hardy but loses the ability to freely use chat, inventory, or the health-pie menu without taking damage — Chris Andrews's Floor 5 race choice
    Arachnid Half-human, half-tarantula race; psionic-class-friendly — Morris Sp's race
    Kuhli Spotted, gecko-like race — Vadim Zbar's race
    Infiltrator (Scree) Aquatic brain-parasite race, distinct from Intellect Hunters/the Valtay — hijacks a living host's body entirely over several days, locking out the original mind rather than reanimating a corpse; Maggie My's race choice — see Changeling for Mordecai's full comparison
    Nude Glaber Undead, naked, mole-rat-headed people native to the Necropolis of Anser's upper settlements — see Mobs
    Pazuzu Scorpion-centaur race with a post-apocalyptic punk-rock aesthetic; residents of the Land Quadrant town of Pandinus

    Other Book 5 species¶

    Name Notes
    Ursine Bear-like humanoid race running a strict, puritanical theocracy on Floor 6, rebuilt around religious order after only their clerics survived Scolopendra's attack; Point Mongo (see Locations) is an Ursine settlement before Donut conquers it
    Bugbear Goblin/hobgoblin-tree engineer race prone to involuntary explosions; common in the floor's "Kapok district," rivals of the Ursine
    Mantis Insectoid hunter race with a head-collecting hive culture; split between the private, for-profit Dark Hive and the mantis system's actual government, the Burrowers — see Vrah and Circe Took
    Were-castor / Chee See Were-castor — survivors of a High Elf genocide, transformed into beaver-like were-creatures
    Yenk Ridged-forehead, bony-exoskeletoned alien race with three genders; adult males are exiled at maturity and become mercenaries/bounty hunters, forbidden from working alongside other adult males of their kind
    Gleener Human-sized, blue-tinged alien species resembling the Kua-Tin; Hurk, designer of the Desperado Club, is one. Book 6: Prime Minister Glory, head of the entire Syndicate, is a Gleener — described by Mordecai as "the boss of the entire Syndicate," and shown on-camera beside Kevin the announcer, which is noted as very unusual. He chairs the emergency Syndicate Council session in the epilogue and blocks the failsafe vote; his own children are among the wealth trapped in the Scolopendra Club. The Valtay's council representative is a worm inhabiting a Gleener body that "was once mother to the current Syndicate Prime Minister Glory" — Princess Formidable calls this "as inappropriate and bizarre as it was confusing, especially since the fish people all looked exactly the same." See The Syndicate Council
    Nodling Multi-headed race (2–6 heads, usually 2–3) that splits into that many new "yearling" creatures upon death; the Popov Brothers are one nodling, and Tsarina Signet's ogre allies (Areson, Apollon, Herman) are three separate nodling-split individuals from a single dead progenitor
    Dreadnought Red-skinned, horned faction-wars species/clan; withdrew from Faction Wars after a leader's wife was injured in the Larracos flood, leaving individual hunters unaffiliated
    Atoll Four-armed, three-fingered, mouthless alien hunter race that communicates via a sign-language translator; hunts to fund life-support costs for its small, non-citizen population
    Curupira Alien race with at least one Faction Wars-caliber crawler (Osvaldo); appearance not otherwise detailed
    Crest Hairless, eyebrow-less human-presenting alien race; several serve as Dream/Nebular Sin Patrol hunters
    Moon Elf A Dream-affiliated elf subrace — pale, scarred, often ex-crawler; Drick and the artillery specialist Akland are both Moon Elves

    Other Book 6 species¶

    Races named or shown on Floor 8 that don't yet warrant a full page. For the Faction Wars teams these races field, see Factions & Organizations.

    Name Notes
    Viceroy The people behind the Faction Wars team the Madness. Bulky bodies, long thin bone-white necks, and a blank white mask "like something out of a creepy anime" over a bone-white, demonic, tusked face. Their race is "somewhat of a mystery" — before Book 6 nobody had ever seen one die, so it was an open question in the cookbook whether they were even biological; one is decapitated on-page and bleeds red. Epitome Noflex notes he regenerates at sunset, at least until the games begin. They specialize in "horrific battlefield spells and necromancy" and are Faction Wars perennials. Off the playing field, their system is a destination for cheap medical procedures for non-citizens and people outside the inner system. Book 7: the anatomy under the robes is finally shown — when Architect Houston leaps up mid-hearing, "his robes flung back, revealing a horrific, bug-like body and six arms, two of which were holding a rifle." Later, unmasked: "the horrific, multi-limbed, half bug, half monster that he was." Their culture is not medical but scientific — "Viceroys have no passion for healing. Their passion is science. Understanding the universe. Understanding everything from the atom to the tree, no matter the cost" — and Houston is "the prime of the Viceroys not because he was elected so... but because he's made the most scientific discoveries." Their medical arm runs a Reaver-like debt trap: sign yourself over to "their post-biological form studies corp" and the bills are wiped. Note that many masked "viceroys" on the battlefield, including the invisible Monkeywrenchers, are ordinary mercenaries in Viceroy masks
    Reavers Machine people and the megacorporation/system government of the same name, fielding the Faction Wars team the Reavers under Warlord Fangs. Like the Nebulars, they began as several different biological races who augmented themselves with machines — but the Reavers "took it a step further and kept only their brains," which is why they look like robots. Shape-shifting at will; normally plastic-skinned android soothers, though their rep appears to Carl as a 1960s-movie tin robot for his benefit. They live on a Dyson Sphere. Once related to the Nebulars; frequent orc allies and multiple-time Faction Wars winners. Book 7: the citizenship-by-cybernetics system is laid out in full. Unless you are "a vat-born progeny of the Initial," Reaver citizenship — which carries profit-sharing and heavy financial benefits — requires losing your biological form first. That first upgrade is free; after that there are only two routes. Pay yourself, which almost nobody can ("the only companies smart enough to hire a moron who allowed the Reavers to cut off a perfectly good hand... are the space equivalent of a Wendy's or the interstellar carwash"), or work for the Reavers for free: food, housing, unlimited healthcare and one more upgrade per three-cycle deployment, dropped "feet-first into the fire" evicting settlers off land leases so the Reavers can expand their factories. Carl's rule of thumb from the AI: "If they have more than two or three cybernetics installed, you know they've voluntarily re-upped at least once." One mercenary, formerly Jake Tortokai, is 23 enhancements into "his 400-cycle journey to gain Reaver citizenship." The Initial is a physical chunk of Warlord Fang's brain kept on a Reaver world, "and every year, they peeled microscopic parts off it to build new citizens"
    Operatic Collective Opaque, quivering green blobs "shaped into three tiers of decreasing size, like a Jell-o mold from the 1970's," with mouths at the very top that speak upward into the air. They breed by splitting apart and can merge back into one another — their Faction Wars rep is "Voting Admin Cell Mass Number Three," three individuals in one body, including one named Hortense. Donut renames him "Green Jiggly." Their later representative, Commander Stockade, is eaten by sharks in the flooded Larracos. Book 7: consistently called "the slimes." Their collective has a formal identity-reset and re-absorption cycle: one Operatic who overreached on a spell "supposedly had to go for an identity reset after the crawl and was re-absorbed. Part of his essence eventually formed into Hortense Leadership Unit" — the Faction Wars warlord — which the AI offers as an explanation for his twitch, adding that "collective minds should really have learned by now how to peel off their own... impurities." Economically they are bankers, running the Operatic Banker's Federation and shown "bomb[ing] settlements back to oblivion if they couldn't pay their mortgages." Their armour is the Weeper-class tank
    Lemig Green, humanoid, "like a cross between a goblin and a nullian alien." The majority race of the Lemig Sortion, a genuinely direct democracy in which the whole population votes on everything and an 80% supermajority is needed to reverse any standing decision — which is why they can't quit Faction Wars despite having soured on it. Their actual leader is a different alien, "a fuzzy caterpillar-looking thing." Mordecai: their system is "in a constant state of disarray." Their Faction Wars rep, Luke, talks like a surfer. Book 7: their team competes as the Democratic Sortion and is the first eliminated. They are confirmed as goblins ("Corpse of Luke. Level 64 Lemig Goblin"), and their warlord Commander Stockade — the "giant, fuzzy thing... an upright caterpillar" with an aged-surfer voice — is the caterpillar leader Book 6 alluded to, not a Lemig himself. Luke was his partner; Stockade dies of grief and an Insanity spell. Victory's verdict on Syndicate politics, late in the book: "The council is just as bad as the Lemig Sortion these days. It's total gridlock." Note: the six-armed, insectoid body under robes seen in Book 7 belongs to the Viceroys, not the Lemig
    Mexx Disc-shaped robot race. U56KL — whom Donut immediately renames "Urkel" — runs the Faction Wars preproduction meetings, seating the warlords and logging action items. Book 7: mexx robots continue to run the warlord councils — seating the warlords, announcing accepted action items — but a different unit each time; Donut names one "Carla." No new physical or cultural detail is given, and the designation "U56KL"/"Urkel" does not recur
    Taurine The race that owns Hereford War Machines, Inc., a manufacturer "known to outfit some of the most elite mercenary squads in the galaxy." Donut's Book 6 personal shield, The Bull Rush, is theirs — a small opalescent longhorn-cow pin. No physical description of the Taurine themselves is given. Book 7: effectively nothing new. A single "taurin mercenary" dies in the assault on the Madness castle, and Hereford is not mentioned at all
    Lutin Gnome-like race specializing in support magic. Randall, one of Imani's squad of former Meadowlark residents, chose it as his crawler race; he dies during a totem battle after a possession attack
    Wisp Crawler race; Yasmine, a level 75 Wisp who worships the goddess Áine, is the secretary of the Club Vanquisher Temple Portal in Book 6
    Hills Cyclops Mordecai's own Floor 8 transformation form (level 50), not a separate NPC race in this book — large, pink, bald, with a single blinking eye in a cone-shaped head. Per the AI: the weakest and dumbest of the cyclops-style monsters, usually shepherds, "always disheveled because they can't see their own reflections in mirrors," and given to bar fights and drinking contests
    Pocket Kuma Likewise a Mordecai transformation form, the "tiny, cute creature" he wore on Floor 7, referenced in Book 6 only in contrast to his much larger Hills Cyclops body. Neither form represents an NPC species Carl meets on this floor

    Other Book 7 species¶

    Races named or shown on Floor 9 that don't warrant a full page of their own. For how the peoples behind the teams actually fought, see The Faction Wars Campaign.

    Name Notes
    Dreadnaught Recurs. Panford, a "tall, purple-skinned creature," is the adjutant for the Democratic Sortion and chairs the minor quorum that rules on Commander Stockade's cheating. He is the one who calls the foul: "I asked you where you got your information, and you refused to answer. All spies must be disclosed to adjutants"
    Grixist Recurs. Deng Bao, the grixist who served as the orcs' adjutant, is later reassigned to the War Mage Rebellion — "He's the only one who would do it." Huanxin Jinx, CEO of Icon Industries, plays the Ascendency through a sponsored goddess and is killed on-page at Victory's feet
    Crest Recurs heavily. Tipid, one of the returning former crawlers and eventual colonel of the 104th, is a crest; he settled in a Crest colony after his release and worked for a waste-management company there. The Homecoming Queen is a Crest behemoth
    Saccathian Recurs. Empress D'Nadia of the Prism is a saccathian; she is the second warlord eliminated, killed by Mongo
    Naga Central. The Blood Sultanate's ruling race, fielding Princess Vinata as the Sepsis Whore, with her husband Rishi as her second. Their system is now "a tourist destination and a location where other, non-Reaver governments built their factories and shipyards because of the cheap labor," and their in-orbit shipyards are the object of the naga strand of The Great Consensus
    Changeling Central, as Team Retribution's spy and officer corps under Juice Box. Book 7 also introduces a formal classification of "class A and B NPC changelings"
    Skyfowl Recurs. Justice Light, the one-winged black-feathered trap master and eighth-edition cookbook author, is the book's most prominent skyfowl — "shorter and stockier than most," with "a twin tuft of feathers upon his head [giving] the impression he had horns"
    Crocodilian Recurs. Clarabelle still holds the Desperado Club door; Bucket Boy, a former Penis Parade attendant, and Dante, Justice Light's executed friend, are both crocodilians
    Porsuk Briefly recurs; no new development
    Caprid Recurs via Prepotente and the Plenty
    Ursine Briefly recurs; no new development
    Mantis / Mantid Recur, mostly off-page and catastrophically — Everly is a mantis crawler murdered by her own sponsor, and the mantis system is destroyed by the AI. See Tenodera for their smaller cousins
    Yenk, Mantaur, Cretin, Were-castor, Dromedarian, Bactrian, Wisp, Nodling, Tigran, Doppelganger All recur as soldiers, guards or named minor characters without new species-level development

    Races the brief expected that do not appear

    Setonix ("quokkas"), Lutin, Igneous, Atoll, Moon Elf, Hobbledehoy, Ifrit, Zebani, Amazonian and Lajabless have no Book 7 mentions worth recording. Ripper Wonton is named once in passing. Their entries above under Books 1–6 stand unchanged.
